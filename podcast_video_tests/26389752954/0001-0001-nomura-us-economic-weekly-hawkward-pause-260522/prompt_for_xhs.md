# Hawkward Pause

- We now expect the Fed to remain on hold indefinitely (versus our prior forecast of two cuts in September and December). Incoming Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is likely still motivated to cut rates. However, we expect easing political pressure, elevated inflation, and strong cyclical growth to support a prolonged pause.   
- Data have remained relatively hawkish lately. Growth has remained robust due to strong investment and steady consumption. Labor markets appear to be stabilizing, while both survey and hard data point to building price pressures.

We now expect the Fed to keep policy rates unchanged indefinitely, versus our previous forecast for two cuts in September and December (Fig. 1 & 2).

Political pressure for near-term rate cuts has diminished recently. Tariff inflation has begun to fade, but new price pressures have emerged from the Iran war and global memory chip shortages. Additionally, although we think Warsh would be inclined to ease policy, support among Fed officials for an easing bias has begun to wane.

He expressed concerns about the breadth of increases in consumer prices and sharp increases in PPI.
