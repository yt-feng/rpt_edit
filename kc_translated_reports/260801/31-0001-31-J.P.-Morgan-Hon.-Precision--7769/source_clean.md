## Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation

## Investment Thesis

We are OW Hon. Precision (Hon). Hon is the global No.1 player in semiconductor test handlers, with a 35%+ market share, with an 80% revenue mix from AI & HPC. Given volume and spec uptrends for AI & HPC chips, we model 41% and 17% CAGRs for handler shipments and ASP in 2025-28, driving 64% and 66% CAGRs for both revenue and EPS. We believe the Street underestimates its multi-year ASP expansion potential amid rapid test spec upgrades. Hon is also a CPO enabler/beneficiary and is becoming a handler total solution provider. We like Hon's EPS power, re-rating potential and enabler role in future AI chip tests.

Overall speaking, it seems Hon's own equipment development remains on track and will not disappoint vs market expectations.

\- Unchanged capacity increase targets to meet robust OSAT capacity increase. Hon's equipment shipment value increased by $>45\%$ YoY in 1H26. Hon's capacity constraints pushed out some deliveries from 2H26 to 1Q27. Hon maintained its prior guidance of shipment value growth $>50\%$ YoY in 2027, but attributed it to three sources for better granularity: $\sim 15\%$ from the Hota Group plant acquisition (ramping in late 26 or early 27), $\sim 20\%$ from its China plant (local manufacturing for local demand) and $\sim 15\%$ from its three existing Taichung plants through layout optimization. Hon also highlighted several, major OSAT capacity expansion projects over the next 18 months, including an IDM's backend factory in Thailand (TAM: 300-500 units for Hon), a Taiwan OSAT's U.S. facility, two Taiwan OSATs' Singapore facilities, multiple China OSAT projects, and a Korea OSAT's U.S. facility.

\- FT handlers (70-80% of business): outlining multiple growth drivers. Hon reiterated that Tesla's AI chips use the highest-spec handlers, supported by three OSATs, with order momentum expected to remain strong from 2H26 through 2027. Hon expects mass-volume deliveries for TPU projects to 3–5 OSATs in Taiwan and Singapore in 2H26, while a U.S. TPU customer has begun planning its 2027 procurement. Hon has also completed qualification for a next-generation server CPU program for an Austin-based GPU customer, with mass-volume shipments targeted for 2027. Moreover, as discussed earlier, China, an IDM's Thailand factory, and the large-package platform are also key growth drivers.

\- SLT handlers (20-30% of business): reiterating convictions in order wins. Hon noted it has started shipments for the Austin-based GPU customer's current-generation server CPU. For the same customer, Hon continues to work on qualification for future GPU projects (i.e. Hon working to improve the cost structure which Hon identified as a major shortcoming vs its peer). For Nvidia GPU project, Hon has completed the first stage of qualification and continues to refine performance. Notably, Hon is developing and qualifying for a major U.S. CSP's next-generation and next-next-generation ASICs, and highlighted the critical need to support up to \~10kW thermal capability by 2028. Moreover, as discussed earlier, Tesla and China are also key growth drivers. Bottom line, there are no confirmed order wins/win-backs in GPU SLT handlers, but its progress over the mid to long term remains a key watch point, in our view. Last, we think SLC handler order competition for AMD's next-generation server CPU projects could be a swing factor for sentiment in the next 6 months.

\- Formidable 2027 growth drivers. Hon expects U.S. semiconductor onshoring to generate meaningful incremental demand from multiple U.S.-based OSATs and end customers, while China AI continues to drive an even higher growth rate. Within Hon's

AI order exposure, management expects ASICs to surpass GPUs in 2027. On key spec migrations, customer roadmaps for higher thermal capability remain intact, with engineering targets at multiple customers moving toward \~10kW in 2027-28. Volume shipments of the large-package ATC handler platform should soon become a meaningful double-digit portion of shipments. Finally, CPO test insertion 4 optical-electrical co-test handlers could carry very high ASPs with a few volumes - effectively an option value for the company. Of note, on pricing, Hon sees limited scope for like-for-like price increases on existing shipments; instead, ASP and profit should lift primarily from new equipment with higher specs.

\- 2Q26 results mixed. EPS of NT\$30.4 beat JPMe and consensus by 4-5%, driven mainly by revenue of NT\$13.8bn (+29% QoQ, +100% YoY). Gross margin was 56.1%, within the company's 55–60% target range, but below JPMe and consensus expectations of \~57.0%. Opex of NT\$1.1bn was higher than expected, reflecting a one-off employee compensation charge of \~NT\$170mn from treasury shares awarded to key R&D staff; the company now has no remaining treasury shares.

\- Implications - much improved entry point. We raise our 2026E, 27E and 28E EPS by 3%, 4% and 9% to NT\$129, NT\$220 and NT\$347. We maintain OW and raise our Jun-27 PT to NT\$8,350, based on a 38x 2027E P/E, in line with its average of plus 1STDs of 39x since Dec-25, when the company started trading on the TWSE Main Board.
