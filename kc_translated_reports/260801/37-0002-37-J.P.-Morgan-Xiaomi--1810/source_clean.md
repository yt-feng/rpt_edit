# JPM
## Xiaomi (1810)

New Extended Range EV launched as expected; later delivery ramp implies FY26 EV delivery target is at risk

\- Xiaomi launches a new Extended Range SUV series under a new brand, with interior space as the key focus: Xiaomi unveiled a new EV brand, SkyNomad, and debuted two new EREV SUVs (the N90 Max, a 7-seater, and the N70 Max, a 5-seater) under the SkyNomad brand at its car launch event yesterday, with standard versions to be released later. Pricing is largely in line with our expectations (presale prices of \~RMB300K and \~RMB260K, respectively), slightly below the YU7 Max and Pro at \~RMB330K and \~RMB280K. The key selling point of the new SUVs is their large and scalable interior space.

\- Lack of initial pre-order data from management points to uncertainty around customer response: While pre-orders opened at 9pm HKT on July 30, management has not disclosed initial pre-order figures so far for the new series, which points to uncertainty around customer feedback. This is a slight negative signal in our view, as management has typically highlighted strong lock-in or pre-order numbers in the past (the SU7 facelift secured 15K locked-in orders in 34 minutes, and the YU7 achieved 122K locked-in orders within just two minutes).

\- Achieving the 550K EV shipment target appears more challenging as we head into 2H26: Management indicated that the new EREV SUVs will only be officially delivered in September. Xiaomi shipped \~185K EVs in 1H26, running short of its full-year target of 550K, implying it needs to ship 60K+ EVs per month to hit the goal—above its record monthly shipment of \~50K in December 2025. We believe some investors had expected strong momentum and high market interest in the new SUVs to help Xiaomi reach its EV target. However, we now think the company is likely to eventually revise down its EV shipment target, given the new EREV will only ship in 4Q26 and initial customer interest remains uncertain.

\- Fundamental improvement remains elusive, stay Neutral: Xiaomi outperformed the HSTECH index by 36% in July, driven by short covering amid weak AI and semiconductor stock sentiment (given Xiaomi stock has been positioned as a hedge against AI within China Tech stocks), as well as some anticipation ahead of the new SUV launch in late July. We believe the stock has moved off its trough valuation levels (10–15x PE, or HK\$24–27); however, we do not see distinct catalysts, given lukewarm consumer electronics demand, continued memory price pressure and uncertainty around Xiaomi meeting its 2026 EV shipment target. Overall, we remain Neutral on the stock.

## Investment Thesis

We remain Neutral on Xiaomi, as we believe the stock is not too far from trough valuation levels (10-15x PE or HK\$24-27), despite a lack of distinct catalysts and Street EPS estimates likely to be revised down. Entering 2Q26, we expect Xiaomi's smartphone profit growth to be capped by intensifying memory cost hikes, while its AIoT is likely to remain slow through 2026, given slower China demand growth. For EV, management is still confident to achieve its 550K FY26 EV shipment target, helped by multiple new product launches. Meanwhile, overseas growth in EV is still at least 12 months ahead and is likely to start slow, given the complexity of setting up sales and service channels in new countries.

## Valuation

Our Dec-26 PT of HK\$35 is based on SOTP, with 12.0x ex-cash 12M fwd earnings for the core business and 1.5x 12M fwd P/S for the EV and new business.

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

Key negative risks are (1) slower IoT demand leading to further cuts to topline, (2) any cut to FY26 EV shipment target due to lack of demand sustainability for SU7 facelift or lukewarm response to Xiaomi's new EV model launches such as YU9 in 2H26, given intensified competition and lower backlog.

Key upside risks are (1) sharp ramp in EV shipments driven by launch of YU9 in 2H26, (2) preparations for European EV launch in 2027, (3) stability in smartphone and IoT GMs with some confidence on slowing memory price hikes, and (4) more stringent cost control.
