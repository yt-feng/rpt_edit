## GCL Tech
## Pivot to Multi-Product New Energy Materials - LFP as Growth Engine

GCL Tech announced in mid-May 2026 a shift from being a granular silicon pure play to a global, multi-product new energy materials platform, with LFP cathodes (via its lower-capex, more environmentally friendly physical iron oxide red method) as the next growth engine and silicon–carbon anodes as a second growth curve, alongside plans to expand overseas through capital partnerships and new operating entities. Management emphasized process-driven cost advantages (unit capex \~Rmb8.5k/ton with potential to reach \~Rmb7k/ton) and a focus on higher energy-density 4th/4.5th-gen moving toward 5th-gen products (already in customer testing), arguing high-end supply is tighter and offers better earnings visibility than oversupplied low-end LFP. Capacity is scaling through an administered 150k tpa (ton p.a.) facility (potentially injectable into the listed entity) and a Sichuan 200k tpa project nearing operation, with a further 400k tpa expansion possible. GCL Tech also remains constructive on silicon–carbon anodes given their energy-density uplift versus graphite, and is expanding a pilot line (>1k tpa). We maintain our OW rating on cost leadership and potential market share gains.

Event Summary: Strategic transformation into GCL Tech announced in mid-May 2026 (link) that it aims to achieve a leap-forward transformation from a “granular silicon single-product champion” to a “globalized multi-product new energy materials platform”. ## Management has positioned the LFP business as the next growth driver:

\- Technological advantage: Mgmt highlighted that they are applying the physical iron oxide red method to produce LFP cathodes, which is a simpler and more environmentally friendly process. GCL Tech has made lots of improvements to this process and they are able to produce high energy-density products. Meanwhile, unit capex is significantly lower than other processes. GCL Tech's current unit capex is Rmb8.5k per ton, and mgmt guided that they see potential to lower this to Rmb7k per ton. Mgmt is targeting to produce high-end LFP cathodes, namely 4th/4.5th-gen moving toward 5th-gen, as higher energy-density products remain comparatively tight, offering better earnings visibility. Its 5th-gen cathodes have already been sent for customer testing, though the verification process typically takes 6-12 months.

\- Capacity expansion: GCL Tech's parent group launched a 120k ton production capacity in end-2022, while GCL Tech has been administrating this production facility since 2025, with the current capacity expanded slightly to 150k ton per year. Mgmt commented that potentially the parent group may inject this asset into the listing entity (GCL Tech), but they have not put out a detailed plan yet. # Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks

GCL Tech (Overweight; Price Target: HK\$1.10)

## Investment Thesis

GCL Tech is a cost leader among polysilicon producers and focuses on FBR (fluidized bed reactor) technology. We believe GCL Tech will benefit from the technology disruption of FBR through (1) lower cash costs and (2) rising penetration of FBR, which may lead to rising market share. Meanwhile, we expect the polysilicon cycle to normalize into FY28, benefitting GCL Tech. We have an OW rating on GCL Tech.

## Valuation

Our Jun-27 PT of HK\$1.1 is based on a 12-month forward target P/BV of 0.75x. Our target multiple is below the historical average of 1.1x. We believe this valuation is justified, as we expect the polysilicon cycle to normalize into FY28.

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

Downside risks include: 1) rising competition from other polysilicon producers; 2) impairment risks related to dividend receivables; and 3) execution risks related to convertible bond issuance.
