# China Insurance

## 1H26 Preview: Dividend surprise could reset the trade; Ping An-H top pick

YTD, only China Life-H has outperformed among Chinese insurers across A and H shares, despite strong 1Q26 results. We think the market is questioning earnings quality, with profit strength seen as largely investment-led rather than driven by core insurance earnings. Capital, however, remains robust. We forecast Jun-26E core solvency at $110\%+$ for major life insurers and $170\%+$ for major non-life insurers, well above the $50\%$ minimum. A potential interim dividend surprise during the mid to late August results season could be the catalyst to reset the trade. We forecast an average $15\%$ oya increase in interim FY26E DPS across the sector. China's H/A-listed insurers trade on 6x/7x FY27E P/E, with dividend yields of $5.1\%/4.0\%$ . Our top pick is Ping An-H, followed by China Life-H.

\- Earnings quality remains the key concern. Positive profit alerts from China Life (link) and New China Life (link) in early July were not well received, highlighting the sector's high earnings sensitivity to China's equity market. Investors appear unconvinced that insurers will distribute unrealised YTD investment gains through interim dividends, rather than retain them as a buffer against potential 2H26 market volatility. We estimate that a $10\%$ move in the SHCOMP index would imply an average $45\%$ sensitivity to FY26E consensus net profit. This caution is also evident in the surprisingly slow pace of FY26E consensus earnings upgrades. For Ping An Group, we forecast 1H26 OPAT (core earnings) of Rmb84B, up $8\%$ oya.

\- Interim dividend surprise could be the catalyst. Except for Ping An, Chinese insurers introduced interim dividends only commencing from 1H24, and capital return policies remain underdeveloped. Consensus forecasts imply average FY26 DPS growth of 4% oya, which we view as conservative versus our 7% oya forecast. If insurers allocate part of their unrealised investment gains to 1H26 dividends, backed by adequate capital, this should improve market confidence. We forecast average 1H26 interim dividend growth of 15% oya, including a 20% oya, or Rmb0.286/share, interim dividend hike for China Life.

\- Fundamentals remain supportive. Life sales momentum remains resilient despite slower 2Q26 activity vs. 1Q26, as life insurers shift towards less interest-rate-sensitive participating products. Improving agent productivity, stabilising agent headcount and steady bancassurance support should drive double-digit NBV growth over the next 12 months. We forecast average $17\%$ oya growth for 1H26E NBV. In non-life, we forecast $94.7\%$ $(-0.1\% p$ oya) 1H26E CoR for PICC P&C, supported by solid auto underwriting and no major claim loss.

\- Prefer Ping An-H and China Life-H. H-listed insurers offer better risk-reward than A-shares, given wider yield support. Ping An-H/A offer the sector's highest dividend yields at 5.8%/5.3%, alongside 6x/6x FY26E P/E. China Life-H offers a 4.0% yield, but we see upside to FY26E DPS, with our estimate at 20% oya versus consensus at 7%, and potential for buybacks subject to regulatory approval. CPIC-H remains OW, supported by its first interim dividend proposal. Lastly, we stay cautious on PICC P&C mainly due to growing reinvestment risk amidst a very low deposit rate and short-term bond yield.

## Investment thesis

## Earnings skepticism priced-in, Dividend upside ahead

Moving into the 1H26 reporting season in mid to late August, we prefer life insurers over non-life insurers, and H shares over A shares. Our H-share pecking order is Ping An-H, China Life-H, CPIC-H, PICC Group-H, PICC P&C and New China Life-H. For A shares, valuations are less compelling, with average multiples at a 35% premium to H-listed peers (Table 2), while A-share insurers' FY26E dividend yields of 2.6%-5.3% also look less attractive. Our A-share preference is Ping An-A, CPIC-A, China Life-A, New China Life-A and PICC Group-A.

Despite trading at 5x/6x FY26E P/E with a 5%/4% dividend yield for H/A-listed insurers, China insurers have remained out of favour YTD, with China Life-H and PICC P&C the only two outperformers across A- and H-shares. We think the key debate is no longer whether earnings are recovering, but whether investment-led earnings strength can convert into visible capital return.

We believe that the August interim results season could be the turning point, with dividend guidance, capital deployment and core earnings recovery likely to matter more than headline profit growth. We roll forward our PT timeframe from Dec-26 to Dec-27 and raise our PTs by 5% on average, reflecting steady business recovery, stronger solvency capital and a better core earnings outlook which drives enhanced cash generation abilities. Ping An-H remains our top OW, followed by China Life-H and Ping An-A.

## Time for capital return to do the talking

China's H-listed insurers trade 5x FY26E P/E on average with a $5.0\%$ dividend yield, which we view as attractive given still undemanding valuations. Yet the sector has remained out of favour YTD across both H- and A-shares, with the only exceptions of China Life-H and PICC P&C. This is notable given strong 1Q26 results across most key financial metrics, confirming that fundamentals remain on track.

In our view, sector underperformance reflects the gap between reported earnings strength and investor confidence in dividend delivery. Much of the earnings upside appears investment-led, helped by equity market gains, while investors remain unconvinced that these gains will translate into higher shareholder returns amid rising macro and political uncertainty.

Against this backdrop, the mid- to late-August interim results season could be an important window for the sector. We believe management commentary on dividends, capital deployment and core earnings recovery could prompt investors to revisit the risk-reward, similar to the sector rotations seen in 2H24 and 2H25 (Figure 5/Figure 6).

To frame the opportunity, we start with the positive profit alerts released by several major insurers in early July. In Table 3, we summarise the profit alert dates, companies' 1H26 earnings guidance versus FY26 consensus net profit, and share price reactions in the week after the alerts. As Table 3 shows, strong profit growth was not well perceived by the market, which we attribute mainly to concerns over earnings quality and equity market sensitivity.

More importantly, despite strong profit alerts suggesting that China Life and New China Life could achieve almost all current full-year consensus profit in 1H26, the pace of FY26E earnings upgrades has been surprisingly slow for both names (Figure 7/Figure 8). Based on current FY26E net profit consensus of Rmb139B and Rmb31B for China Life and New China Life, respectively, the market appears to be now assuming limited earnings delivery in 2H26.

## Three reasons 1H26 could reset the sector debate

We highlight three key points into the 1H26 results season.

## #1. Interim dividend surprise could be the key catalyst.

Except for Ping An, interim dividends among Chinese insurers were only introduced from 1H24, and capital management policies remain underdeveloped. China H/A-listed insurers currently offer FY26E dividend yields of 5.0%/4.0%, with H-shares looking particularly attractive. Consensus forecasts average FY26E DPS growth of only 4% oya, versus JPMe at 7% oya, which we view as conservative given improving solvency, balance sheet stabilisation and CSM recovery.

The interim DPS increase may be only a modest positive surprise, given Chinese insurers typically allocate a larger dividend proposal at the final stage rather than at interim, and we forecast an average $15\%$ oya interim dividend hike in 2026. We view this as a strong starting point for the sector's dividend theme.

More importantly, we expect management commentary on capital returns to shareholders to improve the sector's risk-reward outlook, as the business recovery remains on track and solvency capital remains adequate.

If insurers signal confidence in a decent year-end DPS increase, supported by a more developed dividend policy based on core earnings rather than reported net profit, the current 5.0% yield for H-listed Chinese insurers could become a key driver for sector re-rating. We are increasingly optimistic on the outlook for sustainable shareholder returns among larger-cap insurers.

Lastly, we think the sector's adequate capital base, as shown in Table 5, provides solid fundamental support for more proactive capital return policies. We forecast $110\%+$ for major life insurers and $170\%+$ for major non-life insurers, well above the $50\%$ minimum requirement as of Jun-26E.

## #2. Core earnings recovery strengthens the valuation case

We believe that core earnings disclosure makes the valuation argument more credible, particularly when combined with potential dividend upside.

Given sizeable equity holdings relative to balance sheets, together with accounting classification effects, we believe the sector's reported earnings remain highly sensitive to equity markets. To be fair, the underlying earnings base is also improving, but this appears less well recognised by investors.

Ping An is a useful reference point. The group discloses operating profit after tax, or OPAT, which excludes short-term equity market volatility, alongside reported net profit. We view OPAT as a key proxy for core earnings, largely supported by distributable profit. On this basis, Ping An-H trades at 6x FY26E P/OPAT and 5x FY27E P/OPAT, suggesting attractive fundamental value even before giving credit to unrealised investment gains.

Apart from Ping An and CPIC, most Chinese insurers do not yet disclose OPAT. While OPAT and reported net profit will differ, we expect the direction of travel in core earnings to remain positive across major insurers, supported by CSM balance recovery and improving new business economics. We therefore believe Ping An's OPAT-based valuation provides a useful read-across for the sector, particularly for major names such as China Life and CPIC.

This makes the valuation case more credible, especially when combined with potential dividend upside.

## #3. Life sales momentum remains healthy despite 2Q26 moderation

The market appears concerned about a potential slowdown in bancassurance sales momentum in 2Q26. We are less concerned about q/q moderation, as 1Q is typically the seasonal peak and insurers usually manage sales growth more evenly through the rest of the year. We do not expect this to signal de-growth in 2Q26. For 1H26E, we forecast 17% oya NBV growth (Table 7), confirming that future profit from new life sales should continue to expand at a double-digit pace. We expect this momentum to continue through the rest of the year.

## Non-life

## Resilient, but risk-reward looks less attractive

We do not see major near-term negatives for non-life insurers. We expect resilient underwriting performance among major players, including a 1H26E combined ratio of 94.7% for PICC P&C, improved by 0.1%p oya, and 95.7% (0.5%p oya deterioration) for Ping An P&C and 96.3% (flattish) for CPIC Property (Table 9). This should be supported by solid auto underwriting, improving non-auto margins and no material major-claim loss development in 1H26E.

That said, we are more cautious on the investment profit outlook for non-life insurers, given falling interest rates in China. Non-life insurers have shorter liability duration than life insurers, and their asset duration is typically shorter as well. With China's yield curve steepening and bank deposit rates falling sharply, new money yields on interest-bearing assets could become a risk. Based on our estimates, for every $1\%$ drop in investment yield, the net profit of PICC P&C could decline by $10\%$ . At the same time, the sector is trading at a premium to its history, while we see limited scope for further underwriting margin improvement over the next 12 months. For example, PICC P&C is trading at 8x FY26E P/E, versus its historical average and trough level of 8x/4x. This is why we continue to prefer life insurers over non-life insurers into the interim results season.

## Positioning

## Prefer Ping An-H/A and China Life-H

We prefer H-listed insurers over A-listed insurers, given more attractive dividend yields and risk-reward.

Ping An-H/A offer the sector's highest dividend yields at $5.8\% / 5.3\%$ , alongside 6x/6x FY26E P/E. We view Ping An-H as the best large-cap expression of the dividend and core earnings recovery theme, given its OPAT disclosure, stronger capital return visibility and attractive valuation on both earnings and yield.

China Life-H offers a 4.0% dividend yield, but we see scope for a larger FY26E DPS increase, with JPMe at 20% oya versus consensus at 7%. We also see potential for share buybacks, subject to regulatory approval, over the next 12 months, supported by its strong capital base of 157% as of Mar-26 (link), reserve recovery outlook, and steady core earning delivery.

CPIC-H's consensus dividend yield appears lower at $4.8\%$ , versus $5.4\%$ for both PICC P&C and PICC Group-H. However, its first interim dividend proposal, lower reinvestment risk, stronger capital position and similarly robust life sales outlook to China Life and Ping An justify retaining our OW rating.

New China Life-H offers an attractive 6% consensus dividend yield, but we see risk to this yield, given its weaker insurance profit base and capital position versus peers.

PICC P&C and PICC Group-H both offer attractive yields versus peers. We would revisit opportunities in these names after checking two key points during the 1H results season.

First, for PICC P&C, we would look for resilience in the new money yield despite falling interest rates in China. If this holds, we think the stock's risk-reward would improve, given expectations for resilient underwriting profit.

Second, for PICC Group-H, our SOTP valuation implies PICC Life & Health is valued at around 3x FY26E P/E. PICC Life's capital position remains solid, while PICC Health's new business value is growing faster than peers. If life profit rises in line with major life insurers in 1H26, we think this could further improve risk-reward for PICC Group-H.

These could become potential investment opportunities after the 1H results reporting season.

## PICC Property and Casualty (2328)

## Resilient underwriting, but reinvestment risk caps upside

We expect PICC P&C to deliver resilient underwriting performance in 1H26E, supported by solid motor underwriting, improving non-motor margins and no material major claim loss development. However, we maintain Neutral, given rising reinvestment risk amid very low deposit rates and short-term bond yields.

On a full-year earnings basis, we raise our FY26E, FY27E and FY28E net profit forecasts by 21%, 7% and 9% to Rmb44B, Rmb43B and Rmb42B, respectively. We now forecast a combined ratio of 97.4%, 97.7% and 97.8% in FY26E, FY27E and FY28E, implying changes of -0.2%p, +0.3%p and +0.1%p oya, respectively.

Rolling forward our PT timeframe to Dec-27 from Dec-26, we marginally raise our PT to HK\$15 from HK\$14. However, as we continue to see limited upside, particularly from further underwriting improvement, we remain Neutral on the stock.

## Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation

We believe that the company's underwriting cycle is now peaking, with no further improvement in the combined ratio anticipated. Ongoing price competition in the auto line and normalization of non-auto claims trend would limit further upside ahead. Although we do believe the company offers scarcity value, with reduced risks related to solvency capital and dividends, the stock looks expensive over its historical average P/E of 8x. We have a Neutral rating on the shares.

We use a price to earnings (P/E) methodology for our valuation as the industry adopted IFRS17 accounting in 2023. We assign a 7x FY27E P/E (vs. historical average/minimum: 8x/4x) to arrive at our Dec-27 PT of HK\$15.

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

Downside risks to our rating and price target include: 1) larger-than-expected deterioration in auto underwriting due to heightened competition; 2) slower-than-expected improvement in A-share equity markets; and 3) larger natural catastrophic loss claims impacting non-auto underwriting profitability.

Upside risks include: 1) better-than-expected claim ratio trend leading to further improvement in combined ratio management, 2) stronger-than-expected investment performance lifting concerns on reinvestment risk and a potential decline in the company's investment yield.
