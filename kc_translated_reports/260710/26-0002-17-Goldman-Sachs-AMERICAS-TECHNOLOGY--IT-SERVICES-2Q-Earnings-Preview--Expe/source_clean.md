# 2Q Earnings Preview: Expect guidance to move toward midpoints given increased macro uncertainty

Undemanding setup for stocks, but macro uncertainty takes potential upside off the table

We expect 2Q25 results to be broadly in line, but we anticipate more substantial commentary on macro impact this quarter, given signs that heightened uncertainty began affecting client decision-making as of April-May. Against this backdrop, we expect companies to take down the top end of guidance ranges and anchor expectations at the midpoint, reflecting prolonged macro disruption and decreasing likelihood of a broader spending recovery across 2026. In this context, we see relative upside for IBM, which we view as an idiosyncratic beneficiary of rising enterprise AI demand given its software and consulting mix (with its software portfolio also relatively resilient to the broader AI-driven concerns weighing on sector). Conversely, we see relative downside risk for more discretionary-exposed names such as EPAM, though we continue to see potential for longer-term upside once a spending recovery takes hold given EPAM's solid execution track record and growing AI book of business.

IBM (Buy) – We see modest upside to Street for the quarter, with software momentum likely intact and IBM increasingly viewed as a net beneficiary of enterprise AI demand given its unique software/consulting positioning. We view IBM's software portfolio as relatively resilient to broader AI-driven concerns weighting on the software sector, supported by continued strength in its core portfolio.

EPAM (Neutral) – We expect no upgrade to EPAM's recently lowered guidance, given EPAM's exposure to discretionary spending amid deteriorating macro conditions since April-May. While near-term pressure is likely to persist as clients defer decision-making, we still see potential for longer-term upside once a spending recovery materializes, given EPAM's solid historical track record.

## IBM (Buy): Expect solid software growth, as we believe IBM is a net AI beneficiary

Exhibit 1: IBM - Summary of GS vs. Street estimates


Setup heading into the print: We think investor sentiment on IBM has turned more constructive in recent months (up \~8% in the past month), buoyed by a steady cadence of quantum computing updates and the growing view that IBM is particularly well positioned to capture growing enterprise AI demand. For the stock to move higher, we think management will need to deliver modest upside in Software and reiterate its 2026 revenue guidance, even amid macro-driven softness in overall consulting demand.

Our view on key metrics and our estimates: Our estimates are largely in line with the Street for 2Q, and we see room for modest upside in 2026 given sustained momentum in Software. For 2026, we expect IBM to reiterate its guidance for more than 5.0% in constant currency revenue growth (we model 5.2% CC growth or \$71.32 bn vs. the Street at \$71.47 bn), along with a positive FX impact of roughly \~0.4% (down from 0.5% prior). We also expect IBM tick up guidance for an increase of \~\$1.0 bn or more in free cash flow in 2026 (we model \$16.0 bn vs. the Street at \$15.9 bn). We think investors will remain focused on Software performance given ongoing Confluent integration and expectations of double-digit growth in Red Hat in 2026. At the same time, we expect updated views on Consulting outlook given heightened macro uncertainty (with 2Q bookings key to gauge signs of delayed decision-making), particularly as contribution from the 2025 mainframe cycle begins to normalize in 2026.

Items on the call that could move the stock: (1) Software mix and growth – Strong performance in IBM’s core software growth despite macro headwinds and AI concerns could lift investor confidence; (2) Consulting bookings – The size of consulting bookings in 2Q should provide visibility into demand levels and IBM’s consulting revenue growth throughout 2026; (3) AI – Positive commentary on AI bookings and ARR growth could be a sign that IBM’s organic AI investments (WatsonX) and AI partnerships are nearing meaningful revenue accretion.

Estimate changes: We adjust our 2026/27/28 EPS estimates to \$12.50/\$13.50/\$15.20 from \$12.55/\$13.50/\$15.20 to reflect weaker FX tailwinds.

Price target update: We maintain our 12-month price target of \$335, which is based on 25X our one-year forward EPS estimate. Downside risks: 1) cyclical headwinds in software, 2) slowdown in consulting, 3) AI bookings deceleration, 4) dilutive M&A.

CTSH (Neutral): Expect steady performance amid choppy macro trends with upside unlikely
Exhibit 2: CTSH - Summary of GS vs. Street estimates


Setup heading into the print: We believe investors have turned less positive on CTSH shares as rising macro uncertainty coupled with ongoing concerns of AI's negative impact on large outsourcing deals continue to weigh on the sector. However, we still expect stable growth for Cognizant in this environment as increasingly cost-focused clients are likely to sustain strong outsourcing demand even as discretionary spending stays tepid. The stock continues to decline in recent weeks (down \~20% over the past month), suggesting investor expectations are much lower heading into the print.

Our view on key metrics and our estimates: Our estimates are in line with the Street for 2Q and 2026. For 2026, we expect Cognizant to narrow guidance for constant currency revenue growth to 4.0% - 6.0%, from 4.0% - 6.5% (we model 5.1% CC growth or \$22.33 bn vs. the Street at \$22.32 bn) given prolonged macro uncertainty and one less month of Astreya contribution (since the deal closed in late June versus the single month assumed in the 2Q26 guidance). We now model a positive FX impact of 0.7% for FY26 (vs. positive 0.8% prior). We also expect Cognizant to reiterate guidance for an adjusted operating margin expansion of 20 - 40bps or 16.0% - 16.2% (we model 16.0%, in line with the Street) given ongoing AI efficiencies from Project Leap. For 3Q26, we believe Cognizant may guide for 4.5% - 6.5% of constant currency revenue growth (we model 5.9% and positive FX impact of 0.1% or \$5.74 bn vs. Street at \$5.71 bn).

Items on the call that could move the stock: (1) 3Q26/FY26 outlook – We expect investors to focus on 2026 guidance in assessing the pace of organic growth; (2) Gross margin trajectory – Part of the bull case on the stock rests on margin expansion; we think margins may see some support from ongoing AI-led efficiencies which are partially offset by gross margin pressure from pricing pressure and increased hiring at the bottom of the pyramid; (3) AI – We expect the size and scope of AI engagements to draw increased focus as some pilot cases begin to scale.

Estimate changes: We maintain our 2026/27/28 estimates of \$5.75/\$6.30/\$6.85.

Price target: We maintain our 12-month price target of \$75, based on 12X our one-year forward EPS estimate. Upside risks: 1) cyclical uplift, 2) margin expansion. Downside risks: 1) further demand slowdown, 2) dilutive M&A, 3) margin compression.

## EPAM (Neutral): Expect an in-line quarter, but macro uncertainty may weigh on outlook

Exhibit 3: EPAM - Summary of GS vs. Street estimates


Setup heading into the print: We think investors have turned more bearish on EPAM, as we expect the company's core offerings remain exposed to softer discretionary spending amid a broader macro slowdown (along with bearish software sentiment still weighing on the group). While the stock continues to underperform in recent weeks (down \~12% over the past month), we see limited near-term catalysts in this tepid spending environment, and we think solid 2Q numbers alone are unlikely to offset a potential narrowing of the 2026 organic constant currency guidance.

Our view on key metrics and our estimates: Our estimates are in line with the Street for 2Q and FY26, but we expect softer demand for EPAM's core offerings of application implementation and consulting as macro uncertainty persists. For 2026, we expect EPAM to narrow its guidance for organic constant currency revenue growth to 2.5% - 4.0% from 2.5% - 5.0% (we model 3.2% CC growth or \$5.70 bn vs. the Street at \$5.73 bn). We also model positive FX impact of 1.3% for FY26 (down from positive 1.5% prior). We anticipate EPAM will reiterate guidance for an adjusted operating margin range of 15.0% - 16.0% (we model 15.5% vs. the Street at 15.6%). For 3Q26, we believe EPAM is likely to guide for 3.0% - 5.0% of constant currency revenue growth (we model 3.0% with neutral FX impact or \$1.44 bn, vs. the Street at \$1.45 bn).

Items on the call that could move the stock: (1) 3Q26/FY26 outlook – We expect investors to focus on 2026 guidance to gauge the impact of ongoing macro disruption; (2) Gross margin – We believe investors will be closely monitoring margin trends as the company continues to see margin pressure from rising wages, acquisitions, and ongoing pricing pressure; (3) AI – As AI begins to drive incremental revenue, the growing AI-native revenues will be an increasing focal point for investors, especially given rising AI disruption concerns.

Estimate changes: We maintain our 2026/27/28 EPS estimates of \$13.00/\$13.90/\$15.00.

Price target: We maintain our 12-month price target of \$110, which is based on 8X our one-year forward EPS estimate. Downside risks: 1) further demand slowdown, 2) dilutive M&A, 3) increased geopolitical tensions.

GLOB (Neutral): Lower bar into the print, but macro overhang persists

Exhibit 4: GLOB - Summary of GS vs. Street estimates


Setup heading into the print: We believe investor sentiment toward GLOB has turned more bearish as ongoing macro uncertainty pressures the company's core verticals, reflecting its significant exposure to the Middle East and other consumer-facing segments. The stock continues to underperform in recent months (down \~22% in the past month), which suggests expectations are now considerably lower heading into the print. However, we see potential for modest upside if management signals further acceleration and growing client traction in its "AI Pods" business.

Our view on key metrics and our estimates: Our estimates are broadly in line with the Street for 2Q and 2026. For 2026, we expect Globant to narrow guidance to 0.3% - 1.8% from 0.3% - 2.2% in growth (we model flat CC or \$2,480 mn vs. the Street at \$2,481 mn), with a positive FX impact of 1.0% and minimal M&A contribution. We also expect Globant to reiterate guidance for an adjusted operating margin range of 14.0% - 15.0% (we model 14.3%, in line with the Street). We think investors will be particularly focused on demand trends in the Middle East, the US, and in consumer-focused verticals (Media & Entertainment and Travel & Hospitality) given the outsized impact of ongoing macro uncertainty seen in recent months.

Items on the call that could move the stock: (1) 3Q26/FY26 outlook – We expect investors to focus on 2026 guidance in assessing the pace of organic demand improvement; (2) Regional performance – Commentary on impact from geopolitical disruption in the Middle East or the pace of deal conversion in the US could inform investors on Globant’s pace of recovery across 2026; (3) AI pods – Indications of further acceleration in the company’s “AI Pods” business could lift top-line growth in 2026.

Estimate changes: We maintain our 2026/27/28 EPS estimates of \$6.15/\$6.75/\$7.50.

Price target update: We maintain our 12-month price target of \$60, which is based on 9X our one-year forward EPS estimate. Upside risks: 1) earlier-than expected discretionary recovery, 2) increased AI demand. Downside risks: 1) demand slowdown in unique verticals, 2) further revenue slowdown, 3) dilutive M&A.

## TASK (Sell): Expect pricing and margin headwinds to persist amid rising AI disruption

Exhibit 5: TASK - Summary of GS vs. Street estimates


Setup heading into the print: We believe investors remain bearish on TASK given the company's concentrated exposure to the BPS market, a subsector which we see as increasingly challenged by AI as evidenced in rising revenue headwinds due to rapid AI adoption at top clients. As a result, the stock continues to underperform in recent months (down \~8% in the past month).

Our view on key metrics and our estimates: Our estimates are largely in line with the Street for 2Q, as we think management set guidance conservatively. However, we see some increased risk to margins given continuous investments to accommodate new contract ramps as well as ongoing price pressure as clients pull back on spending in a softer macro environment. We expect TaskUs to reiterate its full-year revenue and EPS guidance.

Items on the call that could move the stock: (1) 3Q26/FY26 outlook - We expect TaskUs's 2Q26/FY26 revenue growth guidance to be largely in line with the Street; (2) EBITDA margin – We believe investors will be focused on margin trends and guidance, as the company expects ongoing margin pressure from additional investments in headcount across 2026 ; (3) Meta demand given rising AI impact – We expect investors to focus on commentary on Meta's demand trends in 2026, as Meta is TaskUs's largest client at \~27% of revenue.

Estimate changes: We maintain our 2026/27/28 estimates of \$1.30/\$1.40/\$1.55.

Price target update: We maintain our 12-month price target of \$7, which is based on 5X our one-year forward EPS estimate. Upside risks: 1) slower-than-expected development of AI to customer service tools, 2) stable demand for US-based headcount, 3) faster-than-expected demand growth at the company's largest customer (Meta).
