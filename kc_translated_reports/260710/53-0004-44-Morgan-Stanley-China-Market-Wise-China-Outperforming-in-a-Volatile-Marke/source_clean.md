# China Outperforming in a Volatile Market – What's Next?

Funding short unwinding and southbound flows helped the HK market regain momentum. Going into August, we expect a further improved setup for a better recovery in the market. Our extreme UW position suggests gradual accumulation now.

Funding short unwinding and southbound flows have supported China's latest outperformance: Hong Kong and China have outperformed regional and global peers over the past week, while most other markets saw a sharp rise in volatility. In our recent marketing feedback report, we highlighted rising interest in Chinese equities among global investors, thus we anticipate a gradual reallocation into the market in coming months.

However, we do not view the recent outperformance as evidence of foreign inflows rushing back into the China market. Instead, we believe it primarily reflects: (1) funding short unwinding – global investors have told us that Hong Kong and China have served as funding shorts for increased allocations to Korea, Taiwan, and Japan. The recent correction in those markets has significantly reduced this pressure on China; and (2) continued southbound inflows, which have provided a stable source of liquidity as the global risk appetite has weakened.

Looking forward, we reiterate that late July/August will be critical for a sustainable China market recovery. We have been highlighting that the Hong Kong market is set up for a meaningful recovery going into August (see our mid-year outlook), given 1) 2Q results for e-commerce confirm the peaking-out of price war damage on earnings; 2) further progress made on AI commercialization outlook; and 3) heavy IPO share unlocking pressure in July will likely be largely digested. So far, events are progressing in line with our expectation - the latest free AI agent has been introduced in Tencent's Hunyuan 3 Model, both cloud business and overall EBITDA look set to beat market expectations per Baba's result preview, and Zhipu's stock price volatility is easing upon IPO share unlocking.

Near-term volatility may persist as global markets seek to stabilize. Investors with significant underweight positions in China may consider using this opportunity to accumulate high-quality names with strong fundamentals. This reflects our view that near-term downside risk is diminishing, while positive catalysts discussed above are lining up nicely timing-wise. A further meaningful market rebound still depends on further developments both domestically and globally. Domestic factors include a trough in earnings revision momentum and improving AI commercialization prospects. Global factors include further stabilization in interest rates and bond yields through a clearer Fed policy path, confirmation of planned capex expansion by major US tech companies, and containment of deleveraging pressure in highly leveraged market segments as we move through summer.
