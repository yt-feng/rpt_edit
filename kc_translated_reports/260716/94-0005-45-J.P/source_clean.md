## Asia Insurance
## Key takeaways from 2026 MDRT statistics: MDRT headcount resets, AIA still leads

We highlight one notable observation from the 2026 Million Dollar Round Table (MDRT) statistics: Asian insurers continue to dominate the global league table, accounting for $89\%$ of the top 100, as shown in Figure 1. However, MDRT recognition declined meaningfully across many Asian insurers versus the previous two years, including AIA Group. In particular, MDRT headcount for AIA China and its India JV fell sharply by $73\%$ and $51\%$ oya in 2026, which surprised us. We do not view this as an immediate red flag. MDRT qualification was relaxed during COVID-19 and the threshold is now normalising, while many Asian life insurers have already surpassed their 2019 new life sales base. On a new business generation per headcount basis, we still see scope for agency productivity upside, particularly for AIA, FWD and Thai Life. Among these, we highlight AIA's favourable risk-reward, with the stock trading at 1.1x FY27E P/EV, or 12x FY27E P/OPAT, and offering a $4.7\%$ total shareholder return yield. The stock has underperformed YTD, seemingly due to uncertainty around the HK MCV market (link). We believe AIA's 1H results in August could provide greater clarity, alongside continued double-digit growth momentum in key financial metrics.

\- MDRT decline looks more like normalisation than deterioration. MDRT headcount declined during 2021/2022 due to the pandemic, but this is not surprising given the post-COVID normalisation of qualification criteria. We believe the comparison with 2019 is more relevant than the comparison with the relaxed COVID period. On this basis, MDRT trends for many multinational life insurers remain broadly healthy. Recent declines likely reflect higher qualification thresholds (Table 2), stricter protection mix requirements (i.e., only 12 markets are eligible to waive the requirement that at least $50\%$ of qualifying products shall be from the risk-protection category; link), and reduced participation by Chinese insurers amid regulatory guidance discouraging MDRT recognition (link), as well as continued diversification into bancassurance.

\- AIA still leads the multinational MDRT league table. AIA remains the clear leader among multinational insurers, with 16,228 MDRT members in 2026, exceeding the combined 14,856 members of UK Prudential, Manulife and US Prudential, as shown in Figure 3. While AIA's MDRT headcount declined by $73\%$ and $51\%$ in Mainland China and India markets respectively this year, the agency new business contribution has risen significantly by $15\%$ oya (Figure 4). We therefore see the MDRT decline as a useful 1H results discussion point, rather than an immediate concern, with focus on agency productivity, headcount quality and margin delivery. Separately, while FWD has historically been positioned as a multi-distribution insurer, its agency channel execution also stands out. Its ranking among the leading MDRT cohorts is impressive, in our view, and points to strong execution capability in agency distribution.

\- MDRT remains a useful productivity proxy. MDRT recognises highly productive life agents globally, based on minimum levels of premiums, commissions and income. Although insurers do not disclose productivity specifically for MDRT agents, our observation is that MDRT members tend to generate higher-margin and/or larger-ticket new life sales.
