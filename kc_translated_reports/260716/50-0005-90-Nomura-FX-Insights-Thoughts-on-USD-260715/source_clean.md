## Thoughts on USD

We look at a few more focus points to judge whether USD has reached a turning point.

\- The weaker-than-expected US June CPI reading bolsters the case for the USD bear camp and supports our medium-term softer USD view.

\- We could be approaching a turning point for USD, but there are a few points of focus that we have to watch and move beyond before we can make a more conclusive judgment.

\- Date wise, these include the upcoming US June PPI, the July FOMC, June core PCE and July NFP, and perhaps even the next July CPI report.

\- Another point of focus is the US-Iran war and whether the recent re-escalation will evolve or resolve into a longer-term de-escalation.

\- We will also watch whether the consistent, strong foreign inflows into US equities continues or slows.

The weaker-than-expected US June core CPI (0%; Consensus: 0.2%) put a damper on the market's near-term bullish USD views. This softer inflation report also added further support to our medium-term view of a softer USD, driven by a) our economics team's view of slower US growth versus some other major economies, as well as our view that the Fed will remain on hold while other major central banks (e.g., the ECB and BOJ) hike; b) some large investor plan to lower their asset allocation to the US; c) Fed independence concerns; d) long US asset positioning and the sustainability of currently robust portfolio inflows, given macro conditions, policy risks and increased AI capex concerns (i.e., raising US twin deficit financing risk) and; e) USD selling FX hedging that could be triggered if USD begins to weaken.

However, even though we could be approaching the point at which USD turns lower, there are a few other key focus points in the near term that we have to watch/move beyond to make a more conclusive judgment. These include:

1. The US data over the coming weeks, such as:

a. June PPI (15 July), with our economics team seeing some risk of a modest positive contribution to the June PCE release, from PCE-relevant PPI data.

b. The FOMC meeting(29 July EST), on which the market will judge whether the FOMC's inflation concerns have changed following the softer June CPI release, and with the June core PCE likely to be known by the Fed chair at the meeting (core PCE release on 30 July).

c. After the FOMC, markets will use the July NFP (7 August) to gauge the relative strength of the labour market/economy.

d. With our economics team noting that the softening in June CPI may be amplified by some "one-off factors" such as the sharp drop in lodging-away-from home prices, this will further raise the importance on the next CPI report (12 August) to judge whether the flat month-on-month June core CPI reading was an anomaly.

2. The US-Iran war and whether the recent re-escalation will soon shift back and begin to ease again. We highlighted in Key Focus and Themes (10 July) that we were cautious on fading the new rounds of US military strikes on Iran (from 8 July) and Iran's strikes on US allies in the Persian Gulf. 3. Strong foreign inflows into US equities continue. Our measure of foreign inflows into US-focused funds (i.e., US ETFs and mutual funds) has shown consistently strong inflows into US equities in MTD July (i.e., until 14 July). The run rate is currently similar to what was seen in June 2026 – also a month of strong inflows into US equities – as well as what was seen during the US exceptionalism period of 2024 (Figure 2). While the lower US inflation readings, lower Fed rate hike expectations and a relatively strong US growth backdrop could drive a near-term return of the US exceptionalism theme, our medium-term outlook is less positive. Our economics team forecasts some slowing of the US economy in the quarters ahead, and we remain watchful for any negative equity market shock (i.e., currently, there are some market concerns around the AI capex cycle, private credit to the AI sector etc.).
