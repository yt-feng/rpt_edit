## Microsoft Takeaways from the 2Q26 AlphaWise CIO Survey:

\- Software: Better Budget Air Cover, Though Applications Still Need More Evidence. With the broader IT budget backdrop improving and the 1-year up-to-down ratio moving above 1.0x for the first time since 1Q24, Software investor focus has shifted back toward whether improving AI momentum can drive a more durable inflection in Software budgets. At the sector level, Software remains the fastest-growing IT category, with CIOs expecting +4.1% growth in 2026, up +29 bps from 2025. That said, we still do not find sufficient ammunition to become structurally more positive on application software broadly. Sequentially, Software spending expectations were effectively unchanged, up just +3 bps QoQ, and vendor-level spending intentions remain highly dispersed, with Microsoft and ServiceNow the only tracked vendors expected to accelerate versus our 4Q25 survey.

\- AI-Native Capabilities and Vendor Consolidation Point to Selective App Software Winners, Not a Broad Inflection. CIO indications around AI-native / LLM-based software adoption do not necessarily point to wholesale displacement of incumbent application software vendors. Rather, the overlap between expected AI-native adoption and vendor consolidation propensity suggests CIOs are looking to adopt new AI capabilities while reducing software fragmentation. In a new 2Q26 survey question, CIOs expect AI-native / LLM-based software adoption over the next 12 months to be highest across BI / Analytics (22%), Customer Service (18%), IT Operations / Observability (17%), and Collaboration / Productivity (13%). At the same time, CIOs show the greatest propensity to consolidate vendors in Collaboration / Content Workflow Management (3.4), Marketing Applications (3.3), IT Operations / Observability (3.3), and Database / Data Storage (3.3), while Security remains the most best-of-breed-skewed category at 2.6. In our view, this points less to CIOs adding another layer of fragmented AI point solutions, and more to an opportunity for scaled incumbents to become the consolidation layer by embedding AI-native

functionality into existing workflows. Across our software coverage, this framework points most constructively to Microsoft and ServiceNow as scaled platforms best positioned to embed AI into existing workflows and consolidate spend, with Salesforce and Snowflake retaining meaningful AI relevance in Customer Service and BI / Analytics, respectively, though with less clean spend momentum.

\- Looking across the suite of vendor growth expectations assessed in our Survey, CIOs continue to indicate expectations for spend with Microsoft to remain durable at +7.6% in 2026, the highest level across vendors tracked in our survey. Spending expectations for Microsoft improved from +7.3% in 4Q25, marking the second consecutive read of accelerating growth expectations. From a net expectations perspective, Microsoft also leads the pack with a 72% net score, though down from 78% in 4Q25, emblematic of the company's AI leadership position. Exhibit 1

\- As workloads shift to the cloud, Microsoft and Amazon remain the clear beneficiaries, though LLM Providers are emerging as a secondary share gainer. Microsoft remains the top expected share gainer from cloud migration in both 2026 and over the next three years (42% and 37% net scores, respectively), followed by Amazon (17% and 13% net scores, respectively). That said, results also point to some budget share shifting toward LLM Providers, which screen as an emerging beneficiary in both one-year and three-year views with 2% and 7% net scores, respectively. Exhibit 5, Exhibit 6

\- CIOs continue to note expectations for Microsoft, LLM Providers, and Amazon to gain the largest incremental share of GenAI spend. In 2026, Microsoft remains the clear leader at 27% (down from 32% in 1Q26), followed by LLM Providers at 11% (down from 12% in 1Q26), Amazon at 10% (down from 11% in 1Q26), Salesforce at 8% (up from 7% in 1Q26), and Google / ServiceNow at 6% each (down from 8% / 7% in 1Q26, respectively). On a three-year view, Microsoft remains the largest gainer at 30% (down from 32% in 1Q26), followed by LLM Providers at 17% (unchanged QoQ), Amazon at 12% (unchanged QoQ), Google at 11% (up from 9% in 1Q26), Salesforce at 8% (up from 7% 1Q26), and ServiceNow at 5% (down from 6% in 1Q26). Net, Microsoft remains the clearest public AI beneficiary on a three-year view. Exhibit 7, Exhibit 8

\- As it relates to Agentic Automation initiatives, CIOs point to Microsoft (36%), Google (8%), Amazon (7%), and LLM Providers (7%) as the top four vendor preferences. Exhibit 10

\- For those building custom AI Applications, CIOs point to Microsoft (47%), Amazon (10%), Google (10%), LLM Providers (7%), ServiceNow (5%), and Salesforce (5%) as their preferred vendors today. On a three year view, they indicate Microsoft (34%), LLM Providers (13%), Google (8%), and ServiceNow (7%) as their top choices for building out custom AI Applications. Exhibit 11

\- Durability of Azure Growth Intentions. Of the CIOs who currently use or plan to use Azure, 62% expect to increase spend over the next 12 months, relative to 57% in 2Q25. 23% of CIOs plan to keep spend unchanged and 5% plan to decrease spend on Azure over the next 12 months. Separately, Azure remains the preferred public cloud provider, with CIOs indicating 43% of public cloud workloads reside on Azure today, increasing to 47% over the next three years, compared to AWS at 32% today and 31% over the next three years. Exhibit 17, Exhibit 18

\- Uptick in M365 / O365 Spending Intentions. 2Q26 data points to a notable uptick in CIO intentions to increase spend on Microsoft 365 / Office 365 over the next 12 months, with 65% of CIOs indicating intent to increase spend, relative to 55% in 2Q25. 28% of CIOs plan to keep spend unchanged or decrease spend on M365 / O365 over the next 12 months, a downtick from the 40% of CIOs indicating flat / lower spend in 2Q25. Exhibit 17, Exhibit 18

\- Mix-Shift Toward Higher M365 Subscription Levels Apparent, with E7 Newly Broken Out. While CIOs note a meaningful portion of employees currently using M365 / O365 remains on E3, 2Q26 data shows a notable skew toward premium subscription tiers, with 47% of employees currently on E5 and 7% on E7, versus 31% on E5 in 4Q25 when E7 was not broken out. Looking ahead, 50% of CIOs expect to use E5 next year, in line with 50% in 4Q25, while 21% expect to use E7 next year. Taken together, expected E5 / E7 mix reaches 71% next year, highlighting an additional premium monetization lever for Microsoft. Exhibit 19, Exhibit 20, Exhibit 21

\- CIO intentions to leverage Microsoft's Generative AI products on a go-forward basis remain robust, though medium-term usage is more mixed. At the broadest level, adoption of Microsoft's Generative AI suite remains strong, with 97% of CIOs expecting to leverage some form of AI tooling from Microsoft over the next 12 months, up from 92% in 4Q25. Microsoft 365 Copilot remains the most popular solution within Microsoft's GenAI portfolio, with 88% of CIOs expecting to use the product over the next 12 months, up from 80% in 4Q25. GitHub Copilot also improved to 58% from 42% in 4Q25. Newer offerings also screen meaningfully in 2Q26, including Copilot Studio (52%), Azure AI Services (42%), Azure AI Foundry (25%), and Microsoft Fabric AI (25%). Exhibit 22, Exhibit 23

\- Looking ahead, usage of Microsoft's Generative AI portfolio is more bifurcated on a medium-term view, though M365 Copilot penetration expectations continue to improve. Microsoft 365 Copilot usage is expected to moderate from $88\%$ over the next 12 months to $63\%$ over the next three years (Exhibit 22, Exhibit 23). By comparison, CIOs expected Microsoft 365 Copilot usage of $68\%$ over the medium term in 4Q25. Partially offsetting, from a penetration perspective, CIOs expect to deploy M365 Copilot across $39\%$ of their Office user base over the next 12 months, up from $36\%$ in 4Q25, increasing to $62\%$ over the next three years, up from $61\%$ in 4Q25. Exhibit 24

\- For CIOs making an ERP / GL upgrade, SAP S/4HANA, Microsoft Dynamics, and Workday screen as the top cloud-based SaaS vendors expected to be leveraged. SAP S/4HANA rose to the top preferred vendor spot at 22% in 2Q26, followed by Microsoft Dynamics at 19%, Workday at 17%, and Oracle Fusion at 14%. This marks a shift from 2Q25, when Workday led at 22%, followed by SAP S/4HANA and Oracle Fusion at 19% each, while Microsoft Dynamics screened at 16%. Exhibit 26

\- Net, Microsoft remains the clearest public AI / Software winner in our

2Q26 CIO Survey. Microsoft screens with the highest vendor growth expectations, remains the preferred public cloud provider, shows improving M365 / O365 spending intentions, benefits from premium M365 SKU mix-shift with E7 newly broken out, and maintains leadership across M365 Copilot adoption and penetration. While medium-term usage of certain Microsoft AI products is more mixed, the broader survey continues to support Microsoft's ability to consolidate software spend while monetizing GenAI across Azure, M365, Copilot, GitHub, Fabric, and broader enterprise distribution.
