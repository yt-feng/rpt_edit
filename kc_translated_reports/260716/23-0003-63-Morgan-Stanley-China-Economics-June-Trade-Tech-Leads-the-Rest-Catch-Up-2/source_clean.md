# June Trade: Tech Leads, the Rest Catch Up
## Key Takeaways

June trade growth beat market expectations, supported by persistent tech tailwinds and a further broadening of non-tech exports.

Some of the headline strength may also reflect catch-up demand, following earlier shipping and energy-supply bottlenecks.

Tech still dominates: Semis+Computers contributed 9.4pp and 17.4pp to headline export (27%) and import (36%) growth, up from 9.3pp and 14.3pp in May.

\- Export breadth improved further: non-tech export growth accelerated to 19.8% YoY, from 11.2% in May, led by autos and labor-intensive consumer goods.

Tech tailwinds should keep headline trade growth robust, but sequential momentum may soften amid renewed tensions in the Strait of Hormuz.

Beyond tech, export breadth is improving...: Auto exports rose 12% MoM SA, taking YoY growth to 70%, from 39% in May. Exports of labor-intensive consumer goods increased 4.2% MoM SA. Upstream exports were also strong. Refined petroleum exports rose another 9% MoM SA, after a 13% increase in May, while aluminum exports surged 17% MoM SA after a cumulative 25% increase over January–May.

The broadening was also visible across destinations, with sequential export growth improving on a broad-based basis.

… and improvement in imports was more commodity-heavy, and uneven: Coal imports rose 17% MoM SA, after a 33% surge in May, while iron ore imports increased 7% MoM SA. For both commodities, sequential strength was driven more by volumes than by prices. Higher coal imports may partly reflect tighter domestic supply following broad safety inspections at coal-production sites.

In contrast, import value for crude oil contracted at -16% MoM SA, despite sequentially stable import prices for crude, which points to a continued slump in import volume.

Outlook: Structural tech demand should keep headline export and import growth elevated in the coming months. Renewed Hormuz tensions pose downside risks: with thinner inventory buffers, a moderate shock could disproportionately hit shipping, production, and global trade.
