# Riding on the Semiconductor Materials Upcycle; Upgrade to OW


We believe Capchem offers semiconductor materials exposure that is increasingly supported by earnings fundamentals. Over the next two years, we expect the company's earnings and valuation drivers to shift from electrolytes to electronic chemicals. We upgrade to OW.

Semiconductor materials exposure with earnings support. Capchem's exposure to the semiconductor value chain mainly comes from supplying cooling and cleaning chemicals to semiconductor fabrication plants. Most of its customers are located in mainland China, as well as Korea and Japan. The company expects its electronic information chemicals segment to deliver 40-50% CAGR, 2026-28e, driven by strong downstream semiconductor demand and import substitution. Together with steady improvements in fluorine chemicals and capacitor chemicals, we think the company's net profit can rise from \~Rmb2.2bn in 2026e to \~Rmb3bn in 2028e.

Looking beyond 2026, we expect the company's growth and valuation drivers to shift from electrolytes to electronic chemicals. In 2026, we expect most earnings growth to come from Capchem's battery electrolyte business, as profitability in its acquired LiPF6 business recovers. In our view, this recovery may not be sustainable given the significant LiPF6 capacity additions expected to begin in 2H26.

Nevertheless, we do not expect potential profitability pressure in electrolytes to materially impact the share price, as the company's growth outlook beyond 2026 will shift to electronic chemicals. This segment offers a higher growth profile and commands higher valuation multiples. We estimate electronic chemicals will account for \~40% of the company's total gross profit, up from 30% in 2025.

Raising PT, upgrading to OW. We raise our 2026-27e net profit forecasts by 33% and 4%, respectively, and introduce 2028e forecasts. We roll our valuation forward to 2027e, as we think the market has largely looked beyond 2026 earnings. We also lift our valuation multiples to reflect a stronger-than-expected growth outlook. We value the company's electrolyte business at 15x 2027e P/E, fluorine chemicals at 30x 2027e P/E, and electronic chemicals at 40x 2027e P/E, based on our new growth forecasts. Our new SoTP-derived PT is Rmb93, implying 32x 2027e P/E.

# Growth Drivers to Shift to Electronic Chemicals

High-quality names with real semiconductor materials exposure and earnings support. We view Capchem as a high-quality name with an established track record in semiconductor materials. Its product portfolio includes high-purity wet chemicals, etching solutions, semiconductor cooling fluids, and semiconductor cleaning chemicals. The company has entered the supply chains of major domestic semiconductor fabrication plants serving both logic and memory chip production. It also supplies selected semiconductor fabrication plants in Korea and Japan. Beyond semiconductor materials, continued progress in fluorine chemicals and capacitor chemicals should provide support for the company's overall earnings and current valuation.

Earnings driver to shift from electrolytes in 2026 to electronic chemicals thereafter. We forecast that a large portion of earnings growth in 2026 will still come from electrolyte-related products, supported by improving profitability of LiPF6 and additive products. However, we do not think this trend will be sustainable beyond 2026 given the long capacity additions planned for both LiPF6 and additives beginning in 2H26. Therefore, we forecast a YoY earnings decline for the company's electrolyte business in 2027 and in 2027. Despite this, we think the importance of Capchem's electrolyte business will decline gradually, as electronic chemicals become the company's primary earnings driver over the next three years. We forecast contribution from electronic chemicals to increase from 15% in 2025 to 24% in 2028, while its contribution to total gross profit rises from 30% in 2025 to \~40% in 2028.

(1) Chemicals for lithium-ion batteries: We value the company's electrolyte business at 15x 2027e P/E (vs 10x 2025e P/E previously), on par with its electrolyte peers Tinci (002709.SS) and Do-Fluoride New Materials (002407.SS), the largest electrolyte and

LiPF6 players globally by sales volume. Based on our estimates and consensus, these two names currently trade at an average of 15x 2027e P/E (using MS estimates for Tinci and FactSet consensus estimates for Do-Fluoride). Thus, we adopt this valuation multiple to value Capchem's electrolyte business, because we think Capchem's electrolyte business should exhibit similar margin trends to its peers, given a very transparent pricing in this market.

(2) Fluorine chemicals business: Capchem's fluorine chemicals business focuses on higher-end products, so we benchmark its valuation against leading A-share high-end fluorine chemical names. We view Haohua (600378.SS) and Juhua (600160.SS) as the closest peers. Based on FactSet consensus estimates, these two names currently trade at an average of 23x 2027e P/E. We believe Capchem's fluorine chemical business fluorine chemicals business warrants a premium valuation given its more advanced product portfolio and stronger profitability. The segment's gross profit margin exceeds 60%, compared with approximately 25-30% for its peers. We therefore assign a 30x 2027e P/E to Capchem's fluorine chemicals business (vs 15x 2025e P/E previously), reflecting its superior margin profile. Valuation for domestic fluorine chemical players that have to semiconductor materials exposure have also expanded significantly YTD. For example, Haohua's forward P/E has risen from 23x at the beginning of 2026 to 35x currently, based on FactSet data. We therefore think this supports our decision to apply a higher valuation multiple for Capchem's fluorine chemicals business.

(3) Electronic chemicals: Given strong market sentiment in the China semiconductor and semiconductor materials space, valuation multiples for domestic electronic chemicals names have risen significantly YTD. We view Jianghua Micro (603078.SS) and Crystal Clear Electronic Material (300655.SS) as the closest peers, as both are leading players in wet electronic chemicals and are expected to benefit from import substitution. Exhibit 5 below shows valuation multiples for these peers have increased substantially, and both peers currently trade at elevated PEG ratios. Against this backdrop, we think it is reasonable to lift the valuation multiple for Capchem's electronic chemicals business from 25x to 40x, reflecting its revised earnings trajectory and growth outlook. Our current valuation multiple for Capchem's electronics chemicals business implies 1x PEG, at a discount to peers at 3-5x PEG.

As a result of our SoTP valuation, our new PT is Rmb93 (vs. Rmb30 previously) and upgrade the stock to Overweight. Capchem's share price rallied in late 2025, initially diven by the recovery in battery electrolyte earnings. In our view, the market only began to reflect the potential of the electronic chemicals business during 2026, particularly in 2Q26. YTD, the share price has risen by \~30%, vs. 20% for ChiNext index and gains of \~50-100% for other A-share semiconductor materials companies that benefit from import substitution trends. Despite the recent share price appreciation, Capchem continues to trade at a discount to electronic chemicals peers based on current valuation metrics. We

therefore view the recent share price correction as an attractive opportunity for the stock to narrow its valuation gap relative to peers.

Bull case scenario valuation: In this scenario, we assume sustained profitability in the electrolyte business alongside strong growth in electronic chemicals. Specifically, we assume the company's electrolyte business maintains profitability at levels comparable to 1H26, while the electronic chemicals business delivers $>50\%$ revenue CAGR, 2026-28e. Our new bull case scenario valuation is Rmb115 (vs. Rmb61 previously).

Bear case scenario valuation: In this scenario, we assume the electronic chemicals business delivers a \~10% revenue CAGR, 2026-28e, reflecting slower-than-expected progress in import substitution and weaker-than-expected semiconductor demand. We also assume profitability in the electrolyte business declines significantly and remains only modestly above breakeven levels. Our new bear case scenario valuation is Rmb35 (vs. Rmb16.50 previously).
