# Re-weakened Sales in May; We Expect a Weaker 3Q

Property sales reversed the improving trend, falling deeper y-y in May amid weaker home prices and construction activity. We expect the fast deceleration in recent secondary home sales to turn negative y-y in 3Q, stressing home prices and primary sales.

Re-widened sales decline in May, as expected, with rebased national sales fell 9.3% y-y in value and 13.2% y-y in volume (vs. -7.6% and -9.5% in April, respectively), leading YTD sales to -13.5% y-y in value and -10.8% in volume in 5M26. The NBS 70-city home price index continued to edge down, at a slightly faster pace, falling 0.2% m-m in primary and 0.3% m-m in secondary markets in May (vs. -0.2% for both in April). In contrast, Tier-1 cities witnessed +0.2% in primary and +0.4% in secondary (vs. +0.1 and +0.4% in April) amid strong secondary sales.

Construction activity weakened further: New starts fell 25% y-y and completions fell 20% y-y in May, widening the 5M26 declines to -23% and -23%, respectively. Combined with the reduced construction scale, the decline of real estate investment worsened notably to -16.2% y-y in 5M26 (vs. -13.7% in 4M26). We expect the construction activity to remain lackluster in 2H, as developers in general are still cautious on landbanking (land purchase of top-100 developers dropped >40% y-y in 5M26), casting downside potential to our full-year forecasts.

We expect deeper sales decline in 3Q: Secondary real-time home sales in high-tier cities started weakening since mid-May, and have decelerated even faster in recent weeks. Considering the still-fragile resident sentiment, diminishing policy effect/pent-up demand, and reduced new saleable resources, we expect secondary home sales to turn negative y-y, and primary home sales drop deeper y-y in 3Q despite the low base. This could lead to a K-shaped home price trend, with broadly m-m decline, while some Tier 1 cities may see a mild uptrend, due to better destocking progress.

Stay selective: With increasing uncertainty around sales sustainability, we still see industry risk/reward skewing to the downside, despite a \~20% share price pullback since mid-May (vs. HSI -7%). We stay prudent and continue to recommend sticking to beneficiaries with both industry beta and self-help alpha. CR Land (1109.HK) remains our Top Pick, followed by C&D (1908.HK), as both offer solid EPS outlooks, attractive dividend yields, and medium-term re-rating potential, even absent notable recovery in the physical market.
