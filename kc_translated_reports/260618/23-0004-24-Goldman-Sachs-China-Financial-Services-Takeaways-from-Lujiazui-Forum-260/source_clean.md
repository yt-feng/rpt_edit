# GS：银行股真正的防御性，来自资产负债表结构而非规模

On June 17th, heads of major financial regulatory bodies, including NFRA, PBOC, CSRC, and SAFE, delivered speeches at the Lujiazui Forum. We summarize the key takeaways and their implications for our covered companies as follows:

For the banking sector, a structural shift in financing towards direct financing, coupled with a deceleration in loan growth prioritizing quality over quantity, is emerging as the new normal. Newly introduced policies, such as the narrowing of interest rate corridors and the provision of liquidity support to non-bank financial institutions under specific circumstances, are poised to stabilize short-term interest rates and reduce bond market liquidity risks. These measures are expected to help banks stabilize interbank liability costs and investment income, thereby enhancing their balance sheet resilience. For stock selection, we reiterate our Buy ratings on 1) CCB and BOC for their larger and stronger balance sheets, and 2) BONB, underpinned by its high-quality growth (see here) and its position as a primary beneficiary of these new policies, particularly given its higher proportion of interbank liabilities and trading account assets.

Turning to the capital markets, the latest policy signals indicate that supply-side tools in the capital market will be expanded at an accelerated pace, while regulatory constraints will be further tightened. For brokers, the core implication is that incremental opportunities will become concentrated among leading institutions that combine strong institutional capabilities, cross-border platforms, and robust risk management frameworks. Among our coverage, we reiterate our Buy rating on CICC-H, considering its advantages in institutional business and cross-border service capabilities.

## Banks: Credit Deceleration while New Policies Enhance Balance Sheet Defensiveness

The PBOC summarized the transformation of China's financing structure and announced a series of new policies at the Lujiazui Forum, including the refinement of short-term interest rate regulation mechanisms, the piloting of offshore RMB FX trading in the Shanghai Free Trade Zone, and the study of establishing a macro-prudential tool for non-bank liquidity support in specific scenarios. Against the backdrop of slowing credit growth, we anticipate these new regulatory tools will enhance the defensiveness of banks' balance sheets.

For stock selection, we reiterate our Buy ratings on 1) CCB and BOC for their larger and stronger balance sheets, and 2) BONB, underpinned by its high-quality growth (see here) and its position as a primary beneficiary of these new policies, particularly given its higher proportion of interbank liabilities and trading account assets.

The specific implications for banks are as follows:

## 1. Increased Direct Financing Proportion and Slower, Higher-Quality Loan Growth as the New Normal

According to the PBOC, China's financing structure is undergoing a significant transformation. Indirect financing, predominantly loans, has seen its share in Total Social Financing (TSF) increments decline from over 80% to 45% by 2025. Similarly, its proportion in outstanding TSF has decreased from nearly 100% to 2/3 by 2025. Furthermore, a notable divergence in loan allocation is evident: new loans to the property and infrastructure sectors have fallen from over 60% to just 10% currently, while new loans to the “Five Key Areas” of finance—technology finance, green finance, inclusive finance, elderly care finance, and digital finance—have surged to over 70%.

The PBOC believes that as the economic structure upgrades from asset-heavy traditional industries reliant on medium-to-long-term loan financing to asset-light emerging industries that depend on more diversified financing methods, maintaining past credit growth rates is both challenging and unnecessary. The central bank emphasizes that enhancing the quality and efficiency of loans is paramount, suggesting that “slower, higher-quality loan growth” is likely to become a new normal for the economy.

This assessment aligns with our recent field trip takeaways and the TSF data trends, further reinforcing our view that credit growth will continue to decelerate, while bond investments will maintain relatively faster growth. In an environment characterized by a slower pace of balance sheet expansion, we believe the intrinsic quality and defensiveness of banks' balance sheets will become increasingly critical.

## 2. Improved Short-Term Interest Rate Regulation Mechanism to Stabilize Interbank Funding Costs


We anticipate that this reduction in the short-term interest rate ceiling and the narrowing of the corridor will help mitigate short-term rate volatility, thereby stabilizing banks' interbank funding costs and thus NIM, particularly during periods of tighter liquidity such as quarter-ends and tax seasons. Given that small and medium-sized banks typically have weaker deposit bases and a higher proportion of interbank funding in their liability structures compared to larger banks, they are expected to benefit more significantly from this policy. For example, among the banks we cover, Industrial Bank, Huaxia Bank, and BONB exhibit the highest interbank liability proportion, recorded at 29%, 26%, and 22% respectively in 2025, in contrast to 15% for the large four SOE banks.

## 3. Offshore RMB FX Trading Pilot to Expand International Business for Participating Banks, but with Limited Earnings Contribution

The PBOC has authorized six banks—ICBC, ABC, BOC, CCB, BoCom, and CITIC Bank—to conduct offshore RMB FX trading in the Shanghai Free Trade Zone, utilizing the China Foreign Exchange Trade System (CFETS) platform. This initiative aims to promote the two-way opening of the foreign exchange market.

This development implies that pilot banks will be able to meet the FX hedging needs of overseas investors onshore (in Shanghai). This capability is expected to facilitate the expansion of their international business, generate new fee income growth points, attract new clients, and enhance client stickiness. However, cross-referencing data from the top international business player like BOC reveals that its entire international settlement business contributes less than 7% to its fee income and less than 1% to its total revenue. Therefore, we believe this new business will not have a significant financial

impact on banks in the short term.

## 4. Macro-Prudential Tool for Non-Bank FIs Liquidity Support to Stabilize Bond Market and Investment Income

The PBOC will study the establishment of a macro-prudential tool designed to provide liquidity support to non-bank financial institutions in specific scenarios. This tool would offer emergency liquidity via swaps when systemic pressure arises in bond or other markets, normal liquidity channels are obstructed, and a collective liquidity crisis among institutions could potentially trigger systemic risks. The PBOC explicitly states that this is not intended as a normalized liquidity provision; moreover, non-bank FIs will be required to furnish high-quality collateral to prevent moral hazard within the financial market.

Under normal circumstances, non-bank FIs primarily source their liquidity from banks. Consequently, when systemic pressure emerges in the bond market (e.g., the wealth management product redemption wave in late 2022) and banks restrict lending, non-bank FIs are often compelled to sell highly liquid assets, exacerbating market volatility and net asset value (NAV) declines. Therefore, the PBOC's provision of emergency liquidity through swaps is expected to help stabilize the bond market, which in turn will contribute to the stability of banks' bond investment income, particularly for their FVTPL and FVTOCI accounts. Among the banks we cover, BONB, BONJ, and BOC exhibit the highest proportions of trading accounts within their investment assets, recorded at 67%, 67%, and 55% respectively.

## Capital market: New Policies May Favor Growth of Market Leaders

The latest policy signals from the Lujiazui Forum indicate that supply-side tools in the capital market will be expanded at an accelerated pace, while regulatory constraints will be further tightened. For brokers, the core implication is that incremental opportunities will become concentrated among leading institutions that combine strong institutional capabilities, cross-border platforms, and robust risk management frameworks. Among our coverage, we reiterate our Buy rating for CICC-H, considering its advantages in institutional business and cross-border service capabilities.

The specific impacts on various business lines are as follows:

## 1. Wealth Management: Expansion of the Trading Ecosystem and Cross-Border Channels

The most visible near-term incremental opportunity for wealth management stems from the expansion of eligible trading instruments and the gradual build-out of a more integrated cross-border ecosystem.

Regulatory support for launching active ETFs on a pilot basis, alongside the listing of the first batch of C-REITs, is likely to increase on-exchange liquidity and stimulate associated market flows, which not only supports retail trading activity but more importantly reinforces institutional brokerage demand through market making, ETF flows, and execution services tied to block trades and structured transactions. Meanwhile, the expansion of QDII quotas and the development trajectory for RMB FX futures and

offshore interest rate derivatives will open up medium- to long-term space for cross-border wealth management and global asset allocation, promoting a shift in the revenue structure from pure transaction fees toward comprehensive income tied to more complex client needs.

The policy mix reflects a dual shift toward broader product supply (active ETFs, C-REITs) and more institutionalized, long-term demand (pensions, insurance inflows), supporting AUM-based and allocation-driven business models. Expansion of fund advisory pilots further strengthens fee-based, client-centric revenue streams and the transition toward buy-side advisory.

## 2. Investment Banking: Increased Deal Visibility but Stronger Execution Thresholds

The policy stance is most directly positive for investment banking, particularly in merger and M&A advisory and equity capital markets. Regulators have emphasized enhancing the efficiency of M&A transactions through faster review processes and the removal of procedural bottlenecks, which should increase both deal velocity and completion certainty. At the same time, a continued push toward optimizing refinancing mechanisms, such as potential introduction of shelf registration and revisions to existing issuance frameworks, improves flexibility for listed companies, especially in technology and advanced manufacturing sectors.

Meanwhile, structural expansion of the listing ecosystem broadens the addressable deal universe, through the extension of STAR Board standards to AI companies, further reforms to the ChiNext board, and a more supportive stance toward Hong Kong-listed companies seeking domestic listings. These changes collectively suggest a more stable pipeline of complex transactions, including cross-border listings, A/H-linked structures, and capital-intensive strategic deals.

However, stricter requirements for information disclosure, pricing, and compliance make it more likely that incremental deal flow will increasingly concentrate among top-tier brokers with stronger underwriting capability, internal controls, and pricing discipline.

## 3. Trading and Investment Business: Improved Market Environment Rather Than Directional Gains

Policy impact is primarily through a more stable and better-instrumented trading environment rather than directional earnings upside, with finer liquidity management helping reduce funding volatility and improve inventory risk control. The narrowing of the interest rate corridor and the refinement of liquidity management will help reduce funding volatility and improve the efficiency of market-making and hedging.

At the same time, ongoing development of derivatives markets (including FX futures and cross-border hedging tools) enhances overall depth and completeness of the hedging ecosystem. These developments are likely to improve risk transfer efficiency and reduce tail risks, but do not necessarily imply outsized proprietary trading gains. Instead, they disproportionately benefit brokers with established FICC pricing capabilities, risk management systems, and balance sheet flexibility.
