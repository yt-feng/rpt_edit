## China: The inflated rating system is challenged by a rising share of loss-making firms

An overhaul of the rating system is needed to better inform investors and policymakers.


Jing Wang - NIHK

Ting Lu - NIHK


Fig. 1: China's domestic rating system fails to reflect corporate financial health

[[KC_IMAGE_001]]


\- According to Bloomberg, Chinese regulators, including the PBoC, have requested domestic rating agencies revamp their rating systems to more accurately reflect the credits risks of local firms. In Q1 2026, a striking $59\%$ of issuers were rated as AAA/AA+, while major international rating agencies award such high grades far less commonly, even in developed bond markets. Even more striking, the share of AAA/AA+ rated debt financing instruments of non-financial enterprises has increased to $74.6\%$ .

\- This proliferation of top-tier ratings is directly contradicted by worsening corporate financial health, casting doubt on the credibility of China's credit rating system. According to the NBS, the share of loss-making industrial companies climbed to $23.8\%$ in 2025, the highest level since 2000, from $11.8\%$ in 2017, and this share reached $32.4\%$ in the first four months of this year, up from $31.7\%$ a year earlier, pointing to a worse situation in 2026.

\- Despite the AI boom, China's economy is still being battered by the unprecedented property bust, which has continued to drive up the credit risks of both corporates and households. In this fragile economic environment, a flawed domestic rating system might mask underlying default vulnerabilities and send misleading signals to investors and policymakers.
