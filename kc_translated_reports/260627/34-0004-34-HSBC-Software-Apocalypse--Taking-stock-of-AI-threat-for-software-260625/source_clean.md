# Software Apocalypse?

\- We reviewed hard evidence of AI disrupting the software sector and see no signs of material AI disruption

\- Market is likely overestimating the productivity boost from AI tools, which is leading it to overestimate AI risk to software

\- We maintain Buy on major software names that have sold off on what we consider are unwarranted fears of AI disruption

No signs of a software apocalypse yet: We maintain our view that AI is unlikely to replace software as explained in: Can AI replace software? (9 Feb 2026) and Software is already eating AI (26 Feb 2026).

Software revenue growth remained strong in 1Q26 (median 12.7% y-o-y constant currency). Current remaining performance obligations also posted strong double-digit growth. Multiple software companies (e.g. SAP, Salesforce, and ServiceNow) provided multiyear guidance and are generally guiding to continuation of strong growth for several years. There are no signs of pricing pressure in the sector. Most software companies reported increased non-GAAP operating margin in 1Q26 and are guiding to further margin expansion.

Software companies are generally downplaying concerns about AI disrupting their business and are buying back shares aggressively. If we leave aside outliers, total share buybacks by major software companies are up more than 3x since 2023-24.

AI impact on employee productivity low single-digit currently: AI-driven cost savings identified by software companies are at about 5-7.5% of their existing cost base. We estimate AI coding tools have boosted productivity at IT services and software companies by 100-500bp, at best. As the productivity boost is modest, there is nothing to suggest that one could replace existing enterprise software applications cheaply by fresh vibe-coded systems as the market seems to be dreading.

Enterprise software stocks oversold: Software shares have de-rated significantly over the last 12 months on concerns that AI will disrupt their business. If we use consensus estimates, major software shares are trading at c50% lower P/NTM non-GAAP EPS than their 24-month averages. As explained above, we consider these concerns unwarranted.

## Software revenue and guidance remain strong

We have reviewed results, guidance, comments, and other actions of software companies in the last few months to review our stance on the software apocalypse debate.

Software revenue growth remained strong in 1Q26 (median 12.7% y-o-y constant currency).

Current remaining performance obligations growth remained strong. Adobe (13.1%), Salesforce (13.5%), ServiceNow (22.7%), and Workday (15.4%) reported robust y-o-y growth in cRPO in the last reported quarter. SAP's current cloud backlog rose 25% y-o-y cc in 1Q26.

Total remaining performance obligations growth also remained strong. Adobe (13.1%), Salesforce (11.5%), ServiceNow (25.3%), and Workday (10.9%) reported robust y-o-y growth in RPO in the last reported quarter. Workday's growth of 10.9% was, however, materially less than 20% y-o-y growth it reported during 2024 and 9M25. Management attributed it to a higher portion of RPO coming from renewals, which tend to have shorter duration than new contracts. Oracle's (362% y-o-y to USD638bn in 2QCY26) and Microsoft's (97.2% y-o-y to USD633bn in 1QCY26) RPO growth rates were off the charts but were driven largely by cloud infrastructure and not software. Oracle and Microsoft do not report separate RPO for software segment.

SAP, ServiceNow, Salesforce, and Oracle have issued/maintained multi-year revenue growth guidance in the last two months. All of them are guiding to continued double-digit revenue growth for several years. SAP, Salesforce, and Oracle are guiding to revenue acceleration, although Oracle's acceleration is largely due to cloud infrastructure segment and not due to software. ServiceNow has guided to an 18.4% revenue CAGR over FY26-30e. If a software apocalypse is in the works, we do not see signs of concerns coming from the largest enterprise software companies on the planet (and their customers responsible for RPO).

## Companies reporting low churn

Most software companies continued to report low churn.

ServiceNow reported 1QCY26 renewal rate of 97%, not far from 97.75% average over the previous four quarters.

Autodesk reported net revenue retention rate of “more than 110%”, which was helped by the impact of the new transaction model (we estimate low single-digit percentage). The company continues to expect NRR to settle at 100-110% as the impact of the new transaction model recedes. NRR above 100% suggests continued revenue growth at existing customers.

Salesforce has reported an attrition rate of about 8% for the last several quarters including in 4QCY25. The company did not report attrition in 1QCY26 but called it “very little”.

SAP described its churn in 4QCY25 as “low”. SAP has historically talked about an annual churn of about 3%.

\- Other major software companies in our coverage universe do not report churn regularly

## No signs of pricing pressure

There are also no signs of pricing pressure in the sector. In absence of better information, we are using non-GAAP operating margin as a proxy for pricing. Most software companies reported increased non-GAAP operating margin and are guiding to further margin expansion.

SAP non-GAAP operating margin was up 276bp y-o-y in 1Q26. The company continues to expect that its operating expense growth over 2026-27 would be 80-90% of revenue growth, resulting in improving operating margins.

ServiceNow non-GAAP operating margin expanded 94bp y-o-y to 31.8% in 1QCY26. CY26 non-GAAP operating margin guidance stood at 31.5% (FY25: 31.2%) despite 75bp adverse impact of Armis acquisition in CY26. The company guided to a further 100bp non-GAAP operating margin and FCF margin expansion in 2027e.

Salesforce non-GAAP operating margin rose 253bp y-o-y to 34.8% in 1QCY26. Margin guidance of 34.3% in FY27 ending Jan 2027 (FY26: 34.1%) suggests more gradual progress after c15ppt of expansion since FY22.

Microsoft non-GAAP operating margin rose 66bp y-o-y to 46.3% in 1QCY26 despite likely adverse impact of rising share of Azure cloud infrastructure in the mix.

Oracle non-GAAP operating margin rose 54bp y-o-y to 44.8% despite likely adverse impact of rising share of Oracle cloud infrastructure in the mix.

## Software companies putting their money where the mouth is

As can be seen above, software companies have downplayed the idea of AI replacing them. On the other hand, Palantir, which is more of a data and AI platform, has heralded the “death of legacy software”.

We note, however, software companies are putting their money where their mouth is by buying back their shares aggressively. Salesforce has been the most aggressive and bought back USD27bn worth of shares in the last reported quarter. In comparison, we have not seen software companies that also have significant exposure to cloud infrastructure capex (Oracle and Microsoft) increase their share buybacks. If we exclude Salesforce, Oracle, and Microsoft, the total amount of share buybacks by major software companies are up by more than 3x since the level in 2023-24.

## We do not expect AI to replace software

We estimate AI coding tools have boosted productivity at IT services and software companies by 100-500bp, at best. There is nothing to suggest that one could replace existing enterprise software applications cheaply by fresh vibe-coded systems

We maintain our view that AI is unlikely to replace software as explained in our notes Can AI replace software? (9 Feb 2026) and Software is already eating AI (26 Feb 2026). In this note, we primarily review our assumptions and premises behind our conclusion.

## Productivity boost from vibe coding is often overestimated

Many of us have used AI tools to make simple programs and have come away amazed at how quickly it is able to come up with programs that would have taken us days manually (two of the authors of the report were previously professional programmers). Many investors, in our opinion, are making the mistake of extrapolating these superlative productivity gains into large enterprise software applications. As the system that you are trying to vibe code becomes larger, your ability to describe it accurately in plain English deteriorates. If you feed incorrect or incomplete description to AI coding tools, they will generate a wrong program. Even if you provide a perfect description, LLMs are still prone to hallucinate and make occasional mistakes. Talking to some of the programmers, we get a sense that AI code generators are dramatically increasing the speed of writing code. But they make subtle mistakes and human employees are forced to spend inordinate amounts of time trying to catch the mistakes largely offsetting the benefit from quickly written code.

## IT services productivity gain

We estimate IT sector productivity may have been boosted by at best 100-200bp due to the adoption of AI tools. We are using revenue per employee as one measure of productivity. Over 2023-24 (before AI coding tools became ubiquitous), revenue per employee for major IT service companies rose by c2% p.a. We have seen revenue per employee rise by c5% y-o-y in 1Q26 and 2Q26. For example:

Accenture's 2QCY26 annualized revenue per employee of USD102K is up $4.3\%$ y-o-y. Three-year revenue per employee CAGR is only $0.8\%$ as revenue per employee dipped in 2024 before rising in 2026 and 1H26. Total number of employees rose by $1\%$ y-o-y to 799K in 2QCY26 and utilization rose by 1ppt y-o-y to $93\%$ .

Infosys's 1QCY26 annualized revenue per employee of USD74K was up by $4.4\%$ y-o-y. Three-year revenue per employee CAGR is only $2.5\%$ . Total number of employees rose $4.2\%$ y-o-y to 337k in 1QCY26 albeit utilization dipped 2ppt y-o-y to $80\%$ .

We see no tangible metric that would indicate that a typical IT service employee is seeing any dramatic improvement in productivity. Revenue per employee is rising barely above inflation while changes in number of employees or their utilization is unremarkable.

## Software productivity gain

We estimate AI tools may have boosted software sector productivity growth by up to c500bp. Over 2018-24 (before large scale adoption of AI coding tools), software companies saw their revenue per employee rise at a CAGR of about 4.2% while the broader technology sector saw its revenue per employee rise at a CAGR of about 4.3%. Revenue per employee for software companies rose by c9% y-o-y in the last few quarters. Broader technology sector revenue per employee rose by 10-13% y-o-y as the growth was dominated by cloud infrastructure, social media, and search sectors, which are much less employee-intensive.

Revenue per employee is an imperfect measure of productivity, especially in the software sector due to high operating leverage. Revenue per employee dipped during 2019 and 2022 due to broader slowdown in growth and not because of any reduction in productivity. Likewise, some of the recent boost may be cyclical as opposed to true increase in productivity (or in other words, we may be overestimating AI's impact on productivity). Unfortunately, we do not have access to data on (say) features developed per programmer to truly assess the productivity boost.

## So what?

If a challenger tried to replicate (say) SAP using a new vibe coded system, he/she may be able to develop an alternative SAP that could be 1-5% cheaper (reflecting 100-500bp improvement in productivity estimated above) than what it cost the original SAP to build itself. But considering heavy costs of replacing an existing ERP vendor and hardly 3% per annum churn, it is far from clear that the challenger will have a winning cost advantage.

Software incumbents have significant advantages including users' familiarization with the system GUI, interoperability with other tools, favourable network externalities including availability of experienced support staff, and bug reduction that comes after decades of being used by thousands of enterprise customers. A challenger needs a lot more than a slight cost advantage to replace the incumbents.

## What have software companies said about AI-driven productivity gains

SAP expects to cut its costs by EUR2bn by 2028, which is $7.6\%$ of SAP's 2025 non-GAAP operating costs.

ServiceNow expects AI to cut its costs by USD500m over 2025-26, of which USD300m could flow to the bottom-line while the rest will likely be reinvested into the business. USD500m is c5.5% of ServiceNow's CY25 non-GAAP operating costs.

## Is there a counter point as well?

There are several anecdotes of sharply improving productivity even though these are not apparent from overall numbers. As cost-cutting guidance from these same companies are more muted (as shown in the above section), we have treated these anecdotes as not indicative of the overall situation. We also note that R&D is only about 10-20% of revenue (15-35% of costs) for major software companies and programming is only part of the total R&D costs. Therefore, sharply increased programmer productivity does not necessarily mean sharply higher companywide productivity. A challenger to incumbent software companies will need to spend money not just on programming but also on designing, testing, and marketing the software system.

Salesforce mentioned in 1QCY26 that AI coding tools enabled it to double the features and code shipped y-o-y, while simultaneously reducing incidents and defects.

SAP CEO said in a recent conference call: “We will also set up our development teams. It's not anymore these teams of 10. It will be teams of five, more agile. You prototype an idea with vibe coding with one or two days, then you turn around because the validation brought you some insights which you need to consider. Then you go into the automation side where we can automate a lot on code generation, agent development, testing, et cetera.”

Oracle said in 1QCY26 earnings conference call: "The use of AI coding tools inside Oracle is enabling smaller engineering teams to deliver more complete solutions to our customers more quickly. We are building brand-new SaaS products using AI and also embedding AI agents right into our existing application suites. By embracing AI with small engineering teams, we have just built three brand new CX applications; lead generation and qualification, sales orchestration and automated selling, and our new website generator."
