# Americas Technology: Hardware: Expert Network Series: CPU server demand driven by refresh, agentic AI

We hosted an investor webinar with Jordan Plawner, Founder & Executive AI Strategist at Pacific AI Advisory and Former Global Director of Intel AI Products and Strategy at Intel, on June 26th, 20226 as part of our Expert Network Series to discuss current trends impact the server market.

Bottom line: (1) Agnetic AI could drive a 5-6x uplift to the CPU server market, as CPUs are better designed for the sequential tasks & heterogeneous workloads that AI agents execute (v. GPUs designed for massive parallel processing); (2) Significant opportunity for performance and efficiency improvements from refreshing the installed based of legacy servers; (3) Only \~1/2 of enterprise server demand seen at Dell and HPE is currently being fulfilled because of industry supply constraints, driving company-level backlog expansion and creating visibility into future revenue.

## Key takeaways

Agentic AI could drive a 5-6x uplift to the CPU server market. To frame the opportunity, Mr. Plawner estimates that on average, AI training workloads currently have a 1:4 ratio of CPUs:GPUs in their systems, which translates to \$1 of CPU spend for every \$50-\$80 of GPU spend. As the market moves towards more diversified inference and agentic workloads, Mr. Plawner sees the ratio of CPUs:GPUs moving towards 1:1 on average over time, which translations to \$1 of CPU spend for every \$6-\$10 of GPU spend. This should create a positive demand tailwind for hardware OEMs including DELL and HPE, who have deep general compute relationships across enterprise customers. CPUs are better designed for sequential tasks & heterogeneous workloads like orchestration (v. GPUs designed for massive parallel processing), which makes them better suited for agentic AI tasks. CPUs also have direct access to DRAM, which makes them more efficient as feeding more data into AI systems, improving their utility.

\~2-3 year payback period for enterprises refreshing aging traditional server installed base. In an illustrative example, Mr. Plawner estimates that an enterprise could replace 1,000 older servers with \~350 current generation servers (approximately a \~3:1 densification), driving significant operational expenses, such as an estimated \~\$1 mn/year in lower spend on power. Mr. Plawner noted that the installed base of traditional servers is \~6 years old on average (v. 3-4 year average life historically), as organizations have prioritized IT budget on GPUs & related AI training

Latest generation CPU servers offer significant performance and efficiency improvements as compared the vast majority of the installed base, driven by a \~4x higher core counts and \~2x denser memory/core. For cloud service providers, this densification is important because it addresses issues around power constraints, which is one of the limiting factors of overall compute growth. Supply constraints are limiting enterprise server shipments. Mr. Plawner estimates that only \~1/2 of enterprise server demand seen at Dell and HPE is currently being fulfilled and that it will take at least another 18 months for a larger share of this demand to be met (in line with the time it will take for more memory capacity to be brought on line, increasing the supply of DRAM allocated to traditional servers). Robust demand for HBM from the hyperscalers & AI model builders is squeezing DRAM allocations for enterprise servers. Mr. Plawner thinks that even when more memory supply does come line, server prices will remain elevated. CPU and memory suppliers are likely to continue to prioritizing their largest and most strategic customers, including OEMs like DELL and HPE, over the long tail of smaller vendors and manufacturers that are less scaled.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

## M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
