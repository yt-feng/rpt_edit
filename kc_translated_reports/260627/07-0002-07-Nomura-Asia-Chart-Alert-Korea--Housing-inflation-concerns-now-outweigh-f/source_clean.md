# Korea: Housing inflation concerns now outweigh fading inflation pressures

Deepening financial stability concerns are likely to support the BOK's hawkish stance, despite easing inflation pressures.

\- Inflation risks are fading. Brent has retraced close to its pre-conflict level since the US-Iran ceasefire, unwinding much of the energy premium embedded in the BOK's H2 2026 inflation outlook. The BOK's latest projections were conditioned on Brent averaging around USD95/bbl, leaving the July MPC meeting with a growing downside risk to the inflation forecast. Unless oil prices reverse sharply higher, the inflation pillar supporting front-loaded tightening (i.e., back-to-back hikes) has weakened considerably.

\- Housing has become the primary policy driver. Seoul apartment prices rose at a 15.8% annualized pace in the final week of June, marking an eighth consecutive week of double-digit annualized gains. These persistence of price gains highlight housing prices as an increasingly important financial stability concern.

\- We continue to expect 25bp hikes in July, October and January (terminal rate: 3.25%). The focus, however, is likely to shift toward financial stability from inflation. Thus, following a highly likely July hike, subsequent hikes are likely to proceed at a measured pace, allowing the BOK to balance financial stability risks against moderating inflation and only a gradual improvement in domestic demand.
