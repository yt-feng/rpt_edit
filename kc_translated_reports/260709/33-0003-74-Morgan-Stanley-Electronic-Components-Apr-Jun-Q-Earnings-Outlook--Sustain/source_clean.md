# Apr-Jun Q Earnings Outlook: Sustainability of Strong Earnings the Key Consideration

We forecast total Apr-Jun OP for 19 firms will increase 33.6% YoY, with strong demand from AI/data center applications continuing, but rising raw material costs warrant close attention.

## Key Takeaways

We expect continued growth for ABF package substrates for AI/DC semis and MLCCs; rising raw materials cost a key risk for aluminum electrolytic capacitors.

Murata Mfg. still our Top Pick, continue to rate Taiyo Yuden UW. We expect the strength of Murata's high-value-added MLCCs to become increasingly evident.

We also expect Ibiden's earnings expansion to continue over the longer term, but remain UW as market expectations and valuation look too high.

Expect strong Apr-Jun results for beneficiaries of AI/DC-related sales growth, inc. Ibiden & Murata: For Ibiden, we expect strong growth in sales of high-value-added ABF package substrates, with sales for NVIDIA's Rubin platform exceeding those for Blackwell. With use of the declining-balance method, we estimate 1Q depreciation expense will decline by ¥4.5bn QoQ, enabling high earnings from 1Q. For Murata, we expect high earnings from 1Q driven by rising sales of high-value-added MLCCs for AI/DC applications and higher utilization rates in core MLCCs. OW on Murata, TDK, Hirose Electric, Niterra, Alps Alpine: Murata's longer-term earnings growth is driven by an improving mix on rising sales of high-value-added MLCCs for AI/DC use. For TDK, profit growth from rechargeable batteries is limited in F3/27, but expect HDD heads and passive components to drive growth, with rechargeable batteries for DC BBUs to contribute from F3/29. For Hirose, we expect sales growth in connectors for both industrial equipment and AI servers, and continued ROE improvement longer term via enhanced shareholder returns and strategic investments. For Niterra, we expect stable, high level profit from spark plugs to increase gradually, market share gains in exhaust gas oxygen sensors, and continued sales growth of electrostatic chucks for SPE customers in Japan/US. UW on Taiyo Yuden, Ibiden, Nippon Chemi-Con, Daishinku: Taiyo Yuden's core MLCCs should continue expanding, but we see the earnings gap vs. Murata (higher weighting of high-value-added MLCCs for AI/DC) widening, and valuation is rich. Ibiden's near-term earnings are strong, but bar to achieving its F3/31 OP target of ¥300bn is high (if achieved, F3/31 P/E still a lofty \~24x). Chemi-Con has risk to 2H F3/27 OP guidance (¥5bn) and high valuation (\~50x F3/27e EPS for common shares, post dilution & preferred divs). For Daishinku, we're cautious on risk of delayed contributions from crystal oscillators for optical transceivers or failure to gain meaningful share, slowing demand for crystal devices in Chinese smartphones/PCs.

# MLCCs: Expect differing product clout to produce longer-term earnings variations

## MLCCs are the most important products for Murata and Taiyo Yuden

In F3/26, MLCCs made up 51% of sales at Murata and 71% of sales at Taiyo Yuden, and we expect them to drive earnings growth at both companies from F3/27. Though earnings growth at both is outpacing the market consensus, of the two the shares of Taiyo Yuden have outperformed in 2026 YTD (up to July 6), advancing by 419% vs. Murata's +216%. This leaves Taiyo Yuden looking overvalued at P/E of 37.3x on our F3/31e diluted EPS, vs. Murata on 21.1x. While growth in sales of MLCCs for AI/data centers stands to lift earnings at both companies, we see limited benefits of product mix improvement due to MLCC miniaturization and capacitance increases for Taiyo Yuden, and downgraded the stock to UW on June 17 on the view that the gap between the share price and the earnings outlook up to F3/31 has widened (report link).

## Feedback from Top Pick on Murata, UW downgrade on Taiyo Yuden

Publication of our report on June 17 provided an opportunity to discuss the near- and longer-term outlook for the MLCC market with many market participants. Three key tenets of our report were that 1) among capacitors that are less likely to be integrated into semiconductor circuits, demand for MLCCs will increase in the longer term; 2) Murata will maintain its #1 position supported by its competitiveness not only in technology but also in costs; and 3) there is a lot of scope for downside in Taiyo Yuden shares, which have risen to trade on higher multiples than Murata, and look overvalued. Despite cyclical swings, we expect MLCC demand to continue expanding in the medium and longer term, and Murata to maintain the #1 position, and we found many who agreed with our view. This is also the case for our view that Taiyo Yuden is overvalued, but some investors have also opined that Murata shares could fall as well in a phase of decline in Taiyo Yuden's share price.

## Expect small, high-capacitance value-added MLCCs for AI/DCs to drive the MLCC market

We estimate global MLCC shipment value at US\$14.67bn (+10.3% YoY) in 2025, and that shares were 40.8% for Murata, 22.5% for SEMCO, 11.3% for Taiyo Yuden, 6.9% for TDK, 5.4% for Yageo, and 13.0% collectively for makers other than the top 5. This implies a 10-year CAGR of +6.5% for MLCC global shipment value, from \$7.8bn in 2025, but for the coming three years we foresee a faster CAGR of +18.2%, to \$24.25bn in 2028. We expect rising sales of small, high-capacitance value-added products for AI servers and data centers to have a considerable impact in expanding MLCC demand, and see Murata as the likely main beneficiary due to its ability to supply small, high-capacitance units in large quantities before competitors.

## Murata leads the way in small, high-capacitance MLCCs for AI servers

Murata has been a pioneer in MLCC miniaturization and higher capacitance, having started shipping mass-produced 1005-size 1μF MLCCs in 2000, and 0402-size 1μF units as well as 1005-size 22μF units in 2020, among other milestones. Demand for 1608-size 100 F, 1005-size 47μF and 0603-size 10μF MLCCs for AI servers is rising sharply, and Murata appears to be the only company capable of securing stable yields in mass production of all of these.

## Expect differences in MLCC product clout to open up mid/longer-term earnings gaps

There are many Japanese electronic component companies, such as Murata in MLCCs, with the ability to compete in both technology and costs. Many of them are not moving quickly to raise prices on the grounds of tight supply/demand, and many of the latter are enjoying high levels of profit backed by their strong technology competitiveness in cutting-edge components. At the same time they are also maintaining high cost competitiveness in general-purpose products. We believe the general-purposes space in MLCCs has many participants, including Chinese companies, and that distributors are often involved in transactions. We further infer that prices for MLCCs charged by distributors to customers and by MLCCs makers to distributors are heavily influenced by the S/D situation at the time. We also think it likely that Murata adjusts its prices for general-purpose MLCCs to distributors flexibly, both upwards and downwards, in response to market prices. At the same time, while increases in general-purpose MLCC prices can help Murata too to maximize near-term profit, there is also a risk of them leading to a lower share for the company in the mid/longer term as entry barriers come down enabling more participation by companies from China, South Korea, Taiwan and elsewhere. For such companies, rising market prices for general-purpose products make it possible to book profit where this has not consistently been the case, positioning them better to develop and enter the markets of not just commodity but also middle- and high-end products. We understand that distributors and MLCC makers who mainly handle commodity products and are seeking to maximize near-term profit in the general-purposes MLCC market are currently warning customers about risks of future MLCC shortages and price increases in a bid to push up prices for general-purpose products. Nevertheless, we expect expansion of the scale of the MLCC market to be heavily influenced by rising average selling prices stemming from growth in volumes of value-added products, such as small, high-capacitance units for AI/DCs, and in other products with higher added-value. As such, taking a longer-term view of the MLCC market, we think companies such as Murata, with superior abilities to compete in both technology and costs, stand to see significantly more growth in corporate value than those who aim to maximize near-term profit via short-term increases in prices of general-purpose MLCCs.

## We forecast earnings assuming ¥155/\$ and ¥180/€

Our forecasts for Apr-Jun and beyond are based on our forex assumptions of ¥155/\$ and ¥180/€. Based on each company's briefing, we estimate that a ¥1/\$ fluctuation would result in an impact of ¥14.9bn (1.0% of FY26e OP) in the total annual OP of our coverage universe. For companies with fiscal years ending in March, the average ¥/\$ exchange rate was ¥112.3/\$ in FY21, ¥135.4/\$ in FY22, ¥144.5/\$ in FY23, ¥152.5/\$ in FY24, and ¥150.6/\$ in FY25. For companies such as Murata, Hirose, Niterra that produce a high percentage of their core products in Japan and have a high percentage of sales denominated in foreign currencies, we expect a weaker yen/stronger dollar to boost profits and a stronger yen/weaker dollar to depress profits. In our view, the impact of exchange rates on the earnings of companies with a high ratio of overseas sales of module components, motors, and others is often unknown until the earnings of overseas subsidiaries is consolidated with that of the Japanese headquarters.

# Key points underlying our earnings outlook

## Ibiden (4062, UW, PT ¥13,000)

We forecast Ibiden's F3/27 OP at ¥94.7bn (.6 FactSet consensus: ¥99.7bn). Our estimate of +¥32.7bn YoY growth mainly reflects an expected +¥32.7bn increase in earnings from the Electronics business. We expect this sharp increase in Electronics earnings to be driven primarily by higher sales of ABF package substrates for NVIDIA, as NVIDIA's flagship products shift from Blackwell to Rubin, with an accompanying rise in unit prices. We estimate that the unit price of ABF package substrates for Rubin will be roughly twice that of Blackwell.

We forecast F3/28 OP of ¥128.5bn (company guidance: ¥150bn; FactSet consensus: ¥151.5bn). The implied ¥33.7bn YoY increase is again mainly due to an expected ¥32.1bn increase in earnings from Electronics. In Electronics, we expect NVIDIA's AI server GPUs to shift from Rubin to Rubin Ultra, supporting a continued rise in the average selling price of ABF package substrates. In addition, against the backdrop of increased procurement of T-Glass/CCL and higher production volumes at the Ohno Plant, we forecast that ABF package substrates sales will rise +31% YoY, or ¥100.8bn. Although we expect depreciation to increase by +¥15bn, from ¥71bn in FY3/27 to ¥86bn in F3/28, we believe Ibiden can still secure profit growth, although we do not expect OP to reach company guidance.

We forecast F3/27 1Q OP of ¥21.6bn (FactSet consensus: ¥20.2bn). We anticipate +¥4.1bn QoQ growth, mainly because we expect Electronics sales to rise by +¥4.8bn, while we look for companywide depreciation to decline by -¥4.5bn.

We forecast F3/27 OP of ¥121.5bn for Minebea Mitsumi (company guidance: ¥120bn; FactSet consensus: ¥121.3bn), +¥17.5bn YoY on expected profit growth across all four segments.
