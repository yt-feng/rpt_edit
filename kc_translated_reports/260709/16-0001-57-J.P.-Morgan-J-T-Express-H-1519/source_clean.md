## Initiate at OW: Share price reflects excessive pessimism, but growth and margin inflection in sight

We are initiating coverage of J&T Express with an Overweight rating. Since its October 2023 IPO, J&T's share price has underperformed, reflecting sector price wars in China, initial loss-making status, and external shocks such as recent fuel price volatility. The market has also questioned the scalability of J&T's model outside China and the sustainability of margins in a maturing Southeast Asia, while New Markets were seen as too early-stage to move the needle. However, the business has now pivoted to profitability, is delivering industry-leading volume and margin growth, and is unlocking new synergies through its partnership with SF Holdings. The stock has de-rated to c.10x forward P/E (Non-IFRS), despite a visible inflection in earnings and a proven, exportable business model. We believe this disconnect is compelling: J&T's scalable platform, accelerating New Markets, and cost leadership are not reflected in the current valuation, and a c.20% premium to sector average is justified by c.50% net profit CAGR over FY25-28E (vs. sector 15–20%).

\- A scalable, platform-neutral model is driving share gains and margin expansion, with Southeast Asia remaining the core profit engine but New Markets now a real growth lever. The company aggregates volumes across all major e-commerce and social commerce platforms, supporting 34.4% market share in SEA (FY25) and double-digit volume growth in New Markets. While investors have worried about yield erosion in a maturing SEA market, J&T's cost leadership and ability to diversify into higher-margin, non-platform business are supporting margin resilience. Meanwhile, New Markets (Brazil, Mexico, Middle East) have reached a profitability inflection, with volumes doubling Y/Y and market share rising, showing the China/SEA playbook is working globally.

\- Profitability inflection is driving robust free cash flow and a clear commitment to shareholder returns. J&T's disciplined investment in automation and network buildout is now translating into strong operating leverage and margin expansion across all regions. As the asset base matures, capex is trending toward 4–5% of revenue, supporting a step-change in FCF generation. Management has initiated share buybacks, signaling confidence in the business and a willingness to return capital to shareholders as profitability and cash flow improve progressively.

\- Valuation disconnect creates a buy opportunity as profitability inflects and growth accelerates across all regions. J&T was listed at a high valuation (mid-cycle non-IFRS P/E of 27x one-year forward) but de-rated due to sector headwinds, macro shocks, and skepticism about the scalability of its model. Now trading at c.10x forward P/E, the stock does not reflect the company's c.50% net profit CAGR (FY25–28E), platform diversification, and visible margin improvement in both core and new markets. We see c.37% upside as the market re-rates J&T on sustained earnings delivery, cost leadership, and a proven, exportable business model.

## Investment Summary

We initiate coverage on J&T Express (1519 HK) with an Overweight (OW) rating and a P/E-based June 2027 price target of HK\$13, implying c.37% upside. Our target equates to an FY27E non-IFRS P/E of 13x, a c.20% premium to the sector average of c.11x, justified by J&T's c.50% non-IFRS net profit CAGR over FY25–28E (sector: 15–20%). The current c.10x P/E is attractive given J&T's dominant Southeast Asia scale, accelerating New Markets growth, proven China cost advantages, and tangible benefits from the SF Holdings partnership. We forecast c.20% revenue and c.50% net profit CAGR for FY25–28E, with ROE rising from 14% in 2025 to 24% in 2028E.

Since its Hong Kong listing in October 2023, J&T's share price has significantly underperformed, reflecting a combination of sector-wide price wars in China and initial loss-making status. While the stock rebounded in 2025 as the company pivoted to profitability and outpaced industry growth, it has recently de-rated again—now trading at c.10x forward P/E—due to renewed macro concerns, including the Middle East conflict, fuel shortages in Southeast Asia, and investor anxiety over the impact on regional consumption. We believe this dislocation has created a compelling entry point for a business with a fundamentally scalable and repeatable model.

J&T's platform-neutral, technology-driven approach is structurally advantaged for long-term expansion. The company's regional sponsor model blends entrepreneurial local execution with centralized technology and process standards, enabling rapid, capital-light expansion and consistent cost discipline. This model is not only proven in China's hyper-competitive environment but has also been successfully exported to Southeast Asia and New Markets, where J&T continues to gain share and drive down per-parcel costs. The ability to aggregate volumes across all major e-commerce and social commerce platforms—rather than being tied to a single ecosystem—ensures that J&T can capture the full benefit of rising e-commerce penetration, social commerce adoption, and omni-channel retailing. The business is not hitting a wall: in fact, J&T's network density, automation, and digital management systems are creating a self-reinforcing flywheel of scale, cost leadership, and service quality that is difficult for less-capitalized or less-flexible competitors to replicate.

The recently deepened strategic partnership with SF Holdings is poised to unlock significant synergies and accelerate J&T's global reach. This is not a simple capital tie-up: the collaboration is structured for operational integration, with joint investment in critical logistics infrastructure, including automated sorting centers and line-haul vehicles. J&T will be able to leverage SF's established first-mile, cross-border, and warehousing capabilities to further optimize its own last-mile and localized express delivery network, particularly in Southeast Asia and other high-growth regions. The partnership also brings governance alignment, with board representation and a strategic coordination committee, ensuring that both companies can rapidly adapt to new opportunities and challenges in international logistics. In China, the partnership allows J&T to access higher-value service tiers and segments, rather than competing head-on in low-margin, high-volume business, and positions the company to benefit from the industry's shift toward quality and sustainable growth.

J&T's business model is designed for flexibility and resilience, not just scale. The regional sponsor structure empowers local teams to adapt to regulatory, cultural, and market changes, while ongoing investment in automation, self-owned fleets, and digital platforms ensures the network remains both efficient and responsive. This adaptability has allowed J&T to double volumes in New Markets from 2023 to 2025, maintain leadership in Southeast Asia, and steadily improve profitability in China, even as the competitive landscape and regulatory environment evolve. The company's ability to replicate automation, digital management, and franchise models across regions has driven industry-leading cost reductions and margin improvement, supporting a multi-year earnings inflection.

In our view, the current share price does not reflect J&T's superior growth trajectory, margin expansion, and unique ability to scale profitably across multiple regions. The recent de-rating is cyclical, not structural, and has created an opportunity to buy a high-growth, high-ROE logistics leader at a discount. With a proven, exportable business model, a powerful new partnership with SF, and a clear runway for further market share gains, J&T is well positioned to deliver attractive returns as the market re-rates the stock in line with its fundamentals.

## Share price performance review

J&T Global Express's share price has traced a volatile path since its Hong Kong IPO in October 2023, reflecting both sector headwinds and the company's evolving fundamentals. The stock endured a sharp correction in 2024, falling $61\%$ (vs. HSCI $+16\%$ ), as fierce price wars in China's parcel sector and ongoing group-level losses weighed on sentiment, despite J&T's expansion across China, Southeast Asia, and New Markets. External shocks, such as the temporary shutdown of TikTok Shop in Indonesia, further amplified investor concerns around growth stability and platform risk.

Momentum shifted decisively in 2025, as J&T's operational and financial performance outpaced the broader parcel and e-commerce industry. The company pivoted to profitability, with non-IFRS profits surging $+157\%$ Y/Y in 1H25. In Southeast Asia, J&T reinforced its leadership (market share $32.8\%$ by 1H25), drove a $17\%$ Y/Y reduction in per-parcel costs, and delivered $67.8\%$ Y/Y parcel volume growth—more than double the industry's c.30% pace (according to J&T). New Markets also delivered, with annual volume up $43.6\%$ Y/Y, supported by rising e-commerce penetration and platform partnerships. In China, J&T focused on operational efficiency and margin improvement, aided by the government's anti-involution campaign, which helped stabilize the competitive environment.

The share price rebounded strongly in 2025, gaining $70\%$ (vs. HSCI $+31\%$ ), and maintained momentum into 2026, underpinned by record parcel volumes (30B in 2025, $+22\%$ Y/Y). More recently, the stock has corrected $9\%$ YTD (vs. HSI $-8\%$ ), reflecting sector-level concerns over fuel price volatility amid Middle East conflicts. Looking ahead, J&T is poised for further growth as it consolidates its network, invests in infrastructure and AI-driven automation, and leverages its China knowledge base to tailor solutions for a diverse client set. The company's rising brand recognition and expansion into non-platform business also present additional upside opportunities.

## Positive Drivers

Positive driver #1: J&T's systematic cost leadership, rooted in China's operational playbook and scaled globally, is a durable entry barrier and margin driver across all regions.

J&T's cost advantage is anchored in its ability to replicate and adapt best practices developed in China—one of the world's most competitive express delivery environments—across Southeast Asia and New Markets. The company's playbook combines automation, digital management, network optimization, and franchise empowerment, enabling it to drive down per-parcel costs even as industry-wide price competition persists. In FY25, J&T reduced per-parcel costs in China by 6%Y/Y (to US\$0.28), with transport and sorting costs dropping 14%Y/Y and 20%Y/Y, respectively. These measures—optimizing trunk transport, increasing high-capacity vehicles, and investing in full automation—have allowed J&T to maintain positive EBIT despite revenue pressure.

J&T has successfully exported this cost discipline to Southeast Asia, achieving a 15%Y/Y decrease in unit cost in FY25, driven by automation, self-owned fleet expansion, and digital management systems. The company’s scale enables it to maximize asset utilization and negotiate favorable supplier terms, reinforcing its cost advantage. In New Markets, J&T is applying the same systematic approach, investing in automated sorting, route optimization, and last-mile efficiency. This has resulted in positive adj.EBITDA and adj.EBIT for the first time in 2025, with COGS per parcel dropping 12% H/H and 8% Y/Y. Management targets an additional 5–10% decrease in delivery cost in the near term, confident that further room for cost reduction remains.

J&T's sustained commitment to automation and technology is a key pillar of its cost leadership. In China, the company has pioneered advanced automation and digital management systems, piloting over 900 autonomous vehicles for last-mile delivery and investing in AI-driven tools for sorting, customer service, and network management. These investments have delivered tangible improvements in efficiency and throughput, with per-parcel transport and sorting costs in China falling by US\$0.01 Y/Y in FY25. While per-parcel costs in China are still higher than some peers, management is focused on closing this gap through further automation and smart applications.

In Southeast Asia, J&T has rapidly increased automated sorting machines from 52 to 73 in one year (as of end-1Q26), providing a competitive edge as rising costs lead to market share declines for peers. Digital management systems and data analytics, originally developed for China, have been adapted to local infrastructure and labor markets, ensuring maximum impact. As a result, J&T's daily processing capacity in SEA now exceeds 43MM parcels, underscoring the scalability and resiliency of its automated network.

In New Markets, J&T is localizing mature Chinese automation and digital tools to support both new and existing customers. As of end-1Q26, the company had 11 automated sorting machines operational in Latin America and is piloting autonomous vehicles and other smart logistics solutions. These investments are already yielding results, with positive adj.EBITDA and adj.EBIT achieved in 2025, and management citing automation and technology as key drivers of this inflection point. Technology investment is expected to drive further cost reductions and support higher parcel volumes as these markets mature.

J&T's infrastructure investments and scalable network are fundamental to its operational efficiency and cost leadership. The company's proactive capacity planning, rapid deployment of automated sorting centers, and balanced mix of self-owned and partner-operated assets enable it to handle surging parcel volumes and deliver high service quality. In SEA, J&T operates 10,800 outlets and 125 sorting centers across seven countries, supported by 1,400 network partners. The delivery fleet is sizeable and flexible, allowing route optimization and cost management even during demand spikes. In China, J&T's network includes 6,500 outlets and 7,200 line-haul vehicles, supporting substantial daily volumes (60.5MM parcels in FY25, +12%Y/Y). As platforms like Temu, TikTok, and Mercado Libre expand and localize their operations, J&T's infrastructure backbone stands out as a key source of differentiation—enabling the company to capture incremental growth, defend market share, and sustain industry-leading service standards across diverse and rapidly evolving markets.

Positive driver #2: J&T's regional sponsor model is a scalable, capital-efficient engine for rapid market entry, local adaptation, and sustained network control across diverse geographies.

J&T's global expansion is powered by its differentiated regional sponsor model, which blends the entrepreneurial agility and local expertise of franchisees with the operational discipline, technology, and brand standards of a scaled logistics platform. This model, rooted in China's hyper-competitive express delivery sector, has proven highly effective in enabling J&T to scale quickly and flexibly across Southeast Asia and New Markets, while maintaining centralized control over critical infrastructure and service quality.

The regional sponsor model empowers experienced local entrepreneurs to operate and expand J&T's network within defined territories, investing their own capital and leveraging deep local relationships. This structure not only aligns incentives—regional sponsors often hold equity stakes in J&T—but also reduces the capital burden on headquarters, allowing for faster, asset-light expansion compared to traditional direct operation models. As a result, J&T can enter new markets rapidly, adapt to local regulatory and competitive dynamics, and scale coverage without heavy upfront investment.

A key advantage of this model is its ability to combine local decision-making and real-time market adaptation with the benefits of centralized technology, data analytics, and process standards. Regional sponsors bring critical knowledge of local culture, infrastructure, and regulations, minimizing risks and accelerating market penetration. J&T and its sponsors can jointly tailor the operating approach for each market—whether direct operation, network partners, or hybrid—enabling both early-stage entry and long-term optimization. As networks mature, J&T can further

integrate regional entities, as seen in Thailand and Indonesia, to drive operational synergies and efficiency.

The effectiveness of the regional sponsor model is evident in J&T's rapid rise in China, the world's most developed and competitive express delivery market. Entering in 2020, J&T achieved 50MM peak daily parcel volume within three years—faster than any peer—and by 1Q26 was handling c.60MM daily parcels, establishing itself as a top-five player. The model enabled swift integration of acquisitions like Best Express China and allowed J&T to compete on both cost and service quality, narrowing the gap with leading incumbents and building a sustainable platform for further growth.

By combining local entrepreneurship with centralized scale, J&T's regional sponsor model delivers the flexibility, speed, and control needed to win in both emerging and developed markets, making it a core catalyst for the company's ongoing global expansion and market share gains.

Positive driver #3: J&T's SEA leadership is underpinned by scale, platform-neutrality, and the ability to capture both category growth and industry consolidation, with TikTok as a key volume driver.

J&T Global has established itself as the leading 3PL service provider in Southeast Asia, operating across seven countries and maintaining the No. 1 market share for six consecutive years. The company's outperformance is anchored in its ability to aggregate volumes across platforms, leverage a dense network (10,800 outlets and 125 sorting centers as of 1Q26), and deliver high-quality service at scale. In 2025, J&T handled 7.7B parcels in SEA (+68%Y/Y), well ahead of the market's already robust 39% growth, lifting its market share to 34.4% (+5.8ppts Y/Y). 1Q26 volume growth remained strong at +80%Y/Y, highlighting the flywheel effect of network density and cost leadership.

SEA's e-commerce and express delivery markets continue to expand rapidly, with e-commerce retail transaction value reaching cUS\$304B in 2025 and expected to show a 16% CAGR through 2030 (according to J&T). E-commerce penetration rose to 22% in 2024 (+3.9ppts Y/Y) and is poised for further gains, while express parcel volume reached 22B in 2025 (+29%Y/Y) and is projected by J&T to hit 29B in 2026. The competitive landscape is consolidating, with the top five players now controlling 76% of the market (vs. 48% in 2022), which structurally favors scaled, tech-enabled operators like J&T.

A critical incremental driver is the rapid growth of social commerce, especially TikTok Shop, whose SEA GMV reportedly doubled to US\$45.6B in 2025. TikTok is now J&T's single largest customer in SEA, accounting for over 50% wallet share of TikTok's logistics spend. This partnership provides a powerful and relatively predictable demand stream, supporting network utilization and operating leverage. However, management notes that c.80% of SEA revenue comes from Chinese platforms (including TikTok and Temu), which, while supporting volume growth, also creates a concentration risk.

J&T is actively expanding its non-platform business—targeting social commerce sellers, SMEs, brands, and individual customers—which offers higher profit margins and helps optimize customer mix. While non-platform parcels currently account for less than 10% of SEA volume, this segment is a strategic lever for future margin expansion and risk mitigation. The company’s established cash-on-delivery infrastructure and ability to deliver industry-leading service (delivery times below two days, lost parcel rate down 13%Y/Y in 2025) further reinforce its competitive edge and reputation as the partner of choice for e-commerce and social commerce players across the region, in our view.

Positive driver #4: New Markets are emerging as a powerful engine for J&T's next phase of profitable growth, with platform partnerships and local execution driving rapid scale and margin inflection.

J&T's New Markets—Brazil, Mexico, Saudi Arabia, UAE, and Egypt—are rapidly evolving into a high-potential growth engine, supported by accelerating e-commerce adoption, favorable demographics, and the entry of major global and Chinese platforms. In 2025, e-commerce retail transaction value in these regions reached US\$162B (+22%Y/Y), with J&T projecting a 16% CAGR through 2030, while express parcel volumes hit c.5B (+9%Y/Y) and are poised to nearly triple to 13B by the decade's end (according to J&T). Despite global macro uncertainty, these economies remain resilient, and digitalization is unlocking vast new addressable markets for logistics leaders. Critically, e-commerce penetration remains relatively low (tracking to c.35% by 2029 according to J&T, providing a long runway for volume and revenue growth.

The competitive landscape in New Markets is still fragmented and underdeveloped, with local players lacking the scale, technology, and capital to compete nationally, and international giants like FedEx and DHL focused on premium segments. This creates a significant opportunity for a scaled, tech-driven operator like J&T to establish a leadership position by offering reliable, cost-effective, and comprehensive logistics solutions tailored to both global platforms and local merchants. Average delivery prices remain well above China or SEA, and service quality is inconsistent—further reinforcing the value of J&T’s proven playbook.

J&T's approach in New Markets has been methodical and replicable, leveraging its China/SEA experience to rapidly build infrastructure, localize operations, and secure anchor partnerships. Since entering these markets in 2022, J&T has established over 2,000 outlets and 44 sorting centers, and deployed 10 sets of automated sorting machines as of Dec-25. In 2025, J&T handled 404MM parcels in New Markets (+44%Y/Y), with market share rising to 7.5% (vs. 6.1% in FY24). The company is now a logistics partner of choice for leading Chinese e-commerce platforms (TikTok,

Temu, SHEIN) and regional giants like Mercado Libre, which has been instrumental in driving volume growth as platforms shift from cross-border to local-local models. Notably, TikTok's local-to-local parcel volume in Brazil scaled from a few thousand to over 100,000 per day in 3Q25, and J&T's 1Q26 New Markets volume doubled Y/Y (+c.101%), reflecting the impact of these partnerships and ongoing investment.

Financially, New Markets have reached a key inflection point. Revenue grew 51%Y/Y to US\$870MM in 2025, outpacing volume growth and reflecting the segment's ability to capture higher-value business. J&T achieved positive adjusted EBITDA in New Markets for the first time (US\$1.6MM in 1H25 vs. a loss of US\$7.8MM the prior year), and full-year adj. EBITDA reached US\$48MM (vs. a US\$43MM loss). Unit economics are trending positively, with revenue per parcel rising to US\$2.15 (+5%Y/Y) and cost per parcel decreasing to US\$1.79 (-8%Y/Y), even as the company continues to invest in network expansion and customer acquisition. The focus on automation, route optimization, and last-mile efficiency—core elements of J&T's China playbook—has enabled the company to offset cost pressures and drive sustainable profitability as scale ramps.

Positive driver #5: J&T's disciplined execution and strategic partnerships are enabling stable share gains and margin improvement in China.

China remains the global benchmark for e-commerce and express delivery, with total logistics spending reaching US\$2.7T in 2023 and projected to rise to US\$3.3T by 2028 (according to J&T). The market's depth is reflected in e-commerce GMV of US\$2.2T in 2025 (+7.4%Y/Y), online penetration of physical goods at c.26% (far above global peers), and social commerce alone reaching US\$754B in 2024. Parcel volumes remain robust, hitting 199B in FY25 (+c.14%Y/Y), with the State Post Bureau projecting 214B in 2026. Critically, the regulatory environment has shifted decisively since 2H25, with the anti-involution campaign driving rational pricing, improved ASPs, and a sector-wide focus on service quality and profitability over pure volume.

J&T has capitalized on this more rational landscape, leveraging targeted M&A (Best Express, Fengwang) and a deepening partnership with SF Express (now a c.10% shareholder) to secure platform access, optimize its network, and accelerate integration. As of 1Q26, J&T holds a c.12% market share (No. 5 in China), handling c.22B parcels in 2025 (+11%Y/Y) and c.5.4B in 1Q26 (+8%Y/Y). The company's focus has shifted from pure volume to quality growth, with an emphasis on higher-value, less price-sensitive segments, branded customers, and value-added services. This pivot has supported a sustained profitability turnaround: J&T's China business, loss-making at IPO, turned profitable in 1H24 and has delivered positive adj. EBIT since, reaching USD81MM in 2H25 as pricing stabilized and cost initiatives took hold.

Operational excellence and cost leadership remain central to J&T's China strategy. The company's franchise (Tongda) model enables rapid scale-up and flexible cost management, while ongoing investment in automation (338 automated sorting machines as of Mar-26), network consolidation, and technology (including unmanned vehicles and cloud warehousing) is driving further efficiency. J&T reduced its cost per parcel from USD0.30 in FY24 to USD0.28 in FY25 (-c.7%Y/Y), supporting profit resilience even as industry pricing remains competitive.

J&T's strategic customer relationships and platform access further reinforce its competitive edge. The company has secured positions with all major e-commerce platforms (Taobao, Douyin, Pinduoduo, Kuaishou) and is expanding into new segments such as reverse logistics, individual parcels, and value-added services. Investments in brand building, network partner training, and service quality are designed to enhance customer loyalty and improve mix.

Importantly, J&T's China business serves as a “training camp” for operational excellence, with best practices and technology exported to overseas markets. This exportable model has been instrumental in winning business from global e-commerce leaders as they expand internationally, positioning J&T as a credible and resilient player both at home and abroad.

## Positive driver #6: The strategic partnership with SF unlocks powerful synergies, accelerates J&T's global reach, and enhances its competitive edge in both China and international markets.

J&T's partnership with SF, cemented through mutual shareholdings (SF now owns 10% of J&T; J&T holds 4.29% of SF), is designed to go far beyond capital alignment and is structured to drive deep operational integration. The collaboration enables joint investment in critical logistics infrastructure—such as automated sorting centers and trunk line vehicles—directly enhancing J&T's efficiency and network coverage, especially in international markets. For J&T, this means leveraging SF's established first-mile, cross-border capacity, customs clearance, warehousing, and parcel locker infrastructure to further optimize its own last-mile and localized express delivery network in Southeast Asia and other high-growth regions. The combination of SF's cross-border and warehousing strengths with J&T's robust local delivery network is poised to deliver faster, more cost-efficient end-to-end solutions, a critical advantage as Chinese enterprises accelerate their global expansion.

The partnership's governance structure ensures ongoing strategic alignment, with a dedicated coordination committee and direct involvement from both founders. SF's board representation at J&T further cements the relationship, providing J&T with access to SF's strategic direction and operational best practices, while ensuring that joint initiatives can be rapidly developed and adapted to new opportunities and challenges in global logistics.

In China, the partnership is highly complementary: SF's premium parcel business and J&T's economic express services create opportunities for line-haul integration and resource sharing, allowing both companies to focus on quality and sustainable growth. Rather than competing head-to-head in low-margin, high-volume segments, J&T can leverage the collaboration to access higher-value service tiers and segments where pricing is rational and service standards are tightly controlled.

Looking ahead, the partnership opens up new pathways for J&T into developed markets such as Europe and the U.S., where SF's international infrastructure and forwarding capabilities provide a meaningful head start. J&T also aims to expand its product offerings into non-e-commerce and time-sensitive delivery, leveraging SF's extensive pick-up/drop-off point network. This aligns with the broader industry trend of diversifying beyond pure e-commerce parcels into higher-margin segments such as supply chain, cold chain, pharma, and intracity logistics, positioning J&T for long-term, sustainable growth on a global scale.

## Risk Factors

Risk driver #1: J&T's group profitability and growth remain structurally exposed to China's margin dynamics and competitive intensity

While J&T is the clear leader in Southeast Asia and an early mover in New Markets, these regions together still represent a modest portion of the global express delivery opportunity. In 2025, SEA and New Markets accounted for c.11% and c.2.5% of China’s express parcel volume, respectively, and contributed c.37% and 7% of group revenue, with China still making up c.55% of the total. J&T’s position in China is that of a disciplined follower, with c.11% market share and limited pricing power amid intense competition from larger incumbents. Despite recent profitability improvements, J&T’s gross profit per parcel and unit costs in China still lag leading peers, and the company’s ability to further improve margins is constrained by industry-wide price sensitivity, customer mix, and the risk of renewed price wars. The anti-involution policy has brought some rationality, but its long-term effectiveness is uncertain, and any weakening of regulatory enforcement or renewed price aggression could erode margins and slow J&T’s path to sustainable profitability.

## Risk driver #2: Margin pressure and yield erosion in Southeast Asia as the market matures and service differentiation narrows

J&T's scale leadership in SEA paradoxically increases its vulnerability to pricing attacks as the market matures. Service differentiation has largely evaporated, with all major players now offering similar delivery times (2–3 days), making price the primary lever for merchants and consumers. This commoditization of speed, combined with the risk of aggressive pricing by competitors with excess capacity, could strip J&T of pricing power and force margin concessions. The company's reliance on the regional sponsor model, while a source of flexibility, may also limit its ability to cut prices as deeply as fully integrated competitors in a deep price war, given the need to preserve sponsor profitability.

## Risk driver #3: Execution and profitability risks in New Markets due to early-stage scale, high investment, and operational complexity

New Markets (Brazil, Mexico, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Egypt) are a key growth engine but remain at an early stage of operational maturity, with fragmented logistics infrastructure, inconsistent service quality, and a competitive landscape dominated by local players and international giants focused on premium segments. J&T's aggressive investment in network buildout and automation has enabled strong volume growth (+44%Y/Y in FY25), but the segment's path to sustainable profitability is highly sensitive to scale, customer mix, and ongoing cost discipline. Cost per parcel in New Markets remains elevated (US\$1.79 in FY25 vs. US\$0.48 in SEA and US\$0.28 in China), and further cost reduction will require successful execution of automation, route optimization, and a gradual shift toward higher-margin customers. Regulatory, macroeconomic, and geopolitical risks—including licensing changes, tariff increases, and economic volatility—add further uncertainty to the outlook.

## Risk driver #4: High customer concentration and platform insourcing risk threaten volume stability and pricing power

J&T's revenue is highly concentrated among major Chinese and regional e-commerce platforms, with the top five customers accounting for 31% of group revenue and c.80% of SEA revenue derived from Chinese platforms (TikTok, Temu, SHEIN). Platform insourcing is a growing threat: Shopee, once a major customer, has shifted 80–85% of its volume in-house in SEA and Brazil, and continues to invest in its own logistics capabilities. This trend increases the risk that J&T could lose volume or pricing power as platforms internalize more logistics or shift volume to their own networks. The TikTok Shop partnership, while a major growth driver, also creates a “single-point-of-failure” risk if regulatory bans or platform strategy changes occur. In New Markets, customer concentration around Chinese platforms and Mercado Libre also exposes J&T to abrupt volume shifts if platform strategies or trade policies change. The pace and effectiveness of J&T’s diversification into non-platform business will be critical to reducing this risk.

## Risk driver #5: Technology and cost-reduction playbook faces local adaptation challenges and diminishing returns outside China

J&T's China playbook—large-scale automation, high-capacity fleets, and digitalized management—has driven significant cost and efficiency gains, but its effectiveness in SEA and New Markets is constrained by local factors such as fragmented infrastructure, lack of standardized addressing, and high cash-on-delivery ratios. In SEA, only c.30% of the network is currently run by network partners, and the transition to a franchise model is a long-term goal. In New Markets, the franchise model is still being piloted and adapted, with success dependent on careful partner selection and local market realities. Local competitors and platform-owned logistics arms are also investing in automation and digitalization, raising the bar for efficiency and service quality. Execution missteps or slower-than-expected adaptation could erode J&T's competitive advantage and delay the realization of targeted cost savings.

## Risk driver #6: Geopolitical, regulatory, and trade policy risks could disrupt operations and cross-border parcel flows

J&T operates in 13 countries, each with its own regulatory framework governing express delivery, foreign investment, data privacy, labor, and e-commerce. The company's use of contractual arrangements (VIE structures) in China and Indonesia exposes it to regulatory changes that could force restructuring or loss of control. In New Markets, ongoing regulatory changes, local protectionism, and tariff increases (e.g., in Brazil and Mexico) could impact market access, cost structure, and volume growth. The company’s reliance on the “de minimis” loophole for cross-border parcels is a direct headwind, as governments in Brazil, Mexico, the US, and the EU move to close this gap and impose punitive tariffs on Chinese e-commerce goods. Any tightening of trade policy or regulatory enforcement could materially impact J&T’s package volumes and profitability in affected regions.

## Company Background

## Company Background and Evolution

Founded in Indonesia in 2015 by Mr. Jet Li (a former executive at OPPO Electronics), J&T Global Express has rapidly evolved from a regional disruptor into a global logistics enterprise. The company was purpose-built to serve the booming e-commerce sector in Southeast Asia, differentiating itself early on with a “365-day service” ethos and a network designed to handle the high-frequency, fragmented volume of online retail.

J&T's growth has been driven by the sharp rise of e-commerce in emerging markets, its early-mover advantage in underserved regions, and a continued focus on operational efficiency and technology adoption. Since its inception, J&T has evolved from a single-country operator in Indonesia to a global logistics platform spanning 13 countries. The company entered Vietnam and Malaysia in 2018, the Philippines, Thailand, and Cambodia in 2019, and Singapore and China in 2020. In 2022, J&T made its first foray outside Asia, launching operations in key Latin American and Middle Eastern markets. As of 2025, J&T is the number one 3PL delivery operator by parcel volume in Southeast Asia, a top-five player in China, and a fast-growing challenger in its New Markets.

## The "Regional Sponsor" Business Model

A key differentiator for J&T is its innovative “Regional Sponsor” business model, which blends the entrepreneurial drive and local expertise of franchisees with the operational discipline and technology of a scaled logistics platform. Under this

model, J&T partners with regional sponsors—local entrepreneurs who invest in and operate critical parts of the network, including sorting centers, line-haul transportation, and, in some cases, pickup and delivery outlets.

The regional sponsor model enables J&T to rapidly scale its network, adapt to local market conditions, and align incentives through equity participation and performance-based rewards. Country headquarters set overall strategy and provide technology, brand, and operational support, while regional sponsors execute on-the-ground operations and manage network partners. This structure allows J&T to maintain strong operational control, drive cost efficiencies, and achieve deep market penetration with lower capital intensity compared to a fully direct-operated model.

The model has been successfully replicated across Southeast Asia and China, and is being adapted for New Markets, with a mix of direct operation and franchise/partner models depending on local regulatory and market conditions.

## Business segments

J&T's business is organized into three primary segments:

Southeast Asia: This is J&T's largest and most established region, covering Indonesia, Vietnam, Malaysia, the Philippines, Thailand, Cambodia, and Singapore. In full-year 2025, J&T handled approximately 7.66 billion parcels in Southeast Asia (+67.8%Y/Y). The company is the market leader by parcel volume in the region, benefiting from high e-commerce penetration, strong volume growth, and a dense, integrated network.

China: J&T entered China in 2020 and has rapidly become a top-five player. In full-year 2025, the company handled 22.07 billion parcels in China, up 11.4%Y/Y. The China segment is highly competitive, with J&T focusing on operational efficiency, network integration, and expanding its presence in both urban and rural markets.

New Markets: Comprising Brazil, Mexico, Saudi Arabia, UAE, and Egypt, this segment represents J&T's next frontier of growth. In full-year 2025, J&T handled 403.9 million parcels in New Markets, $43.6\%$ Y/Y growth. The company is leveraging its experience from China and Southeast Asia to build out infrastructure and partnerships in these high-potential but early-stage markets.

Business Mix: J&T is primarily an express delivery operator, with the vast majority of its revenue and parcel volume coming from domestic express delivery services across its core markets. Cross-border logistics — including small parcels, freight forwarding, and warehousing solutions — remains a minor portion of overall revenue, serving as a complementary offering to support e-commerce partners' international expansion. By the end of 2025, management also indicated that it is raising brand recognition through non-platform channels and retail/reversal packages, which now account for less than 10% of total volume.

# Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks

J&T Express - H (Overweight; Price Target: HK\$13.00)

## Investment Thesis

J&T Global Express is a leading platform-neutral, technology-driven 3PL operator—the market leader in Southeast Asia, a growing player in China, and an emerging challenger in high-growth New Markets (Brazil, Mexico, Middle East). The company's regional sponsor model combines entrepreneurial local execution with centralized technology, enabling rapid, capital-light expansion, while China-built automation and digital management systems are exported across regions, underpinning a structurally advantaged cost curve. Since its October 2023 IPO, J&T's share price has underperformed, reflecting sector price wars in China, initial loss-making status, and external shocks such as recent fuel price volatility. The market has also questioned the scalability of J&T's model outside China and the sustainability of margins in a maturing Southeast Asia, while New Markets were seen as too early-stage to move the needle. However, the business has now pivoted to profitability, is delivering industry-leading volume and margin growth, and is unlocking new synergies through its partnership with SF Holdings. Overweight on J&T.

## Valuation

Our Jun-27 P/E-based PT of HK\$13.0 is based on J&T's FY27 Non-IFRS EPS of US\$0.12, 13x target P/E (vs industry average of 11x, Bloomberg estimates, as of Jul 7, 2026) and USD/HKD conversion rate of 7.8.

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

Downside risks to our rating and price target include: (1) continued structural margin compression in China, if anti-involution weakens; (2) slowdown of SEA ecommerce GMV growth and increasing competition from competitors like SPX etc; (3) execution and profitability slippage in New Markets as heavy upfront investment and slower-than-expected localization of franchise models prolong the path to scale; and (4) geopolitical, regulatory, and trade-policy shocks could increase compliance costs and directly pressure cross-border parcel flows and demand.
