## More support for CNH amid outbound-curb concerns

Policy announcements across cross-border securities, futures, fund activity, and outbound investment in May–June have revived questions about whether China is materially tightening outbound capital flows. We view these moves less as a crackdown on outflows and more as part of a broader governance framework to manage cross-border flows across capital, technology, data, and talent. Key watchpoints are the follow-on implementation rules that will define the practical boundaries, and whether Beijing expands compliant channels, via more approved routes, higher quotas, or both, to keep the framework aligned with RMB internationalization objectives.

## Near-term catalysts affecting CNY exchange rates

A common investor question is how much further CNY can appreciate after a $3\%$ gain vs. USD in 1H (vs. $+4.4\%$ in 2025) and a $4.9\%$ gain on a CFETS-basket basis (vs. $-2\%$ in 2025). While the practical constraint is more the PBOC's tolerance for the pace of appreciation, we flag several catalysts that could shape CNY moves into 2H.

First, the trade surplus remains the structural BoP anchor amid external trade policy uncertainties. The near-term swing will hinge on the interaction between export performance, the energy import bill (after the recent oil-import contraction), and trade-policy headlines. Risks include renewed tariff uncertainty (US Section 301/232), and a potential intensification of EU-related frictions, including discussion of a “currency accord”. These could shift expectations for the trade surplus and policy reaction function before they show up in the hard data.

Second, flows matter as export strength only supports CNY if dollars are converted. Exporter hedging decisions and seasonal dividend payouts/repatriation needs can create discrete FX conversion windows. The key question is whether conversion stays concentrated in incremental trade income or broadens into more outright reduction of accumulated USD holdings. These behaviours can also be level-dependent on where USD/CNY trades.

Third, the USD backdrop will cap CNY strength. A firmer USD and a more hawkish Fed tilt could slow the pace of CNY appreciation, particularly given policy divergence with the PBOC, even if US–China rate differentials now play a less reliable role in explaining CNY moves than they do for other currencies.

Fourth, July Politburo expectations are a sentiment catalyst. A clearer easing bias could support risk assets and the CNY via improved confidence and inflow dynamics. But we see a high bar for incremental stimulus, with the near-term emphasis more likely on faster deployment of March NPC approved fiscal resources. The setup is asymmetric: disappointment can weigh on sentiment, while upside would likely require a visible step-up that changes the domestic-demand narrative rather than reiterating current guidance.

Fifth, regulation uncertainty vs. RMB internationalization support affects CNH sentiment and access. Markets are watching whether recent regulatory/enforcement actions signal tighter outbound constraints, which would matter directly for CNH via channel access and sentiment. Recent constructive signals reinforce our view that the moves are more about channel clean-up alongside expanded “plumbing” for RMB internationalization, supportive for offshore market functioning and foreign participation, even if it does not mechanically map into a specific spot level.
