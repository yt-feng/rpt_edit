# European Defense: Into Q2'26 - Long Leonardo, Thales, Rheinmetall
## Specialist Sales

European Defense stocks are decoupling after a period of broad performance. Ahead of Q2'26, we favor self-help stories (Leonardo, Thales) and superior growth (Rheinmetall).

Leonardo (30 July). We expect another beat-and-raise. We are +5% above consensus on EBITA and expect FCF to improve too. Growth should be broad-based. We would not be surprised to see the 2026 guidance upgraded across all metrics (except FCF). In a more challenging environment for European defense stocks, Leonardo stands out as a self-help story with room for earnings upgrades and re-rating, if execution continues. The stock trades on 11x EBITDA (vs. 14x for the sector). The correction that followed the surprise CEO change is an opportunity, in our view, as the strategy should be unchanged.

Thales (23 July). We expect a solid Q2'26 print, driven by momentum in the core A&D businesses. We are +6% above consensus on A&D, but -6% below on Cyber & Digital. We expect our tactical upgrade to materialize in Q2'26, as we believe Cyber will return to growth and is no longer material enough to drive downgrades (8% of sales, easy comps and lower expectations).

Rheinmetall (6 August). Q2'26 is the key ST catalyst for Rheinmetall. We expect the company to meet its implied +60% topline target. If it does, we see scope for the stock to regain some losses. The next catalyst will be the Arminius Boxer order. We expect the first tranche to come in full by the end of 2026. This should help rebuild credibility. We are less confident about the following ones (end of the decade). And we see risk to LT consensus estimates, even if they have already come down. We are -18% below consensus on 2030 EBIT. Yet, at these levels, we believe the risk-reward is attractive for a company that has an unmatched growth profile.

Dassault Aviation (22 July). We do not expect particularly strong H1'26 results, due to the soft Falcon deliveries. Last year's results pushed the stock down in a similar scenario, but we believe expectations have been managed more effectively. The key catalyst remains the mega Indian Rafale order, which we expect to be finalized by the end of 2026. Longer term, Dassault benefits from unmatched visibility thanks to its Rafale backlog, but we see limited near-term catalysts post-India order.

BAE Systems (30 July). We expect a relatively soft set of H1'26 results, with growth and margins in line with the annual guidance. The key focus for investors is likely to be the 2027 US defense budget, where BAE is well positioned (45% of sales). It will come later in the year. While we see BAE as a high-quality compounder, we do not expect earnings upgrades before the US budget is approved.

TKMS (12 August). We saw a tactical opportunity, driven by major order wins (here). The thesis has played out, and we do not expect large campaigns to be finalized before the end of the year, at the earliest (India, F127). Hence TKMS should revert to a fundamental “show-me” story, which we view as less attractive. We will update our expectations ahead of the quarter after speaking with the company.

We summarize our views into Q2'26, after speaking to most companies, in order of tactical preference below:

## LEONARDO (30 JULY)

Another beat-and-raise. We expect Q2'26 results to be strong, and stand +5% consensus on EBITA. We also expect FCF generation to be stronger as Leonardo reduces the phasing impact (strong Q4, softer quarters before). We expect the strength to be driven across all businesses. The acquisition of Iveco implies upside to forecasts on DES, in our view. We expect the 2026 guidance to be upgraded across all metrics (except FCF).

Combination of earnings upgrades and re-rating. As we perceive the current environment as challenging for the sector, Leonardo stands out for being more of a self-help story. We believe the stock can keep getting estimates upgrades and should re-rate if it keeps delivering improving fundamentals. Leonardo is also screening well to resist the “new warfare” narrative, as its portfolio is skewed to electronics, missiles and air defense.

CEO change. In early April, media sources reported that the Italian government intended to replace Leonardo's CEO Roberto Cingolani as part of the regular renewal of boards at state-controlled companies. Leonardo was among the three companies mentioned (Leonardo, Eni, Enel). The news came as a surprise and the timing was poor, given Cingolani has been running the turn-around story since 2023. And had just hosted a positive Industrial Plan in March, where he dominated all the presentations. Despite the surprise effect and the lack of communication at the time, we do see the rationale for such a change. Cingolani has been a very successful driver of the performance for the company. But, he is a scientist and an Italian politician by background. The new CEO, Lorenzo Mariani, has been at Leonardo and MBDA since 2002. He brings experience in the key structural segments, namely MBDA and Electronics, and should be well positioned to run the business in the second leg of the story, as Leonardo is shifting from a turn-around story to a growth story within European defense.

## THALES (23 JULY)

Tactical call materializing in H1'26. We upgraded Thales earlier in the year as we saw the set-up as improving. Thales has been a funding short for many hedgefunds in 2025 because it was one of the few European Defense stocks with lower exposure to military activities (just over 50% of sales) and a clear problem: the Cyber business. We believe the drivers behind this negative thesis are weakening. We expect Q2'26 to deliver strong growth in the core A&D business (we are +5% above consensus on each division) and despite our negative stance on Cyber, we think the business does not matter anymore (8% of sales) and will return to growth, thanks to easy comps (detailed notes from the group pre-close below).

Acquisition of Exail. Thales' acquisition of Exail is strategically compelling but unlikely to materially impact the group in the near term given Exail's relatively small size (approximately €479 million in 2025 sales, or around 2% of Thales' revenue). The transaction strengthens Thales' naval franchise by adding leading positions in maritime robotics, navigation systems, mine countermeasures, and naval drones, while complementing its existing defense portfolio and stake in Naval Group. We view the deal positively from a strategic standpoint, as it reinforces Thales' core defense activities at a time when investor attention has been focused on the weaker performance of its Cyber business and the integration of Imperva. However, the acquisition comes at a rich valuation of roughly 28x forward EV/EBIT, well above European defense peers, reflecting Exail's superior growth and margin profile. While management expects meaningful synergies over time and Exail offers attractive growth prospects, we see limited near-term financial upside until those synergies begin to materialize. It supports the view that Thales is focusing on the core A&D business, which we view as a strong positive.

F126 cancellation impact. Following Germany's decision to terminate the F126 frigate program, Thales announced it expects to record an exceptional, largely non-cash charge of approximately €450m in H1 2026. It will reduce net income by around €350. The company stressed that the charge will have no impact on adjusted EBIT, adjusted net income, or free operating cash flow. After the exceptional charge. The impact should be limited (0.5% of 2026 sales and less than 1% of annual sales thereafter). Thales was surprised by the decision from the German DoD and is seeking compensation.

## Notes from the Q2'26 pre-close call

\- Orders. Upgraded book-to-bill guidance for 2026, but order intake is lumpy by nature and the guidance upgrade might not that strong in Q2'26. Difficult comps due to Rafale order (26x) in Q2'25.

\- FX impact -116m in Q1'26. Expect currency impact to be broadly neutral in Q2'26. 2026 guidance is based on €/\$ of 1.18.

\- Scope. Slightly positive due to minor operations.

\- Aerospace. Drivers in avionics are intact. OE is enjoying strong visibility from Airbus and ramp up will continue. In Aftermarket, more cyclical due to the Middle East traffic. April and May cycles were down. Thales say they will see some impact on aftermarket in Q2'26. Space, one-off element impacting: SES renounces to 2 geostationary satellites ordered in 2022. €150m impact in Q2'26. In Space, order momentum remains solid and Thales can reuse these satellites. Qatar order to build GEO program.

• Defense. Solid momentum continues across all domains. Particular strength in ammunition, air defense and naval.

\- Cyber & Digital. Cyber is anticipated to gradually improve over 2026. Q2'26 should see the business return to growth. In Digital, nothing new from Q1'26. Tough comps in Secure Connectivity.

\- Adjusted EBIT. In Aerospace, margins should consolidate in 2026 after solid levels in 2025. The strength in aftermarket from 2025 implies more difficult mix in 2026 as air traffic worsened in Q2'26. Negative element from SES might impact the margins in Q2'26. In Cyber & Digital, margins are expected at 13% this year. In line with 2025 excluding one-offs.

\- EBIT Phasing. H2'26 will be better than H1'26. H1'25 had a one-off benefit. Cyber improvement should drive better mix in H2'26. FX impact was a -50bps impact in Q1'26.

\- Other items: Naval contribution in line with 2025 (\~€90m). French surtax reiterated, between €90m and €100m. Restructuring costs for 2026 should be €120m. All incorporated in the 2026 guidance. Cost of debt €150m, flat vs. 2025. Cash rates decreased in 2025.

• FCF conversion is now upgraded to 100-110%.

## RHEINMETALL (6 AUGUST)

H1'26 results on August 6 $^{th}$ . Our base case is that Rheinmetall meets the implied guidance of +60% topline growth in Q2'26. After speaking to the company, modeling the easy comps and the reverse one-offs from Q1'26, we believe it is achievable. We also checked the company's hit-rate on “informal” quarter guides from the past, which is close to 100%. We view Q2'26 as the most important near-term catalyst for Rheinmetall shares. A successful Q2 print should help break the earnings downgrade cycle and support re-rating.

Arminius Boxer order. The large Boxer order should come later in the year. Rheinmetall's hopeful guidance of the order coming in 2026 has been consistently delayed. Other suppliers have mentioned Q3 and Q4 as a more reasonable expectation, once the German government returns from the summer break. We believe this order will come in full (\~1800 vehicles, between €12bn and €25bn), as Germany needs to send a supportive message and encourage capacity increases. We see the next tranches are riskier. The recording of the Arminius order should be the most important catalyst for the stock in 2026.

Sentiment finally improving. The stock was hammered by the surprise F126 frigate order cancellation. We interpret the cancellation as a rationale decision from the German government for a problematic order (Damen has been late for years) rather than a message sent to Rheinmetall. We also got confirmation from TKMS and Thales that the shift was unexpected and unannounced. We would not extrapolate this to other orders, but flag that Rheinmetall's framework agreement backlog (including non-contracted orders) puts the company at risk on a credibility aspect. The stock collapsed, because of the credibility hit (stock down -19% while the order has a LSD% impact on 2030 earnings). We expect it will take time to recover credibility. But we have finally seen investors more interested in the stock as it remains one of the fastest growing and most profitable companies in the defense, with a clear growth cycle, a great positioning, and a very cheap valuation. We are still substantially below consensus on 2030 earnings (-18% below) but believe that the risk-reward is attractive, at these levels.

## BAE SYSTEMS (30 JULY)

All about the US budget. BAE Systems derives 45% of sales from the US, and is in a particularly strong position to benefit from the growth in the 2027 US budget. The budget proposal of \$1.45tn, combining discretionary appropriations and reconciliation funding, is a record increase. We do not expect such a budget to make it through Congress. But, even if only the "base" 2027 budget was approved, it would imply strong growth (\$1.15tn vs. \$0.9tn in 2026, +22%). The investment account (addressable market) should grow by a significantly higher rate. We see this as the key catalyst for BAE Systems, after what we expect to be a soft set of H1'26 results (details division by division below).

Space, missiles, air defense. We continue to see exposure to space, missiles, and missile defense as particularly attractive. BAE Systems might not be the best exposed to capture these opportunities, but will participate. It is also exposed to some less “hot” areas, which are seeing solid funding (ship repair, land vehicles). In such a supportive environment, we believe that all players will benefit from large order intake and therefore improved growth for the coming years.

Slow but steady. BAE System's diversification (segments, countries) implies that growth should be slower than some of the pure-plays European companies. We are slightly above consensus for the coming years (+3% above on average until 2028) and see the stock as well positioned to benefit from its resilient and higher-quality profile. Especially in a difficult European Defense environment. We believe order intake in the Middle East and the US will support performance, but start flowing later in the year. We believe BAE Systems will remain a compounder in the long-term. But we maintain our Market-Perform as we struggle to see a clear catalyst for earnings upgrades outside the US budget.

## Air (30% of group sales, 33% of EBIT)

Air is guided to deliver 9-11% sales growth and 12% operating margins in 2026, with management indicating performance is likely to trend towards the upper end of both guides. The division continues to benefit from strong demand across combat air programs, with no meaningful change in underlying trends expected. While MBDA delivered exceptionally strong growth in 2025, its growth rate is expected to moderate into the single digits in 2026 due to delivery timing and a less favourable mix. Capacity expansion remains supportive for the medium-term outlook, although the benefits are likely to materialise only gradually.

## Electronic Systems (25% of group sales, 35% of EBIT)

Electronic Systems is guided to deliver 6-8% sales growth and 15% operating margins in 2026. Growth is expected to track towards the lower end of the range in the first half before improving through the year as previously won contracts convert into revenue. Margin progression should follow a similar pattern. Within the portfolio, SMS (the Ball Aerospace acquisition) is expected to see improving performance, supported by a somewhat better US civil space funding environment and healthy

demand from Space Defense customers, helping growth move closer to management's medium-term target of around 10%.

## Platforms & Services (16% of group sales, 17% of EBIT)

Platforms & Services is guided to deliver 9-11% sales growth and 11-12% operating margins in 2026. Demand continues to be supported by a strong outlook for combat vehicle programs in Europe and, more recently, increased production rates in the US. While the recent US production tailwind is expected to moderate over time, European demand remains robust, providing a strong underpinning for growth. As a result, we expect the division to outperform guidance, with revenues growing ahead of the company's targeted range.

## Maritime (22% of group sales, 14% of EBIT)

Maritime is guided to deliver 5-7% sales growth and 7-8% operating margins in 2026. Profitability is expected to remain under pressure from the first-of-class build phase on the Type 26 frigate program, with margins likely to trend below or at the lower end of guidance and potentially remain softer in the first half of the year. As the program moves into more advanced testing and validation phases, execution risk should gradually reduce and profitability improve, although the recovery is likely to be measured and spread over the next two to three years.

## Cyber & Intelligence (8% of group sales, 7% of EBIT)

Cyber & Intelligence is guided to deliver 5-7% sales growth and 8-9% operating margins in 2026. The business is expected to recover progressively from the impact of DOGE-related spending cuts experienced in 2025, supported by an improving US consultancy and services environment. In the UK, funding delays and political uncertainty continue to weigh on program activity, suggesting a softer start to the year. However, trends should strengthen as the year progresses, allowing the division to move closer to its full-year guidance range.

## DASSAULT AVIATION (22 JULY)

No fireworks. Last year Dassault Aviation published disappointing results, missing on profits (partly due to a messy consensus). The miss was driven by disappointing Falcon deliveries, an unfavorable margin mix and the French surtax impact. The group was also impacted by the tariff environment in order to intake for Falcon (large customer base in the US but costs in Europe). We do not expect a particularly strong set of results in H1'26 and would not disagree with a cautious stance into the event. However, we expect the company to have managed expectations ahead of the results (details by division below).

The key catalyst is the Rafale order confirmation. We believe the next important milestone is the confirmation of the Rafale order from India, and more details about the financials and the timeline. We expect the contract to be signed by the end of 2026, which is also in line with some of the suppliers' indications.

Long-term resilience and growth. Dassault Aviation's rich backlog (220 Rafale, going to over 300 including the Indian order) should provide unmatched visibility until the end of the decade. We see Dassault Aviation as a clear growth story, and expect the group to announce more capacity increases (going to cadence 6) later. Dassault Aviation is also relatively cheap, once the Thales stake and the cash positions are excluded from the valuation. But similarly to BAE Systems, we do not see what would be a clear catalyst in the near future, as order intake should have limited impact on the stock given the size of the existing backlog. The selective financial communication and the limited size of the float also reduce possibilities for large investors. The recent FCAS developments are a long-term positive, in our view, but could have some impact on funding. We wonder if that could imply Airbus selling their stake in Dassault Aviation, which we would view as a long-term positive too (larger float).

## Rafale (63% of sales) - 28 deliveries guidance

Dassault is guiding for 28 Rafale deliveries in 2026, and we expect deliveries to be weighted toward the second half of the year, consistent with prior years. We forecast 10 Rafale deliveries in H1 (36% of full-year guidance), with deliveries remaining heavily export-focused at 8 export aircraft and 2 aircraft for France. Beyond deliveries, investor attention is likely to remain on the potential 114-aircraft Indian Rafale order, where negotiations appear to be progressing and could become an important catalyst in the coming months.

## Falcon (37% of sales) - 40 deliveries guidance

Dassault is guiding for 40 Falcon deliveries in 2026, with deliveries again expected to be heavily back-end loaded. Based on available delivery data, we forecast 15 Falcon deliveries in H1 (38% of full-year guidance). While the pace of deliveries early in the year appears somewhat slow, we expect underlying performance to improve versus 2025, when tariff-related disruptions negatively affected deliveries and orders. We also expect order intake to strengthen meaningfully versus H1 2025, supported by a more favorable demand environment and the stronger momentum seen in H2 2025.

Dassault has guided to €8.5bn of revenue in 2026, while we forecast €9.4bn. (Consensus can be low-quality for Dassault Aviation)
