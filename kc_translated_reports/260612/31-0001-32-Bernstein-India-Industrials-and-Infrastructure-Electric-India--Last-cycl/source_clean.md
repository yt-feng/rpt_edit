# India Industrials and Infrastructure
# Electric India: Last cycle vs. This cycle - Not cheap but shortages not going away next 3 years...

We see the Indian power sector a structural and not a cyclical story, but the sector has traded more like air-conditioner stocks - buy before summer and sell before onset of monsoons. The sector peaked in mid-CY24, post which demand slowed growing at \~1% YoY for the next 20 months. This year it got a fresh boost with a) Weather, b) Energy Security, c) Data-Center. In this note we take a look at demand, supply, spot market and valuation indicators to see where we are in the cycle. Key charts -Exhibit 1, Exhibit 4, Exhibit 5, Exhibit 9, Exhibit 13 Exhibit 14.

1) Spot market indicators- when we de-average for non-solar hours - the shortage becomes apparent: If we look at an aggregate basis, spot power prices are at Rs 3.8/kwh in Apr-May, significantly below 2023 highs. Buy-sell bid ratio is also below 1. But when we de-average by time of day, the evening shortage seems to be only worsening with time. The spread between morning and evening price is now Rs 7/kwh (and rising), as against being nearly 0 in the last decade. A perfect arbitrage for battery storage. Buy-Sell ratio in evening continues to inch up as well - e.g. for summer months it was 2.2x this year, vs 0.5x in 2020.

2) Demand- finally some respite, retain our 1x gdp multiplier view long term: We faced many questions on our 1x real gdp power demand forecast in the last couple of years, as demand grew just \~1% in FY26. Now on a low base and supportive weather (rainfall forecasts below norma), we see our 6% demand growth assumption for this year also look conservative now. Usually investors have sold power stocks post summer, but this time we expect demand growth to continue to pint better in the $2^{nd}$ half. Electrification - energy security, are global themes and we see this a structural story.

3) Supply- high chances of evening shortage continuing next 3 years: Last cycle saw a supply glut, in fact \~100 GW of thermal were under-construction in 2012 (equivalent to \~75% of the installed thermal base), this time only \~40 GW is under-construction at present- which is just \~16% of the installed thermal capacity. Further, in the next 3 years, we are likely to add just \~15 GW of thermal capacity, against this the evening power demand is expected to grow by \~50 GW cumulative even at a 6% growth assumption. Hence, unless we add \~15 GW/ year of batteries in these 3 years we don't see how evening power shortage can be resolved. A lot of BESS tenders have happened last year, but most of those were aggressive bids by small players - and both BESS prices and Rs/\$ has become unfavorable since then - so we expect many of those to not see the light of day.

4) Valuations- no longer cheap but can add on correction: State owned utilities continue to trade cheaper than global peers on P/E (NTPC at 13x, Power Grid 15x vs. Global 18x), EV/EBITDA (NTPC at 9x, Power Grid 9x vs. Global 11x). Private Indian utilities on the other hand are at significant premium, but we would add thermal players on dips.

We retain our structural positive stance on the sector-electrification is a global mega-trend and more important for India than any other country (devoid of oil-gas resources). In the medium term as well - there are hardly any thermal plants coming next 3 years, so we see power shortages continue in non-solar hours (despite push for BESS). Our preference is still non-solar power (thermal-nuclear-battery), over solar.

## INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS

We continue to like state-owned companies to invest in India's power sector- NTPC being our preferred name. Followed by ReNew, where there is a take-private offer out, which we expect to be raised. Other than them, we are positive on power lenders (preferring PFC over REC), but they are riskier names, and more a trade in our view (not core holding). On private thermal side, Adani Power was our overall top pick in our coverage, but it has run up too fast - hence we prefer JSW Energy over it. With recent fund raise, their debt issues are resolved and the KSK expansion optionality more real.

## 2) DEMAND - LAST CYCLE VS. THIS CYCLE

## A) DEMAND - FINALLY SOME TRACTION AFTER A BAD 1.5 YEARS...LONG TERM VIEW INTACT

For 2 decades, Indian power sector grew at sub GDP growth rates (0.8x multiplier) as the economy continued to be driven by services rather than manufacturing, the whole country did not have access to power and penetration of electrical appliances remained low. Post COVID-19, we saw a revival of power demand. Then FY25 onward India faced soft demand due to strong monsoons and high base. Now we expect power demand to normalize to 1x of real gdp this year - supported by weather and a low base.

B) DISCOMS - GRADUALLY GETTING BETTER

The most important metric for DISCOMs is ACS-ARR gap (avg. cost of supply and avg. revenue realized), which reduced in FY25 to Rs 0.11/kwh from Rs 0.56/kwh in FY23 - driven by higher ARR and lower power purchase cost (Exhibit 11). Reported AT&C loss also improved to 15.4% in FY25 from 16.3% in FY24. In its annual DISCOM ranking report for FY25, PFC highlighted that at an aggregate level DISCOMs in India became profitable - with Rs. 27 Bn, as against Rs 270 Bn loss in FY24. While still a long way to go, we continue to expect broader DISCOM health to gradually keep getting better, and see central govt. pushing them for tariff hikes to lower ACS-ARR gap.

## 3) SUPPLY- NO SIGNS OF OVER SUPPLY YET

Apart from slower demand growth, the big challenge of the last cycle was over-supply (specifically in thermal and hydro capacities). However, if we look at the thermal supply coming online, there's hardly any capacity addition estimated in the next 3 years with most of the capacity getting added from FY30 onwards.

On the other hand, we expect BESS capacity additions to pick up (Adani Green announced reaching 10GWh BESS by FY27end, 3GWh already added so far). We expect \~13GW/yr BESS capacity to get added between FY27 to FY32 helping curb the evening power shortages.

We can expect to see some shortages similar to Apr'26 (\~5GW) continue for the next couple of fiscals, post which there could be a surplus (assuming such BESS capacity comes online), refer Exhibit 14.

## MODEL CHANGES

For all the model updates, we have incorporated FY26 actuals. For all companies that we value using DCF, we roll forward the cash flows to FY28 from FY27 earlier. Beyond that:

- NTPC Green - We increase the TP to Rs. 85 (from Rs. 80 earlier, WACC changed from 8.4% to 8.5%) using DCF methodology as we add 5.5 GW renewable capacity per year till 2040 (from 5GW earlier). In the near term, this increases our EPS for FY27 from 1.03 to 1.11.
- NTPC - Beyond the changes highlighted above for NTPC Green, we push out the 444MW hydro plant as per management commentary from FY27 to FY28. We also incorporate the recently acquired Sinnar thermal plant into NTPC's thermal JV portfolio. With WACC unchanged, we increase our target price to Rs. 450/sh from Rs. 430 earlier using DCF methodology. Our FY27 EPS increases from Rs. 27.02/sh to Rs. 27.06/sh.
- Adani Power - With WACC unchanged, we increase our TP from Rs. 177/sh to Rs. 220/sh as we increase capacity addition beyond the 24GW pipeline to 1.5GW per year from FY36-FY50 from 1GW per year till FY40 earlier. EPS for FY27 increases from Rs. 6.9/sh to Rs. 7.7/sh.
- JSW Energy - We incorporate the recent Rs. 40Bn QIP and \~Rs. 32Bn cash infusion from stake sale in JSW Steel (not covered) and model it to be used for debt repayment. With WACC unchanged, this increases our TP to Rs. 623/sh from Rs. 575/sh earlier. FY27 EPS reduces mainly due to higher shares outstanding (from the QIP) from Rs. 15.8/sh to Rs. 15.2/sh.
- Tata Power - With FY26 performance flowing into our financial forecasts and valuation multiples unchanged, we reduce the TP to Rs. 430/sh from Rs. 440/sh earlier.
- PFC & REC - We reduce loan book growth CAGR between FY26 to FY28 to 10.1% for PFC and 9.6% for REC from 10.6% and 11.4% earlier with the higher pre-payments coming in from renewable and DISCOM segments (as health improves). With the impact of lowering loan book growth, EPS reduces to Rs. 65/sh from Rs. 67.6/sh in FY27 for REC and to Rs. 86.8/sh from Rs. 88.18/sh in FY27 for PFC. We reduce the FY27 P/E multiple for REC from 6.7x to 6.5x, our TP for REC reduces from Rs. 450/sh to Rs. 420/sh. For PFC, with FY27 target P/E multiple unchanged PFC, we remove holding company discount for its stake in REC given PFC-REC merger being approved by both the companies' boards and the government, we increase the TP for PFC to Rs. 500/sh from Rs. 460/sh earlier.
- Adani Green - We add 10GWh of BESS capacity in FY27 and also lower capacity addition to 5GW from 5.3 GW before as per management guidance. Our EPS reduces to Rs.22.3/sh in FY27 from Rs. 24.78/sh given higher interest cost from BESS addition. Our WACC also reduces from 9.1% to 8.8% with higher debt in FY27 and also lower cost of borrowing from 9%

to 8.5% given the US-SEC/DoJ issues being resolved will allow them to borrow at lower rates than before. With this our TP increases to Rs. 1000/sh from Rs. 864/sh earlier.

- Power Grid: We have revised down our capitalization estimates due to prevailing RoW issues, while keeping the capex estimates unchanged. WACC increased from 8.1% to 8.2%. These changes have increased our target price from Rs 306 to Rs 313.
- Waaree Energies: We have updated module, cell and wafer capacity additions as per company announcements. We expect them to add capacities faster than peers and margins to be largely stable over FY26-28. We have moderated decline in FY27 cell realization from -20% to -15% and also changed FY27 and FY28 utilization%: modules- from 60% and 60% to 60% and 62% and cells- from 80% and 80% to 68% and 75%, driven by their capacity expansion timelines and our demand-supply outlook. Further, we have built in higher raw material costs in FY27 and FY28 (vs FY26) reflecting higher input prices. We are now valuing the company at 10x FY28 EV/EBITDA multiple, to arrive at a target price of Rs 2,539 (vs Rs 2,109 earlier).
- Premier Energies: We update our model by changing cell and wafer manufacturing capacity addition as per company announcements. We change FY27 and FY28 utilization%: modules- from 60% and 60% to 60% and 70% and cells- from 50% and 75% to 60% and 70%; and also increase cost estimates due to the reasons similar to Waaree Energies. We are now valuing the company at 10.5x FY28 EV/EBITDA multiple (vs 11x on FY27 EBITDA earlier), to arrive at a target price of Rs 814 (vs Rs 718 earlier).
- ReNew - With WACC unchanged, we retain our TP of \$8/sh. With the progress on the cell manufacturing capacity ramp-up, we increase their solar PV manufacturing EBITDA margins for FY27 and FY28 along with also incorporating the FDRE bids won by them in FY26. Our FY27 EPS increases from \$0.11/sh to \$0.22 per share.
- IEX - We retain our TP at Rs. 115/sh with WACC unchanged with updating the model with FY26 actuals. Our FY27 EPS reduces from Rs.6.36/sh to Rs. 6.31/sh.
