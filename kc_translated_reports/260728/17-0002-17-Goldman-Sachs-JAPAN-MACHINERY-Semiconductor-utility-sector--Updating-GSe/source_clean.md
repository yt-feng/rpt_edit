# Semiconductor utility sector: Updating GSe, maintain bullish stance on the subsector

We update our earnings estimates for each company based on the recently updated capex plans of major semiconductor companies. However, we make no substantial revisions to our earnings estimates, with the exceptions of our FY3/27 estimate for NOM Micro Science and our FY3/28 estimate for Japan Material. With valuations remaining at healthy levels, we believe the subsector remains highly attractive as it is positioned to benefit from robust semiconductor investment over the medium to long term. We also introduce 2029E for NOM Micro and Japan Material.

## Investment views by company

## Japan Material (Neutral, target price ¥2,300)

Of the four semiconductor utilities companies in our coverage, Japan Material has undergone the most significant re-rating YTD. We think the primary reason for this is heightened market expectations for future growth upside amid the current strength in the memory market, as the company has historically benefited substantially during periods of expanded capex by Kioxia (Kioxia has been contributing 35-52% of Japan Materials sales over the past 5 years). In line with this, we believe that a significant increase in profits of over 40% yoy is likely in FY3/28, when we see a significant increase in initial sales. However, since margins fluctuate depending on the volume of new projects, we think it is still premature to shift our focus to FY3/28 estimates in the current environment, where project schedules are shifting daily. Based on this premise, we raise our FY3/28 operating profit estimate by 17% to reflect improving profitability and new domestic projects, and we raise our 12-month target price to ¥2,300, from ¥2,200. However, we maintain our Neutral rating.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Japan Material

Our 12-month target price is ¥2,300. For Japan Material, we derive our target price by applying a sector-relative premium of +20% (the stock's average EV/EBITDA premium during the current cycle) to the sector-average EV/EBITDA (10X). We use FY3/27E EBITDA as our valuation base year. Key upside/downside risks include (1) the acceleration/delay of initial projects, (2) an improvement/deterioration in profitability in the initial business due to changes in material/labor costs, and (3) potential for expansion into overseas initial projects.

## NOM Micro Science (Buy, target price ¥5,500)

With profit growth driven by large US projects factored into FY3/27 guidance, our impression is that recognition of the company's growth story within the equity market has expanded rapidly. Guidance assumes that the company will secure large orders in 1H and begin booking sales early from 2H. In line with this assumption, we raise our FY3/27 operating profit estimate by 33%, having previously anticipated a more moderate sales increase. We also raise our FY3/28 estimate by 6%, and in conjunction with these changes, we raise our target price to ¥5,500, from ¥5,300. We maintain our Buy rating as the shares appear undervalued relative to their normal range, trading at a P/E of 16-17X based on FY3/27E and 11-12X based on FY3/28E.

## Organo (Buy, target price ¥19,200)

TSMC's latest earnings results indicated an outlook for aggressive capex to continue over the next several years. We thus maintain our long-held view that Organo, which handles TSMC's ultrapure water facilities globally, will continue to see high profit growth over the next several years. Reflecting the latest project schedules and factoring in some risk of construction delays, we cut our FY3/27-FY3/28 operating profit estimates by 1-3%. Additionally, we slightly trim our target price to ¥19,200, from ¥19,500, due to an increased discount rate resulting from the recent rise in the risk-free rate. We reiterate our Buy rating.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Organo Corp.

We are Buy rated, with a 12-month target price of ¥19,200. Our target price is based on the sector-average EV/EBITDA multiple of 10X, to which we apply a premium of +60%. We discount the theoretical value obtained based on our FY3/29 estimates back to FY3/27 at a WACC of 6.5%. Key risks include project delays, a deep/long-term decline in semiconductor investment demand, margin deterioration accompanying business expansion in North America, opportunity losses generated by supply shortages, and longer-than-expected project time frames in the construction business stemming from revisions to the Labor Standards Act, which came into effect from April 2024.

## Kurita Water Industries (Buy, target price ¥10,000)

Per the company, Kurita Water Industries is scheduled to receive a number of large projects for multiple major semiconductor manufacturers in FY3/27, and thus there are currently no signs of weakness in its future profit growth, including from FY3/28. The negative impact of chemical shortages stemming from the Middle East conflict also looks likely to be limited in FY3/27 compared to initial expectations. Excluding delays in other projects, our impression is that there are no notable downside risks. We also expect continuous expansion in shareholder returns as part of the company's efforts to target an equity ratio of 0.50, and we believe higher valuation levels than in the past can be expected to become the norm going forward. We fine-tune our operating profit estimates for FY3/27-FY3/29 by c.-1% to +3% due to shifting in project timing, but our target price is unchanged at ¥10,000. We maintain our Buy rating.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Kurita Water Industries

We are Buy rated, with a 12-month target price of ¥10,000. Our target price is based on applying the machinery sector-average EV/EBITDA multiple of 10X and a sector-relative premium of +10%, which is close to past peak levels, to our FY3/27E EBITDA. Key risks include further margin erosion accompanying the capture of strategic projects, a prolonged decline in demand in the semiconductor market, margin deterioration in the electronics business, and longer-than-expected project time frames in the construction business stemming from revisions to the Labor Standards Act, which came into effect from April 2024.
