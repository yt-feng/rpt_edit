# CHINA HEALTHCARE: MEDICAL DEVICE & SERVICES
# 2Q/1H26 Preview: Recovery Visibility Remains Uneven; Prefer Names with Globalization Optionality

We remain broadly neutral on China Medical Device & Healthcare Services into 2Q/1H 26 results, as visibility on domestic demand recovery continues to be uneven, while maintaining a more constructive view on companies with visible globalization potential. Domestic fundamentals remain constrained by policy-related headwinds, including the normalization of consumables VBP, ongoing reimbursement and cost-control measures under DRG/DIP, and the upcoming rollout of equipment VBP in 2H26 (see our previous note). While pricing pressure is gradually stabilizing on an easy base, we continue to monitor the impact of the latest anti-corruption measures introduced since May, which could weigh on hospital procurement activity and clinical volumes in the near term.

China MedTech & Services has remained one of the weaker segments within healthcare, with stock prices declining 21% on avg. over the past six months. Although the sub-sector rebounded 4.5% over the past month, it continues to lag the broader healthcare coverage universe (+8.9%). Investor interest remains highly selective, largely focused on companies demonstrating either clear operating recovery or with a proven track record of overseas expansion. Notably, Angelalign (+16.8% in 1M) has outperformed likely driven by improving confidence in its international growth trajectory. Surgical robotics companies continue to attract significant investor attention given their long-term globalization potential; however, share price performance has yet to fully reflect recent overseas commercialization progress, as investors remain focused on order verification, sustainability of overseas demand, and the broader sentiment impact from Intuitive Surgical's recent share price weakness (-30% in 3M).

Investor engagement with healthcare service names remains relatively limited amid a still-soft consumption backdrop. That said, the market has become increasingly focused on signs of operational stabilization, margin resilience, and shareholder return initiatives, while Hygeia (+12.4% in 1M), Gushengtang (+7.2% in 1M) have seen some share price recovery following easing concerns over potential exclusion from the Hong Kong Stock Connect program. Looking ahead, the pace of demand recovery during summer, particularly for discretionary and consumer-exposed healthcare services, will be a key area of focus for investors assessing the sustainability of earnings recovery into 2H26.

## Medtech: Overseas Execution Continues to Differentiate; Profitability and Margin Inflection Emerging

Overseas execution remains the key differentiator across MedTech, particularly in surgical robotics and clear aligners. Edge Medical continues to scale internationally, with c.100 cumulative overseas systems installed/shipped by 1H26 and utilization ramping (>20k cumulative procedures), supporting operating leverage and potential upside to guidance. MicroPort MedBot reported c.300 cumulative global orders (240+ overseas) and 80+ installations YTD, while maintaining its FY26 target of c.200 installations, implying continued commercialization momentum. Angelalign: Domestic and overseas growth remain on track, with limited VBP exposure given only c.5–7% of revenue comes from public hospitals. The VBP rollout appears modestly delayed, while recent legal wins in China and Germany help reduce litigation-related overhangs and improve execution visibility (see the news release here and our previous note).

Preliminary results: 1) MicroPort MedBot's July 22 profit alert marked a key inflection point, with 1H26 profitability achieved alongside c.200–230% yoy revenue growth, driven by strong Toumai adoption and >450% yoy overseas revenue growth. Gross margin expanded by more than 15ppt, supported by scale benefits and product mix improvement. Investor focus is likely to remain on revenue sustainability and accounts receivable collection. 2) AK Medical also delivered a positive profit alert, guiding for c.10% revenue growth and >20% net profit growth in 1H26. Overseas revenue increased c.40% yoy, ahead of expectations, supporting margin expansion. Robot commercialization continues to gain traction, with 11 tenders secured as of June (including five overseas), reinforcing the overseas growth narrative.

## Services seeing gradual operational recovery, while visibility remain uneven

Healthcare service providers are seeing gradual operational recovery, alongside a stronger emphasis on capital discipline, shareholder returns, and overseas expansion. Hygeia reported improving trends in the five months ended May 2026, with both outpatient visits (+4% yoy) and surgical volumes (+7.6% yoy) increasing, supported by stabilized pricing and higher-complexity case mix. Non-medical insurance contribution increased modestly (+1–2% yoy), while management indicated a more cautious expansion strategy, slowing heavy-asset capex and focusing on buybacks/dividends, with c.Rmb500mn annually earmarked for shareholder returns (2026–28). Gushengtang maintained solid volume growth (+15% yoy in 1Q; +15–20% in Apr–May), in line with FY26 growth guidance. The company is also enhancing shareholder returns, guiding ≥HK\$450mn annual dividends (c.≥60% payout) and announcing an additional HK\$300mn buyback program; On overseas expansion, Gushengtang is scaling its Singapore operations (targeting c.50 stores by YE26, c.Rmb300mn revenue).

Preliminary results: Jinxin pre-announced 1H26 net profit of at least Rmb90mn (vs. a net loss of Rmb1,044mn in 1H25) and EBITDA of at least Rmb290mn (vs. negative Rmb938mn in 1H25). Operationally, new patient visits increased $9\%$ yoy to 24,169 and OPU cycles rose $6\%$ yoy to 14,653, led by $42\%$ growth overseas and $15\%$ growth in Kunming/Wuhan, partly offset by a 5% decline in Chengdu, while Greater Bay Area cycles remained broadly stable at +1% (see the operating data announcement).

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

## M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
