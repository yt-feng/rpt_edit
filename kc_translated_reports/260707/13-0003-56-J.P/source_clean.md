# JPM
## Indonesia Consumer

Consumer names under our coverage have outperformed the JCI by +1% to +62% YTD despite multiple headwinds. That said, absolute returns remain poor: only MAPI is up +30% YTD, while the rest are down -31% to +2% YTD. We acknowledge that investor appetite for Indonesian consumer exposure has been low this year, but we see a selective opportunity to accumulate quality names for longer-term holdings. Sector valuations have de-rated inline with market valuation (30% YTD) despite minimal earnings cuts for companies under our coverage. We believe the consumer sector will be one of the beneficiaries of a broader market rally and would urge investors to position selectively.

\- Top picks are Indofood Group (ICBP, INDF) and MAPA (sports retail). We downgrade AMRT and MAPI to Neutral, and maintain UW on UNVR. Our positive view on ICBP & INDF is underpinned by (1) an undemanding valuation for the world's largest instant noodle manufacturer, (2) cost headwinds (CPO, packaging) that should begin to abate, and (3) a likely moderation in negative earnings revisions. For MAPA, we see resilient sports demand (14% revenue growth CAGR in FY25–27E) alongside an undemanding valuation (\~10x P/E for \~13% EPS CAGR).

\- Headwinds are easing. We think the market has largely priced in (1) higher input costs (packaging and USD strength) and (2) a weak demand backdrop. Some of the pressures are now moderating oil has eased back to \~USD70/bbl - and we expect YTD negative earnings revisions to start stabilizing. While demand remains soft, Indonesia's improved fiscal position from lower energy subsidies reduces the risk of 2H fuel price hikes and/or cuts to social spending/cash handouts.

\- We downgraded MAPI and AMRT for different reasons. MAPI has already delivered a strong performance (+30% YTD vs JCI -32%) as the company plans a mandatory tender offer (MTO) at IDR1,550/share; we expect renewed price discovery post-MTO, limiting upside. AMRT also appears to be entering a price discovery phase as the market reprices for (1) slower topline growth and (2) less margin upside, given wage pressure and intensified competitive pricing.

\- Key risks to our Neutral/UW calls: Our relative safe haven and easing input-cost thesis could also support UNVR, but we stay UW given (1) lofty valuation (AMRT \~14x and UNVR \~17x P/E vs coverage \~5-10x FY27E) and (2) muted topline growth. Separately, AMRT remains a foreign-investor favorite and could see disproportionate inflows if earnings visibility improves.

We are selective in Indonesia's consumer space, preferring high-end retailers followed by select staples. Our selectivity stems from a continued K-shaped recovery that impacts the growth outlook for consumer staple companies. We believe the sector remains challenged on three key fronts: 1) rising competitive intensity that is compressing pricing power; 2) weak consumer purchasing power driving downtrading, resulting in 3) negative pricing impact at a time when input costs have increased and, ultimately, 4) subdued earnings delivery. These factors have led to a broad-based sector de-rating over the last 2 years and we are adjusting our valuation mental framework to accommodate for these factors.

Investing in Indonesia's consumer companies has historically promised double-digit topline growth, driven by strong pricing power and volume growth which is underpinned by a growing population and better distribution penetration. The situation has significantly changed as 1) inflation has remained subdued at $\sim 3\%$ in the last 5 years and 2) we have more competition coming in, especially from China (see our last year's assessment note). The resulting impact is not trivial. Staple companies under our coverage have undershot growth expectations and their valuation has significantly compressed (see Figure 5 for P/E multiples). Consumer discretionary companies that we cover have continued to enjoy growth. The dispersion in growth is largely attributable to a K-shaped recovery in Indonesia, and also Indonesia's strong affinity for foreign branded products. Having said that, multiple compression has also followed discretionary companies despite strong earnings delivery. Size, liquidity and also concerns on currency continue to negatively weigh the thesis for investing in MAPI.

How do we proceed from here? Three key developments have reshaped our framework over the past few months. Increases in commodity prices (CPO, wheat) alongside oil-derivative costs are likely to weigh on margins across our FMCG coverage in 2Q and 3Q26 results. Packaging costs in particular - which account for 5–15% of COGS for FMCG companies - could hurt HPC companies with higher packaging needs such as UNVR - though companies with pricing power are well-positioned to absorb these. Second, ownership dynamics have moved against us. Foreign ownership across our coverage has trended lower over the past 12-18 months (see Figure 4), following a broad-based selloff in the Indonesian equity market. Some companies under our coverage have also been excluded from MSCI's EM standard cap index in the last 1-year, with AMRT removed from the standard cap index in May -26 and similarly ICBP in Nov-25. There are currently no consumer companies represented in the MSCI EM index for Indonesia. The removal means that investors benchmarked to MSCI EM will be investing off-index for owning companies under our coverage. This off-index positioning, while a near-term headwind, sets up a powerful re-rating catalyst should flows return.

Local institutions are unable to entirely absorb foreign selling. Local institutions (mutual and pension funds) have begun selectively rotating back into consumer (see Figure 10). Local institutional ownership (% of free-float) has risen in our preferred names - ICBP (+3.1% y/y, +1.7% YTD) and MAPA (+2.8% y/y, +2.9% YTD) - and the YTD moves show this is still playing out. The buying has been selective - local ownership has fallen/ remained flat in the names where the outlook has weakened, namely MAPI (-17.7% y/y, -16.1% YTD) and AMRT (+0.7% y/y, +1.0% YTD), in line with our Neutral ratings on both. Two names run the other way - 1) INDF has seen local ownership drift lower (-3.7% y/y); and 2) UNVR has drawn local buying (+4.9% y/y, +3.3% YTD) off a low base, but we would not chase this given limited fundamental support. The bigger picture is constructive. With consumer names now out of the MSCI EM index, the marginal buyers have shifted from both active and passive foreign investors who are usually more sticky to local investors who are more myopic in nature.

Valuation is the silver lining, if there is any. Indonesian consumer companies are never yield plays. However, valuations have fallen to such an extreme that yields have become an integral component of the conversation. We remain selective on our picks in the space, preferring MAPA, INDF and ICBP. Our OWs screen does well on two filters: 1) the ability to pass on input cost inflation through price hikes and 2) earnings resilience that is not contingent on a broader consumer recovery. That said, a massive value unlocking will need foreign investors coming back to invest in the consumer space, in our view. To do that, we need better currency stability, a more sanguine outlook on growth and ultimately clear and consistent policy making. Until then, we believe there will be a cap on valuations and stocks could remain cheap for a long time. This is also why we downgrade AMRT to Neutral and also keep UNVR on Underweight. AMRT still trades at a lofty 14x 1-yr forward P/E multiple at a time when the entire sector is trading at single-digit multiples. Its growth outlook has also deteriorated with the company guiding down store expansion and margins looking tough with rising competition. Valuations relative to history look compelling, but we believe the precedent for historical valuations has no place today. We also downgrade MAPI to Neutral as the stock has outperformed the broader index by +62% YTD, driven by a mandatory tender offer at IDR1,550/sh.

## Indofood CBP

## Lowering estimates amid commodity and FX headwinds

We retain Overweight on ICBP but cut our PT by 39% to IDR7,900, implying \~14% upside. The cuts in our estimates mostly reflect weakening IDR, resulting in higher FX losses. We cut core (ex-FX) earnings by 24-19% for FY26-27E, mostly reflecting lower margins in the noodle business this year.

While 1Q26 results were broadly in line with expectations, we believe the trajectory of earnings recovery is likely to be more gradual. We expect to see a weak 2Q earnings showing due to higher packaging costs and lower noodle volumes. Margins should then recover in 3Q onwards, but we now see close to 200bps reduction in blended EBIT margin for ICBP due to lack of price increases in the noodle business.

What has changed: Key commodity prices have moved against us - CPO is already up +12% YTD - and an 82% probability of El Niño during May - July 2026 with a one in three chance of escalating into a super El Niño should support CPO prices. On top of that, Indonesia's B50 biodiesel mandate and Malaysia's B15 implementation (effective June 1, up from B10) should also support CPO demand.

Compounding this, the IDR has depreciated -7.04% YTD, inflating the landed cost of USD-denominated imports. We now assume USD/IDR of 17,500 (from 16,700) and model more modest noodle ASP increases of 1% in FY26E (from 3.5% previously) as ICBP has not raised prices in 1H26. We estimate +/-2.9% sensitivity to FY26E NPATMI for every +/-100 move in the USD/IDR rate. We have also lowered EBIT margin assumptions across most business segments to reflect sustained input cost pressures. Consequently, our FY26/FY27E revenue forecasts decline by 3.7% and NPATMI estimates by -41%/19%, respectively.

Valuation remains the saving grace. ICBP now trades at 7.9x FY27E P/E which is one of the lowest multiples that the company has ever traded at since its listing. ICBP's operations remain robust and it is the largest instant noodle manufacturer in the world. We believe the market is overly discounting concerns on margins and even on our estimates, which are 26/3% (NPATMI) below street, the company looks very undemanding. We now value ICBP at 9x FY27E P/E, as we believe the earnings decline in FY26 (largely FX driven) is already discounted by the street.

## Taking profit from this trade and downgrading to Neutral

We downgrade MAPI to Neutral (from Overweight) and cut our PT by 25% to IDR1,270, implying 16% downside. This is largely a tactical call. The long-term high-end retail thesis remains intact, and 1Q26 was a strong start to the year - NPATMI rose 33% y/y (-27% q/q) driven by robust specialty store sales (+37% y/y), a still-profitable overseas business, and tight cost control (opex/sales down >400bps y/y). But the stock has outperformed the JCI by 62% YTD already factoring in the strong 1Q print and MTO announcement, leaving risk/reward less compelling.

MTO caps upside in the near term. Pacific Universal Investments' MTO at IDR 1,550/share - a 20% premium to the pre-announcement price - effectively anchors the stock and limits near-term upside until the offer concludes. We view the premium as broadly in line given the sector-wide de-rating (-23% vs 2025 avg).

What we're changing. We revise our forecasts, raising FY26/27E NPATMI by +6/4% bringing us 6.4/4.5/4.6% below the street on FY26/27/28E. We expect revenue growth to slow to 10.1% in FY26E (from 14% in FY25A) as weaker IDR and rising inflation soften H2 demand. We cut our FY26E SSSG estimate to 4.1% (from 4.6%), recovering to 4.9%/4.5% in FY27/28E and implying a 13% revenue CAGR. We see operating margins compressing to \~8.8% (vs. +9% pre-FY25), in line with management's margin of \~9% for FY26E, implying a 10% earnings CAGR over FY25-28E.

Valuation & story ahead. We cut our PT by 25% to IDR1,270, based on 8x FY27E P/E. The 38% reduction in our target multiple (from 13x earlier) reflects: 1) the broader sector de-rating; 2) MAPI's high-growth phase is now largely behind it, with FY25 NPAT up +25% y/y but growth set to moderate; and 3) the stock's recent outperformance, which leaves less room for further re-rating. Nonetheless, the structural story holds - MAPI remains a best-in-class high-end retail operator with a resilient core consumer and a long store-expansion runway. At current levels, though, we see balanced risk/reward and prefer MAPA as the cleaner expression of our K-shaped discretionary thesis. We would turn more constructive on: 1) a meaningful IDR recovery; 2) re-accelerating SSSG as consumer demand normalizes; and 3) accelerated store expansion backed by additional exclusive brand rights.
