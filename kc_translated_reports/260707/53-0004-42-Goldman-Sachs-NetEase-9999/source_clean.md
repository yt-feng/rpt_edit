## NetEase: Core thesis

A compelling "non-AI" compounder, well-placed into 2H26 with catalysts ahead

We expect NetEase to differentiate itself as a disciplined "non-AI" compounder amidst broader market concerns over AI disruption and elevated investment costs among China Internet peers.

\- With a track record of delivering healthy revenue growth and consistent margin improvement in the past years, we look for further profitability expansion through leverage from new games with high incremental margin, structurally lower channel cost and AI benefiting organizational efficiency.

\- The strong IPs and the longevity of NetEase's evergreen titles should continue to provide solid cash revenue growth, and further revenue acceleration is supported by new games catering to global audiences.

We expect sales and OP to accelerate in 2H26E, supported by the upcoming launch of Sea of Remnants, which is estimated to contribute RMB5.8bn+ in first-12-month grossing and 5%+ incremental sales. Trading at 12-13x forward P/E, we believe current valuations present a solid entry opportunity ahead of imminent product and liquidity catalysts.

# NetEase: Key debate 1

## New game pipeline and revenue trajectory

While NetEase has significantly reduced the number of new game launches over time, and evergreen titles remain solid, we see the upcoming launches and pipeline beyond 2026E should still give investors comfort over the longer-term sustainable revenue growth (GSe: games revenue at $9\%$ 2025-28E CAGR) and hence higher multiple on the name.

\- We expect the upcoming Sea of Remnants (PC on July 9th and Mobile shortly after, followed by overseas debut within 2026) to drive double-digit% yoy game grossing growth in 2H26.

\- Looking further ahead, we believe the 2027 pipeline—including the anime-style open-world RPG Ananta and the Unreal Engine 5 single-player title Blood Message—demonstrates NetEase's capability to push genre boundaries and capture global market share, supporting sustainable long-term revenue growth.

## NetEase: Key debate 2 Solid margin expansion and FCF generation

Among all the AI enthusiasm and elevated investments, we view NetEase has been fairly disciplined in its AI strategy and balances well the AI investments and benefits.

\- Over the past 2-3 years, the company's adj. operating margin has continued to ramp up (from $30\%$ in 2023 to $35\%$ in 2025) on the back of shift of 3rd party mobile payment channel to its proprietary channel, and a streamlined corporate structure. We forecast further adj. OPM expansion driven by gross margin improvement on structural channel fee optimization and efficiency gains from AI integration into the R&D process. For 2026E, we look for $20\%$ yoy adj. OP growth and meaningful adj. OPM improvement to $39\%+$ .

• We believe this will also help NTES to deliver consistently healthy FCF (GSe: Rmb49bn+ in 2026E, at c. 9%+ FCF yield) and share buybacks/dividend payouts, in contrast to many Internet peers elevating their AI investments in the past 12 months.

## NetEase: Key debate 3

## Valuation upside to come from earnings upward revisions and game outlook

The valuation upside potential is a constant debate, as NetEase is closely benchmarked to Tencent. Where we differentiate is our view on the multiple's ceiling.

\- We argue that in periods where sales growth accelerates to 10-15% yoy, NetEase's P/E multiple can expand toward 15x+, especially as earnings revisions trend upward.

\- We expects the company to have sales/OP upside vs. consensus, while net income could be temporarily impacted by investment losses (PDD stake mainly) for 2026E.

# Valuation methodology and risks

Our 12-month SOTP-based TPs for NTES/9999.HK are US\$169/HK\$264

We use a SOTP valuation methodology to better capture the different business segment, and our 12m TPs of US\$169/HK\$264 are based on 18x multiple for the Mobile and PC Games segments. Reiterate Buy.

Key downside risks: 1) Weaker performance of legacy games; 2) Slower-than-expected monetization ramp-up of new games; 3) More intensive competition in games, e-commerce, education and other business areas; 4) Rising content costs associated with music and advertising expenses related to the online education business.
