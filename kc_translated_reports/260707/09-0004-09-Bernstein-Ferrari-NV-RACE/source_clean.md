## Ferrari: Manual(e) for Success

Ferrari confirmed after market on 3 July the launch of the limited-edition Special Series 12Cilindri Manuale. This “uses the group’s new, patented, Manuale by-wire system, conceived and developed in Maranello to reinterpret the manual shifting experience through a contemporary technological architecture.” The base retail price in Italy is €590,000, a €200,000 premium over the standard 8-speed automatic12Cilindri. 1,499 units will be produced and all have already been allocated to Ferrari clients. Despite former Chief Commercial Officer Enrico Galliera’s assertion that there are no plans for a Manuale version of the 12 Cilindri Spider, “We came to the conclusion that the coupe is the one that represents the purest Ferrari execution”, we are not convinced. Ferrari employs the “no plans” or “we do not comment about future models” phrasing right up to when the covers come off the next model. Given both its delirious reception and that many of Ferrari’s best customers will not have gained an allocation, we expect Ferrari to announce at a later stage a 12Cilindri Manuale Spider, at potentially half the volume of the coupe run, so maybe an additional 750 units, priced from say €700,000, a healthy premium to the coupe version. Of the €200,000 initial premium charged for the 12Cilindri Manuale coupe, we estimate the incremental fully costed R&D and manufacturing cost to be under €50,000 per vehicle. This suggests some \~40bps of potential group contribution margin uplift per year in 2027-29.

The 12Cilindri Manuale will be exclusively delivered through Ferrari's invitation-only Tailor Made programme which offers a much higher degree of personalisation than is possible through the standard dealer specification process. This suggests that the final MSRPs of these vehicles will be substantially higher - we would estimate closer to €800,000+ per vehicle. Ferrari states that the first customer deliveries are set for 1Q27.

Bernstein view The potential launch of a manual gated version of the 12Cilindri has been one of the most talked about topics within the Ferrari community this year, and moved into overdrive while the massively polarizing Ferrari Luce BEV was being launched towards the end of May. As we noted in our report from 2 June discussing a 12C manual (link), this was a timely reminder of how Ferrari remains on track despite the firestorm of opprobrium that greeted the Luce (link). We had expected 12C M reveal to coincide with Ferrari's 29 June to 6 July Venice to Athens Cavalcade, and this duly occurred. (Continued on Page 2)

The pricing of the 12Cilindri Manuale, and it's positioning within the Tailor-Made programme has everything to do with owning one of the most desirable Ferrari cars in recent years rather than with the incremental cost of the manual transmission that overlays the 12Cilindri's current 8-speed dual-clutch automatic transmission. As such, it will be another contributor to the increasingly hard to ignore mix improvement that we believe will cause Ferrari to comfortably exceed the revenue and EBIT growth targets it set out last October in its 2026-30 plan.

## THE ALLURE OF THE MANUAL

Ferrari has not built a road car with a manual transmission since 2012, when the 599 GTB and California came with the option of traditional six-speed gated manual transmissions. In recent years, the most prestigious segments of the automotive industry have seen a resurgence in demand for supercars with manual gearboxes, including the Pagani Utopia, GMA T.50, and Koenigsegg's CC850, as well as for slightly more accessible cars with manuals, like the Porsche 911 GT3 and 911 Carrera T.

The values of old Ferrari and Lamborghini cars with manual gearboxes have increased at a parabolic rate in recent years. A typical Ferrari 599 GTB Fiorano with the new generation F1 SuperFast automatic gearbox will be listed at around €120,000 – €150,000 in Europe, whereas the much rarer original factory-fitted manual 599 is in the €600,000 to \~€1,000,000 range depending on provenance and mileage. When the 599 was first launched in 2007, the price for cars with the gated manual transmission was around the same price as for the F1 Superfast automatic transmission. Customer demand overwhelmingly favored the F1 SuperFast transmission, and it is widely thought that only some 30 units of the F599 GTB were delivered with the factory-fitted 6-speed gated manual (link). The F12, the successor to the 599 in 2012, was only available with a dual clutch automatic transmission. There is now a thriving aftermarket in conversions of F599 F1 SuperFast automatics into manuals, although, when sold, these do not attract anywhere close to the prices of the original factory-fitted cars.

## AND THE FINANCIAL IMPLICATIONS

Ferrari simultaneously announced the Coupe and Spider versions of the 12Cilindri at the launch event in Miami Beach on 3 May 2024 celebrating the 70 $^{th}$ anniversary of Ferrari in the USA. Ferrari will never discuss the incremental development and industrialization costs of the 12Cilindri Manuale over the standard 12Cilindri, so here are our thoughts. From past discussions with engineering firms, we estimate that the entire research, development and industrialization of the Manuale is unlikely to have cost more than €75m, which equals €50k per car spread over 1,499 examples. This does not even consider the further overhead recovery if some 750 Spider versions are subsequently launched. Considering the €200,000 price uplift demanded by Ferrari for the Manuale over the standard car, that leaves €150k per car as almost pure drop-through margin. This is €225m in total on 1,499 cars, so €75m per year over an estimated 3 years' delivery span. That equates on our calculations to an extra \~40bps of margin per year from 2027 through to 2029.

It is fair to say that some of the €200,000 price uplift can be accounted for by extra standard features that Ferrari includes with the 12Cilindri Manuale, but all can rest assured that there are still plenty of opportunities for the brand's chosen top customers to spend on further personalisation like carbon-fibre sills with the car's logo painted onto it and multiple other carbon additions. All cars will be configured through Ferrari's exclusive Tailor Made customization program, with 25 iconic Ferrari exterior colors that can be mixed and matched with exclusive leather and Alcantara interior trim. There is also an optional livery, paying homage to the six-speed gearbox, offered in a range of color schemes. This is why we estimate that these cars are likely to be delivered with MSRPs closer to the €800,000 level.

## 12CILINDRI MANUALE TECHNICAL HIGHLIGHTS

\- Ferrari developed the 12 Cilindri Manuale purely in response to strong customer demand for a manual driving experience. A traditional manual gearbox was infeasible due to packaging constraints and the inability of existing manual transmissions to handle 818hp and 500 lb-ft of torque from the high-revving 6.5L V12 engine of the 12Cilindri.

\- Instead, Ferrari's engineers created a “Manuale-by-wire” system: a physical shifter and clutch pedal layered over the existing 8-speed dual clutch transmission (DCT) using servos and software. The goal was to replicate authentic analog feel, validated by benchmarking classic Ferrari manuals with the company's storied chief test driver, Raffaele de Simone. The mechanical interface includes a gated shifter with tactile feedback, spring-loaded clutch, and heritage-inspired “click” engagement. Only six of the eight DCT ratios are used in manual mode, optimized for peak power at 9,250 rpm. Operating logic removes steering-wheel paddles, emphasizes full manual control via lever and clutch, supports heel-and-toe driving, and allows seamless switching to automatic mode with added features like gear pre-selection and coasting management.

\- The clutch-by-wire pedal digitally converts pedal pressure into precise clutch actuation, directly controlling the DCT clutch pack. A mechanical system (spring, cam, roller) replicates the traditional load-travel curve, ensuring realistic resistance and pedal feel. The system preserves authentic manual behaviour: smooth shifts with correct timing, or resistance, jolts, and potential stalling with incorrect inputs.

\- The system allows manual techniques while preventing miss-shifts electronically, though stalling remains possible. Performance is intentionally compromised versus automatic mode; the car is faster with the DCT alone, but buyers prioritize engagement over outright speed. According to Enrico Galliera, "Our clients were willing to compromise on performance to

have a manual gearbox. They said, 'If you have to take out horsepower, that's fine. We want the pleasure of driving it."

\- The Manuale By-Wire system introduces a dedicated architecture with new components, including a mechanical gear lever, control panel, clutch-by-wire pedal, and updated control software. The core powertrain hardware (engine and gearbox) remains unchanged, with innovation focused on the driver interface and interaction quality. Development prioritized three factors: kinematic precision, load progression fidelity, and full driver autonomy during gear changes.

\- The gear lever mechanism enables two-axis rotation for gear selection and engagement, ensuring precise and repeatable movements. A central rotating block machined from high-strength steel ensures durability, minimal friction, and no play or positional drift over time. The lever is self-centring via eccentric rollers, returning automatically to neutral and maintaining a traditional gated manual feel. A profiled rotating drum with preload generates progressive resistance and release, creating tactile “mechanical” feedback during shifts.

\- The iconic gated shift pattern is retained, including a push-down action to engage reverse gear. Driver inputs are captured by dual Hall-effect sensors and processed by the gearbox control unit, while a solenoid actuator provides physical locking feedback. The gear knob integrates 12 LEDs with white/amber back lighting, displaying gear positions and system status within a machined aluminum structure.

## 2Q26 BRIEFING CALL RECAP

Ferrari held its 2Q26 round up group call for analysts on 25 June. Its 2Q26 results will be released on 30 July.

After the surprise that Ferrari delivered at the end of March during its 1Q26 round up call - namely its ability to offset the one-month-old Gulf conflict and deliver consensus-busting performance, and then the whirlwind of opprobrium that followed the unveiling of Ferrari's first fully electric model, the Luce, on 25 May, Ferrari's messaging in this 2Q26 sell-side round up call was one of calm. FY26 is largely going to plan, despite the disruptions caused by the Middle East crisis. Ferrari is sticking to its original volume plan for the year. There were no major revelations on the 2Q26 call aside from some comments regarding the €200m previously guided headwind for FX. When FY26 guidance was given at the FY25 results stage on 10 February 2025, the company assumed a €200m FX headwind under the assumption that the EUR/USD exchange rate would be 1.20. The rate for 2026 YTD is 1.16, and the current spot rate is 1.14, implying that the overall headwind would be less than initially estimated. Shipments in 2Q26 are expected to be down yoy as planned due to the high level of model changeover, and price/mix will be up.

The phase-out of the 296 GTB and GTS, and Roma Spider, and ramp-up of the F80 and 849 Testarossa give positive mix effects. This is offset by the absence of the Daytona SP3 (last shipments in 3Q25), and the phase-out of the limited production run of the SF90 XX and the phase-in of the entry-level Amalfi. Lower 499P Modificata shipments also had a slightly negative mix offset.

## Further details from the call

\- Sponsorship, commercial and brand will have a positive effect in 2Q26, which is in line with guidance for the year.

\- F80 deliveries will continue to grow sequentially each quarter this year as initially planned. This progression will be linear and gradual, as previously said.

\- SG&A will include higher marketing expenses yoy with the world premiere of the Ferrari Luce being the main driver behind this.

\- R&D is in line with previous expectations. It is slightly higher yoy due to new F1 regulations.

• D&A should be flat vs 2Q25 levels.

• Tax rate in 2Q26 should be 23% as guided for the full year.

\- We are still awaiting the unveiling of the 3 more models in 2026. This is on top of the Amalfi Spider and the full release of the Ferrari Luce that have already been unveiled this year. (This was before the launch of the 12Cilindri Manuale, which Ferrari counts as its second launch of 2026)

\- Working capital should have a negative effect on Industrial FCF yoy. There is an unwind of the positive effect that was seen in 1Q26 which was due to schedule of some payments being made to the company. Rising inventory of higher-priced cars that are being ramped up will also have a negative effect.

\- Dividend payment is higher yoy. This is not only down to earnings growth, but also due to an increased dividend policy that was presented at the CMD last October.

\- Capex should be roughly stable yoy in 2Q26. This is in line with the company's FY26 guidance of €950m-€1.0bn.

\- No further color was given in relation to show product mix or profitability compared to 1Q26.

\- The company is taking orders for the Luce for new and existing customers. More commentary is expected on the 2Q26 earnings call.
