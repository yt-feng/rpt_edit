## U.S. Machinery
# Machinery Lead Indicators: June data points in 40 exhibits

This note provides a holistic view of relevant data points for the Machinery sector across construction equipment, commercial vehicles and agriculture equipment, with a brief overview of key macroeconomic indicators.

The Construction Equipment market: Non-resi starts increased in May by \~30% Y/Y in terms of value (similar to the prior month), and but declined \~10% Y/Y in sq ft, vs prior month's \~10%. Total Non Resi spending declined 2%, vs +1% Y/Y the prior month. May Construction Backlog increased \~8% Y/Y, vs +1% the prior month. The Architecture Billings Index declined by 4%Y/Y in May (vs prior month's +3%), and still remains below the 50 mark. In Apr, Komatsu retail sales grew in North America, Europe and China by 10%, 10%, and 34%, respectively. May Dodge Momentum Index accelerated to +31% Y/Y from the prior month's +30%.

North America Heavy Duty Truck remains under pressure, but is showing some positive signs. Build rates declined \~10% Y/Y during May, vs \~-10% the prior month. Net orders increased 100% Y/Y reaching \~25k units (vs \~25k prior month). Backlog increased \~70% (vs \~+50% the prior month). In June, the Internet Truckstop Market Demand Index increased \~100% YoY (vs. \~+150% prior month). In the earliest read of Jul, DAT Spot Rates reached \$3.07, which translates to +45% Y/Y, similar to the prior month

In Ag equipment, farmer sentiment deteriorated in both US and European markets. Hence, sentiment remained cautious and unit volumes continued under pressure. The Purdue AG Barometer declined 3 points from the prior month to 119 in May, but remains ahead of the 100 mark. The CEMA Barometer declined again from -9 points in May to -20 points in Jun (-24 points Y/Y, vs prior -16). The North American high-horsepower (HHP) volumes decreased \~10% Y/Y in May(vs \~-15% the prior month), and combine sales declined \~55% (vs +3% the prior month)

Macroeconomic indicators: Money supply for the 3 largest economies (US, China, Europe) continued growing (MayY/Y change was +\$4T, vs prior month's of \~+\$3.7T). Every \$100B reduction in the money supply reduces the backlog growth of capital goods companies by 1%. Conversely, global growth synchronization of top 20 economies by GDP declined to 75% (from +80% the prior month) and most of these countries are showing a positive $2^{nd}$ derivative of PMI growth.
