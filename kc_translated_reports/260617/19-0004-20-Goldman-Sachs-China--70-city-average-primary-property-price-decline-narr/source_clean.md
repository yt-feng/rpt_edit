# China: 70-city average primary property price decline narrowed in May, with Tier-1 cities posting sequential gains
## Bottom line:

The National Bureau of Statistics' 70-city house price data suggest the weighted average property price in the primary market fell by $1.8\%$ mom annualized in May after seasonal adjustments, less than the $2.9\%$ decline in April. Home prices in Tier-1 cities continued to increase sequentially, led by Shenzhen, and the sequential decline in home prices in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities also narrowed. We emphasize the 70-city data are for primary market transactions (new home sales) only; secondary market data by NBS and some third-party platforms suggest price declines of $5 - 10\%$ over the past year.

## Key numbers: $^{1}$

NBS' 70-city primary-market weighted average property price change in May: -1.8% mom annualized (seasonally adjusted by GS), -3.5% yoy. April: -2.9% mom annualized, -3.6% yoy.

## Main points:

1. After seasonal adjustments, weighted average house prices in the primary market fell by 1.8% mom annualized in May (vs. -2.9% in April; Exhibit 1). The number of cities that experienced sequentially higher property prices edged up in primary markets but edged down in secondary markets in May (Exhibit 2). While year-over-year changes in the national weighted-average new home prices fell further by 3.5% in May (vs. -3.6% in April), Shanghai (3.7%), Hangzhou (2.0%), and Hefei (0.8%) continued to post year-over-year price gains. We emphasize the 70-city data are for primary market transactions (new home sales) only; secondary market data by NBS and some third-party platforms suggest price declines of 5-10% over the past year (Exhibit 3).
2. By city tiers $^{2}$ , Tier-1 city house prices increased sequentially by 1.4% mom annualized in May (vs. +0.7% in April). Recent policy easing remains localized (e.g., Guangzhou's buyback program) and is focused on expanding the use of housing provident funds. Shenzhen posted the strongest sequential gain among Tier-1 cities, with prices up 3.9% mom annualized in May (vs. +1.6% in April).
