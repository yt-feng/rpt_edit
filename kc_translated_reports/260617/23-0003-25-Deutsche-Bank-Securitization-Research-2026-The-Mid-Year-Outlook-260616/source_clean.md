## Potential fixes for CMBS market?

Post-GFC CMBS structures improved, but governance remains the weak link. The market is still exposed to conflicted control rights, opaque loan modifications, delayed loss recognition, and documentation that leaves too much room for interpretation.

■ Reduce special-servicer conflicts. Separate B-piece economics from special-servicer appointment rights. Use an independent special servicer with no controlling-class affiliation to reduce conflicts in workout decisions.
■ Make modifications accountable. Require ex-ante recovery estimates for every material modification (e.g., the expected recovery from a mod). Tie a portion of workout/liquidation fees to realized recovery versus prior estimate. After two modifications or 24 months of cumulative extension, further relief should require operating advisor consent or certificate-holder negative consent.
■ Accelerate transparency and price discovery. Require independent appraisals within 60 days of delinquency, engaged by the trustee rather than the special servicer. Disclose all material modification terms within five business days.
- Improve documentation clarity. Simplify waterfall, control-rights, appraisal-reduction, and workout-fee language, so key economic outcomes are mechanical, auditable, and less dependent on interpretation. Standardize disclosure templates for modifications, extensions, fee reimbursements, and realized-loss calculations.

CMBS does not need another full structural overhaul. It needs cleaner incentives, proper accountability, faster transparency, and clearer documentation that limits discretion in workout outcomes.

These are broad, directional ideas. Implementation would require comprehensive refinement and stakeholder alignment. Happy to hear your thoughts.

## Primary Market – room for catchup in H2

The first immediately noticeable data point related to the primary market is New Issue volume, which is tracking lower year-to-date – \$75B priced through last Friday compared to \$92B at this time a year ago, a nearly 20% gap.

The softer YoY comp is due to several reasons already mentioned – concerns over software (a large sector when it comes to exposure in CLOs and Lev Loans), fallout from the Iranian conflict and the higher energy prices its produced, and a challenged arbitrage environment due to what remains a strong bid for leveraged loan debt.

Although loan spreads have marginally widened over the last month compared to CLO primary spreads which have tightened, the current level of loan spreads for a modeled new issue CLO are still 40 bps tighter than the 5-year average, causing many CLO managers to prioritize repricings in the current environment rather than new deal creation.

However, the CLO repricing market has also been challenged in parts – like NI, Resets are also softer YoY, down by 21%, \$73B vs \$93B at this time a year ago.

Since the story of May, however, Reset deal activity has strengthened, averaging \$5.1B per week and narrowing the gap with last year's volume. And given where NI spreads were at this time in 2024, the vintage most in scope for a Reset option – 150 / 188 bps for AAAs / WADMs compared to Reset spreads today – 122 / 156 bps – Reset volume should continue to see a re-acceleration in H2, although likely remaining well off of last year's levels.

Refis tend to perform well during periods of choppy pricing, like we have seen this year, given their ability to offer a partial repricing of the debt stack. Junior debt becomes more difficult to place when price levels are uncertain, enabling Refis to serve as a deal catalyst during conditions like we have seen in H1.

## Loans – a bifurcated market

Primary market Institutional loan activity is marginally softer YoY – \$195B priced compared to \$208B at this time a year ago, a 6% YoY difference.
