# Cool, Calm and Collected

USD: Cool, calm, and collecting carry. The cooler US inflation print crimped a tail that could have allowed for bigger policy divergence and outsized spot FX moves. Instead, carry considerations have once again had the largest contribution to returns and investor preferences, even within the G10 (Exhibit 1). This makes sense in a market defined by low vol and high dispersion in the level of rates, and we expect it to continue, but it is all the more remarkable when concerns have risen in commodities and equities. We have argued that the source of market volatility is increasingly moving from macro drivers to the more micro level and so far this is proving correct. If those factors morph into more macro drivers it would interrupt the carry-friendly environment in FX, but we see a high bar for that to happen given that even volatility within the most-affected asset classes is still low beyond the sector level, regional divergence has been more limited than in previous tech turmoil, and the level of rate dispersion provides a wide buffer for currencies. We think it argues for more carefully selecting funding currencies—as we discuss in more detail below, we prefer CHF, EUR, ILS, JPY, CAD, and THB in roughly that order—and respecting the Dollar’s relatively high-yielder status. We still see a risk of more divergent policy paths ahead, and therefore see value in Dollar calls as cross asset hedges, but in our baseline we expect it to be more of a grind.

GBP: Too strong for keeps, go tactically short GBP/USD. Sterling’s striking July outperformance has continued this week, pushing against our expectation for limited further relief. We are mindful of a series of emerging medium-term tailwinds for Sterling—a procyclical global backdrop, a reach for carry in EUR/GBP, substantial M&A inflows, and the possibility of closer ties to the EU. But the speed and timing of Sterling’s July rally appear to us to be more a function of growing optimism on a Burnham government (including Wednesday’s GBP move coinciding with personnel reports), and of technical factors and positioning rotations following the range-break in EUR/GBP. From a fundamental model perspective, Sterling’s move is fully at odds with its GSBEER-implied performance, which primarily reflects EU-UK front-end rate differentials returning close to their narrowest year-to-date levels (Exhibit 2). We have noted throughout the past year that bouts of premium that see Sterling underperform cyclical fundamentals are typically quickly reversed, and we think the same argument applies in the opposite direction here. We are sceptical of the pace of Sterling’s move when there remain many policy unknowns and fundamental constraints on the fiscal front, alongside more durable macro and valuation challenges for the currency. Our historical analysis of near-neighbour crosses suggests the case for a reversion towards GSBEER-implied levels is ‘cleanest’ in EUR/GBP, but our current long-Dollar lean, energy re-escalation risks, and importance of carry considerations in this backdrop lead us to believe that Cable is currently the higher-quality expression. We therefore recommend GBP/USD shorts with a target of 1.3250 and a stop of 1.3600, targeting a near-term reversal.

\- KRW: Catching a break. The Korean Won has finally caught a break in July, with the currency appreciating month-to-date in July. This bout of appreciation coincides with an interest rate hike, fresh curbs on leveraged investments, and a sell-off in local equity indices. That sell-off may have stemmed any forced selling of concentrated stock positions and may be partly related to a large overseas ADR listing, that at least for the time-being may have catalysed some capital inflow. More broadly, looking at KRW performance on the larger ‘equities down’ episodes earlier this year (up to the KOSPI peak on June 22), the correlation was mostly slightly positive (that is, both assets sold off together), though the Won did appreciate in the more extreme sell-offs, and most of the time was relatively range-bound (Exhibit 4). This was also the case in the best KOSPI days YTD when the currency stubbornly did not generally benefit from strong equity performance (Exhibit 5). More recently, this correlation has shifted in a clearly negative direction as the Won has been appreciating as local equities have been selling off. However, this correlation remains highly dependent on the direction and source of inflows and outflows, and could shift again, as the mixed signal from the prior relative performance suggests. Moreover, the Won appreciation in July only reverses the depreciation from May and June. KRW remains weak on most valuation metrics, and while the AI-driven export boom has been very supportive for Korean exports, fiscal revenues and growth over the past couple of years, that has come alongside a weakening currency, reflecting significant unhedged overseas investment by Korean retail and institutional investors, and FX hedging and rebalancing flows by foreign buyers of Korean equities. While our equity strategists expect local equity markets to recover, that is unlikely to be accompanied by a sustained strengthening in the Won without a shift in investor allocations or hedging behaviour.

HUF: Tactical headwinds vs fundamental tailwinds. While last week's HUF sell-off reversed relatively swiftly, the Forint has been under renewed pressure this week. We continue to attribute the price action to heavy long positioning in a context of limited positive local developments but increasingly unfavourable global factors (higher energy prices, specifically gas prices, and weaker risk sentiment), which has left HUF among the most high-beta crosses in EM. Growing concerns regarding energy prices and supply into the winter months—with Qatari natural gas supply remaining low and new US tariffs on Russian oil importers being discussed—can keep the Forint under pressure in the near term in our view. To be sure, we think the fundamental case for HUF appreciation towards our 6-month forecast of 350 remains strong, but fresh local catalysts may only emerge later in the year, when a lower inflation target is announced (our economists expect this in late 2026) and when the RRF funds begin to flow (the deadline for payment requests is the end of September). Moreover, when it comes to energy prices, we have argued that EU funds inflows can more than offset the energy balance deterioration implied by current oil and gas prices. Nearer term, the upcoming MNB meeting is the next key local development, and follows comments from Governor Varga on both maintaining the guidance for cuts in July and August and that Forint stability in the 355-360 range was helping the economy. This is the first meeting since the elections where HUF is weaker on the intra-meeting period, but given continued disinflation momentum our economists still expect a 25bp cut to be delivered. We think the extent to which the MNB sounds more cautious, while still delivering a cut, will be an important signal of how policymakers see the trade-off between rates and FX in the broader risk premium reduction since the elections. We are still of the view that both FX and rates can rally together as EU convergence draws closer, but over the near term a stronger currency path would likely require rates to bear more of the brunt of the worsening global backdrop.

■ CHF: Funding in focus. Last week, we discussed the exceptionally high levels of carry-to-vol currently being afforded to G10 FX markets, and in many respects, CHF funding is the most striking opportunity within that. Low and stable Swiss inflation has supported a firmly on-hold SNB, which has helped both to keep

rate-differential-driven vol low in CHF crosses, but also to retain an unusually large gap in its policy rates to the rest of G10. This combination underpins the favourable levels of carry-to-vol available in both USD/CHF and EUR/CHF—both close to multi-decade highs (Exhibit 6). The Franc’s terms of trade linkages to gold provide an added layer of interest to the carry proposition in these pairs too. The recent breakdown in gold’s risk-hedge attributes has spilled over to CHF, partly explaining EUR/CHF’s and USD/CHF’s lower betas to risk this year (Exhibit 7). USD/CHF in particular offers exceptional levels of carry for a pair that has acted as a clear risk-hedge in this environment, which we think places it as an attractive portfolio hedge in an environment where energy shock risks are still simmering. At the same time, CHF’s gold connection is also the clearest risk to the case for funding in our view. Gold prices have stabilised well off their January highs in recent weeks, but a return to a strong appreciation trend (as our commodity strategists ultimately expect) would likely provide support for the Franc, and challenge the funding proposition. We continue to see a pivot back to a neutral CHF intervention bias by the SNB as another potential route to Franc outperformance, though after the SNB’s weak tweak in June, this process appears to us to be a more gradual one. We see good value in CHF funding on a three-month basis where these key risks appear more remote, offering greater carry in G10 crosses than the more typical choice of the Yen.

CAD: High but wide. We have been highlighting CAD's attractive attributes as a funder for much of this year, including its ability to reduce risk exposure and improve carry relative to Dollar funding. Those attributes remain intact, and we continue to expect ongoing trade negotiations to weigh on CAD via a wide USD-CAD rate differential—the opening statement of the BoC's July meeting highlighted again that Canada's relationship with the US remains one of the two biggest risks to the outlook alongside the Middle East Conflict. That said, the language was toned down somewhat, noting that businesses are “finding a way to navigate through the uncertainty.” More broadly, the July BoC meeting was balanced, with the statement returning to its pre-war guidance noting that the current policy rate is “appropriate”

in line with our economists' expectations. A BoC firmly on hold should allow for CAD to remain an effective funder, though we have been arguing that sharper spot depreciation in CAD from here appears less compelling. USD/CAD has continued to align closely to rate differentials in recent weeks (Exhibit 8) and on one hand, while support for CAD looks unlikely to come via a more hawkish BoC, on the other hand, the softer US June CPI print should limit the extent of more near-term hawkish Fed repricing, reducing the scope for additional upward pressure on USD/CAD.

THB: Lower on gold and rates. The Thai Baht (THB) has flipped from being one of the best performing Asian currencies in 2025 (trailing only the MYR) to the worst performing low-yielding Asian currency this year. This underperformance comes in spite of the BoT’s recent upward revision to its 2026 GDP forecast of 2.3% yoy (from 1.5%), where +0.6ppt growth boost was attributed to the THB 400bn emergency decree and +0.2ppt due to better tracking growth in Q1 and Q2. Moreover, the political environment appears more stable following the convincing election results in February this year. Apart from the energy shock, we attribute THB’s weakness to three factors. First, there was a sizable equity outbound flow at the end of June, where Singapore’s Singtel sold a 2.3% of its stake in Thailand’s Gulf Energy, which was worth USD 1bn. Second, global gold prices have dropped to around USD 4000/oz from just under USD 5,000/oz at the start of the year. As we have discussed previously, there exists a positive correlation between gold prices and THB, given Thailand’s role as a large gold trading hub. The BoT announced several measures at the end of February to try and de-link this correlation, but it is only likely to take effect and fade gradually. Lastly, we note that real rates differentials have shifted significantly in favor of a stronger USD/THB. The BoT has cut policy rates to 1.0%, while inflation prints have been rising (2.4% yoy in June), leaving real rates in negative territory. Meanwhile, US real rates have risen. Looking ahead, while shifts in oil and gold prices will continue to affect Thailand’s terms of trade and tourism outlook, we expect the currency to continue to underperform on its low real rates and see it as a good funder against higher carry currencies in the region, and continue to recommend short THB/ INR.

AUD/NZD: Turning the tide. A few months ago, we flagged scope for an unwind in AUD/NZD if the relative policy backdrop shifted, domestic data diverged, or the terms-of-trade backdrop became less supportive. Incoming domestic data have increasingly met those conditions, and we recently recommended AUD/NZD 6m 1.1650 puts. While developments over the past week illustrate that renewed energy price pressures remain a risk, AUD/NZD has recently been responding less consistently with increases in commodity prices and more consistently with rate differentials which have been moving in NZD's favor. With our economists flagging the risk of more near-term RBNZ hikes and fewer RBA hikes than they are forecasting, this leaves us more comfortable with AUD/NZD downside despite lingering energy risks. NZD is also vulnerable to a “hawkish policy shock” backdrop in which equities fall alongside higher yields, so the June FOMC meeting introduced a new potential headwind to any NZD outperformance. However, this week’s softer June CPI print leaves limited catalysts before the July FOMC for pricing to shift materially more hawkish. Finally, while we have found AUD is more exposed to China growth, our economists continue to see little urgency for broad-based, sizable near-term stimulus given resilient exports and a still-achievable full-year growth target. Taken together, while risks remain, the conditions we laid out have broadly materialized, and we see a window for AUD/NZD’s catch-down to extend.

\- Frontier FX: Carrying through the near-term vol. Re-escalation in the Middle East has re-injected short-term volatility into Frontier markets, particularly by interrupting the recent strengthening of the Egyptian Pound, our preferred long in that space. While we still expect the currency to gradually appreciate against the Dollar over the medium term, after incrementally raising the target we have widened the stop of our short USD/EGP trade recommendation. This would allow for more room for accruing carry returns despite near-term spot volatility caused by recent outflows from still elevated foreign positioning in local debt markets (Exhibit 10). This is consistent with our view that a high level of carry, particularly in local debt instruments, can amplify spot returns in a ‘relief’ scenario such as that in mid-June following the US-Iran peace deal announcement, but importantly can also cushion total returns in a ‘muddle through’ scenario in which spot returns are more limited, as has been the case recently as geopolitical uncertainty persists. More broadly, we continue to think that contained macro volatility should be conducive to the accumulation of carry returns in EM currencies, and it makes sense to maintain exposure to select high-carry Frontier currencies, including being long TRY, NGN and KZT against the USD (Exhibit 11). Beyond the elevated carry levels that TRY offers despite continued managed depreciation, we see risks as skewed towards spot appreciation in Nigeria and Kazakhstan.
