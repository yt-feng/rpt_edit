# Burberry: Reflections on 1Q27 (= calendar 2Q26)

Burberry reported its 1Q27 (= calendar 2Q26) results today. This report contains key points from its investor conference call and our updated financial forecasts. Results Quick Take: Burberry: 2Q26 calendar in line with expectations

Burberry has rebuilt the foundations for growth. It has returned to an appropriate market position and has reconnected with its core marketing and value proposition. This is resonating with consumers: spend by locals continue to grow across regions, with the brand highlighting the cross-Atlantic halo effect that US marketing activities have created; full-price sell-through continues to improve, with management noting DD growth in flagships in Europe, helping to offset weak off-price performance; wholesale partners are offering positive signals, with wholesale guidance for 1H27E upgraded from MSD to HSD growth.

Burberry continues to build on its top-line momentum. The rest of FY27 will see Burberry following up on recent campaign successes (e.g. ‘Portraits of an Icon’, ‘Good Sport’, etc.) as well as more category destinations that will showcase outerwear and cashmere.

Re-investing in the brand makes sense, despite recent investor concern around operating leverage. Management has promised to keep operating costs under control. This seems achievable, particularly if done through proven marketing and merchandising strategies. Localized exhibitions to further showcase the brand's heritage could add further buzz. We believe gross margin headwinds for 1H27E were well explained as a function of inventory provisioning phasing, but acknowledge that believing management's ambition to return to $70\%$ GM% requires a little more faith in the interim. Ultimately, a return to sustainable mid-teens margins is a function of full-price top-line growth and retail space productivity, first and foremost. In this context, we believe incremental opportunities remain in a further rightsizing of Burberry's retail footprint.

## Investment Implications

We roll our forecasts forward to account for 1Q27 results. Our top-line forecasts are largely unchanged while our EBIT% forecasts fall by -70bps to 8.8% for FY27E. Lower GM% forecasts more than offset FX tailwinds. Thin near-term EBIT margins lead to an outsized -8.7% and -1.6% decline in our FY27/28E EPS forecasts. We continue to value Burberry on a target 1.7x relative P/E multiple to the MSCI Europe applied to a blended forward NTM+1 EPS forecast, equivalent to a 22x NTM+1 P/E, to arrive at a PT of 1,300GBp.

## DETAILS

## BUSINESS UPDATE

Growth by Nationality: Local customers drove growth across most regions

\- American customers: Strong growth supporting both domestic sales and international spending, with American tourists contributing to double-digit growth in London and Paris flagships.

\- Chinese customers: MSD growth remained broadly stable versus Q4, with spending driven by intra-region tourism (to HK and Macau). Localized marketing initiatives and locally relevant product launches continued to resonate with consumers.

\- European local customers Returning to positive growth this quarter (versus negative in 4Q26), although overall EMEA performance continued to be weighed down by weaker Asian tourist flows.

\- Middle Eastern local demand improved versus Q4; however, the region remained impacted by reduced tourism following the ongoing conflict.

Wholesale: H1 FY27 wholesale growth guidance was raised to high-single digits, from mid-single digits previously.

\- Strong partner demand across all regions reflects improving brand momentum.

\- Growth supported by stronger order books despite tighter distribution.

Retail: Traffic remained challenging, consistent with broader industry trends.

\- AUR / Pricing: AUR was slightly positive in the quarter, while higher full-price sell-through and lower markdown activity continued to support quality of sales.

• Strong conversion continued to offset weaker footfall.

\- Flagship stores remain particularly strong, with London and Paris delivering double-digit growth. Outlet villages, particularly in Europe, continue to face challenging traffic trends, with performance increasingly driven by the full-price channel.

Gross Margins: FY27 gross margin is expected to expand YoY.

\- Supported by higher full-price sell-through, lower markdown activity, and an improving quality of sales.

\- H1 GM% may be slightly lower YoY due to normalized inventory provisioning and tougher comparisons, not increased discounting.

\- Management reiterated its long-term ambition to return toward \~70% gross margin.

Opex: FY27 Opex is expected to remain broadly flat.

\- Marketing investment will be maintained to support brand momentum and customer acquisition.

\- Customer-facing investment is expected to be weighted towards the end of H1, reflecting preparation for the festive trading period.

FX: Based on 26 June spot rates.

\- Expected to provide a c.£20m revenue tailwind in FY27, versus a previously expected c.£10m revenue headwind.

\- Broadly neutral impact expected on adjusted operating profit, versus a previously expected c.£10m headwind.

Profitability: Management reiterated confidence in achieving FY27 EBIT expectations. Profitability remains H2 weighted, reflecting the seasonal nature of the business, higher H2 revenue contribution and normalized inventory provisioning in H1.

## BY DIVISION:

Outerwear: Delivered double-digit growth, remaining one of the strongest-performing categories.

\- Strong demand for heritage outerwear, tropical gabardine, lightweight jackets, and seasonal trench collections.

\- Management highlighted the successful extension of outerwear across all seasons, helping address previous seasonality concerns.

\- The Portraits of an Icon campaign continued to reinforce Burberry's authority and leadership in the category.

Ready-to-Wear: Growth across both womenswear and menswear.

\- Marks the first quarter in three years with positive growth across all major divisions.

\- Knitwear, polos, and swimwear were standout performers.

\- Polo Galleries (97 launched by Father's Day) continued to support customer engagement and improve store productivity.

Handbags: Continued to gain momentum, with management highlighting the category as an increasingly important growth driver.

\- Improvement across key families including Cotswold, House Check, and Horseshoe.

• Strong Mother's Day performance in the Americas supported double-digit category growth.

Scarves: Delivered strong growth, despite the summer seasonal backdrop.

\- Silk scarves performed particularly well.

Digital: E-commerce grew mid-teens, marking the eighth consecutive quarter of growth.

\- Younger customer acquisition and improved personalization continued to support momentum.

• Strongest growth recorded in the Americas.

Inventory: Management described inventory as being in the cleanest position in recent years.

\- Inventory build during FY27 reflects preparation for H2 seasonal demand.

## COMMENTARY BY GEOGRAPHY

## GREATER CHINA (+9% COMPARABLE STORE SALES GROWTH IN Q1 FY27)

\- Growth driven by local demand despite a mixed macro backdrop.

\- Double-digit growth in Gen Z customer acquisition supported momentum.

\- Localised marketing (e.g. Chinese National Geography partnership) and global campaigns continued to strengthen brand engagement.

\- Chinese customers increasingly shopping locally/regionally, supported by improved local assortments and exclusive capsule collections: Hong Kong and Macau benefited from stronger regional demand and duty-free channel performance.

\- Management continues to see market outperformance with momentum driven by local and intra-region spending.

## EMEIA (-3% COMPARABLE STORE SALES GROWTH IN Q1 FY27; -1% EX. MIDDLE EAST)

• Reported performance weighed by weaker Asian tourism and the ongoing Middle East conflict.

\- Excluding the Middle East, comparable sales declined only 1%.

\- Local customers returned to growth (vs. negative in Q4), while American tourist demand remained strong.

\- London and Paris flagships delivered double-digit growth, highlighting improving brand traction.

\- Outlet villages in Europe continued to experience weak traffic, with growth led by the full-price channel.

## AMERICAS (+12% COMPARABLE STORE SALES GROWTH IN Q1 FY27)

\- Strongest performing region, driven by local demand and broad-based customer acquisition.

\- Mother’s Day campaigns supported strong handbag sales and customer engagement.

\- E-commerce remained particularly strong, helping attract younger customers.

\- Management highlighted continued momentum from brand marketing and improved clienteling.

## ASIA PACIFIC EX. CHINA (+3% COMPARABLE STORE SALES GROWTH IN Q1 FY27)

\- South Korea remained a standout performer (+11%), supported by local and tourist demand.

\- Japan declined -2% as Chinese inbound tourism remained weak.

\- Regional growth remained positive despite continued tourism headwinds.
