## Recent anti-AI NIMBYism examples in the US:

\- No US state has actually banned AI datacenters.

\- June 2026: New York legislature passes 1-year statewide moratorium on large datacenters (20MW+).

\- AI datacenter moratoriums have been introduced in 14 states — Georgia, Maine, Maryland, Michigan, Minnesota, New Hampshire, New York, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, South Dakota, Vermont, Virginia and Wisconsin. Only the New York state legislature has passed the bill.

\- Most of the moratorium action is at the local/municipal level. Of 155 moratoriums tracked this year, cities accounted for 60, counties for 55 with townships and towns making up the rest.

\- Some more notable examples include Minneapolis (6-month pause on large facilities starting May 22nd), Denver (1-year moratorium passed May 18th), Baltimore (1-year moratorium signed into law June 16th), Reno (pause on new datacenters).

\- Stratos datacenter in Utah (the Kevin O'Leary project). Originally sized at 40k acres (2x the size of Manhattan) and requiring 9 GW of power (more than the entire state's power usage). Meeting local pushback around water usage, air quality and fire risk.

\- Given the growing opposition (in particular among younger voters) to Al more broadly, the issue has been federalized: Candidates in multiple states are running on this issue, and the pushback has grown louder (in some cases actually cancelling projects). That's been mirrored by the introduction of legislation toward these ends in Congress, from both Democrats and Republicans.

\- Our Thematics and US Public Policy Team (led by Michelle Weaver and Ariana Salvatore) recently published a comprehensive overview of AI NIMBYism with public policy views, including a tracker for data center projects and moratoriums.

## European pushback on AI datacenters is a bit more subtle than in the US:

\- Amsterdam/Netherlands: A moratorium on new datacenters set to run until at least 2030 with national rules restricting hyperscale projects.

\- Ireland: De facto moratorium has been in effect for years but was eased at the end of 2025 with strict conditions such as on-site power gen.

\- Denmark: Grid operator (Energinet) has a temporary 3-month pause on new

connections from March 2026 while they study the grid.

## Recent examples of large tech firms jumping on the 'space wagon':

\- Google (Project Suncatcher): Announced Nov 2025 with Planet Labs, planning prototype sats with TPUs for 2027.

\- NVIDIA: Launched Vera Rubin Space Module chips in March 2026 - 'space-qualified' chips for orbital AI data centers. See recent job posting: Principal Architect, System Software - Orbital Data Center | NVIDIA Corporation

\- Amazon: Expanding Amazon LEO (formerly Kuiper) constellation with AI/data center connectivity focus.

\- Blue Origin: Announced TeraWave constellation of around 5,400 sats in Jan 2026 for high-speed data center connectivity. Deployment to begin late 2027 and remains in planning phase.

\- Meta: Announced (Apr 2026) a deal to buy up to 1GW of power from startup Overview Energy to beam solar energy from satellites down to earth to help run Meta data centers.

## Chinese efforts for space-based AI infrastructure:

\- CASC: In Jan 2026, China's main aerospace contractor (China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation) announced plans to build GW-scale space-based AI data centers within 5 years.

\- 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030): China's national blueprint, approved by the NPC in March 2026, writes space-based AI compute into its space-infrastructure agenda, calling for gigawatt-scale orbital "digital and intelligent" infrastructure by 2030. This formalizes the target CASC first outlined in January 2026 and anchors the company-level efforts above.

\- Three-Body Computing Constellation: ADA Space (Guoxing Aerospace) and Zhejiang Lab launched the first 12 of a planned 2,800 satellites in May 2025 (5 POPS, 30 TB storage). In Nov 2025 they ran Alibaba's Qwen3 on the cluster.

\- Orbital Chenguang (Beijing Orbital Twilight Technology): A Beijing startup secured \$8.4bn in credit lines in April 2026 to build GW-scale orbital data centers.
