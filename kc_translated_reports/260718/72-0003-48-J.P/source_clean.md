## China Basic Materials

## June 26 NBS data: Resilient demand to support high metal prices and strengthen miners' earnings

Against a still-cautious domestic demand backdrop, as reflected in June activity data (link to report), we see a growing disconnect between resilient metals fundamentals and subdued equity expectations (MSCI China Materials Index - 14% MTD vs. HSCEI +1.4% MTD), while a less hawkish rate narrative could support a sector re-rating if expectations continue to moderate. Fundamentals remain broadly healthy in our preferred areas: copper supply remains tight; aluminum has been stronger than expected with strong exports and lower-than-expected Indonesian capacity additions; and lithium appears to have largely priced in CATL's Jianxiawo mine restart following land-use permit confirmation. By contrast, coal and steel remain weaker pockets, as Shanxi supply disruption was less severe than feared, hydro substitution weighed on coal prices, and higher raw-material costs continued to pressure steel margins. Recent positive 1H26 profit alerts across copper, gold, aluminum and lithium reinforce our confidence in the sector's earnings outlook (see Figure 1), although gold names appear to have faced higher-than-expected cost pressures in 2Q26. We maintain Zijin Mining as our top pick across copper and gold; in aluminum, we continue to favor Chalco and Hongqiao, where valuation discounts to global peers leave room for further re-rating; and in lithium, we are more positive on Ganfeng given its greater earnings visibility from battery exposure.

\- June 2026 aluminum production rose $4.7\%$ YoY; the LME-SHCJ spread narrowed. China's aluminum fundamentals remain constructive, with June production reaching 4.0mt, up $4.7\%$ YoY, and June exports rising $45\%$ YoY to 711kt; in 1H26, production totaled 23mt, up $3.8\%$ YoY, while exports reached 3.4mt, up $16.3\%$ YoY. Social inventory declined from a late-April peak of 1.47mt to 1.08mt in the second week of July, pointing to a still-favorable inventory backdrop despite elevated prices. LME aluminum pulled back to below US\$3,100/t in early July following the initial US-Iran agreement and the confirmed reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, but spot prices rebounded to US\$3,170/t in mid-July after Trump suspended the ceasefire agreement. SHFE aluminum also recovered to Rmb23,270/t as of July 14, narrowing the LME-SHFE premium to around Rmb881/t from a recent peak of approximately Rmb4,500/t. Looking ahead, we expect domestic aluminum prices to remain range-bound in 3Q, as the market gradually enters the seasonal soft period for downstream demand, which could slow the pace of inventory destocking, while slower-than-expected ramp-up of new Indonesian projects due to delayed power infrastructure should provide support. As we move into 4Q, however, we see increasing upward price risk, driven by a recovery in downstream demand and potential constraints on the pace of overseas capacity expansion by Chinese companies following the State Council's new ODI policy.

\- June raw coal output was down 10% YoY while imports rose 29% YoY. Raw coal output declined 10% YoY to 381mt in June, bringing 1H26 production to 2,365mt, down 2% YoY; raw coal imports rose significantly by 29% YoY to 42.8mt and 1H26 growth turned positive to 2% YoY. Domestic thermal coal prices have pulled back from the June peak, with Qinhuangdao 5,500kcal at around RMB800/t in mid-July (roughly RMB60/t below the June high). The decline was mainly driven by softer-than-expected domestic thermal coal demand, as the onset of the rainy season boosted hydropower generation and reduced coal burn. Meanwhile, the production impact following the Shanxi incident appears to have been more concentrated in regional coking coal (with 96mt of capacity still suspended as of July 8, according to Mysteel), while the implications for thermal coal and production outside Shanxi have been relatively limited. Looking ahead, we expect coal prices to stay soft over the next few months, mainly due to potentially higher supply (including increased Indonesian import availability) and the risk of a warmer winter, which could discourage aggressive restocking from late October.

\- June crude steel output was up 0.4% YoY, while finished steel exports increased 7% YoY. China's crude steel production totaled 84mt in June, flat YoY and +3% MoM, bringing 1H26 cumulative output to 500mt, down 3% YoY. Entering June, steel production showed little meaningful sequential change, with CISA key members' daily crude steel output at 2.02mt (-4.9% YoY and -3.6% MoM) and month-to-date average daily molten iron production flat at 2.4mt/day. Finished steel exports reached 10.3mt in June, up 7% YoY but flat MoM, compared with an average YoY decline of 6% in 1H26. Yet, we expect slowing overseas purchasing and price cuts in Southeast Asian markets to add pressure to exports in the coming quarter. Margins weakened in mid-July versus June across HRC, rebar and CRC, mainly due to higher domestic coking coal and iron ore prices, with the share of profitable mills falling from 56% in mid-June to 40% in the second week of July. We believe steelmakers' profitability is unlikely to materially improve without meaningful policy follow-through, a sustained improvement in domestic demand, and a gradual easing in input costs.

\- Lithium: Support near RMB150k/t; range-bound with news-driven volatility. June 2026 China auto production declined $3.2\%$ YoY, while NEV production increased $29.4\%$ YoY (vs. $+0.9\%$ YoY in 5M26), supported by oil-price tailwinds. As of July 9, lithium carbonate weekly inventories fell to 92kt (SMM old sample) and 124kt (SMM new sample). In recent weeks, price action has remained largely supply-news driven: lithium carbonate spot and futures prices retraced from RMB 190k+/t in mid-May to \~RMB 160k/t in mid-June, as global mine restarts more than offset downgrades elsewhere (link to report), while concerns over domestic “invisible” inventories persisted. The official receipt of CATL’s Jianxiawo mine land-use permit pushed lithium prices toward \~RMB 150k/t; however, the expected restart date and volume contribution have yet to be confirmed. Looking ahead, we expect prices to hover around current levels, with elevated news-driven volatility as the market refocuses on supply-demand fundamentals. On the supply side, June–July Zimbabwe concentrate arrivals and CATL restart momentum are key swing factors, while elevated auto inventories and seasonal EV softness may cap near-term upside until stronger seasonal battery production.
