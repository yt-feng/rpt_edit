## China Quant Strategy

## Growth While It Lasts

On Friday, by coincidence, both the Fed (1Q26 Z.1 flow-of-funds) and the PBOC (May TSF) released the underlying data from which we construct our US and China credit impulses. Read together, they argue for a constructive lean toward Growth over Value.

US/China credit data is one of the macro lenses we monitor. We view credit impulse as a proxy for the risky component of the discount rate, to which the pricing of Growth names is highly sensitive. A simple US/China averaged credit-impulse signal (53% hit-rate, +9.9% p.a. in our Growth vs. Value backtest since 2018) currently remains OW Growth, though with materially less conviction than three months ago.

The Friday prints pull in opposite directions. The 1Q26 US data marks another step-up in US private-sector credit creation, with the US impulse rising to +2.5 pp from +1.6 pp. Against this, China's May TSF was the third consecutive month of slowing, and the China impulse has now slipped to -0.4 pp. The result is an aggregate that is still trending higher, but only marginally above its 6-mth average.
