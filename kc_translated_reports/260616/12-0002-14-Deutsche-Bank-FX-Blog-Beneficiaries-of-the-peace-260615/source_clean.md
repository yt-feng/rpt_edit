# Beneficiaries of the peace
## Benefiriciaries of the US - Iran peace deal

Here are the top currencies which we think have most scope to rally on the back of a US - Iran peace deal. To put it simply, the more supertankers can start moving through the Strait of Hormuz full of oil and the quicker they do so, the more these currencies should rally:

SEK: It is high-beta to EUR, an energy importer, and pro-cyclical to global growth. A deal in the Middle East leaves the domestic economy well placed to outperform European peers in the second half of the year

ZAR: An unwind in oil risk premia, potential rebound in gold, high real-rate buffer and still-improving domestic backdrop should all help.

CLP: The currency has been one of the biggest under-performers versus its aggregate terms of trade. With oil receding and copper remaining elevated, we see scope for the current account deficit to close by year end.

INR: High sensitivity to oil prices and a market that is positioned short. RBI policy measures will support both near-term inflows and longer-term structural demand.

Longs on all of these currencies can be pared against the USD or the EUR depending on one's dollar and Fed view by the end of the year. Our EUR/USD view is neutral at the moment so we prefer a funding mix of both.


## G10: SEK

Our top trade in G10 FX is to re-engage with USDSEK shorts. We have had sympathy with the krona trading as a higher-beta version of the euro since the start of March, given the pro-cyclical and mild energy importing nature of the domestic economy. However, there is now scope for some krona catch-up, which at the time of writing is still the weakest developed market currency against the dollar since the conflict began. On the domestic front, growth this year could still be above 2% thanks to fiscal stimulus, and evidence of Sweden benefitting from higher global defence spending continues to flow. The rate market is not priced too hawkishly as to present a major risk to entering longs, especially with our positioning indicators showing a net short in the krona. Positive news regarding a Russia-Ukraine ceasefire and potential Ukrainian reconstruction is another potential positive external catalyst over the summer.

## CEEMEA: ZAR

Our preferred expression in CEEMEA is to be long ZAR. The currency has remained resilient throughout the conflict and is now set to benefit from lower oil prices as well as high real-rates and still-improving domestic backdrop. While lower oil prices will immediately benefit the rand, it may also benefit from renewed access to fertilizer and tourism flows, which may also be currency-supportive. A potential recovery in gold prices may also help. The domestic story also remains supportive. Growth and fiscal expectations have seen upward revisions, politics has remained relatively benign, and model-based estimates continue to suggest the rand is cheap. At the same time, the SARB has a strong incentive to retain a hawkish bias given the shift towards a lower inflation target, leaving ZAR with a meaningful real-rate advantage.

## LatAm: CLP

We recently flagged that CLP outperformance was being held back by higher oil prices. Indeed, if oil prices continue to recede and copper prices remain elevated, we expect the current account to close by year end, which would clearly be CLP positive. In addition to this, both positioning and valuations remain supportive of the currency. While shorts have been partially pared, CLP still screens as the best value currency in LatAm, leaving scope for further appreciation as the external backdrop improves.

## Asia: INR

First, India stands out as a key terms-of-trade beneficiary from lower oil prices. Second, while the long-USD positioning unwind has indeed already started – as our EPIC+ tracking duly notes – we have seen only about a third of the total unwound so far, as shown by our BoP-based position tracking. Third, the RBI has taken forceful measures that are likely to attract short term inflows and improve sentiment on the currency (see The kitchen sink, and then some), even as its longer-term bond market supportive measures should increase the likelihood of Euroclear settlement and/or Global Agg index inclusion, with prospects of stickier longer-term inflow.
