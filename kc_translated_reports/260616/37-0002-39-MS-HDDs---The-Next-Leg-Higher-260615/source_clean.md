## HDDs – The Next Leg Higher


Our Asia checks from the last 3 weeks make clear the HDD cycle is elongating – with greater shortages now expected through at least CY28 – while also illustrating that HDD pricing is clearly, and meaningfully, strengthening. Raising ests/PTs meaningfully. STX PT to \$1,035; WDC to \$650.

## Key Takeaways

Our checks from Asia over the last 3 weeks highlight strengthening (and broadening) HDD demand and the potential for material pricing upside.
We believe HDD demand is growing 40-50% annually while supply (EB) growth is closer to 30-35%. We are hearing of shortages through at least CY28.
- Current nearline price/TB is <\$15. Our Taiwan checks indicate HDD vendors are targeting \$25-30 Price/TB over the next 2-3 years.
Despite our base case forecasts being far more conservative than our checks (i.e., <\$20/TB pricing) our FY28 EPS ests are now \~70% above Street for STX and WDC.
STX still 'Top Pick' given greater relative margin expansion oppt'y in next 3-6mo, but pounding the table on both our STX and WDC Overweights.

The drivers of HDD demand are broadening, visibility is elongating, pricing is strengthening, and our new bull cases are coming closer into play – STX and WDC remain our most-favored Overweights. We have learned 2 important pieces of information while spending time in Asia over the last 3 weeks (2 in Taiwan, 1 with WDC mgmt) that build conviction in our bullish HDD industry call – (1) HDD demand is strengthening due to core cloud services outperformance + growing inferencing/agents demand, resulting in even greater HDD supply shortages than we had expected through at least CY28; and (2) stronger demand, higher capacity drive introductions, and parabolic NAND price hikes are leaving HDD vendors with greater conviction to meaningfully increase prices over the next 2-3 years – though in a more controlled/predictable way than memory peers. When combined, we believe that the market continues to underestimate the longevity of this HDD cycle, as well as the potential earnings power of both STX and WDC. In fact, not only are our base case FY28 EPS estimates now 70% above consensus – if we were to take the most bullish of our pricing checks as truth, both STX and WDC could 10x EPS between CY25 and CY28. ## What we've learned about HDDs over the last 3 weeks during our Taiwan AI Summit, COMPUTEX, and WDC Asia Roadshow. A few very important, new datapoints:

- HDD vendors are limiting their use of 12+ month LTAs to capture more favorable commercial terms. While hyperscalers are asking for LTAs out to 2032, HDD vendors are less willing to sign deals this long. Why? Because applications for HDDs / cold storage are broadening, shortages are growing, the TCO vs. NAND is significantly improving, and HDD replacement technology remains non-existent. To be clear, STX/WDC want to provide their customers with 5 years of visibility on capacity and technology introductions, but are just less eager to attach commercial terms 12+ months out.

We want to put some of these pricing data points in context. We, by no means, want to minimize checks that point to a strengthening HDD demand environment. But the HDD vendors are very disciplined on supply (i.e., no greenfield capacity buildouts), and thus nearline EB growth over the next 2-3 years will be dictated by supply, which we believe is growing at a 30-35% CAGR. Conversely, we want to be very clear on exactly how impactful these pricing checks could be to STX/WDC's financial model. This is because any incremental pricing upside is effectively all profit (tax effected). As seen in Exhibit 1 and Exhibit 2 below, if we were to assume blended nearline price per TB reached \$25 in CY27 and \$30 in CY28 – in line with our Taiwan checks – STX could generate >\$70 of EPS in CY27 and >\$100 of EPS in CY28, while WDC could do >\$40 of EPS in CY27 and >\$70 of EPS in CY28. For reference, STX/WDC did \$10.24 / \$6.95 of EPS in CY25, implying a 10x-ing of EPS over 3 years. To be clear, we don't want to imply this is the market base case today – our new CY27 STX base case EPS is \$51, while our CY27 WDC base case EPS if \$32. But if these checks prove accurate, our bull case valuations of \~\$1,446 for STX and \$920 for WDC are clearly in play.

Furthermore, HDDs are in the midst of a 10-year technology transition, and the risk of new entrants is limited, in our conversations. Outside of a reversal in cloud capex – which is a risk much broader than HDDs – the most significant risk to our HDD forecasts would be a new entrant that disrupts the powerful, rational HDD oligopoly. During our time with WDC management last week, CEO Irving Tan contextualized this risk for us, explaining that the challenges with becoming competitive as a new entrant are not about capital, but rather: (1) the time it takes to move to the next generation leading-edge node – it's taken over 10 years to qualify HAMR; (2) the ability to internalize/vertically integrate technology across disparate fields of knowledge such as magnetics, material science, photonics, nanofabrication, etc.; and (3) a lack of broad supply chain capacity – the HDD supply chain is very consolidated with limited under-utilization today. Mr. Tan noted Chinese players have tried to create cold-storage alternatives recently (Huawei's Magneto-Electric Disk, for example) but found little success. Overall, we feel comfortable with this risk today.

How we think about valuation today – our new base and bull case EPS estimates imply HDD stocks could still double from here. On our CY27 base case EPS, STX and WDC currently trade at 18x and 17x P/E, respectively. For a >75% 3-year EPS CAGR (FY26-FY29), we don't believe valuation is demanding, even if clearly a premium to history (HDDs also didn't post >50% GMs historically either). However, on our new bull case CY27 EPS, STX and WDC trade at just 11.5x P/E, implying these stocks haven't really re-rated despite material EPS revisions over the last 18 months. Given the upside we see to consensus EPS – we're 70% above consensus in our FY28 base case EPS – which imply recent quarterly EPS beats sustain or even get larger, and the potential for our bull case forecasts to become a reality, we continue to believe HDDs represent the cleanest estimate revision and re-rating story within our IT Hardware coverage.

Our latest views on STX vs. WDC. We are Overweight both stocks and see them as our most-preferred OWs within our IT Hardware coverage. That said, we have a tactical bias towards STX, as we see: (1) the ability to re-price early HAMR customers in the September '26 quarter; and (2) the ability to cost down slightly faster than WDC in C2H26 given a higher cost/TB starting point as creating more material gross margin tailwinds for STX vs. WDC over the next 3-6 months. This is why STX is our current 'Top Pick'. However, on a longer-term basis, WDC screens as modestly more attractive given: (1) greater relative upside to our bull case valuation; and (2) a slightly less-levered balance sheet. In reality, we believe clients would benefit from exposure to each stock.

What are the most notable risks to owning HDDs at this point? The key risks to

HDDs remain: (1) the durability and magnitude of this cycle; and (2) the potential for new entrants. On the former, our Asia checks give us comfort in HDD utilization/ customer inventory, and the broadening of demand + growing supply shortages suggest there is durability to this cycle. On the latter, as we discussed above, we see limited risk of new entrants to the HDD market, and/or of a strategic consortium propping up the distant 3rd player in HDDs. However, these remain the two areas we continue to track most closely as we constantly stress-test our theses.
