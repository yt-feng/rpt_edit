## More JPY Weakness to Come Without a Shift in the Macro Backdrop

The Japanese Yen recently traded to its weakest level versus the US Dollar in 40 years, keeping the MoF focused on the exchange rate and seemingly ready to conduct JPY-buying operations again. In fact, the latest reports suggest that the MoF may end the warnings ahead of official intervention to further discourage short positioning. But the broader macro backdrop of higher-for-longer US yields, low recession risk, lingering fiscal concerns, and only gradual BoJ hikes strongly argues for continued depreciation pressure on the currency. That explains the quick return to an upward trend in USD/JPY within weeks of the last operations, echoing the aftermath of the April 2024 intervention and implying a similar outcome if additional operations occur soon.

The trend higher in USD/JPY over the past year has been highly correlated with rising term premium in JGB yields relative to US Treasury yields. Prior to 2025, shifts in real rate differentials between the US and Japan generally explained USD/JPY performance well (Exhibit 1). But the typically tight, positive beta became less reliable about a year ago, following the LDP's defeat (under PM Ishiba) in the Upper House elections last July.

Indeed, we find that in weeks since July 2025 when Japan term premium rose relative to the US, USD/JPY rallied by roughly 0.35% on average—and closer to 0.60% on average when the move was greater than 1 standard deviation in both Japan term premium and the differential (Exhibit 3). $^{1}$

Intervention can slow the move and buy time for a potential shift in the macro that then leads to sustained Yen appreciation. Indeed, various forms of intervention this year have limited the upside in USD/JPY to some extent. However, we have long argued that intervention can only have a short-lived impact if the macro fundamentals continue to push in the other direction, such as the recent widening of rate differentials in the Dollar's favor as the US economy has looked more resilient than expected. The strong case for further JPY depreciation pressure likely also explains why the April intervention had less “bang for its buck” relative to other rounds of intervention in recent years (Exhibit 4).

We think that the latest unofficial interventions and reported desire within the MoF to further limit speculative activity suggest that there could be smaller, more frequent interventions going forward, as was more common in the 1990s. That can successfully reset the exchange rate to lower levels and suppress vol for some time, especially if done without warning. But the underlying sources of JPY weakness remain and should still dominate over time, despite the Yen's extreme undervaluation versus the Dollar on our estimates. Market concern about inflationary / fiscal pressure in Japan on the back of the government's stimulus plans and only gradual BoJ hikes, should continue to push up term premium in JGB yields relative to US Treasury yields. As a result, we think the trend higher in USD/JPY should extend, barring a negative US growth shock or a BoJ pivot towards more aggressive policy tightening—neither of which appears likely over the coming year. Therefore, we are revising up our USD/JPY forecast path to 162, 163, 165 (vs. 160, 158, 155 previously). We continue to favor using JPY as a funder for high-carry EM expressions, alongside other low-yielding G10 currencies.
