# Powering Up Europe: Electrification keeps driving power demand higher

Following a +c.1.5% increase in power demand across Europe in 2025, year to date consumption continues to trend positively – demand is up by around +2% on average across Spain, Germany, Italy, the UK and Portugal. We believe the pick-up in electrification is the main driver underpinning this. We identify three key factors driving the acceleration in the electrification process across Europe: (1) Heat pumps, which help electrify buildings. Last year in Europe, some 2.5 mn heat pumps were installed; electrifying heating doubles the consumption of a typical household. (2) Electric cars: in Europe more than 2 mn EVs were sold last year (representing a near 20% share of new car sales); switching a family’s primary car for an EV would also double consumption for that household. (3) Manufacturing: electric boilers generate steam (up to 400°C) at better economics than any gas boiler equivalent.

Electrification and AI to drive a generational earnings super-cycle. The likely acceleration in the electrification process (energy security), together with rising AI adoption rates and the continued datacenter build-out, should drive materially higher power consumption. Our hyper-electrification scenario points to +5% annual demand by 2029-30. This would drive €3.5 trn of investment needs in power generation (largely renewables) and power grids, thus supporting a generational earnings super-cycle (see here for details). Several companies have also highlighted an ability to extract AI-driven cost efficiencies, which could provide an additional tailwind to the sector's organic growth.

## Favour transformative electrification stories, renewables, and energy security infrastructure providers. We see three clusters of companies that we favour: (1)

Transformative electrification stories include companies where electrification capex is about to meaningfully accelerate (Naturgy, Enel and Engie), significantly transforming these portfolios on a 3-5 year basis. (2) Renewable developers that would benefit from rising returns and organic growth opportunities (RWE, Solaria, Orsted); and RES manufacturers that would benefit from rising orders and margins (eg Vestas and Nordex). (3) Energy security infrastructure providers, such as ENR, EON and Snam.
