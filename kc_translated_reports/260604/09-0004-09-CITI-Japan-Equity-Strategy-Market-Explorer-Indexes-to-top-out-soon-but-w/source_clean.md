# CITI'S TAKE

Japanese equities have performed solidly over the past month, despite short-term corrections. Supporting factors have included: 1) hopes for a US-Iran ceasefire agreement and a peak in crude oil prices, 2) the continuation of the global AI and semiconductor boom, and 3) initial company guidance shown in earnings announcements remaining at normal levels of conservatism. Given that not only the Nikkei 225, driven by the tech boom, but also TOPIX recently hit YTD highs, and that earnings revision deterioration is expected in the immediate future, Japanese equities may peak for a while in the short term. However, we conclude that upside potential toward year-end remains significant and that there is no need to change our bullish fundamental view on Japanese equities.

FY26 company plans demonstrate earnings resilience — Japanese companies' FY26 earnings plans as revealed in full-year results suggest firms are anticipating +4.2% YoY revenue growth and +7.6% NP growth (TOPIX basis). We view this as strong guidance given uncertainties around Middle East conditions and widespread cost increases. While profit plan misses may lead to deterioration in earnings forecast revisions in the short term, creating a headwind for stock prices, any deterioration in forecast revisions is likely to be modest given that company plans show normal levels of conservatism. Meanwhile, if earnings beats in high-tech sectors/stocks and margin improvements from pricing pass-throughs support overall earnings, actual corporate earnings are likely to exceed company plans and consensus estimates, with earnings revisions turning upward again.

Japanese equity valuations still have room to rise — We believe margin improvements in an inflationary environment will push up Japanese equity valuations via higher RoE. While TOPIX's 12-month forward PER currently stands at 16.8x, near the upper end of its range over the past decade-plus, we see room for further PER expansion as the average RoE sets its sights on the 11%-12% range. Additionally, 1) globally accommodative financial conditions are keeping global liquidity ample, 2) the Japanese equity market is transforming into an investable market for investors as companies with low RoE that are below the cost of capital fall in number, and 3) Japanese equities still appear relatively undervalued based on yield spreads, which should encourage fund flows from overseas into Japanese equities and support valuations. Based on the above, we set our TOPIX target at 4,500 by applying a PER of 17.5x to FY27 forecast EPS (¥258.4).

"Bad rate rises" unlikely to occur in Japan — From mid to late May, stocks with high valuations, centered on tech, sold off as Japanese long-term rates rose significantly, leading to a short-term correction in Japanese equities overall. However, these moves ended quickly and did not lead to a sustained correction in Japanese equities. We believe "bad rate rises" are unlikely to occur in the Japanese equity market as 1) value stock weightings are high, 2) the economy originally had deflationary tendencies, and 3) the BoJ has an easing bias. However, real rates have been gradually rising recently. If concerns about fiscal deterioration strengthen further and long-term rates rise led by real rates, approaching or exceeding potential growth rates, this could turn into a "bad rate rise."

Continuation of a momentum market is our base case — Under conditions where crude oil prices remain firm and inflationary pressures persist to some degree, we expect mild stagflation, with relatively high inflation coexisting with economic stagnation, to continue. In this environment, our base case is that stocks with independent growth factors not dependent on macroeconomic cycles (“theme” stocks, restructuring stocks, or inflation beneficiaries) will continue to outperform. While buying is currently concentrated in tech among momentum names, if uncertainties around Middle East conditions fall away going forward, buying is likely to spread to lagging momentum stocks (construction, real estate, financials, defense-related, energy). Additionally, while not a momentum sector, automobiles, which have continued to underperform due to bottleneck concerns, appear to have real room for short-term rebounds.

# Japanese stocks: firm despite adjustments

Over the last month, Japanese stocks have been resilient, maintaining a firm trend despite some market adjustments. This stability has been supported by several key factors: 1) anticipation of a ceasefire in the Middle East, coupled with a peak in crude oil prices; 2) the ongoing global boom in AI and semiconductors continues to support strong momentum; and 3) the market has taken the generally conservative initial FY3/27 guidance issued by firms alongside full-year FY3/26 results as a positive. Both the Nikkei, heavily influenced by the tech sector, and the broader TOPIX index have recently hit new YTD highs. While a near-term slowdown is possible due to expected guidance cuts, we think the overall potential for growth toward the end of the year remains strong. We see no reason to alter our bullish long-term outlook on Japanese stocks.

# FY3/27 company plans demonstrate corporate earnings resilience

The FY3/27 earnings plans presented by Japanese companies at full-year results collectively aim for revenue growth of +4.2% YoY and NP growth of +7.6% on a TOPIX basis. We view these plans as strong given the headwinds, including Middle East uncertainties and widespread cost increases (Figure 1). We interpret the following as demonstrating the resilience of Japanese corporate earnings overall: 1) downside in company estimates versus prior consensus remained at historical average levels despite a highly uncertain macro environment (Figure 2), 2) impressive earnings growth at AI and semiconductor-related stocks, where global strength continues, are pushing up overall earnings performance (Figures 3, 4), and 3) many companies unveiled sales plans that were ahead of prior consensus, confirming their stance on forging ahead with price pass-throughs (Figures 1, 5).

# Near-term risk of revision deterioration exists but is not enough to change the Japanese equity trend

Downside to profit plans may lead to deterioration in earnings forecast revisions in the near term, potentially weighing on stock prices (Figures 6, 7). However, given that company plans are about as conservative as they are in a typical year, deterioration in earnings forecast revisions will likely remain modest. Meanwhile, if earnings beats in high-tech sectors/stocks (Figure 8) and margin improvements through progress with price pass-throughs support overall earnings, actual corporate results will likely exceed plans and consensus, with earnings forecast revisions turning upward again (Figure 9). If deterioration in earnings forecast revisions remains modest and short-lived, it should not get in the way of the Japanese equity uptrend.

# Japanese firms strengthening their ability to smoothly implement price pass-throughs

The company plans presented at full-year results saw a relatively high frequency of sales plan beats and profit plan misses, similar to FY3/23 and FY3/24, when firms were alert to cost increases (Figure 10). We interpret this as firms demonstration of their determination to implement some price pass-throughs while being cautious about whether they can fully pass on higher costs. However, Japanese firms have recently strengthened their ability to more smoothly implement price increases in response to cost increases than in the past, with corporate and consumer price sensitivity to import prices rising (Figure 11). In FY3/23-FY3/24, we saw a pattern of cost increases whereby price hikes led to margin improvement (Figure 12), suggesting guidance that assume implementation of price increases but deterioration in margins is probably too conservative. In 2022, significant import price increases led to deterioration in the margin outlook through mid-year followed by improvement. Given that corporate price pass-throughs have become easier than before and future import price increases are not expected to match 2022 levels (Figure 13), we believe margin deterioration will end more quickly this time. Based on the above, we anticipate double-digit profit growth for Japanese companies in FY26 and FY27 (Figure 14).

# Japanese stocks still have upside valuation potential

Improvement in margins in an inflationary environment could push up Japanese stock valuations by way of increased RoE. TOPIX trades on a 12-month-forward PER of 16.8x, close to the top of the 10-year historical range (Figure 15), and we see further upside potential for PER (Figure 16) given the emerging possibility of the average RoE passing the 11% mark. We also think factors like 1) plentiful global liquidity stemming from the maintenance of monetary accommodation across the globe (Figure 17), 2) the Japanese market being increasingly seen as investable as the number of firms with low RoE (below the cost of capital) decreases (Figure 18), and 3) Japanese stocks continuing to look relatively attractively valued on metrics like yield spreads (Figure 19) will encourage inflows of overseas capital into Japanese stocks, which would support valuations (Figure 20). In light of these factors, we raise our projected high for TOPIX to 4,500, based on an FY27 EPS forecast of ¥258.4 and a PER of 17.5x.

# We expect the N/T ratio to remain high

The N/T ratio (Nikkei 225 to TOPIX ratio) is marking record highs due to substantial gains by Al/semiconductor-related sectors and stocks (Figures 21, 22). We saw the same kind of tech-driven rise in the N/T ratio in September-October 2025. On that occasion, the N/T ratio declined after TOPIX caught up when a Nikkei 225 correction resulted from a shift to high-tech underperformance from November. However, high-tech sectors now look less overheated than last November, and we do not see a significant correction risk (Figures 23, 24). We therefore conclude that the N/T ratio will remain high and estimate a high of 72,000 for the Nikkei 225 (equal to an N/T ratio of 16x).

# "Bad rate rises" are less likely to materialize in Japan

From mid-to-late May, a significant rise in long-term interest rates prompted a sell-off in high-valuation stocks, especially in the technology sector, causing a brief market-wide adjustment. The adjustment proved temporary, however, and no prolonged market correction ensued. Given higher risk-free rates, long-term rate rises are generally considered valuation compressors for equities. Figure 25 maps out the relationship of long-term rate rises → PER declines, long-term rate declines or sideways moves → PER rises in the case of US equities. Figure 26, meanwhile, shows the strong tendency not just recently but also over the long term in Japanese equities for long-term rates and PER to correlate positively. We see three reasons for the seemingly counterintuitive relationship between Japanese equities and long-term rates: 1) Japanese equities have a lower weighting for growth (high valuation) stocks and a higher weighting for value stocks relative to US equities (Figure 27); 2) while US long-term rate movements are driven primarily by changes in real rates (nominal rates minus inflationary expectations, with longer-term inflationary expectations stable), Japanese long-term rates tend to be strongly influenced by changes in inflationary expectations (Figures 28-31); 3) in Japan there is a bias toward monetary policy easing over the long term, so even if real rates rise somewhat they tend to remain at accommodative levels below potential growth (Figures 32, 33).

Based on the above summary, we believe "bad rate rises" are unlikely in Japanese equities as 1) the value stock weighting is high, 2) the economy has inherent deflationary tendencies, and 3) the BoJ has a bias toward easing. Nevertheless, real rates in Japan have been gradually rising recently, reaching around 25bps on a 10y basis (Figure 10). If fiscal deterioration concerns intensify further and long-term rates rise, led by real rates, approaching or exceeding levels suggested by potential growth, this could turn into a "bad rate rise". A more concerning scenario would

involve the 10y Japanese government bond yield rising above 3% due to an increase in real interest rates. However, the government is currently taking a cautious fiscal approach, for example, by limiting the supplementary budget to the amount available in contingency funds. We believe this suggests the immediate materialization of such risk is low.

# Continuation of momentum-driven market is our base scenario

With crude oil prices slow to retreat and inflationary pressure still in play to some extent, we think conditions are conducive to ongoing mild stagflation, characterized by relatively high inflation coupled with a sluggish economy (Figures 34, 35). The general consensus in such conditions is that stocks benefiting from growth factors independent of the macroeconomic cycle (like theme stocks, restructuring plays, or inflation beneficiaries) will continue to perform well. At present, a focus on high tech is apparent even among momentum stocks, but we think investor interest could spread to other, laggard momentum stocks (construction, real estate, finance, defense, energy) if the uncertainty surrounding the situation in the Middle East eases. We also see real potential for a short-term rally in autos, which, although not a momentum sector, has been consistently underperforming due to concerns about bottlenecks. # Focus on companies with sales plan beats and profit plan misses versus consensus

While more companies came out with sales plan beats and profit plan misses versus consensus in these results than usual, their stock prices have sold off similarly to stocks where both sales and profit plans fell below consensus. As noted above, if these companies ultimately avoid margin deterioration through price pass-through and see profits beat, they likely have significant future potential. Indeed, the FY3/23 experience showed companies with sales plan beats and profit plan misses underperformed around results before recovering (Figure 37). Of course, the time required for input cost increases to be passed through to end-prices varies by industry, with B2B generally having shorter time lags than B2C (Figures 38, 39). We consider it appropriate to view B2B stocks with high profit beat potential as promising in the near term, and then we recommend a switch to B2C later.
