## Equity Strategy

- At the overall equity level, we continue to recommend to use the dips caused by adverse geopolitical news to add into, but have also advised two weeks ago to start looking at Low Vol part of the market - see report. We argued that this group of stocks is likely to find support irrespective of where bond yields go from here, ie, even if they move up. Further into $2^{\text{nd}}$ half, the wildcards for more market gains are the prospect of a turn dovish by Fed, something which is right now clearly very much out of consensus, materially lower oil prices by this time next year, and potential green shoots in China.
- European indices, both SX5E and SXXP, have rebounded nicely from the Iran conflict driven lows, to currently trade near the highs of the year. The question is what would be needed for these to make a clear breakout in 2H.
- Relatively, the Iran conflict has likely hurt European prospects more than other regions, and our call in March to go back to Mag-7 is clearly at the disadvantage of Europe. Near term, Iran conflict retains a potential to escalate, even as we believe both sides have an incentive to come to terms eventually. As the top chart shows, Eurozone has lagged since the conflict began, and this might not change imminently.
- Having said that, as we move through 2H, we think Europe is set to break out to fresh highs, we keep SX5E end year target of 6350, set last November in our Year Ahead, and believe that the region could even show spells of outperformance, especially if Mag-7 shows more sustained rollovers, and once Iran conflict risks subside.
- Beyond AI trade and geopolitics considerations, ECB is likely to tighten this week, but we do not expect this to lead to a series of hikes, nothing along the lines of what transpired in 2022. Then, European real yields were record low, opposite to current levels, so ECB had to catch up. This is not the case at present, and multiple hikes might not be warranted given soft activity and oil price impact on inflation that could mechanically drop out quickly, with wage growth that keeps moving lower. Increasing confidence that rates and yields are not likely to spike could help the region perform.
- Eurozone earnings didn't grow at the headline level for 3 years. That is set to change, especially if the Iran situation improves, as then the present step back in Eurozone PMIs should recover quickly. The relative Eurozone-US activity spread is near wides, such as 100 point plus CESI differential. The drivers of this divergence are real and well known, but this could be as extreme as it gets. On the positive side, despite PMI softness, regional credit growth is improving, and car sales are up for 3 months now.
- Eurozone positioning is subdued, much like it was last January, just ahead of the rally - see middle chart. Eurozone forward P/E at 15x is far from demanding, especially if earnings perform better. Our PB data shows investor positioning in the region is low, at $36^{\text{th}}$ percentile. If China performs better, Europe could benefit. Some increased traction on German stimulus would help, and any improvement on the Russia - Ukraine front, as well.

Looking beyond the near-term volatility, we remain constructive on equities at the overall market level. Global markets have staged a sharp rebound from the geopolitical trough triggered by the Iran escalation, pushing the MSCI World to new highs with gains of roughly 12% so far this year. This has been supported by corporates continuing to beat on earnings, hyperscaler AI spending is providing a structural growth impulse, yields have stayed within a manageable range, and long-term inflation expectations remain well-anchored. Beyond our base case, there are several factors that could drive further upside. Notably, a more pronounced decline in government bond yields, a significant drop in crude prices over the next twelve months, or early signs of a Chinese economic recovery gaining traction.

## European indices are near highs

Figure 4: Euro Stoxx 50 ytd

[[KC_IMAGE_007]]


Source: Bloomberg Finance L.P.

European indices have rebounded nicely from the Iran conflict driven lows. The Euro Stoxx 50 is currently trading near the highs of the year, having recovered the entirety of the 11% drawdown experienced in March. Similarly, the Stoxx 600 is within 2% of its all-time high. The question investors are asking is what would be needed for these indices to make a clear breakout to fresh highs. We believe the answer lies in a combination of factors including easing geopolitical uncertainty, confirmation that ECB tightening will be limited, and an inflection in Eurozone earnings momentum.

Figure 5: MSCI Eurozone vs US (\$) ytd

[[KC_IMAGE_008]]


Source: Datastream

The Iran conflict has disproportionately weighed on European equities relative to other regions, and our March pivot back toward Mag-7 has come at Europe's expense. Since late February, the Eurozone has lagged the US by roughly $11\%$ in dollar terms. In the near term, we continue to see greater upside potential in Mag-7, and while we believe both parties are ultimately incentivised to reach a resolution, the risk of further escalation in the Iran situation remains live. As such, this relative underperformance of Europe versus the US is unlikely to reverse in the immediate term.

A key difference we have been flagging between now and 2022 is the wage backdrop. Going into 2022, wage growth was picking up sharply. Combined with Covid-era supply disruptions and tight labour supply, this drove inflation higher and forced central banks into aggressive rate hikes.

Higher oil prices will mechanically push up inflation and we have already seen US headline CPI tick higher. But it is not obvious that central banks need to hike rates in response to what is essentially a demand-destructive shock. Our medium-term view is still that yields are more likely to head lower. Against that backdrop, we see an opportunity in the low-volatility trade: prior underperformance has created a better entry point, the setup should hold up across a wide range of macro outcomes, and it does not depend on market direction.

Markets have priced in approximately 70bp of ECB tightening since the Iran conflict began. We believe this hawkish repricing is set to partially unwind. The oil price impact on headline inflation is a supply shock, not a demand-pull phenomenon, and hikes risk being viewed as a policy mistake. Any evidence of a more measured ECB approach could serve as a positive catalyst for European equities.

## German fiscal spending has been weak so far, but the pipeline is building

German infrastructure spending has disappointed in the early stages. Through April, only €11 billion of the €40 billion earmarked for 2026 has been deployed (roughly 28% of the annual allocation). Last year was similar with authorities spending €24 billion of a planned €37 billion. The shortfalls span across broadband expansion, rail network upgrades and broader transport infrastructure.

The shortfall occurred mainly because the planning process is lengthy, bureaucracy is entrenched, and the 2025 election cycle delayed the legislative framework from taking effect until late last year. However, these are now being addressed. Merz's government has introduced fast-track legislation for projects of overriding public interest, and the Finance Ministry has explicitly urged departments to accelerate disbursements.

Figure 27: German federal budgetary outturns

Source: JPM.

Early signs of acceleration are starting to emerge. By April, the cumulative federal deficit was already €18 billion wider than at the same point last year, which is already half of the full year widening implied by the 2026 budget. The fiscal plan is targeting a €98 billion deficit in 2026 versus an actual €65 billion deficit in 2025, implying a planned widening of €33 billion. 2026 trajectory is beginning to diverge meaningfully from prior years.

The broader fiscal picture reinforces this. Germany's structural primary deficit is set to widen from -0.6% of GDP in 2025 to -2.2% in 2026 and -2.6% in 2027; a fiscal easing of around 2% of GDP over two years.

Figure 28: Germany total budget balance

Source: JPM.

The total federal deficit, including Special Funds, is projected to remain at around 4% of GDP through 2030.

Figure 29: German Domestic Manufacturing Orders - Ex Bulk

Source: JPM.

We are seeing an inflection in total domestic orders ex bulk orders (largely defense), indicating a broad-based demand recovery. This is an encouraging sign that the fiscal impulse is feeding through into the real economy.

The key point is that the €500 billion fund is real, and the issue so far has been timing and not commitment. Finance Ministry estimates suggest Special Fund for Infrastructure and Climate Neutrality (SVIK) spending alone could lift GDP by 0.5%. With Germany forecast to grow just 0.8% this year, public infrastructure investment is one of the key areas of recovery. Markets have largely faded this trade and we think that is a mistake. As spending accelerates into 2027 and 2028, the delta from here is likely to be more significant.

Source: Bloomberg Finance L.P.
On geopolitics, the expectation of a Russia-Ukraine ceasefire remains low. Consensus has moved from expecting an imminent resolution to pricing in a multi-year conflict. We would be contrarian on this and expect some form of resolution to materialise, and if it does, European equities would be a key beneficiary.

## Equity Strategy Key Calls and Drivers

We have consistently argued since the second half of March to use the equity weakness brought on by the Iran conflict to buy into. To be clear, the headline risk remains, with potential need to escalate in order to de-escalate, but we do not see a sustained softness, and continue to believe that both sides have interest in coming to terms. We have in March also highlighted that Mag-7 has started to trade at its cheapest in 10 years, and that the AI-at-risk group of stocks has already crashed, becoming record cheap. While we do not necessarily expect a repeat of 2025, when the equity rally was almost exclusive to Mag-7 for most of the 2H, we believe there is more upside for Mag-7 over the next months, as earnings are more than compensating for the stocks' rebound. We also find the EM memory trade has legs, as meaningful supply additions are not coming before the start of 2028. Stay OW EM equities. With MXWO and MXEF at fresh highs, having more than V-shaped, is the downside risk greater than the upside one from here? We think further upside is likely as investor positioning is lighter currently than pre-conflict, P/Es are lower, we remain very bullish on the broad earnings outlook, with lead indicators pointing to continued acceleration. Further, equities are not all that complacent beneath the surface – current market breadth is very narrow – we think this is a good sign this time around, as the rally to date was more driven by AI, an orthogonal trade to geopolitics, and nearly all consumer plays are still at lows. This can change, with broadening in participation in 2H. We do not expect bond yields to rise in 2H, we see big differences from the 2022 template, and do not see stagflation as the most likely outcome for 2H. Wage growth is moving lower this time around, and corporates are unlikely to exhibit sustained pricing power, with AI anxiety an overhang on sentiment in labour markets, as well. While some hikes can happen, we think the magnitude that the market is already pricing in is likely to reduce.
