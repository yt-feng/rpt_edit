# Summary of key debates

Why does the first entry of generics constitute a key event in the GLP-1 / diabesity market? Following the expiration of market exclusivity after the end of the patent protection period, generic versions of semaglutide/Ozempic (Novo Nordisk's GLP-1 for diabetes) have been launched in India and Canada, and will launch in Brazil in the coming weeks; other countries will follow in the coming years with the largest markets (US and Europe) seeing generics entry in the early to mid 2030's. With the cost of manufacturing injectable GLP-1 such as Ozempic and Wegovy relatively low, competition from generics will lead to meaningful price erosion (e.g. >65% price reduction in Canada within the first year). This will have a direct impact on Novo Nordisk's existing GLP-1 franchise (\~75% of sales), with the company committed to leverage its manufacturing cost advantage to compete with generic makers on pricing in countries where market exclusivity has expired. Improved affordability of semaglutide should drive greater adoption of GLP-1 across countries, in particular in low-to-medium income countries where these drugs are mostly paid out-of-pocket, which should drive volume expansion for the GLP-1 category. We expect the emergence of a 2-tiered market in countries post-semaglutide patent expiry, with semaglutide becoming an affordable mass-market staple molecule, while oral GLP-1's and next-gen products (GLP-1+) from Lilly, Novo and new entrants (Amgen, Roche, AstraZeneca, Pfizer, Structure Therapeutics amongst others) should compete in a higher value, branded segment.

Why is there a debate on the semaglutide generics supply chain? Semaglutide is a peptide drug, and hence not a traditional small molecule like a statin that is easy to copy and manufacture at scale. The success of Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly's injectable GLP-1 franchises Ozempic/Wegovy (semaglutide) and Mounjaro/Zepbound (tirzepatide) led to shortages in the US between 2021 and 2024 and limited availability globally. The key bottleneck in the manufacturing process of these drugs were on the Fill-and-Finish (F&F) process, i.e. turning the active ingredient into a sterile liquid and packaging it into a ready-to-use pen or device. Fill-and-Finish requires highly controlled cleanroom space to minimise contamination risk, requires FDA inspection/approval, and building new F&F capacity takes 3+ years. Novo and Lilly invested dozens of US\$ billions in building more capacity for F&F, including Novo's acquisition of three F&F sites from Catalent in 2024. Since then, the combination of manufacturing optimisation and new F&F sites coming online have released the manufacturing bottleneck on injectables, and Novo Nordisk and Lilly have been able to supply the US market and expand the geographic reach of their injectable franchise. However, there is an investor debate on whether generic makers will be able to replicate the supply chain of Novo and Lilly and manufacture enough injectable generics to address the growing demand stimulated by improved affordability. Our analysis suggests that while there is plenty of capacity scaling in China for the API (active pharmaceutical ingredient), the situation is less clear for F&F capacity, and generic makers will likely either need to invest to build capacity in coming years to address the largest markets (US and Europe), or introduce formulations where F&F is less of a bottleneck (e.g. multi-dose vials rather than pen/autoinjectors).

Peptide vs. small molecules, why does it matter in the GLP-1 oral pill battle and in the debate around the generics impact? The answer resides in convenience and scalability. Novo's oral GLP-1 is a reformulation of semaglutide, a peptide, into a pill form.

Peptides are a type of molecule which usually cannot be taken orally as they are mostly broken down by stomach enzymes before they can reach their target in the body. To get around this limitation, Novo Nordisk leveraged a formulation technology (SNAC) allowing for improved peptide absorption, but with two drawbacks: 1) the need to take the pill in the morning on an empty stomach and not eat/drink/take other pills for 30 minutes; and 2) the need for a very large quantity of API (active pharmaceutical ingredient) to compensate for a poor absorption through the oral route. Indeed, the pill formulation of semaglutide requires 63-73x more API than the injection for the maintenance dose of equivalent strength of the injectable formulation. This increases manufacturing cost but also limits the scalability of the pill, limiting Novo's reach to the US and some select OUS markets. This also limits the ability of generic manufacturers to scale generic copies of Novo's oral pill. In contrast, Lilly's oral pill Foundayo (orfoglipron) is a true small molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist which is easier to manufacture at large scale and does not have the fasting/food/drink restriction of the semaglutide oral pill. We note however that cross-trial comparison favours semaglutide in terms of efficacy (weight loss of \~12% for Foundayo at week 72 vs. 16-17% for Wegovy Pill at week 64).

# 2) Generic market formation in Canada, India and Brazil indicate sharp price erosion, volume expansion and emergence of a 2-tier market

The market formation has started in Canada, the second largest GLP-1 market; we expect steep price erosion. Canada will be a key battleground for Novo and generic makers, as it provides the best blueprint for what could happen in the US and Europe in the early/mid-2030s. We estimate that Canada was a \$2-2.5bn GLP-1 market in 2025, including \$1.5-2bn sales for semaglutide. Dr Reddy's and Apotex have launched the first two generics in the market; the launch of a third generic in coming months (likely from either Sandoz, Teva, Taro or Aspen) would trigger a mandatory 65% price cut on branded price, leading price to decline from \~\$150/month pre-generic to \~\$50/month before the end of 2026. We expect pricing pressure to continue in 2027 as generic makers compete for market share, and we forecast the Canada semaglutide market to stabilise at \$800-900m through a combination of volume growth and price decline, with Novo retaining a \~\$250m tail.

With the entry of generics, the India market for GLP-1 could scale up from \$123m in CY25 to \~\$1bn in CY30. 13 companies have launched 26 generics of semaglutide since March 2026 across several presentations (single-dose, multi-dose, vials and pills) at price points from \~\$15/month for vials to \$60-70/month for more premium injectable offerings. Two players have launched oral tablets (generic Rybelsus) in the 3mg, 7mg and 14mg strength priced \~\$1-\$2.4 per tablet. We expect more launches to enter the market in the coming months. Semaglutide volumes have increased >6x between February and April, with generics already capturing >80% of semaglutide volume, and key players starting to emerge (Torrent, Lupin, DRL, Zydus, and SunPharma). Post generics launch, Eli Lilly continues to be the largest player in value through premium pricing with Mounjaro (GLP-GIP). LLY noted on 1Q26 earnings that the recent launch of generic semaglutide has had a halo effect on the overall GLP-1 market, and Mounjaro has actually grown 10% in the face of generics (see HERE). Novo Nordisk value and volumes have remained flat in absolute at a low level, with the market expansion driven by generics. We forecast the market to continue to grow from \~\$123m in CY25 to \~\$1bn in CY30, with a segmentation between mass-market volume growth for semaglutide (likely dominated by fewer scaled generics players with supply chain integration, device capabilities, and distribution strength) and a premium market likely dominated by Eli Lilly's Mounjaro.

Brazil's first generic version of semaglutide has been approved; we expect prices to decline followed by market expansion to the benefit of generics, and Eli Lilly in the branded segment. The first generic from local manufacturer EMS was approved in Brazil on 26 May, with a launch expected in June/July and potentially further generic manufacturers to follow (17 generic filings in process). Brazil's branded GLP-1 market is currently underpenetrated, with current prices (\$160-320/month) restricting branded GLP-1 access to higher-income patients in the context of a largely out-of-pocket market; elevated price has also led to the formation of a large compounded/unauthorized import market potentially as large as 3-4x the official market. We forecast the introduction of semaglutide generics to drive down costs (down to \~\$60/month by 2027) and increase penetration, growing the Brazil GLP-1 market from \~\$2bn in 2026 to \~\$8bn in 2028. We expect generic makers (EMS, Hyperia, and global competitors) to be the main beneficiaries at the expense of Novo's franchise, while Eli Lilly should continue to dominate the branded segment with Mounjaro. Risks to our Brazil market forecast could come from further expansion of the compounded/black market.

# API considerations: likely manageable for injectables, challenging for orals

Semaglutide API requirements depend on formulation, ranging from relatively low API quantity needed for Ozempic 1mg to large volumes required for oral formulations of Ozempic/Wegovy.

- Ozempic Injectable (52mg/patient/year to 104mg/patient/year): ramp up dose is 0.25mg/week for 4 weeks in a pen (1mg/multi-dose pen); then 0.5mg for 4 weeks in a pen (2mg multi-dose pen); then maintenance dose either 1mg/week (4mg multi-dose pen) or 2mg/week (8mg multi-dose pen). For patients this implies an API requirement of 52mg/patient/week or 104mg/patient/week depending on the maintenance dose.
- Wegovy Injectable (125mg/patient/year to 374mg/patient/year for high dose): Wegovy maintenance dose is 2.4mg/week (standard), to 7.2mg/week (Wegovy High Dose). This implies an API requirement of 125mg/patient/year Wegovy standard or 374mg/patient/year for Wegovy high dose.
- Wegovy Pill (up to 9.125g/patient/year for a patient on maintenance): the daily doses for Wegovy Pill are 1.5mg for the starting dose (one month) / 4mg (one month), 9mg (one month) / 25mg (maintenance). At the maintenance dose of 25mg, Wegovy pill requires 9.125g of API/patient/year, 73x more API than Wegovy injectable 2.4mg and 24x more API than Wegovy HD. We note that the proportion of patients ramping to high dose is still a debate currently considering the recent launch of the pill.
- Rybelsus / Ozempic Pill (up to 9.125g/patient/year for a patient on maintenance): for semaglutide pill for the diabetes indication, the previous formulation (under the Rybelsus name) was 3mg/7mg/14mg daily; the equivalent under the new brand and the new formulation Ozempic Pill is 1.5mg/4mg/9mg, with Novo working on introducing a higher dose of 25mg. This would simplify the supply chain with Ozempic Pill and Wegovy Pill being effectively the same drug with the same formulation and the same doses (only differing in brand and indication).

# API manufacturing: hybrid recombinant / synthetic (Novo) vs. full chemical synthesis (Generics)

Semaglutide can be produced through a semi-recombinant / semi-synthetic approach (Novo) or through chemical synthesis (most generics). Novo's manufacturing route can be described as semi-recombinant / semi-synthetic, with the peptide backbone manufactured through a yeast-based RNA recombinant method, and chemistry introducing additional modifications. In contrast, many generic developers are producing the API through chemical peptide synthesis, usually Solid-Phase Peptide Synthesis (SPPS) or hybrid SPPS and Liquid-Phase Peptide Synthesis (LPPS), followed by side-chain modification and extensive purification. When considering applications from generic makers, regulators focus on whether the final API is the same and whether route-specific impurities are controlled.

The semi-recombinant manufacturing is more expensive to establish (high initial CAPEX requirement) but is more efficient at scale, while the chemical synthesis route has lower barriers to entry but also lower yield at large scale. The key advantages of Novo's approach (yeast-based peptide backbone) is efficiency and better opportunity for yield optimisation at large scale, but building capacity is more capital intensive and takes longer. This is not an easy route for generic makers as it requires biologics/fermentation infrastructure, technology and know-how. By contrast, the chemical synthesis route is faster to develop and the manufacturing chain is more flexible/modulable, but does not have the same yield optimisation opportunity compared to the semi-recombinant route.

We expect API providers and generics makers to be able to supply the API for the injectable formulation at global scale, but producing sufficient API for the pill will be a challenge.

The API required to supply injectable semaglutide is modest; on a rough estimate, the generic industry could supply >100m patients at the global level with only 10-15 tons of API. The API required to supply at large scale the global market with Ozempic 1mg/week (corresponding to the 4mg pen already launched by Dr Reddy's and Apotex in Canada) appears manageable; with 52kg of semaglutide API enough to supply 1m patients at the maintenance dose. The API requirements escalate with the higher doses of semaglutide, with 374kg of API required to supply 1m patients on the Wegovy High Dose pen at maintenance dose. Assuming that the generic industry will focus the generic opportunity on Ozempic and Wegovy, the combined industry could supply >100m patients with 10 to 15 tons of API. We believe the China generic makers, API suppliers and CDMO companies already have significant API capacity and will be able to ramp up to the level required to supply global markets over the coming years.

We believe API and formulation technology considerations are likely to prevent generic manufacturers from manufacturing semaglutide pill generics at scale, although we note that some companies have already launched oral pill (Rybelsus generics) in India. The API requirements for Ozempic Pill and Wegovy Pill are much larger than for the injectable formulations. As an example, to supply 1 million patients on the maintenance dose assuming 100% compliance over a year, 9.125 tons of API are required for Wegovy Pill, 73x more than for Wegovy 2.4mg (125kg for 1m patients on maintenance) and 24x more than for Wegovy High Dose (374kg for 1m patients on maintenance). Additionally, on top of pure API capacity, generic makers would also need to match Novo's updated SNAC technology in terms of API absorption through the oral route. However, we note that generic maker Torrent Pharmaceuticals has launched a generic version of Rybelsus (Semalix), and Dr Reddy's has launched an oral version (Obeda), although supply capacity is unknown. Several other pill generic projects are in development for ex-US countries.

China has accumulated significant semaglutide API capacity over the years, and is the world's biggest supplier of APIs. China's CDMOs (focusing on novel drugs) and API makers (focusing on generic APIs and formulations) have readied substantial capacity in anticipation of generic launches in China and overseas, although not all producers are choosing to allocate capacity, and commentaries from listed manufacturers suggest a wide range of quality for the semaglutide API.


\- In the US, the Patent Term Adjustments and Patent Term Extensions under the Hatch-Waxman Act offer extensions to compensate for time lost during the regulatory review process. These pushed the expiries of the compound patent (US8129343) and the patent for use of long-acting GLP-1 peptides for diabetes/obesity (US10335462) to December 2031 and June 2033.

\- In Europe, the Supplementary Protection Certificates serve the same purpose, extending patent expiries by up to 5 years. This pushed semaglutide patent protection up to March 2031.

\- In China, however, no such mechanism existed prior to June 2021, when the government introduced a similar extension mechanism modeled on US/EU's. Semaglutide (Ozempic) was first approved in China in April, 2021 for the diabetes indication, thus narrowly missing the enactment of the extension pathway. Application of the law is not retroactive either.

Why is China uniquely positioned to manufacture peptides at scale? With the above as background, we distill the following reasons why China has accumulated so much semaglutide capacity, or peptide capacity for that matter.

1. Pre-existing position as the world's API factory: About 30% of the world's API capacity is located in China. In the 20+ years since joining the WTO, China's cost advantages, vast pool of chemistry talent, and a willingness to start from the lower-end of the pharmaceutical value chain have catapulted China to a leadership position in API, with 40+ export-oriented listed API companies (see our primer). As experts in synthetic chemistry, many have developed solid-phase peptide synthesis capacities.

2. Operating at lower cost: This advantage stems from lower labor costs and raw material prices (note). This positions China as an attractive service provider to more cost conscious customers.

3. Semaglutide's early patent expiry: A delayed enactment of the patent term extension mechanism led to an expiry date (April 2027) that is 4-5 years ahead of those in US/EU. Coupled with a vast Chinese diabetes population (\~12% of the adult population, source), this created a powerful incentive for Chinese producers to invest in semaglutide API capacity. Some are also eyeing the export markets of Canada, Brazil, and India. As of Q1'26, 74 Chinese companies had obtained FDA DMF registrations for semaglutide API supply, while in China, 13 had filed applications for API supply, with none approved yet. And as showed in Exhibit 33, 10+ companies have submitted applications for biosimilars, but the frontrunners are being held up by the China-Switzerland Free Trade Agreement (CSFTA) (news).

4. A vast and under-penetrated domestic market: There were 148 million adult diabetics in China as of 2024. Current barriers to uptake in the prescription channel include low levels of reimbursement, needle phobia (see our survey here), and a gray market which is under increased regulatory crackdowns. In other words, China's API makers could pivot either way: China or overseas.

5. Mastery of SPPS (solid-phase peptide synthesis): SPPS is a widely used lab technique for chemically creating peptides. It strings together amino acids one by one on an insoluble resin bead. Semaglutide is a complex 31-amino acid peptide with a fatty acid side chain, making large-scale, high-purity production technically challenging. Chinese manufacturers have successfully mastered the core technologies for large-scale, high-purity production of long-chain peptides like semaglutide, including technological mastery and process maturity that presents significant technical barriers to entry. A number of

leading manufacturers have achieved ton-scale mass production of semaglutide, with some production lines designed for 10-ton scale.

6. Significant capex in peptide infrastructure: China's leading CDMOs in the TIDES (peptides + oligonucleotides) space have deployed significant capital to capacity buildup. WuXi AppTec is the leader, with SPPS capacity reaching 100,000L by 3Q25 and a projected increase of 30% within 2026 to 130,000L. In 2023 alone, its TIDES business produced more than 15 tons of peptide APIs and intermediates (on 50 preclinical to commercial stage projects). Asymchem had 45,000L of SPPS capacity by end-2025, and expects to expand this to 69,000L within 2026 (+53%). Medtide, a pure-play CDMO specializing in TIDES, has an annual production capacity of over one ton. Other suppliers include Pharmaron, Jiuyuan Genetic, and Sihuan. As of end-2024, Sihuan had two sets of 12,000L fermentation systems dedicated to semaglutide production.

7. Government support: According to commentary (link) by Caplin Point Labs, supportive policies by the Chinese government have created a highly competitive and fragmented supplier base of 500-600 companies in the biosimilar/generic space, versus 7-10 large companies in India.

8. Sizable export markets: What originally incentivized the domestic supplier base was China's own generic market (now delayed to April 2027) and the compounding market in the US. Following restrictions in the US on the latter, Chinese suppliers have since pivoted towards other generic markets in addition to China's, e.g. Canada, Brazil, and India.

Quantifying the domestic base for semaglutide API manufacturing: CDMOs and API makers alike have ramped up significantly their peptide capacities. As of Q1'26, the US FDA had granted 90 DMFs (Drug Master Files) to suppliers for the semaglutide API. Of those, 74 were Chinese companies (82%), followed by 8 for India (9%). Despite a large number of Chinese suppliers for semaglutide, only a few are on FDA's Green List. The Green List comprises foreign manufacturers whose facilities and products have been evaluated by the FDA and comply with current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) requirements. # Canada generic market formation (2026)

We estimate the existing GLP-1 market in Canada at \~US\$2-2.5bn, of which \~US\$1.5-2bn for semaglutide. Novo Nordisk reported \~US\$1.15bn sales for semaglutide in Canada in 2024 (US\$1bn for Ozempic and US\$150m for Wegovy). While Novo did not disclose semaglutide Canada sales in 2025, we estimate that sales were \~US\$1.6bn. This is consistent with the Dr Reddy's management estimate of Canada's existing semaglutide market of \~US\$1.8bn based on IQVIA data. Including Mounjaro sales (undisclosed, launched in November 2023, full supply availability in May 2025), we estimate the Canada pre-LOE market is \~US\$2-2.5bn.

We see Canada as a blueprint for how market dynamics could evolve in the US and Europe after LOE. Novo Nordisk management indicated that India is not an appropriate blueprint for other countries. One key reason is the under-development of the branded GLP-1 market ahead of generic launch, with only \~US\$50m of combined sales in India for Wegovy/Ozempic due to a combination of supply limitation (Wegovy launched in June 2025 and Ozempic only launched in December 2025, although Rybelsus was launched in 2022) and affordability barriers. Therefore, generic makers have entered India before the branded market could be established, forcing Novo Nordisk to cut prices to remain competitive in the context of generic launches. The second key point is the average income differential between India and the Western market making affordability and price a more acute focus in the country. The third key point is the more intense price competition from a large number of local players approved in India (13+ companies approved at IP expiry in the country). In comparison, Canada is much more comparable to the US and Europe in terms of regulatory requirements, average income and maturity of the branded GLP-1 market (Canada is the second largest GLP-1 market in the world).

Dr Reddy's and Apotex products were the first two semaglutide generics approved in Canada. Both companies received approval for a multi-dose 2mg and 4mg pen (corresponding to the 1.0mg/weekly Ozempic dose) and are approved only for the diabetes indication. The API for both products is manufactured through solid-phase synthetic chemistry (i.e. purely chemical process vs. Novo's hybrid biologic/chemical process). Dr Reddy's (approved 28 April, launched 16 May 2026) generic API is produced in-house. Apotex (approved 1 May, launched 14 May 2026) generic was developed in partnership with Orbicular Pharmaceutical Technologies; while the source of the API is unknown, we believe it is likely supplied by the Orbicular CMO network. The fill-and-finish for both Apotex and Dr Reddy's is handled by OneSource with device components provided by Shaily Engineering Plastics. The Apotex semaglutide generic (developed and manufactured with the same partners) also cleared tentative approval in the US.

Hims & Hers (covered by Craig Hettenbach) has partnered with Apotex to replicate the successful consumer-led approach taken in the US in Canada. H&H has announced the marketing of Apotex' generic through its platform for weight loss, despite the generic label indication being restricted to T2D. H&H offers treatment options starting at 149 CAD/month (US\$107/month) and follows the company's acquisition of Livewell, which was announced in December 2025. Livewell was founded in 2023 as a telehealth platform for weight loss, smoking cessation and sexual health and this acquisition was specifically aimed to align with the timeline of generic semaglutide availability in Canada. H&H press release indicates: Generic semaglutide is authorized for sale by Health Canada for the management of Type 2 diabetes. It may be prescribed off-label for weight management in a licensed healthcare provider's sole clinical judgment. Hims & Hers Canada Inc. does not currently offer access to treatment services for Type 2 diabetes. We remain Equal-weight on HIMS on a balanced risk/reward, with pressure on margins and decelerating core growth vs. potential upside in international growth and new category ramps (see Higher revenue guidance comes with a cost, as EBITDA is cut on weaker margins).

We expect the price of Ozempic in the public reimbursement channel to decline from \~US\$150/pen (depending on the region) to US\$70-80 in the near term, then \~US\$50 after a 3rd generic entry. The launch of a 3rd generic in Canada will trigger a price cut for all Ozempic generics in the reimbursed channels. Generic public reimbursement/list prices in Canada are set through the pan-Canadian Pharmaceutical Alliance (pCPA) Tiered Pricing Framework for generics. When two generics are launched, the generic price is set to 50% of the brand reference price; when a third generic is launched, this triggers an automatic reimbursement/list-price tier change and the generic price is set at 35% of the brand reference price (for an injectable). While Novo Nordisk is not required to lower the price of branded Ozempic to the level of generics, a substantial price cut is likely for the drug to remain competitive against generics. We also note that while private plans in Canada do not have to follow the pCPA price, many do, effectively leading to a spillover of the public price into the private market.

Health Canada is currently reviewing generics from four other generic makers, with additional companies expected to file in coming months. In addition to the two approved generics from Apotex and Dr Reddy's, Health Canada is currently reviewing seven additional generics, of which three are additional filings from Apotex, and the other four are generics from Sandoz, Teva, Taro and Aspen. Aspen indicated that they expect approval between May and September 2026; Sandoz expect approval in the second half of 2026; Teva and Taro have not commented. Three additional companies are also developing a generic of semaglutide in Canada but have not yet filed according to Health Canada disclosures: PharmaTher (targeting approval in 2026), Biocon, and Canadian company Vimy Pharma, which is seeking to bring to market a vial generic manufactured by partners in Canada.

# Brazil Market Formation (2026): Semaglutide's LOE Sets the Stage for Formal Market Expansion

Current State Pre-Generic Entry: GLP-1 access remains constrained primarily by affordability. Brazil's GLP-1 market remains materially underpenetrated, not because of weak demand, but because affordability continues to cap adoption in an almost entirely out-of-pocket market. Monthly treatment costs for leading GLP-1 therapies still range from roughly R\$800 to R\$1,600 (US\$160 to US\$320 per month), levels that restrict access to a narrow upper-income tier and keep penetration well below the market's underlying clinical potential. The scale of demand for compounded alternatives and unauthorized imports further supports this affordability bottleneck, suggesting that many patients are actively seeking lower-cost access outside the formal market rather than opting out of treatment altogether. Supply constraints appear less broad-based today, though availability remains inconsistent for Mounjaro/tirzepatide, where recurring stockouts can still limit realized volumes even within the high-income addressable group. As a result, Brazil currently has only \~2.1mn active GLP-1 users by our 1Q26 estimates (1.3% of the current adult population), underscoring the large pool of unmet demand in a sizeable emerging market where category penetration remains well below potential.

Early pricing moves suggest incumbents are already preparing for semaglutide competition. We have also seen some initial discounts and price reductions on semaglutide's authorized generic from Eurofarma, see LatAm Healthcare: GLP-1 Brazil Tracker: April Imports Remain Solid Despite MoM Decline; New ANVISA Data Adds Insights (7 May 2026). These moves are still immaterial to overall market dynamics, as price points remain too high to materially broaden access or trigger a meaningful shift in category penetration. However, they are directionally important: the early discounting suggests that the Novo Nordisk-Eurofarma partnership is already preparing for incoming competition ahead of broader semaglutide generic entry, with pricing likely to become a more relevant competitive lever as additional approved alternatives reach the market.

Generic-led transition in 2026: the beginning of the price reset and accelerating adoption. Following the expected entry of semaglutide generics in 2H26, we forecast a marked expansion in GLP-1 access as affordability improves, though the market may take time beyond 2026 to reach its post-LOE equilibrium. Semaglutide generics have been delayed, with the first approval (for EMS product) granted in May 2026 with a launch planned for June 15th; we are therefore modeling market formation in 2H26; see LatAm Healthcare & Education: Our takes from Brazil Journal HC Conference (28 Apr 2026). Once the first branded generics launch, semaglutide prices should begin to step down, with our forecast for semaglutide generics reaching \~R\$687/month (US\$137/month) by YE26, from \~R\$1k/month (US\$200/month) currently for Novo's offering (Ozempic and Wegovy). We estimate total GLP-1 market sales of \~R\$14.3bn (US\$2.5bn) in 2026, with active users rising to \~3.4mn by YE26, as lower entry prices allow volumes to better reflect underlying demand. That said, 2026 should still be viewed as an acceleration year rather than a steady state, with additional entrants, ongoing price compression, and broader affordability gains likely to play out gradually through late-2026, leaving penetration still below long-term potential by year-end.

Post-LOE Shift: semaglutide generics unlock a step-change in access and market stabilization. Beyond the initial 2026 adjustment, semaglutide's loss of exclusivity may drive a structural shift in Brazil's GLP-1 market, as lower prices unlock a significantly broader addressable population in a country with steep income distribution. # Implications for Novo Nordisk post-LOE: Brazil as an early test case for ex-US erosion.

Brazil is one of Novo Nordisk's first large emerging-market tests of post-LOE semaglutide dynamics, and the recent approval of EMS's Ozivy suggests the generic approval cycle is now beginning to move. While the delayed approval process gave Novo more time to defend its franchise, the structural risk is now becoming more tangible: as approved synthetic semaglutide products reach the market, Novo is likely to face price erosion, volume share dilution and increased competition from local players with strong distribution, prescribing relationships and retail reach. The key debate is how much of the expanding market Novo can retain as semaglutide access broadens from a premium niche toward a more mass-market therapy. In our view, Brazil should be watched as an early indicator of Novo's broader ex-US LOE headwinds, particularly in markets where affordability constraints are high, local generic capabilities are strong and demand is already visible through informal and compounding-pharmacy channels.

Impacts on our coverage: Semaglutide's LOE should be positive for both RADL (OW) and HYPE (OW), though through different mechanisms. For RADL, the main upside is the expansion of the addressable market as lower semaglutide prices move GLP-1 access beyond a narrow high-income niche, alongside the migration of demand currently served by unauthorized imports and compounded alternatives into the formal pharmacy channel. While lower prices may pressure average ticket, elasticity should support higher volumes, traffic and recurring prescription demand, with RADL well positioned to capture this shift given its national footprint, digital capabilities, supplier relationships and ability to sell both branded and generic GLP-1s. For HYPE, the opportunity is more execution-dependent, but potentially meaningful: if the company secures timely Anvisa approval and launches in the early waves of branded semaglutide copies, it could participate directly in the expansion and formalization of a market where demand already exists but is still partly priced out of the legal channel. EMS's first-mover advantage and local manufacturing footprint are relevant competitive hurdles, but the market should evolve into a multi-player branded-copy segment, where HYPE's physician relationships, marketing capabilities, retail reach and brand-building capacity could support share gains as semaglutide shifts from a premium niche into a broader mass-market therapy.

Risks to our estimates: unauthorized imports and compounded GLP-1s highlight unmet demand, but also regulatory and safety risk. The emergence of compounded, informal, and cross-border GLP-1 channels is another signal that underlying demand exceeds the formal market's current affordability curve. Before generic entry, there was an expectation that lower-cost alternatives would reduce average prices and expand access, but the size of the parallel market suggests that demand among lower-income consumers already exists, even before formal semaglutide generics have arrived. For reference, according to RADL's 1Q26 conference call, the unofficial GLP-1 market could be around 3-4x the size of the official market. Going forward, a key catalyst would be tighter enforcement on unauthorized imports and compounded alternatives, which could pressure black-market supply, while lower prices from approved generics should encourage consumers to transition into the legal market.

No such mechanism existed in China prior to June 2021, when the government introduced a similar extension mechanism modeled on US/EU's. Semaglutide (Ozempic) was first approved in China in April, 2021 for the diabetes indication, thus narrowly missing the enactment of the extension pathway.

However, the exclusivity is extended potentially until April 2027 under the China-Switzerland Free Trade Agreement (CSFTA). Novo received approval for semaglutide in April 2021 in China through a filing was via its Swiss-registered entity, Novo Nordisk Pharma AG. In 2014, China and Switzerland signed the CSFTA, a bilateral economic treaty that reduces trade barriers, improves market access, and strengthens IP protection. For the latter, the two countries grant six years of market protection for undisclosed pharmaceutical test data, counting from the drug's approval date (i.e. April 2021 for semaglutide in China). When the patent expired in March 2026, Novo invoked this specific clause, barring generic launches in China until April 2027. Due to this clause, generic applicants cannot use Novo's trial data in their regulatory submissions till then. Hangzhou

Jiuyuan challenged the CSFTA in the Zhejiang Provincial court in January 2026; but at time of writing, no generic has been approved in China.

In anticipation of semaglutide's patent expiry, a host of Chinese developers have geared up applications for a biosimilar. These include major pharma companies like Fosun and Sino Biopharm (Chia Tai), and specialty companies (either in diabetes or in insulins/peptides) like United Labs and Huadong. Most applications in the queue are for the diabetes indication. One of the frontrunners in this race, Huadong, indicated at its end-May shareholders meeting that because of CSFTA, its semaglutide generic would not launch until after April 2027, thus confirming the validity of the extension. Meanwhile, Hybio has also recently submitted a biosimilar application (co-developed with CR Double-Crane) and is expected to serve as the API supplier.

# The entry of orals

We recently updated our views on the evolving GLP-1 landscape across obesity and T2D, refreshing our global obesity model and latest GLP-1 assumptions (see "Tracking the GLP-1 'Unlock' HERE). We continue to see sustained uptake of GLP-1 therapies and believe five key drivers can support further global penetration over time: (1) the launch of oral GLP-1 therapies (Novo's Wegovy pill and LLY's Foundayo), (2) broader access to all GLP-1's in the US for elderly patients via Medicare (at a co-pay of \$50/month), (3) international market expansion (such as China and Brazil, which we discuss in detail), (4) next-generation molecules/pathways, and (5) clinical benefits in new disease areas such as inflammation and neurological indications.

We believe orals, such as Novo's oral Wegovy pill (oral peptide GLP-1, approved on December 22, 2025 for obesity) and LLY's Foundayo (oral small molecule GLP-1, approved on April 1, 2026 for obesity) will also drive the next inflection in the obesity market. On 4Q25 EPS LLY reiterated its view that the entry of oral GLP-1's will expand the market, which was also supported by Novo's 4Q25 earnings call commentary that most oral Wegovy patients are incretin naïve. In our recent AlphaWise consumer survey, 77% of respondents on the Wegovy pill are new to GLP-1 therapy, further supporting our view that orals will substantially expand the category (LINK). On 1Q26 LLY noted that the significant inventory build speaks to upcoming international roll out of the drug as they see a "huge" opportunity for Foundayo around the world, also consistent with our view (LINK).

Oral GLP-1 therapies in development aim to deliver the benefits of existing injectable agents while offering patients an alternative formulation and, in some cases, improved scalability on a global basis. A key scientific challenge is that most incretins are peptides that are rapidly degraded in the stomach, leading companies to pursue either (1) engineering oral peptide delivery systems, such as Novo's SNAC delivery platform utilized in Rybelsus/Ozempic pill and Wegovy pill, or (2) non-peptide small molecules, such as LLY's Foundayo. Oral peptide formulations typically require food and water restrictions (e.g., administration on an empty stomach followed by a waiting period before eating, drinking, or taking other medications) to facilitate absorption. Taken together with manufacturing considerations, we believe non-peptide small molecules may ultimately prove both more scalable and more convenient. As a result, we see LLY's Foundayo playing an important role in OUS markets over time, while noting that Novo's oral Wegovy may face drug-substance scaling constraints as demand expands.

# Generic GLP-1s worth watching for Drug Packaging names

We view the launch of GLP-1 generics as incremental growth opportunities for drug packaging names such as WST and STVN. Both WST and STVN have a strong presence in GLP-1 injectables. Notably:

- GLP-1 represents approximately 18% of WST's total company revenue, with \~10% in elastomer components and \~8% in West Vantage (contract manufacturing).
WST's portfolio participates across multiple modalities with vials, prefilled syringes, auto-injectors, and pens using WST's elastomer components.
Approximately 90% of WST's GLP-1 exposure is from branded products and 10% from generics.
- GLP-1 represents 19-20% of STVN's total company revenue. Key products within STVN's portfolio serving the GLP market include the company's Nexa syringes and EZ-fill cartridges for multi-dose pens.

Minimal trade down expected from GLP-1 generics. Both WST and STVN primarily serve the GLP-1 market through products in their high value portfolios. Compared to products in their standard portfolios, high value products come with higher ASPs and better margin profiles. Per our conversations with management, both WST and STVN expect minimal trade down from branded GLP-1s to generics. We see generic manufacturers adopting similar high value primary packaging specifications used by branded originators in their Drug Master Files to reduce regulatory risk, allowing WST and STVN to continue supplying their most profitable products from their high value portfolio, and allowing for meaningful top line flow through to the EPS line.

GLP-1 generics could provide ASP tailwind from lesser volume discounts. Entrenched in their business models, both WST and STVN generally provide volume discounts to large customers. We believe this dynamic could be a partial explanation for why GLP-1 growth assumptions embedded in both WST and STVN's 2026 guidance come in significantly below street estimates for current GLP-1 injectables on the market.
