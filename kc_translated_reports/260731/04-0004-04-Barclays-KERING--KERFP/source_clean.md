# The future is bright(er) — Upgrade to Market Weight

We upgrade our rating on KERFP to MW from UW reflecting our greater confidence in the stabilisation of the model following H1 26 results as well as progress towards reducing leverage. In this note we also downgrade the European High Grade Retailers sector from UW from MW.

## Good news comes in threes

Hitting the ground running. Kering's CEO, Luca De Meo, officially began his tenure on 15 September 2025 after having stated on 9 September 2025 (at Kering's AGM) that he was determined to make tough decisions to slash debt and cut costs at Kering without delay. De Meo has proven a man of his word, delivering on two key objectives within days:

1. Reducing issuance risks... The next day (10 September 2025), Kering and Mayhoola jointly postponed the latter's put option on its 70% stake in Valentino from 2026-27 to 2028-29, and Kering's call option was delayed from 2028 to 2029. We argue that the additional time to acquire Valentino reduces the financial pressure on Kering, thus allowing management to focus on debt reduction and the Gucci turnaround.


3. CMD targets appear more in sight as Gucci starts showing: Having questioned some of the targets shared by Kering at its Capital Markets day (CMD) on 16 April, namely: 1) ambitions to more than double the EBIT margin from 11% (lowest level since 2010) and 2) to outperform the market while delivering 20% ROCE from capex of 5-6% of sales, given our cautious view on the Gucci turnaround (we thought the turnaround at Gucci accounting for 40% of group revenue in FY25 but producing just 57% of the revenue it generated in FY22 would require more than just an operational reset); we were positively surprised with H1 26 results. Key positives were: 1) the sequential improvement at Gucci (organic growth was down -2% y/y in Q2, beating Bloomberg consensus of -3.27% y/y) with Gucci leather good category back to growth in Q2 according to management, 2) the improvement in operating margin (at 12.8%, a +40bp y/y improvement and a 300bp improvement versus H2 25) and especially the improvement in Gucci operating margin

Net debt reduction gains momentum: Beyond the improved momentum in sales and margins, FCF from operations (excluding real estate and Gucci Beauty agreement) was up c.€0.8bn y/y to €1.8bn and net debt was reduced from €8bn in FY25 to €3.3bn reflecting the improvement in operating cash flow but also proceeds from the Kering Beauty sale (€4bn) and real estate divestitures (€0.5bn). Net leverage (pre IFRS 16) is down 2x from FY25 at 1.4x. When asked about potential bond buybacks on the conference call, management pointed to the significant reduction in net debt achieved in H1 26 and said it sees scope for further reduction from cash flow generation. Having seen scope for potential debt buybacks given Kering's strong cash balance of c.€8.5bn, there was no specific reference to gross debt reduction or buybacks as such, though we think that the combination of net debt reduction and especially the improved momentum at Gucci (bearing in mind that Demna's first show presented in Milan was in February 2026) as well as the improved performance in the other Houses especially Saint Laurent and Bottega Veneta should support spreads. We upgrade our rating on Kering from Underweight to Market Weight reflecting our view that the business has now stabilised with further upside at Gucci. While we retain a preference for higher coupon notes, we also favour the longer-dated bonds (similar to LVMH). Potential catalysts include Q3 results (our equity colleagues forecast broadly flat group organic growth in Q3, followed by an acceleration to 2.7% in Q4, driven by 2.1% growth in FLG). We view Kering as solidly positioned within its BBB+ rating (at S&P); however, we would not rule out a potential positive rating action at S&P should 1) "Kering maintains adjusted debt to EBITDA below 2.5x on a sustainable basis through positive organic growth, with some regains of market share lost in its key markets, coupled with a sustainable improvement in EBITDA and cash flow generation, 2) the company successfully executing its business plan and turnaround strategy of the Gucci brand," as per S&P potential upgrade criteria (from November 2025). We recommend six trades: three outright buy trades across the capital structure, two extension trades and a switch out of LVMH into Kering.

## S&P in focus: BBB+/Stable

Kering is rated BBB+/Stable by S&P with its latest update on 7 November 2025, when the outlook was upgraded from Negative back to Stable. The change back to a Stable outlook in November 2025 followed the Beauté sale, predicated on deleveraging prospects and that the company's operating performance would gradually improve during 2026, driven by the execution of the turnaround strategy, the easier comparison base, and a better operating environment for the whole industry. Thanks to expected cash proceeds from asset disposal, S&P estimates that the company's S&P Global Ratings-adjusted debt to EBITDA will materially improve in FY26 from a forecast peak level of about 4.0-4.5x at year-end 2025 to about 3.0x in 2026. This compares with our previous expectation of leverage remaining above 3.5x until 2027. S&P said it could raise the rating on Kering if it shows a clear organic deleveraging trend with adjusted debt to EBITDA below 2.5x on a sustainable basis. This scenario could derive from a track record of positive organic growth, with some regaining of market share lost in its key markets, coupled with a sustainable improvement in EBITDA and cash flow generation. An upgrade would also depend on the company successfully executing its business plan and turnaround strategy of the Gucci brand.

The KERFP 5y CDS and Kering equity price have historically displayed a negatively correlated relationship. On its Q4 25 conference call (10 February 2026), management stated that it targets a range of 1.0-1.5x from current leverage (excluding leases), but gave no timeline. On the day after the results, the equity price rose c.11% (CAC 40 was up 0.1% on the same day) and the CDS tightened 6bp while the cash bond structure closed the day unchanged. This is despite €123mn of the €4bn in proceeds from selling Kering Beauté were allocated to equity holders in the form of a special dividend. While a credit positive statement, given the lack of timeline, we assume this takes into account a combination of the Beauté proceeds along with the sale of real estate stakes and would therefore expect for that leverage target to apply to FY26. Based on S&P's method of calculating leverage, Kering's 1.0-1.5x target translates to around 2.5-3.0x, which is in line with the BBB+ rating band. In reaction to the Q1 26 results, the equity fell 9.3% intraday while the CDS was 5bp wider. The CMD took the share price a further 3.1% lower while the CDS was unchanged on 16 April 2026. In reaction to the Q2 26 results (28 July 2026), the equity rose 11% (CAC 40 was down 0.8% on the day) while CDS was -4bp tighter to an LTM low of 57.5bp mid. We view the 5y CDS as fairly priced at these levels.

## Operational performance

Q2 26

\- Kering reported 2Q26 revenue of €3.7bn with organic growth at 2% y/y, beating the company-compiled consensus of +1.4% y/y. Fashion and Leather Goods (FLG accounting for c.81% of sales) organic growth was flat in the quarter (versus consensus of -0.6% y/y) while Gucci's organic revenue declined 2% y/y, a solid beat vs consensus $^{1}$ of -4.7% y/y.

\- We view Gucci's beat versus consensus very positively and note that by region, the beat was driven by North America (+6% y/y), up 6%, while other regions improved.

\- As expected, Kering Jewelry grew strongly, with organic growth up 18%, beating consensus of +17.2% y/y, driven by strong double-digit growth in North America, Asia Pacific and Japan.

\- Kering Eyewear reported organic growth of 8.0% y/y versus consensus of 6.1%, accelerating slightly from Q1, driven by expansion of brand portfolio.

\- Focusing on regions, North America increased its contribution to 24% of group revenue in Q2, while Western Europe was stable at 30%. When asked about the Middle East, management said the rest of the world remained challenging, down 8% in Q2, mainly reflecting the instability in the Middle East, even as retail in the region improved sequentially, month after month, through the quarter. The Middle East typically accounts for around 5% of group retail revenue. Its negative effect on group revenue growth was -1% in Q2 26, in line with Q1, when the disruption affected only one month of the quarter.

## H1 26

\- Kering reported H126 revenue of €7.22bn with organic growth at 1% y/y. Fashion and Leather Goods (FLG accounting for c.80% of sales) organic revenue declined 1% y/y while Gucci's organic dropped 5% y/y.

\- Kering Jewelry grew strongly, with organic growth up 20%.

\- Kering Eyewear reported organic growth of 8.0% y/y.

\- Group recurring income was up from €920mn in H1 25 to €921mn in H1 26. Importantly, operating income improved sequentially from H2 25 at €711mn, mostly driven by a reduction in operating expenses (-5% y/y).


• FLG operating margin improved by 70bp y/y to 13.6%.

\- Within FLG, Gucci's operating margin improved by 100bp y/y to 17%.

• Kering Jewelry operating margin improved by 270bp y/y to 6.2%.

\- Kering eyewear operating margin improved by 290bp y/y to 23%.

\- FCF from operations improved by €852mn y/y, to €2.324bn, mostly helped by an inflow from working capital of €602mn in H1 26 versus an outflow of -€26mn in the prior year.

\- FCF from operations excluding real estate and Gucci beauty agreement improved from €1.083bn in H1 25 to €1.816bn.

\- Net debt (pre IFRS 16) was reduced from €8bn in FY25 to €3.3bn in H1 26. Cash on balance sheet amounted to €8.5bn and we calculate net leverage at 1.4x (pre IFRS 16) versus 3.4x in FY25.

## Retailers: Amazon tips the balance

Our earlier upgrade of the Pan European High Grade Retailers sector from Underweight to Market Weight was driven by two factors. First, we saw an improving outlook for KERFP, as we judged that the risk of a rating downgrade had been pushed further out. Second, AMZN's entry to the €-IG market in March 2026 changed the composition of the sector materially, with the issuer now accounting for 25% of the sector. At the time, we viewed this as an addition of a diversified, highly-rated credit that our fundamental analysts rated Market Weight.

The first part of that thesis continues to hold, reflected in our upgrade of KERFP to Market Weight. However, our analysts recently downgraded AMZN to Underweight with issuance headwinds on the horizon (see The Hyperscaler Effect on Retail, 24 July 2026).

Source: BARC

AMZN's influence on the sector is meaningful. Excluding AMZN, retailer spreads are little changed YTD, while the name currently adds almost 7bp to the aggregate spread (Figure 9). In addition, AMZN has increased the sector's average duration, leaving it longer than the broader index. While we are Market Weight on the next two largest constituents, KERFP (17%) and MCFP (21%), the deterioration in our AMZN outlook weakens a key pillar of our previous upgrade. Given the issuer's sizeable weight and influence on sector-level characteristics, we move Pan European High Grade Retailers from Market Weight to Underweight.
