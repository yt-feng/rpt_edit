# Sony Group (6758.T): Announces proposal to make major camera lens maker Tamron a wholly owned subsidiary

Sony Group proposes full acquisition of major camera lens maker Tamron On July 29 at 5:00 PM (Japan time), Diamond Online reported that Sony Group has communicated its intention to acquire major camera manufacturer Tamron (7740.T, NC). In response, Tamron announced at 8:30 AM (Japan time) on July 30 that it had received a non-binding proposal from Sony and had established a special committee in response. According to the Diamond Online report, the offer price is around ¥1,300 per share (implying a 15% premium over the close of July 29th). Based on Tamron's securities filings, Sony holds 15.35% of Tamron's shares (as of December 31, 2025). If Sony were to acquire all remaining shares and using the reported offer price of ¥1,300, this would imply an acquisition cost of approximately ¥188 bn (Tamron's total shares outstanding, including treasury shares, are 170.8 mn; as of March 31, 2026). Sony commented that this acquisition would enhance Tamron's corporate value and contribute to the further development of Sony's imaging business.

## An overview of Tamron is as follows:

Business overview: Per company disclosures, Tamron's main business is its photography-related business, which involves the manufacture of interchangeable lenses for mirrorless and SLR cameras. The company also manufactures and sells industrial lenses for surveillance cameras and factory automation, as well as automotive and medical lenses. The company was founded in 1950.

Sales/operating profits/net profits: ¥85.1 bn/¥16.6 bn/¥11.8 bn (FY12/25)

Share price/market cap (based on closing price on July 29): ¥1,134/¥183 bn

Sony's first major acquisition outside the three entertainment businesses in some time, but the company said that the proposed Tamron acquisition is consistent with its stated strategic investment policy

Sony allocated roughly three-quarters of its ¥1.3 tn in strategic investments during the fourth medium-term plan (FY3/22-FY3/24) to the three entertainment businesses (G&NS, music, pictures). From the fifth medium-term plan (FY3/25-FY3/27) onward as well, major recent investments have centered on the three entertainment businesses, including the acquisition of Queen's music catalog and strategic investments in Bandai Namco and KADOKAWA. On the other hand, Sony has previously stated that its strategic investments target three areas: (1)

content IP, (2) areas that contribute to enhancing IP value, and (3) creation technology. The company commented that the proposed acquisition of Tamron is consistent with its stated strategic investment policy and does not represent a change in investment priorities between businesses (as the purpose is to acquire creation technology).

## Our view

The imaging business is the most profitable core business within Sony Group's ET&S segment (we estimate FY3/26 operating profits at ¥180.9 bn; ET&S

segment/consolidated operating profits are ¥158.6 bn/¥1,447.5 bn). Lenses are a core component, and we believe the proposed acquisition of Tamron, which would give Sony greater control over the value chain, has strategic merit. In addition, while Sony has traditionally had strengths in its premium and professional-grade lineup, Tamron has a well-developed range of products for general users. Although we will need to confirm Sony's future strategy, we see potential for the two companies' product lineups to complement each other. Furthermore, Tamron's disclosures indicate that it has been expanding its business areas beyond interchangeable camera lenses into automotive and medical applications. Sony's automotive image sensor business is seeing accelerating growth, which could offer potential for synergy creation in the sensing module area as well.

Sony is scheduled to announce 1Q FY3/27 results at 12:00 PM (Japan time) on July 31. Going forward, key items to confirm with the company will include (1) the synergies expected in the ET&S and I&SS areas, (2) the growth strategy and growth potential for the imaging business, and (3) the prioritization of strategic investments.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Sony Group

Our 12-month target price of ¥4,100 is based on an EV/EBITDA driven sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) valuation. Our base year for the valuation is FY3/29E. Key downside risks to our target price include: (1) G&NS business; (i) Lower-than-expected PS5 hardware unit sales; (ii) lower-than-expected revenue per console unit due to delays in the launch of new software, live service games, and downloadable content (DLC) from first and third parties, or weaker-than-expected performance of these offerings; and (iii) deterioration in hardware profitability due to higher promotion expenses or component costs. (2) Pictures business: Slower-than-expected growth in paying subscribers at anime streaming platform Crunchyroll, which we consider a key driver of earnings in this segment, or higher-than-expected promotion and marketing expenses. (3) I&SS business: Slower growth in the market for CMOS image sensors for mobile devices, ASP declines due to intensifying competition (particularly from Chinese companies), or deterioration in product yields due to increasing manufacturing difficulty accompanying performance improvements in CMOS image sensors.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

## M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
