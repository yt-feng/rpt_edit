# ASML: Best Idea 3Q26 – WFE upcycle, rising litho intensity, and pricing upside

ASML is our Bernstein Best Idea for the third quarter of 2026. We rate ASML Outperform with a target price of €2,500.

ASML provides the best growth visibility with rising litho intensity. With huge WFE spending to support AI growth, ASML benefits by providing the essential EUV/DUV tools that are gaining share. Litho intensity is rising steadily from 20% to close to 30% as DRAM migrates to more EUV layers, and stays elevated above 30% for advanced logic. We also expect HNA EUV to be adopted gradually virtually all leading edge players in DRAM and logic by 2030.

## ASML REMAINS OUR TOP PICK IN THE SECTOR

## LITHO INTENSITY CONTINUES TO INCREASE, DEFYING INVESTOR CONCERNS

We forecast litho intensity to continue its upward trend, increasing from 24% to 28% by 2028, driven by the faster growth of DRAM and advanced logic relative to the overall semiconductor market. Both segments have considerably higher litho intensity than mature logic and NAND, supporting a structurally higher blended litho intensity over time.

\- In DRAM, we estimate litho intensity increased from slightly below 20% at the 1Y and 1Z nodes to 26% at 1c. This significant increase was driven by the rising number of EUV exposures, starting with 3–4 exposures at 1a, increasing to 4–6 at 1b, and then to 6–7 at 1c. With 1c penetration expected to increase substantially over the next few years, we expect overall DRAM litho intensity to rise considerably. In addition, with the introduction of double-patterning EUV or HNA at the 1d node, we expect litho intensity to increase further to around 30%.

\- Similarly, logic litho intensity increased significantly from around 20% at 10nm, driven by a sharp rise in EUV exposures, peaking at 5nm and 3nm at above 35% intensity. While logic litho intensity declined from 3nm to 2nm due to the adoption of GAA, which requires more non-litho process steps, it remains elevated at around 32%. We expect litho intensity to remain at similar levels, or potentially increase, with HNA at 1.4nm, and to rise further at the 1nm node.

## DRAM GROWS FASTER THAN LOGIC TO BECOME THE BIGGER GROWTH DRIVER OF ASML

DRAM has now overtaken logic as the most important driver of ASML's litho demand. ASML guided 2026 memory system sales growing more than 75% YoY, compared with only 25% growth for advanced logic/foundry. This has already become visible in ASML's order mix, with memory's share of system sales increasing from roughly 30% historically to

\~50% in recent quarters (Exhibit 4).

\- The main reason is that, as we mentioned, DRAM litho intensity is rising faster than in logic. As DRAM manufacturers migrate to increasingly advanced nodes such as 1c and then 1d, the number of critical litho layers continues to increase. In particular, DRAM is seeing a rapid increase in both EUV and immersion layer counts, with EUV increasingly replacing multi-patterning flows that previously used multiple DUV exposures. Every time a customer replaces multi-patterning with single-exposure EUV, a larger share of process spending shifts toward lithography. ASML specifically highlighted that both EUV and immersion demand are rising in DRAM because of this node migration trend.

\- At the same time, DRAM capacity expansion is accelerating faster than logic. Tight supply conditions and elevated pricing in both DDR and HBM have triggered significant investment in new fabs and megafabs. ASML noted that customers are adding meaningful capacity today while also planning multiple future fab expansions. The HBM buildout adds a volume component on top of the technology migration, creating what management described as a "perfect storm" for DRAM litho demand.

\- This is reflected in our EUV exposure growth forecasts with total DRAM EUV exposures will increase 4.5x over the next 5 years, rising from roughly 3.4 MWPM in 2025 to 26.3 MWPM by 2030, compared with logic EUV exposures increasing by just over 3x during the same period. While logic continues to benefit from AI-related demand at 2nm, 3nm and future 1.4nm nodes, the combination of higher litho intensity and faster capacity additions makes DRAM the more powerful growth engine for ASML over the next several years (Exhibit 5).

## EUV ADOPTION CONTINUES TO DRIVE LITHO SHARE GAIN, ESPECIALLY IN DRAM

DRAM scaling continues to support rising lithography intensity as memory manufacturers push to ever-smaller feature sizes. Feature dimensions are expected to shrink from approximately 14nm at 1a to around 10nm at 1d and ultimately toward sub-10nm at 1d node. While the industry may transition from a $6F^{2}$ to a $4F^{2}$ cell architecture starting around 0a, this should be viewed primarily as a temporary easing of scaling requirements rather than a departure from the long-term trend of shrinking feature sizes. Management believes continued node migration will still require increasing EUV adoption, with HNA eventually becoming necessary later in the decade (Exhibit 6).

For this reason, EUV exposure counts are rising rapidly across each DRAM generation. Average EUV layers increase from roughly 2 exposures at 1a, to 3 at 1b, 5 at 1c, and 8 at 1d. EUV usage is expected to rise further at 0a/0b. Increasing EUV layer counts and the ongoing substitution of multi-patterning DUV continue to drive lithography spending growth above overall WFE growth (Exhibit 7)

## HNA ADOPTION: HIGHER LITHO INTENSITY THROUGH PROCESS SIMPLIFICATION

HNA should drive a step-up in litho intensity, similar to the increase seen when EUV LNA replaced DUV multi-patterning. The key reason is that HNA shifts process value from non-litho steps into lithography. Just as EUV LNA increased litho intensity by replacing complex DUV multi-patterning schemes with fewer but more valuable lithography exposures, HNA can replace multiple-patterning LNA flows with single-exposure solutions on critical layers. HNA's higher resolution and contrast allow many critical layers that would otherwise require LNA multi-patterning flows, such as SALELE, to be printed in a single exposure. By eliminating multiple masks, exposures, litho-etch loops, and cut steps, HNA simplifies process flows, improves pattern fidelity and yield, and reduces overall process complexity. Although an HNA exposure is more expensive than an LNA exposure, it replaces a significant amount of non-litho processing and can reduce total process cost. As a result, a larger share of WFE spend moves into lithography, increasing litho intensity. We therefore expect a step-up in litho intensity as customers transition from LNA multi-patterning to HNA single-exposure patterning (Exhibit 8, Exhibit 9).

Intel's adoption of HNA in 18A this year (Intel Foundry is using HNA on the Intel 18A) confirms that the technology is ready for high-volume manufacturing and removes a key uncertainty around adoption. However, we believe the strongest economic case for HNA is in DRAM, as DRAM dies are smaller than logic dies and therefore do not require stitching. This translates into higher throughput and a lower cost per exposure, making HNA adoption easier to justify economically. SK hynix has the clearest early signal: the company announced in September 2025 that it had assembled ASML's TWINSCAN EXE:5200B, the first production-oriented HNA system, at its M16 fab for next-generation DRAM development. Samsung is also moving quickly, with industry reports indicating that it ordered two ASML EXE:5200B tools, with the first expected by late 2025 and the second in H1 2026. Samsung logic should follow shortly after, while TSMC appears likely to delay broad HNA insertion until around 10A, after prioritizing R&D first and extending LNA for earlier nodes (Exhibit 10).

## DUV IS ANOTHER GROWTH DRIVER INSTEAD OF DRAG

While investor attention is understandably focused on EUV, we continue to believe the outlook for DUV remains underappreciated. As we highlighted in our (ASML: Don't forget DUV. Outperform) report, the blended DUV:EUV capex ratio remains approximately 1:1, implying that every incremental dollar spent on EUV drives a corresponding dollar of DUV demand. In DRAM, DUV still accounts for roughly 50% of litho spend even at the 1c node, meaning the rapid expansion of EUV-intensive DRAM capacity also drives substantial DUV demand. In advanced logic, the DUV:EUV ratio is lower at roughly 1:4, but absolute DUV spending remains resilient. Although EUV has replaced some DUV multi-patterning, higher litho intensity, increasing wafer capacity, and a richer mix of ArFi tools have kept DUV capex broadly stable (Exhibit 11).

A second driver is China, where we expect WFE to grow at a CAGR of more than 15% over the next three years, reaching approximately US\$77Bn by 2028. With no access to EUV and limited domestic lithography capabilities, China remains heavily dependent on DUV, often requiring additional multi-patterning steps that further increase DUV intensity. We estimate China's advanced logic capacity will increase massively over the next few years, providing a structural tailwind for DUV demand (Exhibit 12).

Despite consensus DUV estimates revision following ASML's Q2 results, our DUV revenue forecasts remain above street throughout the next five years (Exhibit 13).

## CHINA COMPETITION CONCERN IS OVERDONE

On July 27th, it was reported that China is mass producing its own DUV (ArFi) litho tools, with 5 units expected this year and 20 units next year. Even if that is true, we see little to worry about, given that:

1. China's litho tools are likely to be much slower in throughput and inferior on specs like critical dimension, resolution, overlay, and uptime. While the reported tool appears to be real and installed at several customer sites, the key issue is not whether it can print patterns, but whether it can meet HVM requirements. Based on channel checks, performance appears materially below ASML's current ArFi platforms and likely below older NXT generations (\~1950). In lithography, throughput, uptime, overlay accuracy, defectivity, and manufacturing consistency ultimately determine fab economics, and there is currently no evidence that Chinese tools are competitive on these metrics.

2. When China's tool is ready, it actually alleviates the risk of further restriction of ASML's ArFi tools to China. A viable domestic alternative could weaken the policy argument for restricting mature imported ArFi systems, as tool availability would no longer be the limiting factor. Ironically, progress by Chinese litho vendors could over time support a relaxation rather than a tightening of restrictions.

3. There has always been competition in DUV, as Nikon has been and still produces ArFi tools. ASML has competed against Nikon in immersion lithography for decades. Despite entering only a few years behind ASML, Nikon never closed the gap in throughput, overlay, and productivity, and consequently retained only a limited market position.

4. In a fair market competition, ASML has an absolute technology advantage. Chinese fabs care primarily about productivity, yield, and cost of ownership. While domestic suppliers will benefit from strategic support, customers are unlikely to replace superior equipment before local alternatives demonstrate comparable performance in production environments.

5. Worst case, it just puts ASML in the same situation as every other equipment vendor, and not worse. Most WFE companies already face local Chinese competitors in at least part of their portfolios. Even under a downside scenario where domestic lithography gains share over time, ASML would simply face the same localization dynamics that other equipment suppliers already navigate.

AMAT has not yet reported CYQ2 results, so we use only CYQ1 data. Source: Company reports, Bloomberg, Bernstein analysis

6. ASML's China exposure has dropped to just 16% in 1H26, the lowest among peers. China represented 32% of ASML revenue in 2025, but management now expects the figure to be around 20% in 2026. At the same time, future growth increasingly comes from EUV, where China remains excluded. Importantly, Chinese lithography demand remains so strong that both ASML and domestic vendors are likely able to coexist for many years, as China's planned capacity expansion exceeds the supply currently available from local tool makers (Exhibit 14).

We hence find the market concern around ASML's China competition overdone.

## CAPACITY EXPANSION ENSURES SUFFICIENT SUPPLY

A common concern among investors is whether ASML's capacity can keep pace with the accelerating demand for advanced logic and DRAM. We believe this risk is increasingly limited. ASML is planning to expand both LNA EUV and immersion DUV capacity by roughly 30% annually through 2028, increasing EUV capacity from 65 systems in 2026 to \~85 in 2027 and \~110 in 2028, while ArFi capacity rises from 130 systems to \~170 and \~220, respectively. Additionally, HNA capacity should reach 20 units in 2027/28 (Exhibit 15).

Importantly, management indicated that these targets do not represent a hard ceiling and are supported by strong order visibility extending into 2028. ASML highlighted that customer demand remains exceptionally strong, order momentum is robust, and capacity plans are still being refined with customers. As a result, both EUV and DUV capacity could be increased further if demand warrants. In our view, this aggressive capacity expansion significantly reduces concerns that litho supply could become a bottleneck for advanced-node growth. Instead, it provides confidence that the expected ramp in advanced logic, DRAM, and rising litho intensity can be supported by adequate tool availability over the next several years

## PRICING UPSIDE REMAINS UNDERAPPRECIATED

We believe investors might underestimate ASML's pricing power. Today, pricing is primarily driven by productivity improvements, with customers paying more for higher throughput and better performance. Consistent with management's comments, we forecast EUV ASPs to increase by roughly 10% in 2027 and a further 7% in 2028, driven by higher productivity and the transition to more advanced EUV systems.

Importantly, there may be additional upside beyond productivity-linked pricing. Industry reports suggest ASML is also exploring like-for-like price increases, reflecting its unique market position, strong demand environment, and expanding value proposition. Such increases are not included in our current forecasts. Recent commentary from both TSMC and Intel also appears to acknowledge ongoing equipment price inflation.

Our current estimates of margin expansion do not include any broad-based like-for-like price increases, yet we remain above street. We forecast GM expanding to 60% and OPM to 50% by 2030 versus Cons at 59.1% and 46.7%, respectively. Our earnings estimates are materially ahead of street, with our EPS 22% above cons in 2029 and 41% above cons in 2030. As a result, any successful implementation of like-for-like price increases would represent incremental upside to revenue, margins, and earnings beyond our current forecasts (Exhibit 16, Exhibit 17).

## VALUATION LOOKS INCREASINGLY ATTRACTIVE

We expect ASML to significantly outperform its WFE peers, with revenue growing from €32.7bn in 2025 to €71.9bn by 2028 (30% CAGR), ahead of LRCX (26%), AMAT (25%), TEL (23%) and KLAC (21%). Growth is driven by rising lithography intensity as advanced semiconductor manufacturing increasingly relies on EUV, concentrating a greater share of process value in lithography. This positions ASML to capture a larger share of semiconductor capex. As a result, we expect ASML's EPS to more than triple by 2028 (45% CAGR), significantly outpacing the broader WFE peer group (Exhibit 18), (Exhibit 19).

Despite this superior growth outlook, ASML's valuation premium to the WFE group has effectively disappeared. Historically, the stock traded at an average premium of around $60\%$ to peers, reflecting its dominant competitive position in Lithography and strategic importance to the semiconductor ecosystem. Today, however, ASML currently trades at 31x 1-year forward P/E, slightly below the WFE average of 31.3x. More strikingly, ASML trades at just 23.5x 2-year forward P/E, one of the lowest multiples within the peer group and below the WFE average of 24.9x, despite having the strongest expected EPS growth through 2028 and a solid competitive moat (Exhibit 20). Relative to broader benchmarks, ASML currently trades broadly in line with its historical premium to the SOX and EDM (Exhibit 21, Exhibit 22, Exhibit 23, Exhibit 24).

We believe ASML deserves to re-establish a premium valuation. The company is the sole provider of EUV lithography systems and faces no credible competition in its core market. At the same time, increasing lithography intensity across advanced logic and memory manufacturing should continue to expand ASML's value capture opportunity. This unique positioning enhances pricing power, supports margin expansion and increases the durability of future earnings growth.

We keep our target PE of 40x and our TP at EUR 2,500. We reiterate our Outperform rating and top pick. For ASML's U.S. listing, our price target is USD 2,859.
