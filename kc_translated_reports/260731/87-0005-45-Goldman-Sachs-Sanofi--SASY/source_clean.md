# Sanofi (SASY.PA)
# Strong commercial execution, but pipeline misses continue to weigh on the multiple


Following 2Q26 results, we update our model for: (i) latest FX trends; (ii) 2Q26 results; and (iii) key product-related commentary/trends from the print as well as announced asset discontinuations. In the short term (FY26/FY27) we increase Business EPS by 2-3%, however longer term we cut EPS by c.1% as we increase R&D investment and as we have taken out the revenue contribution from discontinued assets. Commercial execution remains strong, particularly for Dupixent, further supporting the strong near-term earnings momentum. However, the string of R&D failures with the release as well as product discontinuations, as some assets did not meet an internal efficacy bar for success, has reignited investor concerns around the pipeline's ability to replace/ offset the long-term competitive threat from Dupixent biosimilars, sending the stock back towards YTD lows - which on balance, could have been an over-reaction as expectations for some of the R&D programmes was arguably low. Key levers management have to offset long-term growth concerns including (1.) BD/M&A, (2.) adding assets to the Regeneron alliance and (3.) improvements in R&D productivity, are all likely to take time before each can have a meaningful impact, therefore we remain on the sidelines and reiterate our Neutral rating. Following the changes we cut our 12m target price to €79 (prev. €84), which implies c.9% upside post the results sell-off price.

Progress on the R&D turnaround and BD/M&A will be key to rebuilding investor confidence. With the shares closing c.-9% following results, we believe investors are likely to require a more structured turnaround plan and tangible evidence that management can successfully execute on the pipeline transformation before sentiment meaningfully improves. ## Key takeaways from earnings call/ investor conversations

R&D transformation underway, although a broader turnaround will take time. Management indicated that the early phases of its R&D transformation have begun through the strategic review of the late-stage pipeline to enable resource reallocation towards assets with stronger scientific merit and commercial potential and acknowledged that further discontinuations (beyond already announced) remain possible. The new CEO highlighted an initial focus on managing operational risk by strengthening scientific rigour, streamlining governance and ensuring decisions are made at the appropriate level, alongside greater accountability and fact-based decision making. The company also expects a moderate increase in R&D investment in the short term, with spending evolving alongside potential business development and M&A opportunities, noting that the majority of its R&D budget is allocated to clinical development. Management acknowledged he historical pipeline failures and R&D challenges and indicated that a turnaround will take time, although no specific timeline was provided.

☐ Our view; Given R&D cycle times to fully turnaround an R&D organisation we believe this could take 7+ years. It is difficult to assess if the lack of R&D productivity lies in operational execution of trials or knowledge of the science. Therefore, we would expect investors to remain cautious on the potential speed of recovery in R&D productivity. Management hinted that there could be a dedicated R&D update outlining a more comprehensive turnaround strategy, including defined priorities, milestones and timelines, as well as more details of potential further pipeline pruning in 2H26. Such an event could be useful for the market in understanding the new vision for the R&D organisation or new head of R&D, but as above, it will likely take time before full credit is given on the pipeline.

Regeneron alliance remains strategically important, with scope to enhance collaboration and expand the partnership. Management reiterated the strategic importance of its alliance with Regeneron, highlighting the significant success achieved with Dupixent while acknowledging that there is scope to make the collaboration more agile and transparent. On the potential expansion of the alliance, Sanofi noted that discussions are ongoing, with options to eventually include additional assets from both companies. Further, management also clarified that the alliance does not constrain Sanofi's M&A strategy and that there are no barriers to pursuing acquisitions independently. However, when evaluating external opportunities, the company indicated that it considers whether an acquired asset could generate greater value if developed within the Regeneron collaboration.

Our view; the Regeneron alliance remains complex, particularly with ongoing disagreements between the companies related to allocation of resources. Ultimately, we believe it is in the best interests of each company to leverage the Dupixent rebates to enhance competitive positioning of the immunology pipeline assets, however with a potential imbalance of assets to contribute to the relationship (Sanofi - pre-clinical STAT6 vs. Regeneron's long acting IL13 and IL13/IL4 portfolio), this could lead to a cash payment from Sanofi to

Regeneron - as part of the alliance - adding another layer of complexity. Therefore it could take time before the market has greater visibility over the potential for the Alliance to drive long term growth.

BD/M&A strategy to extend beyond early-stage assets, while maintaining a disciplined, science-led approach. Management indicated that, while Sanofi has historically focused on acquiring early-stage assets, it is now also considering late-stage and potentially commercial assets where they offer an attractive strategic fit. The company emphasised that future transactions are unlikely to be centered on a single large acquisition, but rather on opportunities that offer the strongest scientific rationale, strategic relevance and financial returns. Management also reiterated that BD/M&A activity will remain focused on its core therapeutic pillars of immunology, rare diseases and vaccines.

Bulls we spoke with are focusing on commercial strength, and believe that there may be too much of a focus by the market on the Regeneron partnership. While Dupixent underlying beat was strong, with 80% of the Pharma beat driven by Dupixent, and the market focused on post Dupi LoE, strength could create an overhang longer term. The cash generated is a key positive, in our view, but the market needs to better understand how management will deploy the cash for future BD/M&A. Some investors pushed back on our First Take where we suggested there was an underlying miss of 2-3%, as we did not add back one-time R&D expenses relating to discontinued products (>€200m increased R&D costs). In our view, pipeline discontinuations are a real cost and should not be added back when analysing the results, otherwise adding back/ ignoring this cost could reward poor pipeline productivity. Some investors questioned why the market was so focused on the Regeneron partnership – and here we think the partnership does matter as the internal pipeline has not delivered. If the fully owned pipeline were to deliver a number of positive trials in the near-medium term, it would reduce reliance on the Regeneron partnership.

Bears questioned why there is not more downside potential given peer performance ahead of LoEs, as well as the limited pipeline. Bearish investors we spoke to flagged that BMY traded as low as 6x forward PE, which suggests potential for more downside with Sanofi currently trading around 8x. While we have decreased our target multiple to 8x (from 8.5x), we believe the strong commercial performance and cash flow (despite potentially increasing the impact of the patent cliff) could support the multiple as it gives Sanofi more capacity for deals. Bears continue to see an urgent need for M&A - both commercial/ late stage and clinical stage to help diversify the portfolio.

## Valuation and risks

We are Neutral rated. We derive our EU large cap pharma price targets using a 50:50 blend of a DCF-based valuation and a P/E multiple-based approach. Our bottom-up DCF methodology utilises an 8% WACC and -2% TGR and suggests a valuation of €75 per share (previously €83 per share). Our P/E methodology utilises an 8x (down from 8.5x reflecting pipeline asset discontinuations which impact long term growth potential) target multiple applied to 2027E EPS, and suggests a valuation of €82 per share (previously €85 per share). Blended, this results in a 12-month price target of €79 per share (previously €84 per share) and ADR price target of \$45 (previously \$49).

## Upside risks to our view and price target:

1. Strong clinical pipeline readouts, including Phase 2 readouts, driving a positive reaction;

2. Faster-than-expected sales growth of Dupixent, Altuviiio, Ayvakit and Beyfortus, driving positive earnings momentum; and,

3. Capital allocation (i.e. extending the current buyback programme or M&A) could also have a positive impact.

## Downside risks:

1. Disappointments on clinical pipeline readouts driving concerns over the long-term replacement strategy for Dupixent;

2. Downward EPS consensus revisions due to FX in the short term but also longer term in other operating income as Regeneron payaway is factored in; and,

3. Slower-than-expected sales growth of Dupixent, Altuviiio and Beyfortus.
