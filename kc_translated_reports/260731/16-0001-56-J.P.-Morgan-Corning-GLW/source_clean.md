# JPM
## Corning
## 2Q26 Review: Falls Short of Demanding Buy-Side Bar; However, LT Optical Story Intact Despite Mixed Print

Corning's 2Q26 print delivered a beat relative to sell-side expectations but fell short of the more demanding buy-side bar, an outcome we had flagged heading into the quarter, as a genuinely robust Optical-Enterprise result (AI sales nearly doubled and order rates accelerated) was overshadowed by Optical-Carrier performance that came in well below expectations (traditional customer lumpiness), while the balance of the portfolio tracked more in line. Beyond Optical, the print also carried a cautious near-term tone on Gorilla Glass and the smartphone market into 2H due to memory headwinds, which was layered on top of a weaker forward yen assumption of ¥150 for 2027 and beyond vs. ¥120 prior (intends to neutralize net income impact through its established toolkit of hedging, pricing, and cost reduction) and rounded out by a higher capex run-rate (\~\$2 bn in 2026 and \~\$2.5 bn in 2027) to support the optical build-out (though notably underpinned by customer funding and long-term agreements). Importantly, while these factors likely disappointed near-term expectations, we do not see any of them altering the long-term earnings power, as management remained emphatically bullish on optical across both enterprise and carrier, as well as scale-out and the upcoming scale-up opportunities. Solar, meanwhile, is also beginning to hit its stride now that the maintenance headwind is behind it, with management guiding to improving sales and profitability into 3Q26 and beyond, which is likely contributing to its more constructive tone on the operating margin trajectory, even as it reiterated a 20%+ target near-term. To that end, we are raising our near-term estimates for GLW modestly, on better results and guidance relative to our pre-print forecast, while leaving our out-year forecast largely unchanged, as we see the print continuing to reinforce the medium- to long-term outlook. That said, we are lowering our Dec-27 price target to \$170 (from \$200 prior), as we now ascribe a 30x target multiple, which is more in line with the broader optical peer group.

\- 2Q26 Results: Earnings beat relative to sell-side consensus led by better-than-anticipated revenue. Revenue tracked to \$4,738mn (vs. JPMe of \$4,641 mn, consensus of \$4,645 mn, and guide of \$4,600 mn), supported by robust Enterprise Optical Communications demand from AI products. Meanwhile, gross margins tracked to 39.6% (vs. JPMe of 38.6% and consensus of 38.5%) primarily on account of better-than-expected Optical margins/profits, while operating margins tracked to 20.9% (vs. JPMe of 20.7% and consensus of 20.4%). Net-net, better operating margins led to EPS tracking at \$0.78 (vs. JPMe of \$0.77, consensus of \$0.76 and prior guide of \$0.73-\$0.77).

\- 3Q26 Guide: Revenue tracks softer than expectations while earnings modestly beat. Revenue was guided to \$4,950 mn (vs. JPMe of \$4,912 mn and consensus of \$4,997 mn heading into the print), with a strong Optical outlook offset by a softer outlook for Glass Innovations. Meanwhile, earnings were guided to \$0.85-\$0.89 (midpoint at \~\$0.87 vs. JPMe of \$0.86 and consensus of \$0.86, as higher Optical mix should drive improving profitability overall.

## Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation

## Investment Thesis

We are maintaining our Neutral rating on Corning on the balance of a strong underlying optical demand environment where the company is well positioned to benefit given its leadership in fiber cables and connectors both inside and outside the data center, offset by a lofty valuation premium that investors are ascribing to the shares that in our view embeds blue-sky scenarios and leaves little margin for error on risks related to optical capacity and adoption as well as the remaining portion of the business tied to non-optical markets.

## Valuation

We are lowering our December 2027 price target to \$170 from \$200, based on valuing our 2028E EPS estimate at a 30x P/E multiple (from 35x prior). We believe the \~30x multiple is more in-line with the broader optical peer group, while still accounting for Corning's unique positioning as a U.S.-based, vertically integrated, large-scale supplier of optical fiber cable and connectors, products that are well positioned to benefit from the AI infrastructure investment backdrop.

# Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks

Corning (Neutral; Price Target: \$170.00)

## Investment Thesis

We are maintaining our Neutral rating on Corning on the balance of a strong underlying optical demand environment where the company is well positioned to benefit given its leadership in fiber cables and connectors both inside and outside the data center, offset by a lofty valuation premium that investors are ascribing to the shares that in our view embeds blue-sky scenarios and leaves little margin for error on risks related to optical capacity and adoption as well as the remaining portion of the business tied to non-optical markets.

## Valuation

We are lowering our December 2027 price target to \$170 from \$200, based on valuing our 2028E EPS estimate at a 30x P/E multiple (from 35x prior). We believe the \~30x multiple is more in-line with the broader optical peer group, while still accounting for Corning's unique positioning as a U.S.-based, vertically integrated, large-scale supplier of optical fiber cable and connectors, products that are well positioned to benefit from the AI infrastructure investment backdrop.

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

## Industry Upside Risks

Display pricing could strengthen more than expected. Despite solid growth in displays, including glass used in consumer devices over the last few years, pricing has historically declined, though in more recent years has shown signs of stabilization and improvement. Nonetheless, greater than expected strength in pricing for the display market would likely lead to upside to our forecasts.

## Industry Downside Risks

Delays in the copper-to-optical transition in the scale-up domain. A slower than expected adoption of optics inside the rack, driven by customer architecture choices, supply chain challenges, or innovations that push out the photonics roadmap, would likely lead to downside to our forecasts.

## Company Upside Risks

Supply chain leadership in optical fiber cable and connectors. Corning is one of the few vertically integrated U.S. optical companies, spanning raw fiber to cables and connectors. Better-than-expected market share in passive optical content due to better cost, reliability of supply, or U.S.-sourced manufacturing could drive upside to our estimates.

Faster than expected adoption of Hemlock's solar solutions. We expect Hemlock's solar solutions to find increasing adoption as customers look to benefit from U.S. federal support, which is encouraging domestic substitution. Faster than expected adoption could drive upside to our estimates.

## Company Downside Risks

Memory headwinds could adversely impact unit volumes in Corning's Glass Innovations segment. Continued growth in memory prices would alter the cost structures for end-markets like TVs and mobile phones, which can reduce demand for display panels and, in turn, adversely impact unit volumes in Corning's Glass Innovations segment.

Drop performance requirements from Gorilla cover glass could reach a ceiling. One of the primary drivers of the premium pricing for Gorilla cover glass is the superior drop performance relative to the competition, which Corning improves further with every generation of the product. However, customers could see further improvements beyond a certain level of drop performance as unnecessary and could be unwilling to pay continued premiums for improvements beyond a certain level, limiting ASP opportunity for Corning.
