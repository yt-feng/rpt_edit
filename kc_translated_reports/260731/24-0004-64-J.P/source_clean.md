## Semiconductor/SPE
## Implications from Teradyne's results: Progress in achieving second-vendor status with hyperscaler

Teradyne (covered by Joseph Cardoso) announced Apr-Jun results at 05:35 JST on July 29 and held a briefing at 21:30. As of the end of the briefing (22:45), the stock was up 11% on the day (the S&P 500 was flat). As planned, during the Apr-Jun quarter the company shipped multi-system testers to a GPU customer that had placed an initial order in Jan-Mar. In addition, as expected by some market participants, it appears that Teradyne is making progress in winning second-vendor certification from a major hyperscaler (we note that the first vendor is Advantest). This would be the company's second such customer win. Although single-vendor purchasing has been the norm in SoC testers to this point, we are starting to see a gradual shift toward dual-vendor purchasing. However, we think any market share shifts will happen only gradually, and likely not until 2027 at the earliest. Amid the rapid expansion of the CPU-related equipment market, Teradyne's strength is in equipment for ARM, while Advantest (based on management's comments at its July 29 results briefing) is strong across a wide range of customers and architectures (e.g., ARM, x86, fabless, IDM), so we do not think major concern is warranted at this time.

\- Teradyne's results: Company-wide Apr-Jun sales totaled \$1,329 million (+4% QoQ, Bloomberg consensus estimate \$1,217 million), with semiconductor tester sales amounting to \$1,122 million (+1%). Sales guidance for Jul-Sep is \$1,200-1,300 mn (-10% to -2%, Bloomberg consensus \$1,035 million). For the full FY2026, Teradyne looks for (1) a 1H weighting in Compute, (2) weakness in Mobile, and (3) a 2H weighting in Memory and Auto/Industrial.

\- Market outlook: After reporting Oct-Dec 2025 earnings, Teradyne forecast that the ATE market would grow by 20-40% YoY in 2026, with SoC testers growing faster than memory testers (low double-digit growth YoY), but made no further comment on this at Jan-Mar earnings. Management did say that with regard to memory testers, it sees potential for 40%+ YoY growth, driven by growing demand for HBM base die testing. Teradyne projects that ATE's share of the WFE market will rise from around 4% in 2023 to around 8% in 1H 2026, eventually stabilizing in the 7-9% range. It also updated its outlook for the CPO/SiPh tester market from around \$100 million in 2026 and \$300-700 million over the medium term to \$300-700 million by 2028.

\- Market share: Although single-vendor purchasing has been the norm in SoC testers to this point, we are starting to see a gradual shift toward dual-vendor purchasing. Achieving second-vendor status takes 9-12 months and involves four steps: (1) acquiring an opportunity to compete, (2) developing a working solution, (3) supplying a correlated solution, and (4) ramping up production. This is followed by a “fast follower” phase, during which market share rises incrementally to around 30%, then a mature phase, during which market share is in the 30-70% range. It appears that in the Compute field, Teradyne is currently in the mature phase with one customer, the expansion phase with another customer, and awaiting certification before ramping up production with a third customer.
