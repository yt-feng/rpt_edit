# Beyond the print: the growth algorithm debate is still on

# "There is no way Hermes will be trading anywhere close to its current multiple in 12 months. It will either be much higher or much lower"

— Investor, early June 2026

Key debate still on and the valuations are down. Hermès is one of the most debated names in our coverage universe. Unfortunately, 2Q results did not provide the confidence needed for the bull case and bullet-proof sustainability of the business model, leaving room for the bear debate to continue.

Bull and Bear views: Bulls view current valuation levels as an extremely attractive entry point and see the recent slowdown as temporary. Bears, by contrast, argue that the desirability of iconic bags has weakened, competition for the high-end consumer has intensified, and softer trends in ex-leather métiers signal lower barriers to quota bag purchases pointing to an extended slowdown and potential change in leather goods growth algorithm, which implies 6% volume growth.

What does the company say: The company continues to highlight challenges in APAC and no major sequential change/acceleration in all regions except for MENA and France, both impacted by tourism and MENA consumer. While Leather Goods growth remains strong at +DD (10.2% in 2Q26), this is below the FY growth algorithm of 11-12% growth with c.6% volume contribution, but we believe 6% volume expectation is still in place (despite somewhat less clear communication at the conference call).

What does the company do? Expand capacity for 6-7% volume growth: Our analysis suggests supply is there, at least until 2030, with the number of production employees, the closest proxy, growing at c.7% 5Y CAGR in 25-30E (our estimates), 1% below the previous decade.

We employ production employees as a proxy to understand volume constraints and the investments needed to sustain c.6% volume growth going forward. We estimate that Hermes' capacity expansion should allow 6-7% volume growth in the next 5 years, in line with our Leather Goods forecasts. As a result, we expect the metier to expand to c.49% of Hermes' sales by 2030 from c.44% in 2025.

...In this context, change in algorithm could be painful: We believe that the Leather Goods demand model remains intact and we assume that there are no material risks to our mid-to-long-term volume assumptions. Any major changes in demand in leather goods could raise concerns around over-investment and over-capacity, and could put significant pressure on margins and returns.

What is the (soft) data saying: Outside of anecdotal evidence, we have not been able to find data to indicate 1) lower demand for iconic bags, 2) materially increased supply of iconic bags in resale (we have some comments on increase in availability of "other" bags, though), 3) deterioration of price premium for iconic bags in resale (on the opposite) and 4) lower required spend needed to qualify for quota bag purchase at Hermes store. Our interviews with industry participants, including a brick and mortar Hermes reseller, suggest increase, not decrease, of resale premium, given recent price increases.

What we think: For FY26, we are more comfortable with 10.5% FY growth in metier, with 11/12% in 3/4Q26 rather than FY. We believe Leather Goods could comfortably deliver HSD-LDD (we are at 10.5%) CAGR in 26-29. Our EW rating is focused on 1) 2026 market challenges, which underpin our below-consensus estimates of c.8% cFX growth over FY27–29e; 2) medium-term risks in Asia Pacific (52% of sales incl. Japan), where we forecast c.7% CAGR; 3) confidence in longer-term scalability of the artisanal model.

Valuation is becoming more compelling, but we think the stock needs clear evidence that group growth can sustainably re-accelerate into the HSD range, whether via Leather Goods or stronger non-Leather métiers momentum. We do not expect 3Q26 to be that catalyst (cFX growth 7.6%).

## Model Update

We lower our top-line growth estimates for 3Q26 from 8.3% to 7.6%, as we take a more cautious view on APAC given continued weakness. We only slightly lower our Leather Goods 3Q26 growth by 50bps to 11.0%, and cut RTW growth by the same amount. We take larger cuts in Perfumes and Cosmetics. This brings us to a FY26 growth forecast of 7.0% cFX at group level, from 7.3% previously.

As far as the gross margin is concerned, we leave our 2H26 GM forecast unchanged at 71.3%, down 30bp y/y. Overall, we increase our FY26 EBIT margin estimate to 40.8% following the beat in H1. Our estimate changes imply a c.1% EPS increase in FY26-28. We keep our PT unchanged at EUR 1,700 with unchanged DCF assumptions at 7.5% WACC and 3.5% long-term growth. We remain EW.
