# Samsung Electronics (005930.KS)
# Earnings Review: 2Q26 OP inline with prelim: solid memory fundamentals and attractive risk/reward; raise TP to W490K and reiterate Buy


Inline 2Q26 OP with sequential improvements in DRAM/NAND margin despite special bonus provisioning

Samsung Electronics (SEC) reported 2Q26 revenue of W171.5tn and operating profit of W89.5tn, in line with its pre-earnings guidance of W171tn/W89.4tn, respectively. Memory profit was largely inline with our expectations post the pre-earnings guidance, where we believe solid memory pricing growth led its DRAM operating margin to improve qoq to close to 80%, while same for NAND exceeded 60% despite the provisioning for the special bonus during the quarter.

While the share price corrected close to 40% during the past month, we do not believe that there have been meaningful changes in the core fundamentals of the company - memory demand is continuing to significantly outpace supply with the gap expected to widen in 2027 and continue in 2028, LTAs are being signed that protect downside risk, and the company continues to see improvements in its HBM business where it is now aiming to become the market leader. Adding the potential for a meaningful upside in shareholder returns, we remain constructive on the name now trading at 3.1X P/E and 1.2X P/B on 2027E numbers offering close to a 50% ROE. Reiterate Buy with an updated 12m TP of W490,000 (up from W480,000).

2027 a tighter year than 2026 for memory, with tightness to persist in 2028: SEC is seeing the expansion of agentic AI leading to an exponential growth in token demand and, as a consequence, strong growth in both AI server and general server demand. Despite suppliers' effort to increase spending to build new capacity, with the substantial lead time to see a meaningful increase in supply, SEC does not expect a meaningful supply increase until 2028. Also, based on the pre-booked demand from customers that the company is seeing, SEC continues to expect the supply and demand imbalance this year to widen in 2027, and expects the tightness to persist even into 2028.

expectations: SEC mentioned that the substantial memory tightness is leading to customers asking for long-term agreements (LTA), and given the limited supply available, the company is engaging in discussions prioritizing the customers who would be able to guarantee demand over the long-term. SEC's LTA discussions are typically based on a five-year term, and structured as rolling contracts where negotiations would be conducted each year to extend the contract period by an additional year. The company also disclosed that it has completed contracts with the top five global data center customers, and is currently in the final stage of negotiations with five additional large customers related to AI demand. Once the contracts are finalized, multi-year contract volumes are expected to reach approximately 60–70% of planned production capacity. To strengthen the binding nature of contract, SEC is including substantial advance payments in the form of deposits held over multiple years. Different pricing models are applied for LTAs depending on the customer group and product category, and for general-purpose products, the company has set floor prices at a level sufficient to prepare for future investment risks that may arise from fluctuations in market prices. Based on recent conversation with investors, we believe the details and terms of the LTAs that SEC disclosed were better than expectations, and expect these LTAs to help provide much better earnings visibility and stability compared to previous cycles.

Confidence in HBM leadership: SEC aims to maintain a balanced supply mix between HBM and conventional DRAM independent of the margin gap between these two products, to support the sustained AI demand. At the same time, the company continues to see a significant improvement in its HBM business, where we believe revenue grew by more than 80% qoq in 2Q26 on the back of a solid shipment of both HBM3E and HBM4, and believe the company is on track to see its HBM sales grow by more than 3X this year. The company expects its HBM4 revenue to grow by more than 3X qoq in 3Q26, and expects HBM4 to reach more than 60% of its HBM revenue in 2H26. As a result, SEC targets to achieve HBM market share in 2H26 similar to its DRAM market share, implying that it is targeting to become the market share leader in the AI memory space.

## ■ Special shareholder return announcement may be imminent: Despite some

expectations in the market (based on our conversation with investors) for the company to provide an update to its shareholder return plan and potentially announce special dividend and/or buyback, SEC did not provide a new update at the earnings call. However, the company did mention that it is currently going through an in-depth discussion internally, and considering several options including a special dividend, for which the company plans to provide an update in the near future. We continue to believe that the significant FCF generation by the company could lead to meaningful upside in the company's shareholder return outlook.

## Raise earnings estimates and TP to W490,000; reiterate Buy

Mainly reflecting higher conventional DRAM, HBM, and NAND bit shipment assumptions and higher DRAM margin estimates more than offsetting slightly lower DRAM/NAND ASP estimates, we tweak up our 2026E-2028E EPS estimates by \~2%. Accordingly, we raise our 2026E-2027E average SOTP-based 12m TP to W490,000 (from W480,000).

While the share price corrected close to 40% during the past month, we do not think there have been meaningful changes in the core fundamentals of the company - memory demand is continuing to significantly outpace supply with the gap expected to widen in 2027 and continue in 2028, LTAs are being signed that protect downside risk, and the company continues to see improvements in its HBM business where it is now aiming to become the market leader. Adding the potential for a meaningful upside in its shareholder returns, we remain constructive on the name now trading at 3.1X P/E and 1.2X P/B on 2027E numbers offering close to a 50% ROE, and reiterate Buy.

For the preference shares (005935.KS, Buy), we maintain our 12m TP of 360,000, applying our target preferred-to-common discount of 27% (prior 25%), derived from averaging: 1) the pref discount of the 2-factor model, and 2) the average preference shares discount to common shares during the past 1 month. Key risks include 1) major deterioration in memory supply/demand, 2) sharp contraction in smartphone margins, and 3) mobile OLED market share loss.

## Investment Thesis - Samsung Electronics

Samsung Electronics (SEC) is one of the largest tech companies in the world, with the #1 market share in several products including memory chips, OLED panels, smartphones, and TVs. We expect the strong earnings power led by memory to continue, supported by our expectation of solid memory pricing continuing and also remaining at an elevated level. We expect this to be helped by the long-term agreements (LTA) with customers, with a higher level of binding commitment likely this time compared to the past. The company is starting to show meaningful progress in HBM, and also considering the higher scope for meaningful shareholder returns, we remain constructive on the name and reiterate our Buy rating.
