## GSK and AZ 2Q26 results: Read-through to Hansoh, Hengrui and Kelun Biotech

GSK and AstraZeneca's 2Q26 results reinforced their commitment to several therapeutic modalities where Chinese pharma/biotech companies have out-licensed. GSK announced an acceleration of late-stage R&D investment, including multiple new Ph3 studies for risvutatug rezetecan (Ris-Rez, B7-H3 ADC) and mocertatug rezetecan (Mo-Rez, B7-H4 ADC) that it licensed from Hansoh, as well as Ph3 initiation for GSK5784283 (GSK'283), the long-acting anti-TSLP antibody originally from Hengrui. AstraZeneca (AZ) announced sonesitatug vedotin (Sone-Ve, CLDN18.2 ADC) met OS (overall survival) endpoint in the global Ph3 trial CLARITY-Gastric01, making Sone-Ve the first CLDN18.2 ADC to show OS benefit in CLDN+ gastric cancer. AZ in-licensed Sone-Ve from Lepu Biopharma and Keymed. Meanwhile, AZ updated key registrational timelines for datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd, TROP2 ADC) across lung and breast cancer and guided to a Ph3 readout for its B7-H4 ADC in endometrial cancer in 2H27. Overall, we view these updates as another strong validation of China's innovation capabilities. More importantly, they demonstrate that multinational companies are not only in-licensing Chinese assets, but are also committing substantial late-stage development resources to maximize their global commercial potential.

\- For Hansoh, GSK's intention to launch multiple Ph3 studies for its B7-H3 and B7-H4 ADC portfolio signals increasing strategic confidence in both programs. By 2028, GSK could be running 6 Ph3 trials for Ris-Rez and 5 Ph3 trials for Mo-Rez. GSK targets to submit BLA for Ris-Rez in 2L SCLC in 2028. This is especially meaningful as GSK is simultaneously reallocating capital to prioritize its highest-value late-stage assets, implying these programs have successfully cleared internal efficacy, safety and commercial hurdles. Together with the recent positive Ph3 data for Ris-Rez in SCLC and osteosarcoma (see our note here), GSK's continued prioritization further strengthens the investment case for Hansoh's ADC platform which we think can generate long-term commercial value.

\- For Hengrui, the most important takeaway is GSK's decision to initiate Ph3 development for GSK'283 in asthma, COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease), and CRSwNP (chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps), the long-acting anti-TSLP antibody originally from Hengrui. Progression into registrational studies demonstrates that the asset has successfully passed GSK's internal technical and commercial evaluation hurdles following GSK's acquisition of Aiolos Bio which in-licensed the drug from Hengrui, reducing execution uncertainty and increasing confidence in milestone realization over time. Hengrui is eligible to receive over US\$1 billion in total milestone payments plus tiered royalties for the further development and commercialization of the anti-TSLP antibody.

\- For Kelun Biotech, AstraZeneca's updated catalyst roadmap for datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd) deserves close attention given its role as the principal benchmark for TROP2 ADCs in multiple solid tumors. Upcoming readouts from Avanzar/TROPION-Lung07 in 2H26/1H27 and TROPION-Breast03/05 will continue to define the efficacy benchmark for TROP2 ADCs. We don't think Dato-DXd will pose much competitive threat to Kelun Biotech's sac-TMT in NSCLC (non-small cell lung cancer) based on existing clinical data.
