# Monthly Tracker: June Data Deteriorated; Weaker 3Q Ahead

Home sales softened further in June amid weaker home price trends. We expect the continued slowdown in secondary home sales to accelerate home price declines in 3Q, especially in Tier 2 and below cities that witness increasing inventory levels.

Stay selective: Considering potentially weaker property sales in 3Q, weak 1H results, limited policy upside, and continued fund flow disruption, we still suggest sticking with quality players that have credible self-help alpha. We see CR Land (1109.HK), C&D (1908.HK), and Seazen (601155.SS/1030.HK) offering better risk/reward at current valuation.

(-) Home sales decelerated: CREIS 65-city primary registered sales volume declined 7% y-y in June (vs. +1% in May) and 33-city secondary registered sales volume rose 6% y-y (vs. +14% in May). This brought YTD sales to -14% y-y for primary and +7% y-y for secondary. Also, Bingshan 25-city secondary real-time sales volume softened to +8% y-y MTD (vs. +10% in June), indicating potential further weakness in registered sales ahead.

(-) Weaker home price trends: NBS 70-city new home prices fell 3.5% y-y and 0.2% m-m (vs. -0.2% m-m in May), while secondary prices fell 5.6% y-y and 0.3% m-m (vs. -0.3% m-m in May). Tier 1 cities recorded milder home price gains, +0.1% in primary and +0.3% in secondary (May: +0.2 and +0.4%) amid softer secondary sales. CREIS top-10 cities' secondary prices fell 7.5% y-y and 0.2 m-m (vs. -0.2% m-m in May), while top-100 city prices fell 7.7% y-y and 0.4% m-m (vs. -0.3% m-m in May). Bingshan secondary listing prices fell 10.5% y-y and 0.5% m-m.

(-) Higher secondary listings: Total listings rose 0.2% m-m on average across \~50 sample cities, with slightly more cities recording m-m increases (\~65% vs. 59% in May). New listings continued to drop (-3% m-m and -13% y-y) as more cities recorded m-m declines (76% vs. 67% in May). Relative to end-2025 levels, total listings are higher in 45% of the cities, with 35% reaching record highs (vs. 51% and 31% in May, respectively).

(+/-) Diverging primary inventory: CRIC primary inventory months increased to 31.8x in June (vs. 31.6x in May) in the 70 cities we track. Among these cities, Tier 1 declined to 21.8x (vs. 22.1x), Tier 2 rose to 30.4x (vs. 30.2x), and Tier 3 increased to 42.7x (vs. 42.1x).

(-) Land sales remained sluggish: CREIS 300-city land sales fell 23% y-y in GFA and fell 15% y-y in value in June (vs. -31% and -13% in May, respectively). The average land auction premium was 5.7%. New saleable resources added by the top-100 developers declined 14% y-y, bringing YTD growth to -37% y-y as of 6M26. This may add downside pressure to developers' new launches in 2H.
