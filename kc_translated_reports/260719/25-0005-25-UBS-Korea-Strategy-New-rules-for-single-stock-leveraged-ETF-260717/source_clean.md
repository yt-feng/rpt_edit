## Korea Strategy
## New rules for single-stock leveraged ETF
## New rules on single-stock leveraged ETF announced

The gov't has disclosed measures to curb speculative trading in single-stock leveraged ETFs, including raising the minimum cash deposit to Won30m (5Aug) from an effective Won3m, eliminating the use of substitute collateral for up to 70% of required deposit (19Aug), prohibiting drawdowns of deposits during the investment period, increasing the minimum trading unit from 1 to 20 shares (tentative, Nov) and requiring longer investor education (3hrs vs previous 2hrs). In addition, the gov't suspended launching of new single stock leveraged ETF and banned marketing of the product.

## Impact of the new rules

We see suspending new launches of single-stock leveraged ETFs as likely to support the government's objectives, alongside the Won30m all cash minimum deposit requirement. We expect the latter could reduce retail participation as it is equivalent to 7% of household assets in the third income quintile and 27% of their financial assets. However, we expect limited impact from increased education and higher trading units (20 shares = cUS\$190).

## But market moves are already forcing leveraged ETF down

Importantly, we believe market price actions are already forcing single-stock leveraged ETFs down – AUM (Korea and overseas listed) has fallen from Won24trn peak on 25Jun to current Won17trn (Figures 3-5). Retail net buying of single-stock leveraged ETFs, although still active, is slowly declining (Figures 6-7). This is as investors would have incurred 30-32% loss if ETFs were held since 27May (Korea listed ETF launch) and 44-55% loss if held since 25Jun (peak share price) vs underlying asset performance of 7-9% loss and 22-29% loss respectively. We find total leveraged ETF AUM (Korea and overseas listed) to have fallen as well from Won48trn peak in 22Jun to Won33trn (Figure 9).

## Similar actions seen with leveraged battery sector ETFs launched in 2023

Leveraged ETFs are not new in Korea, unlike the single-stock leveraged ETFs. Notably, the sharp rally in battery stocks in July 2023 was eclipsed by the launch of leveraged battery ETFs (Figure 10). The negative share price moves thereafter grinded down the ETFs, although new ETF launches kept the volatilities high for c6-9M. Battery stocks did not impact KOSPI like now as battery comprised only 16% of KOSPI market cap in Jun2023. The dynamics are quite different now – SEC/SKH comprised 56% of KOSPI market cap as of Jun2026 and single-stock leveraged ETFs turnover is equivalent to 54%/24% of SKH/SEC turnover in July MTD. Considering the 2x leverage, we believe the impact of ETF related flows on the underlying asset could be larger.

## Affirm 9,200 KOSPI target but see volatilities in the near term

We affirm our KOSPI target price of 9,200 (9x NTM PE) (downside/upside 5,500/10,500) given solid 265%/66% EPS growth in 2026/27E and undemanding valuations. But in the near term, we believe AI demand concerns and SEC/SKH profit outlook could keep volatility high, which conversely will help lower the AUM of leveraged ETFs. Given the volatility, we shift our portfolio to barbell strategy – we add Shinsegae, Celltrion and Samsung E&A, and remove HDEC, KAI, KSOE and Coupang from our preferred list.
