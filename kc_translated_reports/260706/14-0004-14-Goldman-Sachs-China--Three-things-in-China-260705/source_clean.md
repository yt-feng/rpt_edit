# China: Three things in China

■ Official PMIs rose in June: The official NBS manufacturing PMI increased from 50.0 in May to 50.3 in June, with the new orders subindex picking up noticeably. The pattern of a higher reading in the quarter-end month (March, June, September, and December) is consistent with the experience over the past 18 months. The official NBS non-manufacturing PMI ticked up from 50.1 to 50.2, with both construction and services PMIs showing marginal increases.

PBOC's used new liquidity tool: The PBOC conducted its first overnight reverse repos on June 29 and June 30, injecting a total of RMB900bn in liquidity. The new tool allows the central bank to prevent interbank interest rates from jumping at month-end, quarter-end, and year-end. In previous years, the 7-day repo rate often spiked to more than 50bp above the policy rate around end-June; this did not happen this year with the PBOC's new liquidity tool. On net, this should lead to slightly lower swap rates in China.

Local marketing takeaways: During the last week of June, we met with local clients in Beijing and Shanghai. Over the past two months, onshore investors' sentiment toward domestic economic growth has become more cautious as macro data have weakened. Expectations for major policy easing remain low, as investors believe policymakers are prioritizing technological innovation and national security, while domestic political cycles may be constraining local officials' ability to boost the economy. In contrast, we have received more inquiries from overseas clients about the household consumption outlook in search of investment opportunities amid depressed valuation.
