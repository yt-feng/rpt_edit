# Global Luxury Goods: Chinese & Western Social Media Boxing Ring 2Q26 - Mixed signals
## Specialist Sales

We dive into luxury brands' 2Q26 relative performance on social media platforms relevant to Chinese and Western consumers.

We observe relatively ‘spiky’ social media trends in 2Q26. Across both Western and Chinese social media sites, we see spikes in interest at brands such as Alexander McQueen, Stella McCartney, and Marc Jacobs, driven by (1) frigid brand heat, creating a very low comparable base, and (2) idiosyncratic events such as the H&M x Stella McCartney collaboration in May'26, and newsflow around LVMH's disposal of Marc Jacobs generating a spike in social media activity. We would expect these one-off spikes in ‘brand heat’ to pass.

Parsing the signal from the noise sees: Chanel sustaining its momentum across both regions, Ralph Lauren in the China, and Zegna maintaining its lead in the West. Prada clinches the top spot in China even as Miu Miu decelerates in both regions.

## Chanel seems to have overshadowed turnarounds at Gucci and Dior to some extent.

Our momentum scores see Chanel taking the $4^{th}$ position in West and the $2^{nd}$ position in China. Dior, in contrast, lands at $16^{th}$ and $14^{th}$ , Gucci at $15^{th}$ and $13^{th}$ - an improvement from 2025's nadir, but still a far cry from Chanel's viral rebirth. We would expect Chanel to enjoy a more significant re-acceleration of performance than Dior and Gucci. Nevertheless, we believe Dior's revival remains on-track, with the LVMH-owned brand likely to deliver a gradual return to positive growth (see Comment: LVMH 2Q26 preview). Gucci, in contrast, has spent 12 to 15 quarters in the wilderness as 'Quiet Luxury' dominated the fashion zeitgeist. These headwinds, though attenuating, continue to persist, even as competition from a resurgent Chanel and improving Dior heats up. We would expect a much more gradual turnaround at Gucci (see Kering: Key themes ahead of 1H26).

Momentum at Zegna - the high-end menswear specialist - remains robust in the West. We believe there are three potential factors to consider: (1) Zegna enjoys an advantage of a low base - it remains a relatively niche brand; (2) The menswear zeitgeist seems to have shifted in Zegna's favour as consumers move towards a slightly more formal "dressy" look, particularly in footwear (see Global Sportswear: The death of sneakers?) - a potential boost to Zegna's iconic Triple Stitch; and (3) consumer polarization and buoyant high-end demand add another tailwind (see Global Luxury Goods: Preparing for consumer polarization and Part 2). Together, these trends dovetail with recent company commentary pointing to solid performance in the West (see Comment: Zegna 2Q26 preview).

Prada Group offers a contrasted performance. The fashion pendulum has swung away from Miu Miu. We see the brand falling -6 places to 18 $^{th}$ in the West, while a 6 rung climb up the ladder still leaves it at 9 $^{th}$ place in China. The main Prada brand continues to see average brand momentum in the West, but clinches pole position in China. The main Prada brand may well deliver incremental brand acceleration in 2Q26 even as Miu Miu struggles against tougher comparables and a higher than average exposure to the Middle East (see Prada: Key themes ahead of 1H26).

## INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS

Questions abound on the long-term rate, trajectory and drivers of global luxury goods demand.

If China was to go back to producing wealth to a larger and larger aspirational consumer audience, global luxury goods would go back to being a global middle class play. In that context, best in class mega-brands would once again lead growth and returns (See Global Luxury Goods: The Six Rings of Quality). These would include LVMH with LV, Hermès, Richemont with Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels.

If instead income and wealth inequality continued to increase, the richest consumers would be driving the global luxury market growth, putting smaller and higher brands at an advantage. These would include high-end specialists like Ferrari, Brunello Cucinelli and Zegna, as well as private companies like Stefano Ricci or Graff.

Given its flexibility and credibility to serve both the high-end and the aspirational consumers, we see Richemont thriving in both scenarios. Which confirms it as our most preferred long-term exposure in the sector. Alternative, investors should also be served well by backing specialists at the high end in our coverage, such as Zegna and Brunello Cucinelli, to surf the wave of wealth creation and resilience at the high-end (Global Luxury Goods: The Future of Small Brands).

We update our financial forecasts to incorporate recent company commentary (see Kering: Key themes ahead of 1H26). We trim our 2Q26 retail CFx forecasts for Gucci by -300bps to -4%. We continue to see Gucci delivering a sequential improvement vs 1Q26's -9%. However, we believe brand re-ignition has yet to be achieved, while we incorporate an incremental, sequential,-100bps headwind from the war in the Middle East. Company commentary guides to a sequential improvement in EBIT margins from 2H25A (at 9.8%) to 1H26E, and then to 2H26E. Assuming Kering would have opted for the most parsimonious form of guidance, we rule out a YoY improvement in 1H26E (vs. 1H25A at 12.4%). As a result, we trim our 1H26E EBIT% forecasts by -70bps from 12.6% to 11.9%. Our FY26E EBIT% forecast fall -100 bps to 12%.

Lower top-line growth and EBIT% estimates drive a -8% and -1% fall in our FY26E/27E EPS forecasts, which leaves us -8% and -3% below VisibleAlpha consensus estimates. Kering has warned that VisibleAlpha consensus estimates are likely unreliable. The company has yet to provide company collected consensus, which would be the preferred reference point.

We value Kering on a target 1.70x relative P/E multiple to the MSCI Europe, applied to a blended NTM+1 forward EPS forecast, arriving at a target price of €220.

## Prada Model Update

We update our financial forecasts to incorporate recent company commentary (see Prada: Key themes ahead of 1H26). We trim our 2Q26 forecasts for Miu Miu to reflect a higher than industry average impact from the war in the Middle East and a continued deceleration of momentum.

We reduce our Group 1H26E EBIT% forecasts to account for: (1) -150bps in FX headwinds, (2) -100bps in marketing costs, representing a temporary phasing effect which will be recouped in 2H26E, and (3) a -350bps to -360bps headwind from the consolidation of Versace (which was only consolidated for 1M in FY25A). This leads to a fall in our FY26E EBIT% forecasts to 18.2%, -190bps below consensus. This leads to a -15% fall in our FY26E EPS forecasts, -9% below consensus.

## CHINESE SOCIAL MEDIA METHODOLOGY

Summary ranking table: Scores rank the different metrics from highest (100) to lowest (0) in relative terms per app. The scores are then averaged by app and then for all apps as representative for all platforms analysed.

We assigned zero score if no presence of the brand on the platform.

RED engagement equals to total # of likes, comments & favorites on all the posts related to the brand search keywords.

WeChat Index (known as 微信指数) is a mobile-end index product based on WeChat big data, which reflects the popularity of key word search within the WeChat ecosystem. The change in popularity reflected by the WeChat Index comes from a comprehensive analysis of WeChat searches, public account articles, and publicly forwarded articles in Moments.

Douyin Luxury Index (known as 奢侈品品牌热 DOU 榜) is a weekly ranking of the 30 hottest luxury brands on Douyin. The Douyin Luxury Index is the weighted average of # of videos published, # of comments and # of purchases based on platform big data. We calculate the monthly average from Weekly Douyin Index. Douyin Luxury Index updated its calculation algorithm since May 2022 causing yoy growth data no longer meaningful.

Weibo engagement equals to total # of posts published which includes both User generated posts and Official Account posts.

Baidu Index (known as 百度指数) allows users to look up the search volume and trend for certain hot keywords and phrases. It can serve as a Baidu keyword research tool.

## LIMITATIONS OF ANALYSIS

Search engines (Google, Baidu and WeChat) are subject to noise, events and news that may not be directly related to the brand. For example, Prada's search volume on Baidu was boosted in January 2021 on back of one of the brand ambassadors' surrogacy controversy news and articles. Burberry's and D&G's search volumes were boosted on back of the Xinjiang Cotton controversy in March 2021.

Weibo data is subject to noise as well, especially for the # of posts metrics. # of posts can be boosted due to interest in the Chinese celebrities the brands work with rather than the brand itself. In addition, due to time constraints, we record total number of posts data on Weibo for April and May 2021 only. We will continue building the data on monthly basis and provide more insights in the future.

Douyin is still new for luxury brands. Unlike Weibo where each brand has launched official accounts, only c.50% of luxury brands have launched official accounts on Douyin. For smaller brands, it might be due to the high cost associated in launching and maintaining the accounts. For some big brands like Chanel, Rolex and Hermès, they may find the Douyin image conflicts with the brand image and exclusivity of the brands.

## WESTERN SOCIAL MEIDA METHODOLOGY

## Summary ranking table:

Scores rank the different metrics from highest (100) to lowest (0) in relative terms per app. The scores are then averaged by app and then for all apps as representative for all platforms analysed.

We assign no score if no presence of the brand on the platform.

We aim to capture both brand momentum and scale. We analyse momentum in terms of quarterly (L3M) year-on-year growth in a metric, for example follower count growth between 3Q23 and 3Q22, in order to minimise seasonality. We analyse scale in terms of the absolute level of a metric, for example follower count as of the end of 1Q24. We note momentum analyses can be volatile both due to brands' organic variance and data quality. Due to data availability, sometimes our dataset spans a period that is slightly longer or shorter than a precise calendar quarter, which is adjusted for and footnoted.

## Platforms:

Instagram: we track followers as a measure of scale, and likes as a measure of engagement.

TikTok: we track followers as a measure of scale, and likes as a measure of engagement. TikTok, given its relatively more recent ascendancy as a mainstream social media platform, often witnesses brands growing from very small (or zero) platform presence, which is worth noting in analyses.

Youtube: we track subscribers as both a measure of scale and engagement. We disregard video views as many brands frequently delete videos from their pages, negatively impacting their view count and rendering any inferences based on view count spurious.

## Pseudo BCG matrix:

We use Google Trends to compile a pseudo-BCG matrix. We look at where brands stand relative to their history, and compare that to where “reference brands” (LV, Gucci, Chanel, Hermes, Prada, Burberry for soft luxury and Rolex, Cartier, Bulgari, RayBan for hard luxury and eyewear) in the same category stand relative to their history. We subtract the latter from the former and obtain a “relative altitude” score for each brand vs. the average of the mega-brands in each category. This is our X. Then, we obtain a relative growth score by subtracting from the “altitude” score growth for each brand the average “altitude” growth of the “reference brands” in the category. This is our Y. It follows from this that:

\- a lower relative altitude brand with a lower than average relative altitude growth is a “dog”,

\- a lower relative altitude brand with a faster than average relative altitude growth is a “question mark”,

\- a higher than average relative altitude brand with a lower than average relative altitude growth is a “cash cow”,

\- while a higher than average relative altitude brand with a faster relative altitude growth is a “star”.

Ideally, you would want to own strong brands getting stronger = stars. We look at the monthly progression of trends.
