# Murphy USA Inc. (MUSA)
# Favorable macro tailwinds should support faster EBITDA growth moving forward - Upgrade to Neutral
## NEUTRAL

Our View - We upgrade MUSA to Neutral (from Sell) as we now see a more balanced risk-reward for the stock as falling crude prices should help drive sustainably higher fuel margins - at least in the near to medium-term - which we think should help support healthy EBITDA delivery. With this in mind, we now believe mgmt's current FY28 I.t. EBITDA target of \$1.2B is within reach, especially considering likely very strong FY26 results (in our view) should provide the company a solid base to build upon. Along these lines, it's important to note that April all-in fuel margins were in the 35-40 CPG range (per mgmt) - and while May industry-wide fuel margins' data suggests some sequential deceleration - June industry-wide fuel margins were very strong (+92% vs May, and +37% vs April) per OPIS data, supported by falling crude prices (fuel margins & crude are typically inversely correlated) and continued volatility (which creates pricing opportunities). Beyond fuel margins, we also note that MUSA has the potential to be a fuel volume share gainer in this environment: as retail fuel prices fall, which we've seen recently, and begin to flatten out - that provides an opportunity for the company to further lower their prices at the pump (MUSA is the low-cost leader) in an effort to gain incremental market share/volume. While this incremental market share/volume is likely to come at the expense of some fuel margin, we think total fuel contribution in aggregate should move higher. Taking this all into consideration, we raise our Q2/FY26/FY27 EPS ests by +10%/+14%/+10% to \$9.30/\$32.69/\$31.49 (vs FactSet cons ests of \$9.48/\$32.11/\$29.50) - see additional details below. We also expect MUSA to raise its FY26 guidance with the company's upcoming Q2 results (likely in early August) - particularly considering mgmt's guidance was provided with 4Q25 results (early Feb) prior to any tailwinds (to MUSA) from the Iran war. Since we added MUSA to the Sell list on 12/1/20, the shares have risen 331.9% vs the S&P 500 up 104.3%. We think the shares outperformed given the durability and upward bias of fuel margins

## MUSA's Fuel Supply business

MUSA mgmt recently provided additional color on their Fuel Supply business with their Q1 results, which we found very helpful considering we do view this as a competitive advantage for the company - and something we don't think is being fully appreciated by investors. Along these lines, mgmt noted that they can disaggregate the results of their Fuel Supply business into three distinct activities, including (1): Controllable activities - which comprise the set of assets, expertise, and capabilities MUSA can deploy to provide a ratable & low-cost source of supply for \~50% of their retail volume - including sourcing product from the refinery, transporting it along the pipeline to the terminal, blending it with ethanol (which generates a RIN - which can then be sold), and selling it at the rack to their retail business at a market-based internal transfer price. Considering these capabilities, and given the refined product market & infrastructure in the US remain stable and well-supplied, market conditions where MUSA can earn abnormal profits (i.e., price arbitrage) typically exist for only a brief duration (as seen recently). In normal market conditions, MUSA's Fuel Supply business has historically generated \~2-3 CPG - which the company expects to continue. In Q1, these controllable activities generated 2.4 CPG; (2) The unpredictable timing of changing prices and/or the timing of inventory movements - which depends on the magnitude and duration (up or down) of retail fuel prices. Given the significant move higher in retail fuel prices in March, MUSA effectively sold fuel inventory more than they paid for it - with this part of MUSA's Fuel Supply business generating 6.9 CPG in Q1; and (3) Their Wholesale and Terminal function - MUSA's wholesale fuel segment is not built as a stand-alone profit center - and built more so to manage the logistical complexities of purchasing a significant volume of fuel they sell at their retail stores. However, the seven terminals that MUSA owns & operates also house fuel for \~100 other third-party terminals - historically generating \~\$1-2M in margin/month over the last several years. This part of MUSA's Fuel Supply business generated 0.3 CPG in Q1.

## Our Estimates

Raise our EPS estimates to reflect stronger fuel margin & fuel gallon delivery. We raise our Q2 all-in fuel margin estimate to 37.0 CPG (vs prior of 35.0 CPG, and vs cons ests of 33.8 CPG) - driven by our expectation for stronger retail fuel margins of 32.0 CPG (vs prior of 30.0 CPG, and vs cons ests of 29.2 CPG). Elsewhere, we raise our SS fuel gallon est to flat y/y in Q2 (vs prior of -1.8%, and vs cons ests of -0.5%) - as MUSA is likely to be a share gain beneficiary in this falling retail fuel price environment. Elsewhere, we continue to expect SS Nicotine Merch sales of +3.0% in Q2 (vs +4.9% in Q1) - while we expect SS Non-Nicotine merch sales to be flat y/y (vs -1.0% in Q1), as we see a potential for non-discretionary merch sales to be under some pressure in this higher retail fuel price environment. Taking this all together, we raise our Q2 EPS est by \$0.86 to \$9.30 (vs cons ests of \$9.48) - while also raising our Q2 EBITDA estimate to \$328M (+14.5% y/y, vs prior of \$304M, and vs cons ests of \$334M). Looking ahead, we also raise our FY26 all-in fuel margin estimate to 34.8 CPG (vs prior of 33.0 CPG, and vs cons ests of 32.6 CPG) - driven by our expectations for stronger retail fuel margins of 30.0 CPG (vs prior of 28.2 CPG, and vs cons ests of 28.7 CPG). Elsewhere, we raise our SS fuel gallon est to -0.2% (vs prior of -1.5%). With this we raise our FY26 EPS est by \$3.93 to \$32.69 (vs cons ests of \$32.11) - while also raising our FY26 EBITDA estimate to \$1.191B (+16.9% y/y, vs prior of \$1.096B, in-line with cons ests).

Phasing considerations for this year. We note that MUSA is lapping a significant ZYN promotion (3Q25) - which contributed \~\$12M in incremental merchandise contribution, with SS merchandise margin growth of +8.3% - and as a result, we expect 3Q26 Nicotine margins to be under pressure (we model -150 bps y/y to 15.3%). Along these lines, MUSA recently noted that there are a lot of other new entrants into the nicotine pouch category recently - many of which are receiving strong promotional support, which is meaningful to MUSA's core customer demographic which skews more low-income. With this, MUSA previously noted that JOEY nicotine pouch cans are currently retailing for just \$0.99 (on MUSA's rewards app) - with strong retail engagement, especially as many of MUSA's customers are often looking for the cheapest absolute price-point product. This is important as MUSA over-indexes to nicotine/tobacco vs peers - and given how quickly some of these newer/smaller entrants are gaining share (behind significant promotions), this will likely force larger established manufactures (i.e. ZYN) to continue to step up their promotional spend as well. This is a dynamic that we'll continue to watch closely - and we think is at least part of the reason behind the recent ZYN scanner data slowdown.

Expect MUSA to raise its FY26 guidance. Looking ahead, we expect MUSA to raise its FY26 guidance with the company's Q2 results. As a reminder, MUSA typically does not adjust guidance (up/down) with Q1 results - though historically has revisited their guidance with Q2 results, if necessary. With this in mind, we expect MUSA to raise their FY26 guidance - specifically for SS fuel gallons, which is currently at -3% to -1% y/y (vs our updated -0.2% est). Further, we also see upside to mgmt's (for modeling purposes only) guidance of: (1) all-in or total fuel margin (currently at 30.5 CPG, vs our updated 34.8 est); (2) Net Income (currently at \$439M, vs our updated \$600M est); and (3) adj EBITDA (currently at \$1B, vs our updated \$1.192B est).

## Valuation and Risks

We raise our 12-month price target to \$550 (vs prior of \$380), which is based on an equal-weighted EV/EBITDA of 10.6x (vs prior of 8.5x) and P/E of 17.0x (vs prior of 12.9x) multiples, based on our updated Q5-Q8 estimates. We raise our valuation multiples to reflect strong near to medium-term fuel margin delivery. Key upside risks to our thesis include better-than-expected EBITDA from the company's merchandising initiatives, a step-up in fuel margins, further incremental share gains in tobacco/nicotine, an additional step-up in shareholder returns, and better-than-expected execution on the company's recently updated capital allocation plan. Key downside risks to our thesis include a prolonged consumer downturn, a significant decline in fuel margins - and tobacco/nicotine share losses.

## Forward EV/EBITDA (absolute & relative)

MUSA is currently trading at an FY27E EV/EBITDA multiple of 11.3x, which is an $8\%$ premium to the company's 1-year historical average multiple and a $7\%$ premium to the company's 3-year historical average multiple.

\- Our price target implies a Q5-Q8 EV/EBITDA multiple of 10.6x, which is a $6\%$ discount to the company's current FY27 EV/EBITDA multiple, a $2\%$ premium to its 1-year historical average multiple and a $1\%$ premium to its 3-year historical average multiple.

MUSA is currently trading at an 11% discount relative to its C-store/Food/Dollar peer group on a forward EV/EBITDA multiple basis. This compares to MUSA trading at a 19% discount to the C-store/Food/Dollar peer group on a 1-year historical average forward EV/EBITDA basis and a 16% discount to the C-store/Food/Dollar group on a 3-year historical average forward EV/EBITDA basis.
