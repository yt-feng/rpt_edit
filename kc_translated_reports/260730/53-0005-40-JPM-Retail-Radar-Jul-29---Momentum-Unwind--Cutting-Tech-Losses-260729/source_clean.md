## Factor Crowdings and Rotations

\- Amid weakening retail sentiment, a Momentum Unwind, and the recent market drawdown, we provide a few additional observations on investor positioning:

(1) Crowding remains high across higher-risk segments (e.g., Low Quality at 94.8%ile and Speculative Growth at 98.0%ile), with Value also elevated (88.7%ile);

(2) Momentum's Beta exposure is high, and Momentum–Beta correlations have declined from extremes during this unwind; and

(3) Implied stock correlations increased over the past week from historically low levels (COR3M <Index>), even as peak-Earnings season typically coincides with lower stock correlations. In many risk-model frameworks, higher implied correlations translate into higher implied betas and higher portfolio risk estimates, which lead systematic/quant portfolios to reduce net exposure as volatility targets and risk constraints bind.

\- In combination with month-end Momentum rebalancing, these conditions are consistent with the elevated volatility observed in Momentum and higher-risk factors this week.

## Single Stock Stories with Elevated Retail Activity

Hedge Funds vs. Retail: High Short Interest Meme Stocks

\- DBGI (\$0.4M bought last week, 0.3z, Figure 19): The company expanded its secured U.S. program by 32% to \$165M, driven by the addition of new apparel and footwear categories. Retail interest has been strong, with net purchases totaling \$70M since May 2026, amid moderate short interest of \~14% of float, up 10% over the same period.

\- INFQ (\$0.2M sold last week, -0.7z, Figure 20): The company has undergone a series of management changes and secured three Genesis Mission projects from the U.S. Department of Energy focused on AI-optimized quantum circuit design for nuclear applications. The stock has been on retail investors' radar, with net purchases totaling \$120M since March 2026, alongside elevated short interest of \~16% of float.

\- JOBY (\$13.4M bought last week, -0.0z, Figure 21): The company signed a binding agreement with Virgin Atlantic, naming it Joby's exclusive airline partner for air taxi services in the UK. Retail investors have purchased \$400M year-to-date, against a backdrop of high short interest of \~18% of float, which has increased by 8% over the same period.

## Earnings Stories

\- AAPL (\$18.6M sold on 7/29, -0.0z, Figure 22): Apple reports earnings tomorrow AMC, with price elasticity of demand the biggest watchpoint for the company across its key hardware product lines following the announcement of a \~20% price increase on average across iPads and Macs, which also raised expectations for the magnitude of price increases likely to be enacted in conjunction with the launch of iPhone 18. However, even as short-term consumer responses to the unprecedented price increases, precipitated by the rise in memory costs, are being debated by investors, our analysts believe there are several drivers to lead the revenue and earnings outcomes to be much more favorable than currently feared by investors (link).

\- AMZN (\$9.8M bought on 7/29, -0.1z, Figure 24): Amazon reports tomorrow AMC, with our analysts expecting AWS growth acceleration of low-mid 30%’s, higher capex guidance and retail margin expansion (link).

\- MSFT (\$23.4M bought on 7/29, -0.2z, Figure 26): Microsoft's Cloud unit posted a larger-than-expected increase in sales at +43% y/y, with shares rising \~3% in post-market trading. Capex increased +70% y/y to \$41B during the quarter.

\- META (\$2.2M sold on 7/29, -0.4z, Figure 28): Meta narrowed its 2026 capex guidance to \$130-145B, lifting the low-end from \$125B. Revenue for the quarter came in line with expectations, but next quarter's outlook was lackluster. Shares fell as much as \~10% in post-market trading after the results were published.

\- QCOM (\$6.8M sold on 7/29, -0.6z, Figure 30): Qualcomm's earnings missed expectations, but top line was better than expected. Additionally, the company expects non-handset revenue growth to accelerate to $60\%$ in fiscal year 2027, as it tries to reduce its dependence on phones with a new push into the data center market.

\- V (\$2.9M sold on 7/29, -0.2z, Figure 32): Visa reported another impressive quarter highlighted by robust 12% organic growth on healthy drivers including U.S. volume accelerating 2ppts to 10% growth (the fastest rate since late 2018, excluding COVID) and cross border accelerating to 13% growth. While this momentum was partially supported by one-offs like the World Cup, tax refunds and fuel prices, headline results also overcame headwinds like tough currency volatility and incentive comps (link).

\- NUE (\$0.8M sold on 7/28, -0.4z, Figure 34): Nucor delivered a strong quarter, beating expectations on earnings and profitability, led by outperformance in its Steel Mills and Raw Materials segments. Results benefited from higher shipments, better margins, and a one-time tariff refund, with utilization running above peers and backlog continuing to build. Downstream operations also contributed through stronger volumes and steady pricing, while construction-related demand drivers (including infrastructure and data centers) remained a key support. Raw Materials improved on higher shipments and better transfer pricing dynamics. The company generated solid free cash flow, used it to fund aggressive buybacks, and the stock rose \~7% on the constructive outlook (link).

\- INTC (\$5.2M sold on 7/24, -0.3z, Figure 36): Intel's 2Q results and 3Q guidance cleared expectations by wide margins, reflecting supply unlock (yields, cycle times, wafer starts), record DCAI/server momentum, and firm pricing and mix, but a step-function capex cycle that puts a potential equity/debt raise on the table, memory-driven erosion of the client TAM beginning in 2H26, and an external foundry business still lacking an anchor customer keep our analysts UW even as they materially raise estimates (link). The stock was down \~8% post-earnings.

\- RTX (\$3.5M sold on 7/24, -0.5z, Figure 38): Investors seemed pleased with RTX's strong operating performance in 2Q as the stock outperformed the S&P 8.5% by on earnings day. Defense and aftermarket were especially strong and while Pratt mix debates will likely remain unsettled. Longer term margin expectations for 19-20% at Collins are unchanged and given the growth and incrementals required, this seems achievable at decade end. And of course, among the defense “primes”, Raytheon performance remains quite strong in both absolute and relative terms (link).

\- DLR (\$12.3M sold on 7/24, -6.0z, Figure 40): Digital Realty Trust was up +11% after reporting strong 2Q results and raising 2026 guidance for total revenue, adj. EBITDA and core FFO, primarily driven by a robust pricing environment. Subsequent to quarter end, the company signed two leases with hyperscale tenants for \$205M of annualized GAAP rent (\$410M at 100% share), which was excluded from the reported 2Q26 backlog (link).

\- TMUS (\$2.1M sold on 7/23, -0.3z, Figure 42): T-Mobile pulled back \~11% on 2Q results, which likely reflected positioning into the print, which anticipated a beat-and-raise. While our analysts trimmed their 2026 service revenue and EBITDA estimates marginally on forward commentary from the call, their long-term estimates remain largely unchanged, supporting an \~8% core EBITDA and \~11% FCF/share CAGR through 2028E, above peers (link).

\- CSX (\$1.6M bought on 7/22, 1.1z, Figure 44): CSX proved why sentiment was skewed positive heading into the print with another operating beat and raise, despite service metrics which continued to slip throughout the quarter (link). The stock was up +6% post-earnings.

\- ROL (\$1.7M sold on 7/22, -1.8z, Figure 46): Rollins posted a soft 2Q, with the company missing Street expectations across segments. The shortfall versus management's plan was most pronounced in Residential brands tied to consumer-initiated demand (search, digital media, inbound calls) as lead volumes softened. Commercial and Termite/Ancillary still showed momentum but came in below expectations. Compounding the demand softness, costs were positioned for a stronger peak season that failed to materialize, driving roughly 120 bps of YoY margin contraction (link). The stock fell \~9%.

## Macro & Fundamental Stories

\- SPCX ( $81.2M bought on 7/24, 1.0z, Figure 48$ ): SpaceX undertook a successful $13^{\text{th}}$ Starship test launch. This was also the second V3 test launch. The rocket achieved its objectives, including deploying 20 Starlink satellites. Importantly, the upper stage landing in the Indian Ocean was relatively soft and controlled, though the booster landing in the Gulf was harder than expected, as not all engines lit for the return to Earth. After the launch, Elon Musk indicated that unless issues emerged from the flight 13 mission data, SpaceX would attempt to catch the second stage in its Mechazilla “chopstick arms” on flight 14. This is a key milestone planned for this year (link). The stock has continued to underperform, reaching a level $\sim 15\%+$ below its IPO price.
