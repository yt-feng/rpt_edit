# China Consumer Pulse 2Q26: Premium resilience against a persistent weak consumer backdrop

Overall consumption remained under pressure in Q2 despite some improvement in confidence and inflation indicators, and we continued to see relative outperformance in premium across sectors. In this comprehensive quarterly tracker, we analyze key metrics across consumer sectors including Apparel, Sportswear, Alcohol, Beauty, Travel, Luxury and Autos, spanning both mass and premium categories. We also cover the implications for Japanese Consumer companies.

GDP growth moderated to 4.3% YoY in Q2 from 5.0% in Q1, with retail sales slowing to +0.2% YoY despite Consumer confidence indicators gradually improving vs late 2025 levels, and positive CPI (c.1%) helping to ease concerns around outright deflation. Premium and Luxury segments were a relative bright spot, with Premium Beverages recovering, Apparel, Sportswear and Beauty brands outperforming mass-market peers, and Luxury Hotels continuing to outperform. Meanwhile, big ticket goods and autos remained weak, and we remain cautious for the H2 outlook.

Beverages: Moutai's Fetien wholesale pricing continued to rise in Q2, with a second ex-factory price increase this year indicating that demand remains solid. Fetien pricing is now +13% from the trough, and other high-end variants are now +17-30%. In Beer, overall sell-out value improved materially, up 2% in the quarter compared to -1% in Q1, and momentum in restaurants was particularly healthy at +2%, while the off-trade strengthened sequentially to +1%. Moutai remains our top Pick in China Beverages, followed by Nongfu Spring.

Apparel and Sportswear continued to outperform broader retail, driven by online. 3P Online sales grew 10% in Q2 (vs. 2% for online retail overall), whereas Offline trends remain muted (-3% Pou Sheng, -1% offline retail overall). Nike is seeing positive marketplace sell-through (+3%), an improvement vs. prior quarters, as new product resonates and inventory clears; however, we expect total growth to remain negative due to sharp reductions in sell-in over the coming Qs. Adidas is growing rapidly (+32%) and gaining share with strong product and brand resonance. Lululemon is seeing marketplace declines (-MSD%) from the recent PFAS controversy which should normalize soon, while emerging brands grow double digits.

Hotels: Chinese hotel RevPAR continued its recovery in 2Q26, with demand growth driving strong performance. RevPAR growth was particularly strong at the extremes of the market - Luxury and Economy, while other segments continued to struggle. Given the resilience of the performance in Luxury, and with slowing supply growth, we expect the recovery to continue into H2 absent of incremental macroeconomic headwinds. IHG has the most meaningful China exposure of our asset light hotel names under coverage. However, China's 3-year underperformance means the country is now a smaller component of fee revenues, softening the impact of recent strength.

Luxury Goods: China luxury demand is stabilising but not yet reaccelerating, with value-consciousness and ongoing K-shaped polarisation remaining the defining features of the market. While most companies that have reported so far showed stable or improving trends, there is still little evidence of a broad-based recovery, with strength concentrated in specific categories (most notably jewellery), higher-income consumer cohorts, and a handful of brand-specific turnarounds such as Burberry. Chinese consumers continue to optimise both where and what they buy, arbitraging across Mainland China, Hong Kong, Japan and Korea based on FX dynamics and relative pricing advantages, while gravitating towards products that offer stronger perceived value, craftsmanship, exclusivity and scarcity. This behaviour continues to support the outperformance of jewellery relative to soft luxury and helps explain the divergent impact of Chinese tourism on brands and regions. At the same time, demand remains skewed towards affluent consumers, with elevated products, bespoke offerings and high-ticket purchases outperforming broader luxury spending (e.g, Zegna), while aspirational demand remains selective and subdued.

Autos: China auto sales came in weak in 2Q26, weighed down by the expiration of trade-in subsidies nationwide and a high comparison base. China passenger vehicle sales dropped by -20% yoy in 2Q26, down from -17% in 1Q26. We forecast domestic retail demand of 20-21mn units, declining 10-12% yoy. EV sales (BEV & PHEV) moderated to -7% yoy in 2Q26, compared to -23% in 1Q26. EV penetration reached 59.9% in 2Q26, vs. 51.4% in 2Q25 and 42.9% in 1Q26. By segment, premium-brand vehicle sales weakened further in 2Q26, declining -16% YoY versus -1% in 1Q26 and -7% in 2Q25. The slowdown was attributable to subdued demand for ICE powertrain. Audi, BMW, Mercedes's sales volumes were down -31%, -30% and -26% yoy respectively. Porsche's sales volume was down -41% vs. -29% in 1Q26, as it continues to be impacted by ultra-luxury tax. Meanwhile, mass market volume was also weak at -21% yoy in 2Q26, vs. -21% yoy in 1Q26. BYD topped PV sales with 983k units (-37% yoy) and Geely Group fell to the second with 959k units (-10% yoy).

Cosmetics: China e-commerce beauty names delivered a mixed performance in Q2, despite a strong market backdrop. Total beauty e-commerce GMV grew +13% YoY during the quarter, while our tracked beauty companies delivered +1.3% GMV growth and +62bps of market share expansion. Monthly trends were volatile, with tracked companies declining -10.0% YoY in April, before rebounding to +6.2% YoY in May, supported by the start of 6.18 pre-sales activity, and then declining -3.2% YoY in June. June trading coincided with the peak 6.18 shopping festival, during which overall online beauty sales declined -10.5%, with our tracked companies outperforming (-3.2% in June) and gaining a further +72bps of market share. Overall, the data points to a mixed demand environment, with leading beauty brands continuing to consolidate share despite uneven trading conditions across the quarter.

Apparel and restaurants for Japan consumer brands: China's retail environment remains soft, with weak headline sales reflecting a cautious consumer and uneven discretionary recovery, of which, apparel grows $3.9\%$ YoY in 2Q and restaurants stay broadly flat. Against this backdrop, Japanese consumer brands in China are at very different development stages. Uniqlo China is in a "scrap-and-build" phase, rationalizing its network from around 1,000 stores to below 900, and this process now appears close to completion as constant-currency store productivity finally turned from negative to positive in 2026. Muji's China store base has been broadly stable at around 500 locations, with East Asia sales growth driven more by improving store productivity, e-commerce and FX tailwinds than by new store openings. Asics' China business is delivering high growth. The brand's LFL store productivity rising faster than growth from new stores for the most part of 2026, suggesting this is still an early phase of its China growth trajectory. Sushiro's store openings in China have accelerated to over 110 locations. Ongoing pace and quality of expansion are key indicators of whether it can ultimately secure a meaningful foothold in the market.

## INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS

## Global Hotels & Leisure

The Chinese hotel market continues to recover in 2026, with demand growth and slowing hotel supply expansion driving improved performance. RevPAR growth was driven by the extremes of the market, with Luxury and Economy particularly strong. Other segments continued to struggle, all struggling to grow RevPAR YTD. Q2 to date has shown the inflection in China RevPAR growth has continued to be resilient, and we expect continued growth into the back half of 2026 with supply growth continuing to slow. Performance in China ultimately impacts the asset light hotels in our coverage through RevPAR growth/declines or through NUG. RevPAR underperformance over the last two years means that the country is a smaller component of fee revenues, softening the impact of recent strength. However, China represents a significant portion of the pipeline for all our coverage - slowing supply growth in the region is likely to weigh on NUG. IHG, the most exposed player, derived just 6.7% of revenue from China in 2025, down from 9% at peak. However, an inflection in performance in the region will still be a tailwind.

None of our OTA coverage have yet managed to crack the China domestic market, but they do have good international businesses. Booking's primary exposure is through its Agoda brand, but the focus for Agoda is markets in Asia outside of China, where it has been competing head on with TCOM. Airbnb has exited the Chinese domestic market but is uniquely positioned to benefit from the outbound demand in vacation rental. Cumulatively this means recovery in China demand will likely have a limited impact, but there remains a structural opportunity from the improving Chinese macro outlook through outbound travel.

## Global Luxury Goods

The upcoming 1H26E updates could represent a turning point for the sector. Many long-only investors have been sitting on the fence waiting and seeing for global luxury demand to improve. Major companies providing a combination of better than 1Q26 growth, (slightly) positive beats, and a more than decent 1H26E profit performance could be a catalyst for investors to engage or at least put a toe in the water. The sector is 20% cheaper than the 10-year history, and the market may be looking for alternative places for funds to work as the tech boom seems to be reaching a peak.

In this context we have a bias for quality, as the first port-of-call for long-only investors, as well as self-help stories with more promising trajectories:

\- High Quality (see Global Luxury Goods: The Six Rings of Quality): In this group, we find continue to see Richemont as our top-pick. In the near-term, jewellery momentum remains strong and could well persist for longer than the market expects. In the medium-to-long-term, improved governance and a structural advantage in an increasingly polarized consumer environment remain underappreciated (see Global Luxury Goods: Preparing for consumer polarisation and Part 2). Brunello Cucinelli is also favored for its quality and potential for mean reversion. It is more difficult to be positive on Hermès in the short-term, but one or two weak quarters can be forgiven by the market, if they return to high single digit growth (see Hermès: Valuations already discount a 'Ferrari reset'). We believe Hermès could do more to achieve this (see Hermès: Stretching upwards).

\- Promising Self-Help: We find LVMH sitting between high quality and self-help. Lingering concerns around the W&S turnaround and the Arnault family's ‘Darwinian’ succession process are counter-balanced by Dior’s revival, cost efficiencies, and LVMH’s traditional position as a sine quo non within any luxury portfolio. Turnaround at Burberry is well on track. One-year anniversary of the Burberry Forward strategy has paid off, in the form of improved brand momentum and stronger full-price sell-through. With a solid foundation in place, the next leg of the turnaround will likely see focus shifting to driving store productivity, by capitalizing on the extended momentum in other categories from the core outerwear & scarf; Ferragamo is at the earlier stage of and trading at the lower end of the trading range. Our recent conversation with management suggests a possible turnaround in the making, as a team of “old hands” are tackling key issues around the brand, assortment and retail store network.

## DETAILS

## SUMMARY

Category-level and sector data suggest that the recovery remains narrow. Service and online consumption outperformed, but retail sales, especially in big-ticket categories, stayed weak, with autos a key drag as retail volumes declined YoY in Q2. Fixed Asset Investment returned to decline in Q2, while household deleveraging continued and credit impulse indicators deteriorated further. Retail sales remained subdued across most goods categories, with autos acting as a major drag following the expiration of trade-in subsidies nationwide, an 5% increase in EV purchase tax, and a high comparison base.

By contrast, premium and luxury segments continued to show relative resilience. Premium beverages sales saw stabilization on the back of the price increase of the prestige Moutai brand. Luxury hotels and travel-related services benefited from domestic tourism, and global luxury groups saw either stable or improving trends with luxury consumption becoming incrementally more constructive versus earlier quarters. This mirrors ongoing divergence across consumer categories, with higher-income cohorts maintaining discretionary spend, while mass-market consumption remains constrained.

Looking ahead, risks to Chinese consumer health remain skewed to the downside in H2. Household leverage continues to contract, credit demand remains weak, and the property sector is unlikely to provide meaningful support to consumption in the near term. While exports, manufacturing activity and selected confidence indicators have remained relatively resilient, domestic demand continues to lack a clear catalyst for sustained recovery. Against this backdrop, we expect the divergence between premium and mass-market consumption to persist, with services consumption, travel and category-specific share gainers likely to outperform broader discretionary spending categories and overall retail sales growth in 2026 still well below pre-pandemic norms and insufficient to offset structural drags from housing and investment.

## BROAD CONSUMER KPIS

External demand remains firm, but domestic demand continues to lag — Exhibit 1. GDP growth slowed to 4.3% YoY in Q2 from 5.0% in Q1, while retail sales growth deteriorated to just 0.2% YoY for the quarter. In contrast, export growth accelerated materially, supported by robust demand for AI- and data center-related products, improving developed-market import demand and firmer export pricing. Industrial production also remained relatively resilient. However, domestic demand is yet to recover with fixed asset investment returning to decline during the quarter, consumer spending remaining subdued, and credit growth weakening further. The widening gap between external and domestic demand suggests the economy is increasingly reliant on exports as its primary growth engine.

The manufacturing backdrop slightly improved. PMI indicators continue to point to sub-threshold conditions, but slightly improved to 50.3 in June above the 49-pt line in Q1. Housing still remains under pressure, with 70-city home prices still declining on a YoY basis across most tiers. Newly built residential buildings prices in Tier 1 cities maintained positive MoM growth since Q1 and Tier 2 cities continuously improving, suggesting downside pressure may be easing at the margin, though the property sector remains a drag on overall growth and sentiment.

Household consumption remains under pressure and retail sales growth softened. Consumer spending has struggled to gain momentum despite modest improvements in confidence readings and income growth. Goods consumption and restaurant spending weakened during Q2, with retail sales growth remaining near zero, particularly autos, seeing the greatest pressure due to the expiration of trade-in subsidies nationwide and a high comparison base. By contrast, services consumption has remained comparatively resilient, supported by continued travel and leisure activity. Household credit demand continues to weaken, with households remaining reluctant to take on additional leverage amid uncertain income expectations and a still-fragile property market.

## Macro Summary (from our Autonomous China macro analyst Charlene Chu)

\- Running on one engine: The gap between external and domestic demand in China widened sharply in Q2, and we expect this divergence to persist through the second half and likely well beyond. Exports surged 20% y/y in Q2, largely on the back of strong demand for AI- and data center-related products and a rise in export prices. However, the fruits of this aren't trickling down to the rest of the economy. Retail sales barely grew at just +0.2% y/y in Q2, while fixed-asset investment contracted -11% in the quarter.

\- Consumption stuck in low gear: Household consumption has been under pressure for several quarters and came under renewed strain after the outbreak of the conflict in Iran, which further dented consumer confidence. Most of the recent weakness has been concentrated in purchases of goods and restaurants/catering. Sales of goods involved in the consumer trade-in program, such as autos, have seen the biggest drag due to last year's inflated sales and smaller subsidies this year. By contrast, sales of services have been holding up much better, growing a robust +5.1% y/y in Q2.

\- Household credit still contracting: Chinese households continued to deleverage in Q2, with outstanding debt falling by -CNY1.1trn or -1.3% y/y in June. This has added to the drag on household consumption and doesn't appear to be ending anytime soon.

\- Property still under pressure: After a short-lived rebound in activity earlier in the year in Tier 1 cities, China's property sector returned to double-digit declines across all major areas of activity in Q2. Rolling 12-mo. new starts are now down -78% from their 2021 peak, while sales of new homes are down -58% and property investment -51%. All of this continues to weigh on property prices, which continue to fall, albeit by a smaller margin than in the past.

## SECTOR TAKEAWAYS

## CHINA HOTELS & LEISURE (Exhibit 30 – Exhibit 31)

From 1H24 to September 2025, China was the worst performing region in the broader context of global RevPAR growth, having experienced y/y RevPAR declines every month since June 2024 before returning to growth in September 2025. Yet we have reached an inflection - performance recovered through Q4 2025 and the strength has continued through Q1 (despite some noise created by the shift in Chinese New Year from January to February). Occupancy is up YTD, after declines through most of 2025, while strong ADR growth has continued.

Top and bottom end outperforming. Unlike in the US, where hotel performance has been driven by a bifurcation in high and high-end demand (the higher end the stronger the RevPAR), in China Luxury and Economy have been the best performing segments, with Economy showing notable resilience through the Chinese New Year shift. The Midscale - Upper Upscale segments instead are struggling, all struggling to grow RevPAR in in every month YTD but February.

EXHIBIT 31: Luxury and economy were the only segments to deliver growth in Q2 2026, while the midscale segment recorded the sharpest decline in June

Slowing supply growth a tailwind to RevPAR. Despite a weak RevPAR environment prior to late 2025 China hotel demand had not seen declines, with room nights largely flat. Instead RevPAR declines have been driven by supply outgrowing demand - driving declines in occupancy, resulting in lower pricing. The recovery in hotel RevPAR since H2 2025 seems to have been driven both by a recovery in China, but also slowing supply growth, which has consistently trended down over the last two years, providing support to RevPAR (Exhibit 33). If these trends continue, we expect to see the Chinese hotel market continue to improve and with the lapping of easier y/y comps to come.

## CHINA APPAREL & SPORTSWEAR (EXHIBIT 41 TO EXHIBIT 49)

For more on the broader context of the China market and our views on growth, see our note from June 2026: Global Sportswear: State of the sector in China

Q2 total retail sales grew 0.2% in Q2, a deceleration vs. 2.4% in Q1 (Exhibit 41), in part due to the prior quarter benefitting from a pull-forward of sales related to a later Chinese New Year and a longer Spring Festival, but also in part a reflection of the tough macro environment and continued pressures on household demand. ## CHINA LUXURY GOODS (Exhibit 50 - Exhibit 59)

China luxury demand appears to be stabilising rather than reaccelerating. Across the sector, the tone on Chinese consumption has become incrementally more constructive versus earlier quarters, with most companies seeing either stable or improving trends. However, there remains little evidence of a broad-based rebound in luxury demand. Instead, the market continues to be characterised by pockets of strength (e.g, jewellery momentum) offset by lingering softness in more discretionary or aspirational spending (e.g, Gucci highlighting exponential price elasticity). The overall read-through is that Chinese consumers remain engaged with luxury categories, but spending growth remains selective.

Chinese consumers remain highly value-conscious, influencing both what they buy and where they buy it. Across the sector, spending patterns continue to reflect a search for the best combination of product desirability, pricing and perceived value. This has supported ongoing shifts in purchasing locations, with consumers arbitraging across Mainland China, Hong Kong, Japan and Korea depending on FX and relative pricing advantages. The opposite of this phenomenon is the varying degree of tourists impact on luxury companies performance in the Europe, where higher reliance has led to more severe headwinds (e.g, Richemont vs. Moncler). At the same time, spending is becoming increasingly concentrated in categories and products that offer stronger value perception, craftsmanship or exclusivity. This has led to the continued outperformance by Jewellery, compared to the soft luxury categories.

The K-shaped nature of China's luxury recovery remains firmly intact. Demand continues to be strongest among affluent and ultra-high-net-worth consumers, with elevated products and high-ticket purchases generally outperforming broader luxury spending. New customer acquisition is increasingly occurring at the upper end of the market (e.g, new customers in Greater China regions coming to Zegna for Su Misura), while aspirational demand remains comparatively subdued and selective (think the revival of Burberry in China).

Luxury watch demand in China remains on a fragile stabilisation. Total Swiss watch exports by value to Mainland China and Hong Kong on a combined basis declined -0.9% YoY for 2Q26, down slightly from the -0.4% in 1Q26. The 12-month moving average YoY changes for Jun-26 is now a touch below flat, at -1.2%. That said, YoY trends are likely to remain volatile in the coming months, as the comparison period will factor in last year's import duty fluctuations.

## CHINA AUTOS (Exhibit 63- Exhibit 69)

China's passenger vehicle retail insurance volume registered at 4.6mn units in Q2 26, down by -20.4% yoy. The sharp yoy decline was mainly pressured by the expiration of trade-in subsidies nationwide, an 5% increase in EV purchase tax, and a high comparison base. EV sales (BEV & PHEV) reached 2.8mn units, down by -7.1% yoy in the quarter. As a result, EV penetration (BEV & PHEV) increased to 59.9% in Q2 26, higher than 51.4% in Q2 25 and 42.9% in Q1 26; BEV and PHEV penetration was 41.5% and 18.4%, respectively. Premium brand autos sales declined -15.8% yoy in Q2 26, vs. -1.1% yoy in Q1 26, and -7.1% yoy in Q2 25, while still showing more resilience than mass brands. The slowdown was attributable to subdued demand for imported vehicles amid macroeconomic pressures and consumer downtrading, a demanding comparison base for domestic EV brands in Q2 25, and fading growth momentum at HIMA. Premium brand penetration trended to 15.6%, decreased from 18.0% in Q1 26 while higher than 15.0% for the full year of 2025. Performance of traditional premium brands diverged: Over Q2, Audi, BMW, Mercedes's sales volumes were down -31.0%, -29.7% and -25.9% yoy, respectively. BYD topped PV sales with 983k units (-37.2% yoy) and Geely Group fell to the second with 959k units (-10.3% yoy).

While retail SAAR recovered to 20.7mn units in June from 19.2mn in May, we see limited catalysts for a meaningful recovery in domestic demand before November–December, when base effects are expected to normalize, even in the absence of additional policy stimulus. At the brand level, we expect Xiaomi and HIMA (Huawei-backed brands) are well positioned to take further share from incumbent premium brands, leveraging superior technology and product offerings. ## CHINA APPAREL AND RESTAURANTS FOR JAPAN CONSUMER BRANDS (EXHIBIT 70 TO EXHIBIT 76)

China's overall retail environment remains soft, but the picture is mixed across categories and for the Japanese consumer brands under our coverage.

China's overall retail sales remain weak, underscoring a cautious consumer and a patchy recovery in discretionary spending. Within this backdrop, apparel is “holding up” reasonably well, with 2Q apparel retail sales posting 3.9% year-on-year growth, which points to relatively stable demand for everyday wear and value-for-money brands. In contrast, restaurant sales are essentially flat, indicating only a limited recovery in dining-out frequency and ongoing pressure on foodservice operators (Exhibit 70). Japanese brands under our coverage are at very different positioning and development stages in China, which drives divergent growth and capital allocation strategies.

\- Uniqlo: Uniqlo China is currently in a “scrap-and-build” phase, rationalizing its store network from close to 1,000 locations to below 900 (Exhibit 71) as it prunes underperforming stores and optimizes its footprint. We believe this rationalization process is now close to completion; in 2026, constant-currency store productivity has finally turned from negative to positive (Exhibit 72), pointing to healthier unit economics and the early benefits of portfolio clean-up.

\- Muji: Muji's China store count has been broadly stable, hovering around the 500-store level (Exhibit 73), which points to a more measured expansion approach. East Asia sales for Muji continue to grow (Exhibit 74), likely driven primarily by improving store productivity, increasing e-commerce contribution, and FX tailwinds rather than pure store count growth, underscoring a deliberate quality-over-quantity strategy.

\- Asics: Asics' China business has been delivering high growth, and based on $3^{\text{rd}}$ party offline data we estimate the overall company is expanding at close to $30\%$ on a constant-currency basis, underpinned by strong momentum in the China market (Exhibit 75). The Asics brand is performing particularly well, with both new store openings and like-for-like store productivity increasing; importantly, for the most part of 2026, productivity gains are outpacing growth from new stores, which is a very healthy signal that demand has not yet been fully met and that this may still represent an early phase of the brand's growth trajectory in China.

\- Sushiro: Sushiro's store openings in China have accelerated in recent quarters, and the chain is now operating over 110 stores based on third-party data (Exhibit 76). This still represents only a small fraction of overall restaurant demand in China, so the key metric to watch is the pace and quality of continued store expansion.
