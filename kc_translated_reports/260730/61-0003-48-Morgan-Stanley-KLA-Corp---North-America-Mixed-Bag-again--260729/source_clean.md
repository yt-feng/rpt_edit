## Key Takeaways

2026 WFE+AP revised up from \$140bn+ to low-\$150bn (25%+)

Now see 2026 advanced packaging of \$1.1bn (2025 \$650mn).

What happened? KLA's SepQ guide came in a touch below MSe (KLA Corp: Long-term positive, near-term cautious (9 Jul 2026)). On the outlook the company revised its 2026 WFE forecast, including AP, from \$140bn+ to low-\$150bn (25%+), and talked toward ensuring that KLA themselves can prepare to supply and support the more bullish 2027 WFE views out there (\$190bn+). 2026 advanced packaging revenue was revised up from \$1bn to \$1.1bn (+70% y/y), with growth rate 2x the market (KLA's estimate).

Mixed bag (again). Similar to last quarter, we think this print offered something for both bulls and bears, but with the negatives concentrated in the near term, and vice versa. The bull case (primarily CY27): KLA's confidence on outgrowing WFE in 2027 is based on backlog, as the company spoke of \$12.5bn in RPO (vs FY25-end \$7.86bn). We feel more confident in our thesis that process control intensity will improve in 2027 led by 1D and 1.4nm and were also encouraged by the company's commentary around capturing share in advanced packaging on the back of hybrid bonding in logic and SolC. The bear case (primarily CY26): Near-term numbers for KLA were uninspiring as the company wasn't able to beat and raise vs intra-quarter commentary. Given KLA's lead-times (12-months+), numbers in-line with intra-quarter commentary aren't a surprise, but earnings does serve as a reminder that mix has been a significant headwind for process control intensity in 2026 and KLA is a share donor this year.

Where does this leave us? KLA's earnings have been underwhelming vs etch/dep peers who have continued to show significant near-term upside. While there is a longer term debate around whether AI is a tailwind or not for process control (more N-1/2 wafers vs larger die/broader customer base), we are strong believers that process control intensity will be a tailwind for KLA in 2027.
