## Adidas Q2: Small sales beat, but margins disappoint

Q2 results delivered better sales than expected, but featured a big operating profit miss due to higher-than-expected marketing costs and an additional DTC-driven shipping cost and labor headwind. FY26 outlook raised slightly on sales, while operating profit maintained. New CFO announced, with Birgit Kretschmer to succeed Harm Ohlmeyer as CFO at the end of the year. See tables below for Results vs. Consensus. Earnings call is at 3pm CEST (link).

Q2 reported $14\%$ cc topline growth, vs. $13\%$ Cons. [Note: FY26 is the first fiscal year since the Yeezy partnership termination in October 2022 with no residual Yeezy impact, as there is no longer a YoY headwind from discontinued Yeezy sales or the lapping of leftover inventory sell-through that extended through 2024.]

\- Growth was driven by Performance. Performance grew 39% cc (vs. 29% cc in Q1 and 27% cc in Q4), led by DD% growth in Football (World Cup product, retro-inspired collections), Running (Adizero franchise, Hyperboost Edge), and Motorsport. On the other hand, Lifestyle grew 2% (vs. 6% cc in Q1 and 3% cc in Q4), driven by DD% growth in Originals apparel as well as momentum in Low Profile and Lifestyle Running. By product division, growth was driven primarily by apparel: Footwear grew 1% cc (on top of 9% in Q2'25 ex-Yeezy), Apparel grew 35% (supported by jersey launches), and Accessories grew 20%.

\- Regionally, growth was strong for the Adidas brand, though limited by conservative wholesale sell-in into Europe (+6% cc, in line with Q1), partially offset by strong DD% growth in DTC. N.Am (+17% cc vs. 12% cc in Q1) maintained its DD% growth despite the continued conservative sell-in, and sales continued to grow DD% in Greater China (+15% cc), Emerging Markets (+12% cc), Latin America (+28% cc), and Japan/South Korea (+18% cc) - See Exhibit 4.

\- DTC outperformed Wholesale, posting 25% cc growth in the quarter, driven by Ecomm up 27% and Own Retail up 23%. On the other hand, Wholesale revenues grew 6%, as a result of the tough lap (+14% Q2'25) and a conservative sell-in approach in Europe given the uncertain consumer environment and heightened promotional activity.

Operating margin declined 70 bps YoY to 8.5%, a miss vs. 9.4% Cons. Gross margin expanded by 80 bps to 52.5% (vs. 51.3% Cons), driven by healthy full-price sales, more favorable channel mix, small tariff refund, partially offset by increased freight and sourcing costs and higher US tariffs. However, other operating expenses % of sales increased 140 bps, driven by an increase in 170 bps in marketing % of sales (due to marketing around World Cup and major product launches, e.g., Adizero Dropset Elite and Pro in Training), partially offset by 30 bps of operating overhead leverage, which was lower than Street expectations due to higher DTC-driven shipping costs and labor. EPS came in at €2.10 (vs. 2.40 Cons).

2026 outlook raised on sales: 9-10% cc growth, HSD% margin (\~9.5% incl. tariff refund). Upgraded sales guide reflects the better-than-expected top-line performance in H1, but also the continued uncertainty ahead in H2, given macroeconomic challenges and promotional marketplace, particularly in Europe. On margins, the guide excludes \$250-\$300m in tariffs.

Birgit Kretschmer to succeed Harm Ohlmeyer as CFO of Adidas at year-end. She comes with 25 years of experience at Adidas and most recently 6-years as CFO at C&A.
