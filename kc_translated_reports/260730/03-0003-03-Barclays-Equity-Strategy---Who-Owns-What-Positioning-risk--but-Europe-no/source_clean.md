# Positioning risk, but Europe not in a bad spot

Record inflows & extended HF/CTA longs limit positioning cushion. And while strong earnings support equities, Fed/rate jitters & adverse seasonality warrant hedges, with oil a wild card. Momo bounce likely post 2nd largest drawdown, but crowding still high. Less Tech-centric flows aid Europe/Value.

Equity positioning near the highs. Long only equity flows were very strong again in July (albeit skewed by record China inflows), at \$129bn, even as retail participation softened and sentiment indicators like AAI bull-bear and fear/greed index moderated considerably. However, after modest unwind in June, HFs and systematics longs crept up again, pushing up aggregate equity positioning near the highs. Ytd global equity inflows stand at \$659bn, the highest on our record.

Strong earnings keep X-asset dynamics tilted towards equities...Equities continued to attract more inflows than bonds in July, while cash once again saw outflows. For now, corporate fundamentals remain supportive and Q2 earnings are tracking well ahead of expectations. So EPS revisions remain healthy, underpinning continued equity inflows and preference over bonds.

...but macro/technical risks warrant hedging. Despite renewed tensions in the US-Iran conflict, investors remain largely sanguine on inflation risks, as reflected in rising oil short positions and muted inflows into TIPS. Yet, financial conditions continue to tighten, with Fed rate hike expectations moving higher and US real yields approaching levels that have historically become a headwind for equities. Rates positioning reflects this shift, with CTAs turning short Treasuries and speculators adding to shorts at the long end. However, as increasingly hawkish central banks and elevated oil volatility coincide with softer summer liquidity, lower mutual fund cash levels and unfavourable mid-term election seasonality, tactical hedging seems wise given current limited positioning cushion.

Less US-centric equity flows benefit Europe. US equity inflows have slowed to the lowest level since March, helping to reduce concentration and performance of non-US equities. In Europe, nascent signs of pick-up in inflows are visible, especially from US investors, with periphery seemingly preferred over core. Diversification away from Tech/AI exposure is helping the region, with higher oil risks contained for now. EM saw most demand in July, albeit driven largely by domestic Chinese demand. Korean flows remain healthy, even as levered ETFs assets decline and CTA exposure rolls over. Japan flows picked up modestly as well.

Factor swings see broadening gaining steam as Momo continues to falter. Factor rotation has seen market breadth improving the most since Covid in July. Over 70% of global stocks outperformed the index, lifting global funds' performance. However, Momentum drawdown has been sharp and painful, in fact the 2nd largest in Europe so far, and crowding has moderated. So potential for a reversal, although crowding is far from depressed, with CTA exposure and Tech inflows still high. Meanwhile, Value continues to outperform, underpinning Europe's relative strength, where investor flows have shifted toward AI-agnostic sectors such as Healthcare and Consumer Discretionary. However, funds remain heavily exposed to capex beneficiaries, including Semis, Utilities, Industrials and Materials, which hit returns in July. Banks remain crowded so a key support to performance in Europe, with Insurance seeing more bid again.

Key pain trades. Equities down, bonds up; Europe > US; Momentum down; AI winners & Taiwan/Korea down; AI agnostic sectors (Healthcare, Consumers) up; Banks down.
