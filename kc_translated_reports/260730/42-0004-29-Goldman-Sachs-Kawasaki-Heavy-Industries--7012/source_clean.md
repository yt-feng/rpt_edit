## What happened?

Polaris' 2Q adjusted sales were \$2,023 million (+9% yoy; +17% organic growth), and its adjusted EBITDA margin was 11.8% (+540 bp yoy). Profit growth was driven by higher volumes, an improved product mix, higher net pricing, and tariff refunds. Even when excluding the \$74 million tariff refund, the company achieved yoy profit growth and margin improvement. Polaris raised full-year guidance, incorporating the strong 1H performance and recorded tariff refunds.

Sales in the Polaris Powersports segment, which includes ORV (Off-Road Vehicles), were \$1,715 million (+17% yoy), with a gross profit margin of 21.4% (+77 bp yoy). ORV retail unit sales were up +5% yoy (overall market: up low-single digits % yoy), and the company noted market share gains for a fifth consecutive quarter. It recorded double-digit yoy growth in utility ORV retail unit sales.

## Implications for Kawasaki Heavy Industries

We estimate 1Q3/27 KHI business profits of ¥28.0 bn vs. the LSEG StarMine SmartEstimate of ¥25.9 bn. Factoring in persistently high sales promotion expenses and the impact of tariffs, we forecast PS&E segment profits of ¥7 bn, a decline from ¥8 bn in 1Q3/26. Our estimates do not incorporate any IEEPA tariff refund impact, nor is there any IEEPA tariff refund impact factored into KHI's guidance.

It is not yet clear whether KHI will also receive tariff refund benefits in 1Q3/27, but if it does, this could represent upside to our segment profit estimate. Polaris' management expects flat yoy sales in 2H, with an expected positive impact on net pricing from factors such as lower promotional expenses. If KHI's marketing of new models goes well, sales promotion expenses could come in below our estimates, potentially resulting in upside to our full-year segment profit estimate of ¥26 bn (vs. guidance of ¥30 bn). At the same time, given that Polaris appears to be gaining share in utility ORVs, we will also be watching closely for trends in Kawasaki Motors' market share. We maintain our Neutral rating.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Kawasaki Heavy Industries (7012.T)

Our 12-month target price of ¥3,500 is based on FY3/28E EV/EBITDA, applying the Japan Aerospace & Defense subsector-average multiple of 14X and a 30% sector-relative discount.

The key upside and downside risks include (1) yen depreciation/appreciation relative to our assumed exchange rate, (2) faster-/slower-than-expected expansion of defense order and revenue momentum, (3) lower-/higher-than-expected cost variability in PS&E, particularly promotional expenses in the North American four-wheeler business, and (4) faster-/slower-than-expected progress on portfolio restructuring and the turnaround of low-profitability segments, with implications for the conglomerate discount.
