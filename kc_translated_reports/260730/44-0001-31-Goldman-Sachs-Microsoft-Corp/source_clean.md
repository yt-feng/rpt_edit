# Microsoft Corp. (MSFT)
# 4QFY: Microsoft's role is increasingly strategic as enterprises move from frontier models to frontier ecosystems

Microsoft is indicated +7% after hours post a solid 4QFY (June) report. 4Q revenue of \$90.0bn (+17% cc yoy) was 3% above the Street and non-GAAP EPS of \$4.74 (+23% yoy) was 11% above the Street; and we detail key pieces to guidance below (FactSet). We view this quarter as a meaningful step in reversing the stock's multi-quarter period of underperformance, as Microsoft delivered tangible proof points across several key investor debates: Azure acceleration against ongoing capacity constraints and 1P mix allocation, improving AI unit economics with stable cloud margins, and increasing evidence of Copilot monetization.

There are several levers to Azure monetization and ROI. We believe the impact of higher inflation costs on capex are well understood (\~14% impact to CY26 capex). However, internal margin targets do not change even when component pricing is higher. Microsoft provided several datapoints on better monetization of Azure that we think are key in stabilizing ROI on a higher cost base, including a) nearly 50% faster dock to live times for GPUs, and b) ongoing scaling and block and tackle on cost, c) thoughtful bundling and pricing, including on Github power users, PaaS and CPU-related services. We look to whether improvements in pricing, internal silicon and model diversification can drive a further step function change in margins in FY27.

The center of gravity in the enterprise is shifting away from frontier models to frontier ecosystems. Copilot is showing stronger adoption under E7 and Copilot Cowork, reinforcing Microsoft's view that AI monetization extends beyond seat growth alone. Microsoft exited 4Q with >30M paid Copilot seats, or +10M net seat adds, more than 2x sequentially. Several indicators suggest deployments are broadening, and that the compounding effect of product innovation is driving higher usage. User satisfaction scores have doubled over the last three quarters, conversations per user are up \~2x yoy, and average engagement is now comparable to Outlook and Teams. Importantly, Microsoft expects M365 Commercial Cloud growth to accelerate throughout FY27, driven by Copilot adoption, premium SKU mix including E5 and E7, and consumption-based monetization opportunities. Two months after launch, E7 has been adopted by hundreds of enterprise customers representing millions of seats, including a 400K-seat deployment at E&Y. We view the shift toward a seat-plus-consumption model as a meaningful expansion of Microsoft's TAM, particularly as customers embed Copilot into enterprise workflows and the industry shifts to value defined by work units. GitHub's recent transition to a new pricing framework drove stronger consumption revenue and improving margins, providing a good precedent for how Copilot monetization could evolve as usage-based revenue scales.

## Valuation and Key Risks

We increase our 12-month price target to \$640 (vs. \$610 prior), based on a 28x P/E (unchanged) multiple to Microsoft's SNTM adjusted net income while updating our estimates for 4QFY results and initial FY27 guidance. Key downside risks include: longer ramp for internal silicon which can limit market share gains or gross margin expansion, greater investments in projects outside of expectations (e.g., non-Azure), key leadership changes, and more meaningful shift to custom software that could negatively impact its applications business.
