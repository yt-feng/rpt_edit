## China: Rising evidence of a geographic K-shape

The widening geographic inequality will likely force Beijing to speed up fiscal reforms to provide local governments with a more solid tax base.

## Ting Lu - NIHK

\- We see more hard evidence supporting our views that the fruits of AI-driven growth are mainly reaped by a few “smart” cities and the AI boom will lead to a geographic K-shape.

\- Although real national GDP growth slowed to 4.7% y-o-y in H1 2026 from 5.0% in 2025, we estimate weighted average GDP growth of the seven “smart” cities, including three tier-1 and four select tier-2 cities, rose to 5.6% from 5.4%. Notably, the higher growth was broad-based across all seven cities, which jointly contributed 20% of the national growth in H1, the highest in at least two decades. By contrast, real GDP growth for the rest of country, which accounts for about 83% of national GDP, fell markedly to 4.5% from 4.9%.

\- Details suggest that “smart” cities are particularly benefiting from AI-related economic activity, such as FAI. Although national FAI recorded a steep contraction of 5.7% y-o-y in H1, Beijing and Shanghai reported positive FAI growth of 3.0% and 6.8%, respectively.

\- As solid growth in a few “smart” cities is unlikely to offset mounting growth headwinds across the rest of country, we believe Beijing will likely step up policy efforts in H2. That said, the K-shaped economy naturally limits the rationale for aggressive, sweeping stimulus.
