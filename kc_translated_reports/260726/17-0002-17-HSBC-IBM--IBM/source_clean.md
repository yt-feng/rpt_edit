# Reduce: Impressive margin guidance but better value elsewhere

IBM cut its 2026 constant currency revenue guidance but still guided to non-GAAP PBT margin improving 100bp y-o-y

\- Increasing our 2026-30e non-GAAP EPS estimates by 1-2% to reflect higher-than-expected margin guidance

\- TP cut to USD174 (from USD175) despite 1-2% higher EPS estimates due to lower multiples. Maintain Reduce

Impressive margin guidance: As pre-announced on 14 July 2026, 2Q26 revenue rose $1\%$ y-o-y cc to USD17,161m (1Q26: $6\%$ ). The company cut its 2026 cc revenue growth guidance to $4\%-5\%$ from more than $5\%$ previously. Despite the cut to revenue guidance, the management maintained it expects 2026 non-GAAP profit before tax margin to rise by 100bp y-o-y. 2Q26 non-GAAP profit before tax margin rose 30bp y-o-y but it was largely a result of a favourable USD280m non-GAAP other income in 2Q26 (2Q25: USD65m). The extent to which the expected margin improvement comes from one-off items such as other income is a key monitorable.

Software segment disappoints: Software segment cc growth slowed to 4.6% from 8% in 1Q26. Organic growth slipped to 0% y-o-y in 2Q26 (1Q26: we estimate 7%). Management cut 2026 cc revenue growth guidance to 6-8% from more than 10% previously. Transaction processing subsegment dipped 9% y-o-y (cc) (1Q26: +2%). The management expects low- to mid-single-digit % decline in 2H26 but suggested most of the missing revenue is likely delayed and not lost. Hybrid cloud subsegment cc growth accelerated to 11% y-o-y in 2Q26 (1Q26: 10%). Automation segment cc growth slipped to 3% in 2Q26 (1Q26: 7%). Reported growth for the data subsegment accelerated to 19% in 2Q26 (1Q26: 16%), but, net of c18-19pp inorganic contribution from the Confluent acquisition in 2Q26, organic growth slowed considerably.

Infrastructure segment inflecting: Infrastructure cc revenue dipped 7% y-o-y in 2Q26 (1Q26: +12%) as the new z17 series entered its fifth quarter since launch. The weakness in the mainframes subsegment was partially offset by a strong distributed infrastructure subsegment where revenue rose 37% y-o-y (1Q26: 13%), on a low base. The management now expects infrastructure segment cc revenue to rise by low-single-digit % in 2026 (previously low-single-digit decline) largely due to stronger distributed infra subsegment.

Expensive valuation: IBM shares are trading at 14.2x CY27e non-GAAP EV/EBIT (sector median: 13.9x). We expect IBM's non-GAAP EPS to rise at a CAGR of $6.7\%$ over CY26-28e (sector median $19.2\%$ ). Our TP of USD174 (was USD175) is based on our SOTP valuation. Our TP implies $15.4\%$ downside, and we maintain a Reduce rating as we see better value elsewhere.

## Impressive margin guidance

IBM announced its 2Q26 results on 22 July 2026. The company pre-announced most of the key income statement items on 14 July 2026 but had not updated its full year guidance.

2Q26 revenue rose 1% y-o-y (constant currency) to USD17,161m (1Q26: 6%). The company cut its 2026 constant currency revenue growth guidance to 4%-5% from more than 5% previously.

Despite the cut to revenue growth guidance, the management maintained it expects non-GAAP profit before tax margin to rise by 100bp y-o-y due to higher productivity and efficiency, better supply chain optimization and lower third-party spend. 2Q26 non-GAAP profit before tax margin rose 30bp y-o-y but it was largely a result of a favourable USD280m non-GAAP other income in 2Q26 (2Q25: USD65m). Non-GAAP operating margin dipped 106bp y-o-y to $20.4\%$ . But for the favourable impact of other income, the non-GAAP PBT margin would have been down about 90bp y-o-y.

## Disappointing software performance

Software segment cc growth slowed to 4.6% from 8% in 1Q26. Organic growth slipped to 0% y-o-y in 2Q26 (1Q26: we estimate 7%). Management cut 2026 cc revenue growth guidance to 6-8% from more than 10% previously. The management flagged that c20% of IBM's software revenue comes from a point in time (as opposed to subscription revenue). A large part of the point in time revenue comes from customers' "capex budget". As customers redirected their capex towards servers/storage/memory, ahead of expected price hikes, IBM's mainframe hardware and point in time revenue faced setbacks. Annual recurring revenue, however, rose 8% y-o-y in 2Q26, suggesting continued strength.

The transaction processing subsegment dipped $9\%$ y-o-y (cc) (1Q26: $+2\%$ ), which we consider largely a timing issue driven by a lumpy revenue recognition cycle as explained in our note Disappointing preannouncement; limited read across for the broader sector dated 16 July 2026. The management expects low- to mid-single-digit $\%$ decline in 2H26 but suggested most of the missing revenue is likely delayed and not lost. The management hinted weak 2026 revenue could help boost 2027 growth.

Hybrid cloud subsegment cc growth accelerated to 11% y-o-y in 2Q26 (1Q26: 10%).

Data and automation segments however disappointed. Automation segment cc growth slipped to 3% in 2Q26 (1Q26: 7%).

Reported growth for the data subsegment accelerated to $19\%$ in 2Q26 (1Q26: $16\%$ ). However, growth in 2Q26 likely includes an additional 18-19pp inorganic contribution from the Confluent acquisition that closed on 17 March 2026. The management had previously guided to a constant currency growth of $20 - 25\%$ in 2Q26. Consequently, we consider the reported growth a significant sequential slowdown. The company blamed the slowdown on a largely lumpy revenue recognition cycle.

## Infrastructure segment to inflect on distributed infrastructure subsegment growth

Infrastructure cc revenue dipped 7% y-o-y in 2Q26 (1Q26: +12%) as the new z17 series entered its fifth quarter since launch. Typically, revenue from new mainframe series generally begins to decline from the fifth quarter onwards, but the 2Q26 decline of 42% was unusually strong (e.g., z16 revenue was down only 30% y-o-y in 2Q23, its fifth quarter since launch). The management flagged customers were forced to prioritize their capex elsewhere due to rising component costs.

The weakness in the mainframes subsegment was partially offset by a strong distributed infrastructure subsegment where revenue rose 37% y-o-y (1Q26: 13%). Acceleration in growth is partially due to a low base. Revenue slipped 17% y-o-y in 2Q25 (1Q25: -4%).

The management now expects infrastructure segment cc revenue to rise by low-single-digit % in 2026 (previously low-single-digit decline). The management flagged strong growth in distributed infrastructure as the foremost driver for the increased growth guidance.

## Consulting segment continues to disappoint

Consulting segment cc revenue rose 1% y-o-y in 2Q26 (1Q26: 1%). IBM's consulting segment growth continued to underperform the broader IT services sector. Reported growth of 0.2% lagged the broader IT services 2Q26e reported growth of 3.6%.

The management expects cc revenue growth to accelerate to low- to mid-single-digit for the full year 2026. We are modelling 2H26 cc revenue growth of $1.6\%$ y-o-y (1H: $1\%$ ).

## Changes in HSBC estimates

There are no material changes to our revenue estimates.

We are increasing our non-GAAP operating profit estimates by 1-2% as we were previously expecting the company to strongly cut its margin guidance along with the revenue growth guidance.

We will, however, focus on the quality of margin improvement and whether it comes from one-off factors. One-off factors such as favourable other income and/or the impact of cheaply acquired historical inventory are unlikely to change IBM's investment merits, while better efficiency and productivity obviously will.

## Software

Our target EV of USD115,144m is 10.9x 2027e non-GAAP EV/EBIT. SAP, a pure play software company, is trading at 10.9x 2027e non-GAAP EV/EBIT. We expect IBM software's non-GAAP EBIT to rise at a CAGR of 10.2% over CY26-30e, in line with 10.3% for SAP. Over 2023-26e, IBM's software non-GAAP EBIT rose at a CAGR of 13.2% vs 21.6% for SAP.

We previously used a target EV/EBIT of 11.3x and have cut this to 10.9x to reflect the sector-wide (and SAP's) derating.

## Consulting

Our target EV of USD15,647m is 6.7x 2027e non-GAAP EV/EBIT. Accenture, a close peer for the segment, is trading at 6.7x 2027e non-GAAP EV/EBIT (using Visible Alpha consensus). We expect IBM consulting's non-GAAP EBIT to rise at a CAGR of $3.4\%$ over CY26-30e, slightly below the $4.6\%$ for Accenture. Over 2023-26e, IBM's software non-GAAP EBIT rose at a CAGR of $6.6\%$ vs $5.5\%$ for Accenture. Accenture generally outperformed IBM in revenue growth, but IBM likely did a better job of improving margins on a low base. We estimate IBM consulting's non-GAAP operating margin rose to $10.4\%$ in 2026e from $8.7\%$ in 2023. In comparison, Accenture's non-GAAP EBIT margin improved to $15.8\%$ from $15.5\%$ in the same period.

## Infrastructure

Our target EV of USD21,079m is 8.7x 2027e non-GAAP EV/EBIT. We do not have any true peer for IBM's mainframe focussed infrastructure/hardware business. HP is trading at 6.7x CY27e non-GAAP EV/EBIT. We use a $30\%$ premium to HP to be conservative\* and because we consider IBM's infrastructure business strategically better and differentiated.

Financially, we consider HP a close enough peer. We expect (and management guides to) a roughly flat revenue trajectory for IBM's infrastructure segment through the cycle. HP's business of selling printers/printer supplies and personal computers is similar. We expect 2025-30e non-GAAP operating profit CAGR of $0.5\%$ (2020-25e CAGR: $-1.1\%$ ).

\*As our rating is Reduce, a higher multiple is conservative
