# Investor Presentation | Japan
# Autos: Stock Selection Considering the Middle East Situation

Focus on valuation appeal despite numerous headwinds (supply chain risks, higher raw material costs, slower auto demand). Individual stock ratings include OW for Suzuki, UW for Isuzu, and EW for Toyota but with a positive bias.


- In addition to existing structural risks including 1) competition vs. China OEMs and 2) the investment burden from electrification and software, significant headwinds since developments in the Middle East are 3) lingering risk of auto production stoppages on supply-chain constraints as passage through the Strait of Hormuz has yet to be normalized, 4) higher raw material prices, and 5) a slowdown in auto sales (demand shift from ICE to HEV and EV).
- That said, share price falls have boosted the valuation appeal of individual names (Suzuki trading near historical low on P/B, Honda, SUBARU, Mazda offering dividend yields of \~5%).
• S&P Global forecasts global auto production declining 2% YoY.
- Effects of rising raw material prices: We have adjusted our earnings forecasts assuming cost growth/vehicle of \~¥80k (US\$500) for C-D segment passenger cars. Of this, we allow for increases of \~¥40k (US\$250) each for crude oil-related materials and for other materials such as steel products, aluminum, copper, precious metals and semis.
- Other key points to watch include the US midterm elections and developments around the USMCA, which is due for review in July. Under USMCA, if the required ratio of components produced in the US is raised, we would expect a negative impact on Toyota, Honda, Nissan and Mazda.

# Individual stock ratings

• Overweight (OW): Suzuki: Slower demand due to rising gasoline prices in India is a concern, but price increases are absorbing some of the growth in raw material costs and the earnings undertone is relatively firm.
- Equal-weight (EW): Toyota, Yamaha, Honda, Mazda, SUBARU, Mitsubishi Motors, Nissan: We think Toyota is likely to gradually raise conservative guidance, also has prospects of increased earnings from HEVs. Our focus for Honda is on business relating to energy storage systems.
- Underweight (UW): Isuzu: We think the firm faces high hurdles to delivering guidance assuming delayed recovery in CVs in N. America and LCVs in Thailand.

# OP Impact of Rising Raw Material Prices: How did companies factor this into their guidance?

- We believe it is appropriate to compare the impact of cost increases across companies on a per-unit basis by aggregating both Middle East-related factors (e.g., petroleum-based resins) and non-Middle East-related factors (e.g., copper, aluminum, semiconductors, supplier payments).
- Based on a comparison of total cost increases per unit, Toyota, Honda, and SUBARU appear to be using relatively conservative assumptions.
- Suzuki has not incorporated Middle East-related impacts into its guidance. However, the figures presented as outside the guidance can be viewed as somewhat conservative.

# Environmental Regulations

European CO2 emissions regulations

Source: EEA, MS

- EU tightened new vehicle CO $_{2}$ limits in 2025 from 118g/km to 94g, with fines of €95 per gram over the limit.
- In March 2025, in response to requests from automakers, the European Commission announced that it will keep the 94g limit, but it takes the average over three years (2025-27), rather than the result for 2025 alone.
- In December 2025, the European Commission announced plans to scrap the 2035 ban on new internal combustion engine cars, signaling a shift toward looser environmental rules.

US Zero-Emission Vehicle (ZEV) regulations – EV and PHEV sales mix (volume)

Source: CARB, MS

- Advanced Clean Cars II (ACC II) enacted by California Air Resources Board (CARB) requires a ZEV sales ratio of 35% for 2026 model year cars, gradually increasing to 100% by 2035 (automakers that fail to meet the standards must pay a fine of up to \$20k per vehicle short of the target).
- However, in May 2025, the US Congress passed a resolution to nullify the ZEV regulations.
- In response, in June 2025, states such as California filed a lawsuit seeking to overturn the congressional resolution.

# Environmental Regulations and Subsidy Policies in the US

# ☐ Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) Regulations

- If the fuel economy target is not met, a fine of \$5 per vehicle (up to \$10) will be imposed for every 0.1 mpg shortfall.
- The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) estimated that by the 2032 model year, the industry could face fines totaling \$14 billion.
• However, in July 2025, a decision was made to nullify this penalty.

# ☐ Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Regulations

• In Feb 2026, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) announced the repeal of greenhouse gas (GHG) emission standards for vehicles and engines manufactured or imported into the US.
- The repeal will take effect on April 20; however, products manufactured or imported since 2012 may be subject to retroactive application, even if they were produced prior to the effective date.

# ☐ Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) – EV Tax Credit

\- The tax credit for purchasing electric vehicles (BEV, PHEV, FCEV), up to \$7,500, expired at the end of September 2025.
