# ASEN Banks Evolving Industry Landscape

# Indonesia

- Rebuilt in the wake of the 1998 Asia Financial Crisis, Indonesia's banks have grown loans at an $8\%$ CAGR (2014-25) This has been driven by the country's private debt to GDP ratio of less than $50\%$ , the lowest in the region, and strong economic growth in the recent past.
- The industry's loan/deposit ratio (LDR), climbed from 44% in 2002 to 96% in May-19 as deposits lagged loan growth. In the past two years, softer loan growth and rising liquidity has driven a fall in LDR to 80% (Dec 2022), but has recovered to \~90% now.
- Sector is dominated by four large banks, there is also a long tail of mid and small sized banks, potentially leading to more consolidation.

# Thailand

- A developing market with structural issues due to highly indebted economy, (household debt of GDP of \~87%) and ageing demographics.
- Loan growth has slowed significantly since 2014 to 2-5%. Asset quality has been an issue since 2012 when the banks faced consumer/ auto credit quality issue, followed by SME credit issues in 2014 and more recently credit quality issues from Covid-19. After several years of loan book consolidation, the question is whether credit quality is now on a sounder footing.
- Returns have been under pressure from lower fees and higher credit charges. Banks looking to develop higher margin businesses outside of traditional competencies e.g., digital unsecured lending, wealth
- Relatively concentrated banking system leaving little room for further consolidation

# Philippines

• Banking sector predominantly caters to corporate customers; this is one of the drivers of low historic RoA. Loan growth slowed materially in 2019-21, before recovering to 8% loan growth in 2023 and 12% in 2024 and 10% in 2025.
- Financial inclusion is on the central bank's agenda and there is a strong push towards cashless transactions, but banks have lagged in their digital transformation.
- Cost to income ratio is one of the highest in the region driven by investment and the Philippines archipelagic geography. Asset quality has not been an issue since the GFC and the banks have managed credit risks from Covid-19 well, helped by low system leverage.
- Historically, capital ratios tend to be at the lower end, and capital raising is common in each credit cycle but low loan growth over the past years has changed the dynamics.

# Evolving Industry Landscape

# Singapore

• A mature economy with a relatively low loan growth of 3-5%. Interest rate has been one of the largest drivers of returns. Market is relatively consolidated.
- Most of the banks have regional presence with DBS and OCBC having a Greater China focus and UOB has more of a focus in ASEAN.
- Singapore banks have built a respectable fee franchise, particularly wealth management. In recent years there has been a greater focus on capital returns. The banks are seen as global leaders in digitization, especially DBS. Singapore is a leader in the digital assets space.

# Malaysia

- A relatively mature market and loan growth has slowed to 5-6%, with mortgages as one of the largest drivers of loan growth. Asset quality in Malaysia has been relatively stable.
• Some of the banks have regional ASEAN presence. CIMB has significant exposure to Indonesia and Maybank has significant exposure to Singapore.
- There is a renewed focus on managing returns with rising shareholder payouts. In the past there has been discussions on banks' consolidation, even though the sector is already relatively consolidated.
- Since Malaysia has a large Muslim population, the country has a dual banking system, conventional and Islamic.

# Who are the HK Banks?

# HSBC (5.HK)

- A London-headquartered bank with a dominant market share in HK (33% group revenues) and a large presence in UK (20% group revenues). The rest of the group spread across the rest of Asia and Europe, the Middle East and the Americas.
• A deposit-driven franchise with a strong CASA ratio, especially in HK. HSBC is rate sensitive.
- The key differentiating factor is its global network, with a strong focus on leveraging that network to support trade and capital flows and to grow its wealth businesses.
• RoE improving and the group targets 17% or better RoTE in each year from 2026 to 2028.
• We expect increased IT investment as HSBC looks to transform its business.

# Standard Chartered (2888.HK)

• A London-headquartered, emerging markets-focused bank, with 60% of income from Asia, 6% from UAE and 14% from the UK and US. It has 81k employees across 54 markets.
- It survived GFC well but was hit by credit problems in 2015-17. The turnaround and transformation are now complete. Management is focused on achieving leadership in four key areas, EM wholesale banking, mass retail, affluent client business (wealth) and sustainability.
- RoTE is improving (11.9% in 2025), the key driver of share prices will be the extent to which RoTE can be lifted towards the >15% target by 2028e.

# BOCHK (2388.HK)

• 65.65% owned by Bank Of China (BOC).
- HK-focused business, with 15%/20% deposit/loan market share in HK and a CASA ratio of 53.4%. Historically, it has grown loans and deposits ahead of the market.
• CNY clearing bank, dominant market share in China-linked IPOs.
- Mainland China presence is lower than other local HK banks (5% total loans). In 2016/17, it acquired most of BOC's ASEAN businesses.
- Operates with a low cost/income ratio (\~24%), low credit costs (<50bps), and high capital ratios. \~50% dividend payout.

# SGX – Nasdaq Dual Listing Bridge


Allows eligible companies to simultaneously raise capital and access liquidity in both Asia and the US using a single set of offering documents.


Consultation on the listing rule book was published in Jan 2026.


SGX RegCo announced new listing rules for GLB in April 2026.


Parliament has passed the Bill for Global Listing Board which is set to go live in the middle of 2026.


Who this targets

Quality growth companies with:


Market capitalisation of ≥ S\$2bn


An Asian nexus and global ambitions


At least 15% of the fundraising for the IPO or S\$75 million, whichever is higher, must be raised in Singapore.


# Value Unlock Programme


SGX is co-launching the S\$30m "Value Unlock" programme with MAS.


SGX's role focuses on:

• Helping listed companies improve investor communications
• Supporting clearer articulation of strategy, capital allocation and value creation
• Facilitating engagement platforms and peer learning with ecosystem partners


SGX RegCo proposes requiring disclosures for dividend, remuneration and IR policies.


What companies get

Grants (funded by MAS) to build capabilities in:


Corporate strategy


Capital optimisation


Investor relations


# Market Structure Enhancement


Modernise post-trade custody model: SGX will facilitate investor adoption of broker custody accounts. Retail investors will have the option of keeping their CDP direct accounts if they wish to do so. SGX published consultation paper in Jan 2026.


Lower board lot size to improve investor access – reducing the board lot size for securities above S\$10 per share from 100 to 10 units.


# Small and mid-cap Stocks


SGX Indices launched iEdge Singapore Next 50 Indices in Sep 2025 to track the top 50 mainboard companies by market capitalization, excluding the largest 30 constituents of the Straits Times Index (STI).


Institutions net-purchased small- and mid-cap stocks for a fourth consecutive month, as of end April, taking the 12-month tally to S\$700 million.


Collectively, these initiatives enhance market competitiveness, expand investor access and support sustainable value creation for Singapore-listed companies.

# Hong Kong — A Hybrid Market Influenced by Key Global Trends and China Regulatory Influence

- As a regional financial center, it is important for HK to be at the forefront of developing digital assets. HK's digital asset development has been at least five years in the making, with the launch of project Evergreen alongside the Fintech 2025 strategy in 2021.
- RWA and Tokenziation could provide opportunities for the HK capital market. However, the ban on tokenization of China-related assets could slow development.
- Stablecoin usage remains crypto-focused, and penetration into key financial payment ecosystems hinges on progress of linking stablecoins from Hong Kong issuers to crypto trading.
- On the positive side, we believe the development of stablecoin regulations will help certain financial firms from Hong Kong and mainland China to gain market share in the global crypto trading market. We see Futu as a key beneficiary of stablecoin development in the Hong Kong market.

# Project Ensemble

Focuses on wholesale CBDC infrastructure to support tokenized money interbank settlement. HKMA provides foundational rails, while banks commercialize.

# HKMA Stablecoin Ordinance

Granted the first stablecoin licenses to HSBC (planning HKD stablecoin 2H26) and a Standard Chartered-led consortium (Anchorpoint Financial) in April 2026.

# Singapore — Digital Assets an Important Component of Its Hub of Hubs Strategy

- Singapore probably represents the most advanced tokenization ecosystem in Asia, characterized by multi-participant networks, regulatory clarity, and a transition from pilot to commercialization.
- MAS is keen to foster the development of digital currencies, but not speculation in cryptocurrencies. Project Dunbar involved the creation of central bank digital currencies and their use in settling international payments.

# Project Guardian

A collaborative initiative among policymakers — including international bodies such as the FCA (UK), FSA (JP) and IMF — associations and cooperatives such as SWIFT and ISDA, and the financial industry to enhance liquidity and market efficiency through asset tokenization

# Partior

Provides a ledger allowing banks and ultimately corporations to make secure, real-time, 24/7 transactions.

# Contour Network Hub

A digital treasury and trade platform hub that enables the digitalization of treasury and trade finance processes such as Letters of Credit (LCs) and bank guarantees.

# Project Dunbar

Involved the creation of central bank digital currencies and their use in settling international payments.

MAS has allowed regulated exchanges, including SGX, to set up cryptocurrency futures. DBS also has a digital asset exchange and provides digital asset storage to HNW customers.

# Emerging ASEAN — Slower Paced Adoption

# Malaysia

- Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) launched the digital asset innovation hub (DAIH) in June 2025 and published a discussion paper "Asset Tokenisation in the Malaysian Financial Sector" in October 2025.
- Three initiatives - Ringitt stablecoins for B2B settlement led by Standard Chartered and AirAsia; and two separate tokenized deposit initiatives from CIMB and Maybank.

# Thailand

- The Bank of Thailand (BOT) has advanced tokenization across wholesale, retail, and cross-border domains through multiple initiatives over the past several years. Its wholesale CBDC journey started in 2018 with Project Inthanon, which engaged local participating banks to explore the feasibility of DLT to enhance Thailand's financial infrastructure.
- Founding member for Project mBridge, a platform demonstrating enhanced efficiency of cross-border transactions using multi-currency CBDC compared to the current system by eliminating the correspondent banking network

# Indonesia

- Bank Indonesia pursued tokenization through Project Garuda in 2022, its CBDC initiative designed to explore the architecture of the Digital Rupiah. BI published a White Paper containing the high-level design of Digital Rupiah.
• In December 2024, Bank Indonesia completed the proof of concept for Project Garuda.

# Philippines

- The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) completed proof of concept of its wholesale central bank digital currency initiative, Project Agila, in 2024. The project engaged various FIs and tested the technology for large value payments.
- BSP targets to issue its wholesale CBDC by 2029. BSP is also exploring the use of CBDC to settle government securities following the pilot of the tokenized treasury bonds.
• The BSP has no immediate plans for retail CBDC.
