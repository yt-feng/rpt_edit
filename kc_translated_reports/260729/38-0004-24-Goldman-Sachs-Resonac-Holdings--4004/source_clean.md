# Resonac Holdings (4004.T): Announces additional investment in HD media for AI data centers, unusual price hike agreement

Resonac announced additional investments in increasing HD media capacity on July 28. The company plans to invest c.¥15 bn at its Singapore site to increase HD media capacity for AI data center nearline applications by c.20 mn units/year, utilizing some idle equipment to raise investment efficiencies (production lines will be brought online in phases from 2029 onward, depending on market trends and demand). Moreover, in a move that is highly unusual for a Japanese materials company, the company said it had agreed to price revisions with its HDD manufacturer customers that are equivalent to the planned investment. Management aims to simultaneously expand supply capacity and enhance profitability. Resonac only recently announced a 50 mn unit/year capacity increase on May 11, 2026 (slated to commence operations in phases in 2027), and including both announcements, its HD media production capacity is set to increase by c.44% from c.160 mn units at present to c.230 mn units. The company’s HD media generates the second-largest portion of sales in its semiconductor and electronic materials business after copper-clad laminates/prepregs (based on our FY12/26 estimates). With the positive impact of price hikes likely, in addition to medium- to long-term volume growth, our impression of the news is positive. We reiterate our Buy rating.

Citing data from an external research firm, the company expects strong demand growth, with a $+23\%$ CAGR in nearline HDD shipment capacity from 2025 to 2030. Against this backdrop, the latest release reflects management's intent to solidify a stable supply framework through timely and flexible investments as the world's only merchant HD media supplier. While a 50-mn-unit/year capacity increase is already under way at the Singapore site, with operations set to come online sequentially from 2027, this new investment plan will add a further 20-mn-unit/year capacity increase on top.

Furthermore, the explicit mention that the company has secured agreements from its HDD manufacturer customers for price hikes equivalent to the investment amount on this project (although the magnitude and timing of the price hikes have not yet been disclosed) leaves a favorable impression, as it formally validates the company's efforts to raise prices. Disclosures that go so far as to explicitly state price revision agreements within a capacity expansion announcement are rare in the Japanese materials sector. As such, we believe this will be seen positively as a signal of Resonac's strong industry positioning as the only merchant manufacturer, its high technological prowess, and its disciplined approach to investment.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

## M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
