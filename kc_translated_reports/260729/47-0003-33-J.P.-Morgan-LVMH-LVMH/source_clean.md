# JPM

Our Take: LVMH reported slightly better H1 26 results yesterday after market close, with sales broadly in line and stronger margins thanks to very tight cost control. Q2 sales were +3% organic (JPMe: +2%), led by very strong momentum at Watches & Jewellery (+11% ex-FX vs JPMe +8%, including Tiffany and Bulgari up mid-teens), Selective Retailing (+6% organic vs JPMe +3%) and a nice surprise by Wines & Spirits (at +5% ex-FX vs JPMe -4%). The all-important F&LG returned to positive territory, after 7 consecutive quarters of declines, albeit slightly so despite the very easy base of comparison (-9% in Q2 25) and a tad below expectations (at around +2%, JPMe: +1%). Encouragingly, Dior accelerated materially, back to positive and above divisional average, a positive sign that consumers are responding to the refreshed offer that continues to build as we go into the rest of the year. However, the divisional delivery remained muted, in a luxury backdrop that in our view was not that unfavourable (e.g. Richemont sales +20% ex-FX in the quarter, Zegna Group DTC +17% ex-FX, even Swatch +9% ex-FX), leaving the debate on LVMH F&LG growth to return to more attractive levels still open in our view. This confirms our views that category exposure is still driving strong polarisation, with hard luxury and very high end RTW (Loro Piana was called out as outperforming) continuing to benefit from healthy luxury consumer trends, vs soft luxury still digesting some of the past excesses (on pricing, elevation, volumes growth). The main positive news of the release was the solid delivery on margins, both at Group and F&LG level, as LVMH continues its hard work on opex control. This, and more favourable fx, lead us to raise our forecast by 1%, in our view enough to provide some support to the shares at these levels (notably given the recent pullback into the print), but also likely not enough of a catalyst for the debate to turn more constructive for the time being.

\- Zooming in on FL&G: 1) Q2 sales growth missed expectations, +1% ex-FX (JPMe +1%, cons +2%), improving sequentially vs Q1 on comps 400bps easier. Excluding the Middle East impact, Q2 was +2%, with moderate price increase & flat mix. 2) By brand, both LV and Dior were positive, with LV consistent with the divisional average and Dior higher than the average. Loro Piana and Rimowa were outperforming the division average, while Celine and Fendi are making "progress." 3) On Dior, management remained very bullish on the creative renewal under Jonathan Anderson, calling out the strong reception for Women's Ready-to-Wear and for handbags. All key clusters accelerated, including +DD in Americans and Japanese. However, the creative renewal and the transition means it is "a bit complex" for the supply chain. 4) By nationality, the bulk of growth came from the Americans, +HSD, while Koreans also contributed although from a small base. Europeans and Chinese were flattish in Q2 (in line with Q1), Japanese were flattish (Q1 -L/MSD%), while Middle Eastern customers were still negative although improving from the DD% decline in Q1. Chinese demand is increasingly centred around shopping events. 5) On margins, F&LG H1 EBIT margin was down 60bps to reach 34.1% (JPMe 33.7%, cons 33.4%), entirely driven by FX headwinds. At constant currencies, EBIT improved modestly and EBIT margin was flat.

## Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation

## Investment Thesis

LVMH is the leader globally in Luxury Fashion & Leather Goods with Louis Vuitton, the leader in premium Champagne & Spirits and the leader in Perfume & Cosmetics selective distribution with Sephora. Following years of stellar performance, notably in F&LG (incl. at mega brands LV and Dior), the division has been going through a period of normalisation, with FY25 its first year of segmental sales declines. We do also wonder whether the leather goods category might have started experiencing some fatigue, following years of exceptional demand and very positive pricing and mix backdrops. While recent moves in creative directions are expected to infuse some freshness and energy in the product offering across different brands as 2026 progresses, this seems to be already factored in the estimates, leaving limited upside risk, and if anything we see some downside risk to estimates given the increasing volatile geo-political and consumer environment. In the absence of positive earnings revisions from here, we retain our Neutral rating and would not chase the stock despite the recent correction.

## Valuation

Our Dec-27 price target is based on a SOTP valuation methodology. We use a '27e EV/EBIT multiple for W&S at a 5% premium to the sector average, for Louis Vuitton at a 25% premium to peers, 10% discount vs Richemont for Watches & Jewellery and for Perfumes & Cosmetics in line with HPC; we use a '27e EV/Sales multiple for the rest of F&LG in line with the rest of the sector. Our SOTP is supported by our DCF (mid-term growth of 5%, terminal growth of 3.5%, WACC of 9%).

\- Changes to estimates: Banking the H1 EBIT beat, and better fx assumptions in H2. we have increased our forecasts by 1%. Within the mix, though, we lowered our F&LG growth forecasts, to +1% in Q3 (from +3.5%) and to +2.5% in Q4 (from +4%), leading to F&LG sales flat ex-FX in FY26.

\- Peer read-across: We think this reporting season has so far showed very mixed trends and very company/brand/category specific dynamics. On the categories, the LVMH print echoes the strong trends we saw at Richemont (albeit with a different magnitude) and that we will likely see also at Kering Jewellery today after market close. In contrast, for leather goods we think the LVMH print highlights still challenging trends overall, with capped pricing and price mix dynamics, leaving growth highly dependent on volumes and hence on execution. This could potentially limit the ability of other leather goods stories to accelerate from here (Hermes possibly more than Kering given the much tougher base of comparison). Regionally, LVMH echoed the positive messages on the American, Japanese and Korean consumers (again for hard luxury more than soft), albeit the Chinese remained soft (flat on double digit negative comp) confirming strong polarisation in the quarter (Richemont and Zegna improved sequentially, Swatch sounded cautious, Burberry slowed slightly). This again could limit the ability for both Kering and Hermes to reaccelerate for the time being. For the read-across to Beauty and Spirits players see the note from JPM analyst Celine Pannuti here.

\- Valuation: LVMH trades on 17.5x P/E on 2027 JPMe, vs. the luxury sector average 19.5x (ex-Brunello Cucinelli, Ferragamo, Hermes, Kering, Ferrari, Ferretti and Swatch), i.e. a 10% discount.

Key downside risks to our Neutral rating and target price:

\- Deterioration in the macro-economic environment, including a deterioration in the US

\- A euro re-appreciation vs the USD or RMB or yen

• Negative impact of large M&A

\- Lower-than-expected impact from normalisation

\- Consumers turning selective and gravitating to top brands

\- Innovation in leather goods driving brands out of the down-cycle faster than peers

\- Faster-than-expected recovery of the Chinese consumer

\- Forex
