## China Auto Industry

## June sales: Weak domestic, robust exports; Chinese brands gain share rapidly in Europe

CAAM's June sales release was broadly in line with expectations, with the sector still showing a clear split between strong export momentum and weak domestic demand. We believe the continued softness in domestic PV demand was likely one of the key reasons behind China auto sector's weakness in June, with MSCI China Autos -19% vs. MXCN's -8% during the month. June domestic PV sales fell 26% YoY to 1.5mn units, while PV exports rose 80% YoY to 0.9mn units, reinforcing our view that the sector's near-term positive fundamentals remain export-led rather than domestic market-led. Against this backdrop, we view the current pullback as a selective buy-the-dip opportunity especially for the one(s) with potential earnings beat at the upcoming 2Q26 result season in Aug (e.g. Geely Auto, Nio while BYD likely meet) rather than a broad-based sector call. We would only become more aggressive with clearer evidence of 1) domestic PV stabilization, 2) pricing/profitability discipline, and 3) export durability, while continuing to favor OEMs with resilient export growth, improving overseas competitiveness and positive earnings revision trends. Our preferred picks in 2H26 are Geely Auto, BYD and Nio. Event-wise, we will watch closely: 1) the upcoming interim results season in mid to late Aug; and 2) the next Trump-Xi meeting, tentatively scheduled for late Sept, for any incremental policy shifts from the US side on China's auto industry.

In this report, we continue to monitor what we view as a structural overseas expansion cycle for Chinese OEMs with a focus on three areas: 1) latest export dynamics, including growth momentum and geographic mix; 2) powertrain trends, particularly the rising role of PHEVs, which we believe could surprise overseas investors as adoption follows a similar path to China; and 3) Chinese OEMs' NEV market share in Europe, which has continued to rise and is becoming an increasingly important proof point of global competitiveness.

\- CAAM June data showed a modest sequential recovery, but the underlying mix remained uneven (Table1). In June, 1) total PV wholesales reached 2.4mn units, up $7\%$ mom but down $5\%$ yoy. 2) Domestic sales were still under pressure (up $4\%$ mom but down $23\%$ yoy), while exports reached a new high and continued to absorb part of the domestic shortfall. 3) PV NEV penetration rose further to a record high at $63\%$ , up from $61\%$ in May. 4) CV was the relative bright spot, with $10\%$ yoy growth in total sales and continued export strength, leading to a 1H26 run rate of $8\%$ yoy growth.

\- 1H26 PV exports maintain strong momentum, growing over $70\%$ yoy: Exports provide a meaningful growth and profitability opportunity for Chinese brands. In 1H26, China exported over 4.4mn units of PVs $(+72\% \mathrm{y / y})$ per CAAM. Europe (including the West and East) accounted for $37\%$ of the total - the largest destination by Chinese brands, followed by Asia's $30\%$ and South America's $17\%$ , based on ThinkerCar customs data in 5M26.

In addition, Chinese brands' PV share in the overall Europe/WE5 is also increasing steadily, reaching $\sim 10\% / 11\%$ in May (Figure 3-4). This supports our view that Chinese OEMs' overseas share gains are increasingly structural rather than cyclical, driven by 1) stronger NEV product competitiveness, 2) multiple-powertrain options from BEV to PHEV and ICE/HEV, 3) faster model rollouts, 4) attractive feature-to-price positioning, and 5) improving brand acceptance in overseas markets.

China's export mix has shifted quickly toward more NEVs: $52\%$ of PV exports were NEVs in 5M26, up from $42\%$ in 2024, based on ThinkerCar customs data. Within NEV, PHEV plays an increasingly important role, accounting for $38\%$ of total NEV exports by May-26 vs. only $11\%$ in Jan-24 (Figures 10-11).

\- Major Chinese OEMs export structures are becoming more regionally diversified, though along distinct paths by OEM: Among selected players: SAIC is the clearest Europe-led case, with Europe's share rising from 34% of exports in 2024 to 46% in 5M26. Chery Auto and BYD are also showing a rotation toward Europe and South America, while Asia's share has moderated. For Geely Auto, Asia remained the largest region but fell from 50% in FY25 to 37% in 5M26, Europe was the second-largest region at 28%, while South America rose sharply from 4% in 2024 to 18% in 5M26. In contrast, Great Wall Motor is shifting focus beyond Europe, with Europe's share of its overseas sales down from 65% last year to 38%, while Asia and South America have increased relatively. All in all, we find that Chinese OEM's overseas expansion is becoming more balanced, rather than dependent on one region. We summarize the key export OEMs' export structure by region in Figures 13–25.

JPM's “China Auto Buyer Sentiment Index” still suggests muted underlying demand: Our AI / data-driven proprietary sentiment index indicates overall buyer interest remains low - below the 25% quantile in the first week of July, after a moderate rebound in late June. Historically, we have found our “Buyer Sentiment Index” to be a useful indicator of inflection points in stock performance when 1) the index sees >2 consecutive weeks of a directional move - especially breaking downwards from the high-end 75% quantile or breaking upwards from the low-end 25% quantile, 2) combining the sentiment trend with fundamental analysis e.g. industry volume or the company’s new model launches. Given muted buyer sentiment, we believe China autos collectively may still trade sideways in the near term.

\- Pricing discounts are narrowing in June: Average price discounts (measured by MSRP - actual transaction price) in the domestic market have steadily narrowed in 2Q26, from $17.5\%$ in early March to $15.7\%$ at the end of June (Figure 27-30). This is encouraging, although the magnitude of the discount remains wide in absolute terms.
