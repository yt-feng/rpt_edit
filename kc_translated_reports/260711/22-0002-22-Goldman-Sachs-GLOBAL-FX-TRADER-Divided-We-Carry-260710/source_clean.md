# GS：美元走强下人民币继续升值，G10利差交易迎来黄金窗口

Our thoughts on USD, CNY, European satellites, CAD & MXN, HUF & PLN, ILS, and G10 Carry

USD: Forecast feedback. Last week we revised our forecasts to show modest Dollar appreciation against the Euro and Yen over the rest of the year, and we are making a few related tweaks this week to other Euro crosses. At the same time, we revised some of our EM FX forecasts to show further strength and have emphasized the importance of carry as a driver for total returns in both EM and the G10; we expect more divided Dollar returns to continue. In the near term, investors see some risk to our forecast for Dollar strength in G10 from our economists' view that the Fed will stay on hold this year. We agree that should likely be a headwind for the Dollar in coming months, but only a modest one. While pricing for the next few meetings reflects uncertainty about the policy tactics, recent volatility in rates across the curve continues to reflect moves in oil prices. But as that settles down, upgraded investment expectations from the AI boom should be positive for the currency. Over the more medium term, investors are concerned that the Dollar's depreciation trend should resume before too long given its lofty valuation. We still see this as a risk, but think it is at least delayed. In our view, FX valuation played an important role in returns last year because the factor that had preserved the Dollar's strength—superior asset return prospects—looked less likely. We note that the valuation gap is much narrower now than at the start of 2025, and more concentrated in Asia (we still expect it to play a key role in policy-supported CNY strength—more in the CNY bullet). But against the G10, the focus on valuation coincides with relative cross-border investment demand, and we think this has shifted substantially since the start of the year. According to our GSBEER model, the Euro outperformed other cyclical indicators like rate differentials by about 5pp in 2025, and we think the data demonstrates this reflected reduced demand for Dollar exposure from foreign investors. We see room for this to reverse somewhat, with a serious questioning of AI investment returns the more significant risk to our expectations of modest Dollar strengthening.

■ CNY: Not so fast. Dollar strength over the past month, particularly against low-yielding currencies in G10 and EM, has led to question marks about whether CNY appreciation can continue against that backdrop. However, while the CNY appreciation pace may have slowed recently, we do not think that this signals the end of the move stronger. The upcoming monthly trade data are likely to reinforce the overall picture of historic export strength and currency undervaluation, and with producer price inflation topping out as well, we continue to see sustained but gradual nominal currency appreciation as an equilibrium outcome. Policy authorities also continue to appear more comfortable with such a path, which may serve to deflect protectionist pushback from other parts of the world. Reflecting this, it is striking that even in this somewhat firmer Dollar backdrop, the USD/CNY fix has made new lows in the last couple of weeks, and on Friday, broke below 6.80 for the first time since early 2023 (Exhibit 1.) We continue to expect a gradual grind stronger in the Renminbi, targeting 6.50 for USD/CNY in 12 months, and expect it to outperform other low-yielders such as EUR or SGD.

European satellites: Euro hopping. We run through our latest European FX views and forecasts following our EUR/USD downgrade last week. Sterling outperformance has accelerated in July, with recent gains supported by premium compression, short-GBP positioning unwinds, and an exceptionally strong pace of cross-border M&A inflows, in our view. Sterling's gains in recent weeks have been at odds with shifting rate differentials though, which we think raises the risk of a reversal weaker in the near term. We also continue to see key challenges to Sterling in the longer-term—most notably overvaluation and an on-hold BoE—though a procyclical global backdrop and the potential for closer UK-EU ties push in the opposite direction. We mark-to-market our EUR/GBP forecasts to look for more modest Sterling underperformance ahead (Exhibit 2), and continue to see more room for Sterling shorts to clear the forwards in GBP/USD than in EUR/GBP. Scandi currencies have seen sharp divergence in recent months on account of the energy shock, with higher-for-longer oil prices providing durable support for NOK in our view, and evidence of a stickier terms of trade imprint on NOK/SEK so far. Longer-term, there remain more tailwinds (procyclical macro backdrop, solid fiscal and valuation fundamentals) than headwinds (higher global yields, European cyclical risks post-energy shock) for Scandi FX performance in our view. We adjust our Scandi FX forecasts to account for the post-oil-reset price action, but continue to look for steady NOK and SEK gains versus EUR over the coming year (Exhibit 2). And finally, the Swiss Franc continues to screen favourably as a funding currency, with a potential shift in SNB intervention policy less of a concern for CHF funding now after recent data has pointed to subdued domestic inflation pressures. And in an environment where many other central banks are hiking or considering hikes, a firmly on hold stance puts the SNB on the dovish end of the central bank spectrum. This dovish divergence, waning inflationary pressures as energy prices decline, and a broadly risk positive backdrop should weigh on the Franc. Taken together, we expect some continued CHF underperformance in the near term, though an eventual resumption in the gold rally remains a key risk to longer-term CHF funding via its terms of trade linkages.

CAD & MXN: USMCA into extra time. US Trade Representative Greer recently announced that the US decided against renewing USMCA and will instead conduct annual reviews in line with our expectation that negotiations would likely extend past the July 1 deadline. We have argued for CAD funding for much of this year, given its ability to reduce risk exposure and improve carry relative to Dollar funding. Those attributes remain intact and ongoing negotiations should continue to weigh on CAD via a wider USD-CAD rate differential as uncertainty remains elevated and the likelihood that US-Canada talks will prove more difficult than with Mexico. That said, the argument for sharper spot depreciation in CAD from here appears less compelling after the substantial underperformance over the past two months and some recent improvement in the domestic data, including this week's employment report which added to recent signals of a gradual recovery in Canadian activity. Meanwhile for Mexico, the government has framed recent developments as more

positive with a focus on the left-tail scenario of a unilateral US withdrawal having been avoided ahead of the July 1 deadline. A US delegation is due to travel to Mexico on July 20, so headlines regarding USMCA discussions may be in focus for MXN at that time. Our broader take is that USMCA negotiations should have a limited impact on MXN because tensions have been less elevated than in Canada's case, but the trade relationship itself is an important long-run anchor for the currency, and at current strong levels there is very little premium for a significant break in that relationship. And while domestic activity has remained weak—and this has driven Banxico to cut rates and narrow the Mex-US rate differential to historically low levels—MXN performance has been less impacted by local factors, in contrast with CAD. Instead, the Peso has traded more in line with its global betas, including its high sensitivity to relative US equity outperformance. Plus, there are tentative signs that Mexican activity data is starting to recover, though our economists expect trade-policy uncertainty to continue to weigh on investment. Bottomline, while in our baseline we don't expect USMCA negotiations to be a primary driver of spot moves in either currency, a significant rupture in the trading relationship should lead to underperformance, and more so in MXN than CAD given starting levels.

The G10 carry comeback. We argued recently that carry bears more relevance for G10 FX markets now than it has at virtually any other point since the year 2000, and we continue to see value in carry strategies funded by G10 low-yielders (JPY, CHF, EUR, CAD) in the months ahead. G10 FX currently boasts the combination of historically elevated levels of outright carry, and historically subdued levels of vol (Exhibit 4). A stabilisation in G10 policy rates at high and varied levels following the post-Covid inflation surge and recent energy shock has helped allow for this dynamic, but in practical terms for FX markets, it has produced exceptionally high

levels of carry-to-vol currently, including near multi-decade highs for the likes of EUR/CHF and USD/CAD (Exhibit 5). Another striking feature of the current G10 carry landscape is the breakdown in the relationship between carry and sensitivity to risk. This allows for high carry-to-vol offerings in crosses that have traded as risk-neutral in recent months, as well as relatively high carry-to-vol offerings in risk-off expressions (such as long Dollar versus SEK or NZD). We view the opportunity to earn carry in G10 FX while remaining relatively insulated from (or even hedged against) drawdowns in risk as a useful option in multi-asset portfolios in the current backdrop.
