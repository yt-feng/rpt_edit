# European OEMs in China Jun-26: European premium loss widens amid German product lull

In June, a wave of domestic product launches extended domestic share gains, while European premium slipped further amid a German OEM product transition lull. In this series, we track the monthly market share change of c.90 of the c.100 active brands in China, accounting for over 90% of the market retail volume.

\- Premium segment - European premium is defending share better than mass peers, but the loss is widening. Mercedes Cars share fell -49/-27bps R1M/R3M yoy, BMW brand -29/-21bps, while Audi (-39/-3bps) and Volvo (-14/-9bps) held up more resiliently. Audi was lifted by the new AUDI E7X, which launched in May and became the brand's 3rd best-selling nameplate by June, and Volvo's locally developed XC70 PHEV remains its top-selling model. In contrast, Mercedes and BMW are caught in a product transition lull, leaving them heavily reliant on legacy ICE offerings, in our view. Combined, the four European premium brands' share changed by -131/-60bps.

☐ Domestic premium expanding into European price bands - The China Tech & Premium cohort (definition Exhibit 4) gained +354/+337bps, driven by key product launches such as the Xpeng GX EREV and Zeekr 007 GT EV in June. All brands gained share last month (except Aito), led by Xiaomi (+95/+90bps, covered by Timothy Zhao) and Zeekr (Geely Group, +90/+92bps).

Mass segment - VW Group mass brands remain the most under pressure. VW mass brands ceded -203/-209bps, the steepest decline of any foreign mass player, led by the VW brand (-195/-205bps). Conversely, Leapmotor's gain stands out (+236/+226bps, covered by Tina Hou), supported by the launch of its D-series model D19 and D99.

The mass brands of domestic OEM's ceded share - BYD and Geely Group's mass lines lost ground, led by the BYD brand (-421/-337bps, covered by Tina Hou) which showed the sharpest decline in the share of any OEM, partly offset by growth in their premium lines (Exhibit 2, Exhibit 3).
