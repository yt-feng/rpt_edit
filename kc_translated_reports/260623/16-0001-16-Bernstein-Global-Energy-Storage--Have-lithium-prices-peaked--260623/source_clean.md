# Global Energy Storage: Have lithium prices peaked?

Lithium carbonate prices have tripled from 2025 lows, reaching \~\$30k/t in May before easing to \$20–25k/t on supply restarts, which we view as a short-term buffer. We continue to view the market as being in a mid-cycle recovery rather than approaching a peak. Inventories are tight at \~20 days, capex has yet to recover, and supply growth is starting to moderate. On the demand side, robust battery demand led by ESS continues to absorb higher prices. Taken together, we see further upside risks, with prices likely peaking closer to 2027.

Lithium demand has grown \~32% year to date, outpacing supply growth of \~24% and driving a meaningful market tightening. The key driver has been ESS, with demand up \~100% YTD, offsetting more moderate EV growth of \~9%. On the lithium supply side, growth has been led by China (+30%), Chile (+25%), and Argentina (+106%), while Australia (+6%) and Zimbabwe (+16%) have lagged, contributing to a tighter-than-expected balance.

We estimate \~200kt LCE of curtailed supply is gradually returning to the market although this only provides a short term buffer. While this may help partially close the supply gap in 2026, it is unlikely to keep pace with demand growth as capacity additions slow beyond 2026, leaving the market increasingly tight into 2027.

Inventories have tightened significantly, with lithium inventory days falling to \~20 days year to date. Historically, lithium prices tend to rise more sharply once inventory falls below \~20 days, with spikes often occurring closer to \~15 days or lower. Current levels suggest the market has moved from balanced toward tightening, with inventory now firmly within a range that has historically supported stronger pricing momentum.

Current higher lithium prices have not led to any meaningful demand slowdown. We expect ESS markets can absorb lithium carbonate prices in the \~\$30k/t range based on underlying project economics. Importantly, sustained higher prices are required to incentivize new supply, particularly given long development timelines and the lag in capex following the recent downturn across the sector.

We have revised up our lithium price assumptions to reflect tighter lithium market. We now estimate lithium carbonate prices of \~\$25k/t in 2026 (from \$21k/t previously) and \~\$32.5k/t in 2027 (from \$25k/t), while maintaining our long-term price assumption of \$16k/t. This reflects both tighter near-term market conditions and required to sustain long-term supply growth.

## SUMMARY

We expect the lithium market to remain a highly cyclical industry with supply responding elastically to price changes. Since our last lithium supply and demand update from last December (Global Energy Storage: 2026 Lithium Outlook. How high can prices go?), lithium carbonate prices have more than doubled and market sentiment has turned far more constructive. We still view the industry as mid-cycle rather than late-cycle. The key change has been stronger-than-expected battery demand, led by energy storage, which has tightened the market much faster than we previously expected. While lithium equities have already rebounded sharply, historical cycles suggest the move in the underlying commodity can still have room to run once inventories tighten and supply response lags. Lithium stocks often recover 6 to 12 months ahead of spot prices. In our view, the current rebound is consistent with the middle part of the cycle rather than the end, with the setup still supportive through 2027.

Chile possesses the world's largest lithium reserves, primarily found in brines beneath salt flats like the Atacama Salt Flat. These brines have high concentrations of lithium, making them economically attractive for extraction. Lithium extraction in Chile primarily involves pumping lithium-rich brines to the surface and allowing them to evaporate, concentrating the lithium. The Chilean government has a target to double lithium output over the next decade with six new brine developments announced. The main companies operating in Chile's lithium industry are Sociedad Química y Minera de Chile (SQM) and Albemarle. Lithium extraction from brines can have environmental consequences, including water depletion in arid regions and impacts on fragile ecosystems. Chile's regulatory framework for lithium extraction has evolved over time, with the state playing a significant role in overseeing and managing lithium resources. There are ongoing discussions and considerations regarding the rights and involvement of indigenous communities in lithium mining projects. China holds a dominant position in the global lithium market. ## LITHIUM SUPPLY

The global lithium resource supply system is largely divided into two extraction methods: lithium extraction from ore and lithium extraction from salt lakes. Lithium extraction from hard rocks has increased faster than brine due to its mature and easy to scale industrial processes. Hard rock lithium deposits are found in several locations with most located in Australia, Canada, US, Brazil and Africa. Although lithium extraction from brine is lower costs, limited availability of high-quality brines and requirement of constant dry weather conditions are disadvantages. Lithium brine deposits are primarily found in the Lithium Triangle (Boliva, Chile and Argentina), US and China (Tibet and Qinghai).

## SUPPLY AND DEMAND

While 2025 was a challenging year for lithium producers, the setup for 2026 and 2027 looks constructive as market tighten. We estimate lithium demand to increase by 33% y-o-y rising from 1.7MTPA in 2025 to 2.3MTPA in 2026. Based on current planned capacity, average production capacity is expected to increase from 1.9MTPA in 2025 to 2.2MTPA in 2026 which is an increase of 16%. This implies that demand as a percent of average production capacity to increase from 88% to 102% implying further market tightening.

In the long run, we expect lithium demand to more than double from 1.7MTPA in 2025 to 4.4MTPA by 2030. To reach expected demand by 2030, lithium production capacity will need to rise to 5.2MTPA (assuming 85% utilization level). Based on cumulative capacity announcements, total capacity could increase from to 3.7MT in 2030. This implies a further 1.5MTPA of capacity additions from current planned levels through to 2030.

## LITHIUM PRICING

Lithium carbonate prices have already rebounded sharply from their mid-2025 trough and are now around \$20-25k/t. The recovery has been driven by tighter market conditions, falling inventory and stronger battery demand, especially from ESS. In contrast to our prior note, prices are no longer simply recovering from the bottom, but are already at levels that justify partial restarts while still leaving room for further upside if the market tightens again in 2027.

China holds a significant lead in lithium refining capacity with more than 70% of global refining capacity. Lithium refining takes raw lithium minerals such as lithium ore or brine and processes them into high-purity forms such as lithium carbonate or lithium hydroxide suitable for battery manufacturing. Spodumene is traditionally shipped to China for processing, which has world-class refining technology to produce lithium salts. Lithium refining margins are driven by fluctuations in supply and demand balance. While refining margins vary by company depending on refining process and costs, we estimate the average margin is near zero driven by oversupply and high inventory levels.

On the flip side, how high can prices go is determined by the price at which buyers are less willing to buy lithium. In other words, at what price will we see demand destruction? At \$100/kWh battery pack price or lower, OEMs can produce and sell EVs at \$30k price level and compete with ICE vehicles in the mass market. The conclusion is that lithium prices can stay above long term price as EVs remain affordable. Reaching historical high of \$80k/t is almost certainly impossible.
