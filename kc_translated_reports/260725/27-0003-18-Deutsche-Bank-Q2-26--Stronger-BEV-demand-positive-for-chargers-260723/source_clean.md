# Q2-26: Stronger BEV demand positive for chargers
## Further elevated charging demand expected

The European EV market is showing substantial shifts in both adoption rates and charging demand patterns. Recent analysis indicates a strengthening of charging demand that does not seem to follow typical seasonality. This initial surge suggests a higher utilization of the existing BEV fleet, potentially influenced by external factors like elevated oil prices. At the same time, new BEV registrations in key European markets are now showing significant upward momentum, outpacing the general automotive sector. These trends collectively point towards sustained growth in overall charging demand, creating a favorable environment for charging point operators and the broader EV infrastructure supply chain. We anticipate further clarity and detailed insights from upcoming financial disclosures by Fastned and Alfen in August.

## Developments in EU charging infrastructure: Build rate down y/y

By the end of Q2, the EU had 1,170k public charging points, up 18% y/y and 4% sequentially. The quarterly build rate came in slightly lower than the five year average at 45k units, down 20% y/y and 20k lower than in Q1. Assuming the average build rate of the past years leads us to a charging network of 1,267k by the end of 2026. According to the AFIR regulation and the focus on power output, the EAFO website indicates that almost all member states are fulfilling the specific targets (see here). Meanwhile, the DC share increased sequentially again by 30bps to 18.9% (vs. 16.8% in Q2-25).

## Developments in the top-five countries: Growth across the board with Germany falling behind EU growth rate

According to EAFO data, the charging network in the top-five European countries (Germany, France, UK, Italy, Spain) grew further in Q2. Italy and Spain outperformed the EU market with 21% and 20% y/y, respectively. France and the UK developed in line with the wider market, while Germany fell behind with a growth rate of 14% y/y. We do note that Germany was outperforming the market in Q1, hence indicating the well-known volatility in the charging construction industry. In line with the ongoing trend, the DC shares of the EU5 countries remain above EU average, with Spain leading the list (27%).

## Q2 hot topic - Stronger BEV sales leading to even more charging demand?

Fastned's Q1 reporting indicated stronger charging demand, contrary to typical seasonality (see our note here). As BEV demand wasn't significantly outperforming the market back then, we attributed this to increased utilization of the existing BEV fleet amidst higher oil prices (see our note here). This was primarily due to the typical three-month order-to-registration lag. However, June figures show initial upticks in BEV registrations in EU5 countries, outperforming the overall market by 50ppts in June, potentially linked to elevated oil prices (see our note here). Consequently, we anticipate further increases in charging demand, benefiting charging point operators like Fastned and construction supply chain companies such as Alfen. We expect more insights during Q2 reporting, with Fastned publishing its figures on August 13th and Alfen on August 18th (see our previews here and here).
