## The future of AIDD platforms/tools lies in closed loop, underlying LLM, and agent use with strong pharmaceutical knowledge

The integration of AI across the pharmaceutical value chain has produced a distinct ecosystem of AIDD platforms and tools but we don't see a single winning AIDD platform yet. These tools fundamentally shift research from a trial-and-error physical paradigm to a predictive, data-driven methodology, addressing distinct bottlenecks ranging from initial biological target discovery to molecular design and clinical trial forecasting. Based on our observations, we think AIDD tooling has converged around three “stacks” that differ mainly in (i) what is treated as ground truth (physics/chemistry/biology vs. learned representations) and (ii) where AI is used (speed-up, surrogate modeling, or end-to-end generation/reasoning). 1) Physics/pharmacology/biology/physiology models with AI used to accelerate computation. These platforms keep classical mechanistic formalisms (molecular dynamics [MD], quantum mechanics, PK/PD models, systems biology) as the core, and add machine learning (ML) for speed, scoring, or parameterization. Typical use: virtual screening triage, faster free-energy approximations, improved scoring functions, or adaptive sampling in molecular dynamics. Examples include: Schrödinger-style docking/FEP workflows; OpenMM-based MD ecosystems with ML potentials/surrogates, and Simcyp physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) modeling tool; 2) Modern AI architectures (transformers/ diffusion/graph nets) grounded in structural or biological data. Deep learning is the primary engine, trained on biological data often with physics-inspired constraints (equivariance, energy-based objectives) or structure supervision. Well-known examples include AlphaFold 4, RoseTTAFold, graph neural network property predictors and multitask models for ADMET (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicit); diffusion/transformer molecular generators used for scaffold hopping and constrained optimization; and 3) Large language models (LLMs) post-trained on pharmaceutical data for data analysis, reasoning, planning, and knowledge workflows. These systems are used in medicinal chemistry, pharmacology, CMC, and literature/ patent corpora, sometimes connected to tools (search, ELNs, assay databases) for grounded answers. Typical use: hypothesis generation, SAR summarization, protocol drafting, target/indication landscape reviews, and decision support. Examples include domain-adapted LLM assistants built on top of general LLMs; chemistry-aware LLM toolchains that call retrosynthesis/property predictors; literature/patent copilots used by R&D teams. In addition, various agent systems are built on top of LLMs to further facilitate the use of all three classes of AIDD tools. Overall, the development of AIDD is moving toward hybrid stacks: domain knowledge such as physics for correctness, deep models for priors and speed, and LLMs for orchestration and human-facing reasoning.

\- Deep learning structural prediction models provide the foundational three-dimensional blueprints required for drugs to target previously "undruggable" proteins. Tools like AlphaFold utilize advanced diffusion-based architectures to translate one-dimensional genetic sequences into highly accurate 3D models of isolated proteins, and its newer iterations can predict interactions within complex biological systems involving proteins, nucleic acids, and small molecule ligands. This allows scientists to visually hypothesize binding pockets and interactions computationally before initiating costly physical experiments.

\- Biocomputational platforms function as advanced biological search engines to rapidly identify and validate disease-driving targets. Applications such as

Insilico Medicine's PandaOmics deploy natural language processing and machine learning to analyze massive datasets spanning clinical trials, scientific text, and multi-omics (genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic) data. By recognizing complex biological patterns, these tools can pinpoint highly specific, novel therapeutic targets and biomarkers that traditional human-led research might entirely overlook.

\- Generative chemistry and physics-based simulation engines automate the creation and refinement of entirely novel molecules. Platforms like Chemistry42, AtomNet, and ID4Inno do not merely search existing chemical libraries; they act as automated molecular architects that invent new chemical structures optimized for specific targets. They seamlessly integrate quantum physics-based calculations to predict binding affinities (such as Schrödinger's FEP+ or XtalPi's XFEP) and utilize predictive algorithms to proactively forecast critical drug-like properties, such as toxicity and metabolic stability (ADMET), ensuring only the most viable candidates advance to physical synthesis.

\- Predictive clinical software optimizes trial architecture to significantly reduce the risk of late-stage development failures. Tools like inClinico analyze historical trial data, electronic health records, and biological markers to identify patient subgroups most likely to respond to a given therapy. By dynamically simulating patient responses and suggesting adaptive trial protocols, these AI applications help pharmaceutical companies design highly targeted, efficient clinical trials that improve the probability of regulatory success.

\- Multi-agent AI algorithms and advanced robotics reduce manual trial-and-error, driving unprecedented R&D throughput. Platforms like XtalPi deploy comprehensive multi-agent architectures—such as Agentic Genius, MolAgent, and X-Buddy—that function as intelligent project managers to autonomously orchestrate over 10,000 compound synthesis experiments each week. In the physical laboratory, proprietary hardware overcomes micro-scale material bottlenecks, allowing robotic workstations to autonomously execute up to 1,500 parallel experiments daily. Insilico deploys agentic AI as a "digital research workforce" of 660 specialized agents (as of mid-2024) that autonomously automate multi-step scientific workflows and heavy documentation, cutting the manual burden of literature review and data interpretation. Its flagship DORA (Draft Outline Research Assistant), housed in the Science42 platform, is a multi-agent system that generates hypotheses, interprets data, and drafts academic papers and regulatory documents by synthesizing vast scientific literature into contextual summaries.

# The 3 Key Pillars of China's AIDD

Pillar 1: AIDD Could Constantly Improve R&D Efficiency via "Dry-Wet Lab" Closed Loop

"What can AI solve for drugs?" The core value proposition of AIDD is a step-change in R&D unit economics. Most importantly, the "dry-wet lab" closed loops (AI + automation + wet-lab execution) utilized by major AIDD players in China are substantially compressing discovery timelines and lowering capital burn in drug discovery processes. The "dry lab" utilizes generative AI to virtually design molecules and predict their biological properties. These digital predictions are then sent directly to the "web lab" – a highly automated physical laboratory equipped with intelligent robotics - which rapidly synthesizes the compounds and tests them in the real world. The resulting experimental data is fed back into the dry lab to validate the initial prediction, effectively retraining the AI models to become smarter for the next round of drug design.

# We Answer Yes to The Top 3 Critical AIDD Questions

## Q1: "Zero Approved Drugs — Is the Clinical Promise of AIDD Real?"

\- The crux of this question is that, despite years of marketing and hundreds of preclinical candidates, no AI-discovered drug has yet received commercial approval in the world, raising the question of whether AIDD companies are selling infrastructure rather than therapeutic outcomes. As of mid-2026, the global AIDD sector has generated 173 clinical-stage programs, of which 94 are in Phase 1, 56 in Phase 2, and only a handful in Phase 3. However, none have crossed the regulatory finish line. The AI-acceleration thesis has been demonstrated convincingly at early stages (target-to-PCC in 12–18 months versus the industry average of 4.5 years), but the critical question, whether AI-optimised molecules outperform traditionally designed ones in late-stage clinical settings, remains unanswered currently. However, based on the following arguments, we are confident that AIDD will generate drugs which will achieve Ph3 success and gain marketing approval.

\- The counter-argument is strongest at Insilico, where rentosertib (ISM001-055) for IPF has moved from AI-generated drug target and drug molecule to a Ph3 trial with its discovery to clinic path published in Nature Biotechnology and Phase 2a data published in Nature Medicine, representing a genuine scientific first. At 60 mg QD, the drug achieved +98.4 mL FVC (forced vital capacity) at week 12 versus -20.3 mL in the placebo arm, earning China CDE Breakthrough Therapy Designation in May 2025. A Phase 3 trial (GENESIS-IPF-3, n=192, 52-week primary endpoint of FVC change) has been initiated in China in 2026, with topline data expected by 2029, while a US Phase IIb is simultaneously underway.

\- Other examples of promising drugs discovered or optimized through AIDD include Recursion's REC-1245, which is a highly potent, first-in-class RBM39 (RNA-binding motif protein 3) degrader in Ph1, where RBM39 is a vital pre-mRNA splicing factor that regulates gene expression and cell survival. Generate Biomedicines' GB-0895 is the first long-acting TSLP mAb in Ph3 development. According to the company, it has the best profile among all known TSLP targeting programs. Schrödinger and Nimbus discovered the TYK2 inhibitor TAK-279, which was acquired by Takeda in 2023 and is currently being evaluated in Ph3 for psoriasis.

\- As AI helps solve or provide new solutions to numerous long-standing scientific problems in physics, chemistry, and mathematics, which have puzzled humans for years, we believe AI will be able to eventually discover drugs that can achieve global Ph3 trial success. We think LLMs are quickly improving their frontier R&D ability. For example, for the OpenAI FrontierScience-Research Benchmark, the best model at that time, GPT-5.2, achieved only 25% accuracy in 2025. However, with the release of GPT-5.6, we see its accuracy in various science benchmarks is consistently \~20% (absolute number) higher than GPT-5.4. Hence, we think GPT-5.6 might achieve 50% in the FrontierScience-Research Benchmark. In specific subjects, we see AI models help solve or improve answers to long-standing problems. In physics, AI models have advanced our knowledge in the 150- year fluid dynamics dilemma in Navier-Stokes equations, loop geometries in gravitational radiation in string theory, and the electron spin problem in magnets in quantum physics. In chemistry, AI helps generate 2.2 million potential new crystals, 380,000 of which could be stable; AI partially solves the Schrödinger Equation to understand electron interaction, and designs chemical synthetic pathways for retrosynthesis. In math, AI discovered a new family of solutions for the Erdos Unit Distance problem and the Cap Set problem. And AI broke long-standing records for five complex Ramsey numbers. If AI models already have strong capabilities in solving difficult scientific problems, agents and self-improving features can only make AI models more powerful in solving complex AIDD problems.

\- Our view is constructive: AI has been proven mainly in language and code, but the same first-principles rigour is now extending into the life sciences. Biology is harder — human systems are only partially understood, cell-line and animal results don't always translate to patients, and DMPK and toxicology remain real bottlenecks; even AI-native programs still face the better part of a decade to approval, and no fully AI-designed drug has yet been approved. Still, the trajectory is encouraging: in AI-driven materials science, physics-based modelling and autonomous "self-driving" labs aim to compress discovery from 10–20 years to just 1–2 — screening battery-electrolyte chemistries down to a tested shortlist via active learning and accelerating perovskite solar-cell design through robotic synthesis. Since biology obeys the same physical laws that XtalPi's quantum-physics engine exploits, our thesis is that as first-principles data compounds through wet-lab flywheels, the translational gap should narrow — though AI-discovered molecules still revert toward the historical \~40% success rate in Phase II, so early proof points must survive the clinic to become approvals.

## Q2: "Does China Origin Imply Structural Discount — Can AIDD Drugs and Platforms Go Global?"

\- China-domiciled AIDD companies might be facing a so-called "China discount" driven by (i) US legislation and administrative orders aimed at curbing China's biotech sector, which threaten future data-sharing and licensing/collaboration deals, (ii) FDA/NMPA regulatory divergence that complicates multi-regional trial strategies, and (iii) the NRDL (national reimbursement drug list) price cuts could limit the upside of commercialisation economics in China. In June 2026, Representatives John Moolenaar and Debbie Dingell introduced the bill of Biotech Investment National Security Act (BINSA). This legislation would amend the COINS framework to subject U.S. pharmaceutical licensing agreements, joint ventures, and equity investments in Chinese biotech entities to strict Treasury and Defense Department reviews. In addition, the COINS Act already gives the Treasury Department the discretionary authority to expand its “covered sectors” to include biotechnology through the rulemaking process without needing additional legislation. The House Appropriations Committee advanced an Agriculture/FDA spending bill report (spearheaded by Rep. Andy Harris) seeking to bar the FDA from accepting or reviewing clinical trial data generated in China in support of IND (investigational new drug) applications. However, we don’t expect a material impact from those actions in the near-term.

\- The regulatory convergence argument that US-China AIDD collaboration is ultimately in both parties' interest is gaining traction among scientists even as politicians diverge. Published analysis notes that indiscriminate restrictions on collaboration risk undermining partnerships that are accelerating therapeutic

progress, and that intelligence concerns about IP transfer apply to specific firms rather than the sector wholesale. The FDA's Good AI Practice guidelines and NMPA's parallel 2026 AI roadmap suggest a pragmatic regulatory evolution toward accepting AI-generated evidence from credible, well-documented sponsors, regardless of country of origin.

\- A pointed investor question worth addressing directly is whether the systematic exclusion of Chinese companies and users from leading US frontier AI models, most visibly Anthropic's September 2025 ban on Claude access for all China-controlled entities, structurally disadvantages Chinese AIDD platforms relative to US peers. We don't think so given China-originated AI LLMs continue to show on-par capabilities with their US peers. Anthropic announced on 5 September 2025 that it would prohibit any company more than 50% owned by a Chinese entity from accessing Claude's API, citing national security and misuse risks; this followed the pre-existing blocks on ChatGPT, Gemini, and Meta AI in mainland China. The GPU chip export controls reinforced the access asymmetry: US Commerce BIS regulations progressively restrict advanced semiconductor shipments to China, with the May 2025 BIS guidance explicitly extending controls to Chinese-headquartered firms' overseas subsidiaries. The result is a structural compute and model-access gap: as of mid-2026, the best Chinese frontier models (DeepSeek V4) lag US frontier models by approximately 6–7 months on general benchmarks, and DeepSeek's own technical paper concedes it "trails state-of-the-art frontier models by approximately 3 to 6 months". However, on July 16, 2026, Chinese company Moonshot released Kimi K3, a highly disruptive LLM. Packing an estimated 2.8 trillion parameters and a 1M token context window, it achieves benchmark parity with frontier US models like OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol and Anthropic's Claude Fable 5, while undercutting them significantly in price.

\- Hence, the China discount argument towards AIDD is more nuanced than the general AI narrative suggests. First, AIDD platforms like Pharma.AI and XtalPi's quantum physics engine are domain-specific systems built on proprietary biological and chemical datasets, physics-based algorithms, and wet-lab feedback loops — their core value is not derived from access to GPT or Claude, but from in-house first-principles scientific modelling and experimental validation. Second, Chinese models have shown surprising competitiveness in the specific scientific subdomains most relevant to drug discovery: DeepSeek's coding and mathematical reasoning benchmark scores are broadly comparable to US peers, and Insilico's own MMAI Gym training data showed a Qwen3 model outperforming all frontier foundation models on clinical target retrieval benchmarks after a single training session. Third, the hardware constraint has historically driven Chinese AI efficiency innovation rather than absolute capability collapse — DeepSeek R1's efficiency at a fraction of US compute costs is the most prominent example. Kimi K3 token cost is also much lower than US frontier models.

\- Our conclusion is that the compute access gap is a legitimate risk that might warrant further consideration of valuation for general-purpose Chinese AI companies, but for domain-specific AIDD platforms whose competitive moat lies in proprietary scientific data, specialized AI model training, and physical wet-lab infrastructure, the discount is materially smaller than the headline geopolitical narrative implies. The more important performance arbiters for AIDD are clinical trial outcomes and BD deal quality — domains where both Insilico and XtalPi are demonstrably competitive on a global stage.

## Q3: "Revenue Model can be Lumpy and Unsustainable — Is AIDD a Real Business?"

\- One critique of China AIDD companies is that their revenue streams are often driven by one-time, large-ticket BD events that are inherently unpredictable and create year-on-year volatility, making financial forecasting unreliable for institutional investors. Insilico's FY2025 results illustrate this case: total revenue fell 34.5% YoY from US\$85.8M to US\$56.2M, almost entirely because pipeline development revenue dropped from US\$76.6M to US\$23.9M — a 68.8% collapse — as the Lilly US\$115M upfront slipped into 2026. Management itself disclosed the business "operates under a project-based business model with no guarantee or clear visibility on future revenue generation". XtalPi's trajectory is the positive mirror image: FY2025 revenue surged 201% to RMB802.6M, but Customer A (undisclosed) accounted for \~RMB365M — approximately 45% of total revenue — creating some concentration risk.

\- AIDD companies are attempting to address this structural fragility through recurring-revenue layers, but the transition is still early-stage. Insilico is building its Software Solutions subscription business (Pharma.AI platform: Biology42, Chemistry42, Medicine42, Science42), which grew 23.8% to US\$4.9M in 2025 as the subscriber base expanded from 153 to 181 customers. More ambitiously, the Science MMAI Gym — a membership-based AI training environment for frontier foundation models — aims to generate recurring licence fees; its first partnership with Liquid AI was inked shortly after launch. XtalPi's AI4S Intelligent Solutions segment (RMB264.7M, up 62.6% in FY2025) represents platform deployments for pharmaceutical and materials-science clients, including 17 of the world's top 20 pharmaceutical companies.

\- The key investor question is whether BD deal value can be recycled into a more annuity-like model to support long-term cash runway. XtalPi ended FY2025 with RMB7.1B in total cash and raised a further RMB2.5B in convertible bonds in early 2026. Insilico ended FY2025 with US\$393M in cash post-IPO, with the Lilly US\$115M upfront in Q1 2026 meaningfully extending cash visibility. The bear case remains that until recurring software/platform revenues constitute >30–40% of total revenues (currently \~10–15% for both companies), AIDD financials will remain BD-event-driven, which carries some uncertainty in timing.

\- Crucially, however, the lumpiness of recognised revenue should not be confused with a slowdown in underlying deal formation — BD momentum at both companies is, by any measure, accelerating. For Insilico, the post-IPO deal streak has been exceptional: the Lilly global R&D collaboration (US\$2.75B total deal value, US\$115M upfront, March 2026), a Servier oncology deal (up to US\$888M, 2025), a Tenacia CNS expansion (up to US\$94.75M, March 2026), and most recently a record-breaking SK Biopharmaceuticals CNS partnership (total deal value exceeding US\$2.5B, June 2026) — making it the company's largest-ever APAC partnership — mean Insilico has contracted well over US\$7.5B in aggregate potential deal value since 2021. For XtalPi, the Eli Lilly relationship has deepened into a second landmark: subsidiary Ailux sealed a US\$345M AI-powered bispecific antibody partnership in November 2025, making XtalPi one of the very few AIDD companies globally to have major collaborations with a top-5 pharma in both small molecule and large molecule discovery. The cumulative deal pipeline and the quality of counterparties (Lilly, Servier, SK Biopharm, Stemline, Qilu) demonstrate that the largest global pharmaceutical companies continue to allocate meaningful capital to

China AIDD platforms — validating the underlying technology. We think with continued new BD deals and milestone payments emerging, AIDD companies' revenue could become more even.

## Investment Risks

\- Clinical Failure Risk. Rentosertib is the most advanced pure-play AI-discovered drug globally, making its upcoming Phase III readout a sector-defining event. Despite strong Phase IIa efficacy and regulatory recognition, IPF remains a difficult indication with historically low approval success rates. A Phase III failure would likely trigger a broad de-rating across the AIDD sector, given no AI-discovered drug has yet achieved global commercial approval.

\- Lumpy, Licensing-Driven Revenue Model. Insilico and XtalPi remain heavily dependent on milestone-based licensing and business development (BD) deals, resulting in highly volatile and difficult-to-forecast revenues. Insilico's FY2025 revenue decline reflected timing shifts in major licensing payments, while XtalPi remains reliant on a small number of large customers. With recurring platform revenues still limited, financial performance remains largely driven by the timing and size of partnership transactions.

\- COINS Act & US Cross-Border Licensing Risk. The proposed BINSA and passed COINS Act highlight growing US scrutiny of collaborations involving Chinese biotech and AI companies, creating regulatory and political uncertainty around future licensing, co-development, and commercialization agreements with US partners. Even without direct restrictions, heightened compliance requirements and geopolitical concerns could slow deal-making, reduce partner appetite, and increase execution risk for China-based AIDD firms seeking access to US markets and capital.

\- FDA Regulatory Uncertainty for AI-Generated Evidence. Emerging FDA guidance on AI in drug development could require greater disclosure of model architecture, training data, and validation methods for high-impact AI systems. For companies whose competitive advantage rests on proprietary AI platforms, balancing regulatory transparency with IP protection remains an unresolved challenge, potentially complicating future regulatory submissions.

\- AIDD Model generalization risk. A May 2026 Nature Structural & Molecular Biology study (Škrinjar et al.) exposed a key weakness in the AI tools that predict how a drug molecule fits into a protein—the core task behind AI drug discovery. The researchers tested leading models (AlphaFold3, Chai-1, Boltz-1, Protenix) on 2,600 protein–ligand structures the models had never seen during training. On familiar cases, accuracy was about 70%; on genuinely new ones, it collapsed to single digits (AlphaFold3 \~9%, Boltz-1 \~5%). In plain terms, the AI was largely copying answers it had memorized rather than truly figuring out where new molecules bind—and it couldn’t tell when it was wrong, since its confidence scores had almost no link to actual accuracy. Adding more training data (Boltz-2 vs. Boltz-1) did not help. Because drug discovery is fundamentally about brand-new, uncharted targets, this challenges the “structure prediction is solved” narrative and favors platforms that pair AI with physics-based validation to double-check predictions.

\- Unproven Clinical Advantage of AI Discovery. The core AIDD thesis assumes AI can improve drug development productivity and success rates, yet there is still no

clinical evidence that AI-discovered molecules outperform conventionally discovered drugs in late-stage development. Until multiple AI-originated programs achieve Phase III success and regulatory approval, the industry's ability to translate preclinical efficiency gains into superior clinical outcomes remains unproven.

## Rentosertib (TNIK inhibitor): Most-Advanced Pipeline Asset with AI-enabled Novelty in Targeting IPF

\- Rentosertib (ISM001-055), Insilico Medicine's lead asset for Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF), stands out in the biotech landscape not merely for its rapid development, but for the fundamental way its biological target was selected. By leveraging generative AI to uncover a completely novel therapeutic target, Rentosertib serves as the definitive proof-of-concept that artificial intelligence can successfully guide target selection, unlocking previously unexplored biological pathways. In our model, we forecast the peak global sales for the drug to be US\$3bn.

\- AI-driven target discovery identified a causal disease mechanism that traditional human research has historically overlooked. Utilizing its PandaOmics platform, Insilico analyzed massive multi-omics datasets, scientific literature, and clinical trial text data to pinpoint TNIK as a highly confident therapeutic target for IPF. While TNIK was known to be associated with key fibrosis-driving pathways (such as WNT, TGF- $\beta$ , and NF-kB), it had never been studied as a therapeutic target for IPF until the AI algorithm explicitly selected it based on causal inference and biological network scoring.

\- The seamless integration of AI-driven target discovery and molecular design validates the industry's first end-to-end generative pipeline. After the AI identified the novel TNIK target, Insilico utilized its Chemistry42 generative platform to design the specific small molecule to inhibit it, creating a highly selective compound with desirable drug-like properties. This dual-engine approach makes Rentosertib the first drug in industry history where both the novel biological target and the chemical structure were entirely discovered and designed by AI, leading to its historic United States Adopted Name (USAN) designation.

\- Phase IIa clinical efficacy is de-risking and appears much better than the Ph3 efficacy of recently FDA approved nerandomilast. The true differentiator of rentosertib is that the AI's novel target hypothesis successfully translated into human clinical efficacy in a fraction of traditional development timelines, reaching Phase I trials in just 30 months. In a double-blind Phase IIa trial published in Nature Medicine, IPF patients receiving rentosertib 60mg achieved a mean improvement in lung function (+98.4 mL forced vital capacity [FVC])

compared to a decline in the placebo group (-20.3 mL), proving that the AI's biological target selection was clinically relevant and commercially viable. However, we note patients concurrently taking 60 mg rentosertib QD with either nintedanib or pirfenidone did not exhibit significant changes in FVC, while patients receiving 60 mg rentosertib QD not concurrently taking SOC antifibrotic therapy exhibited significant improvement in FVC (+187.8 ml).

Patients who received 30 mg rentosertib QD showed a mean reduction in FVC of -27.0 ml, patients who received 30 mg rentosertib BID experienced a mean change of +19.7 ml. As a comparison, in the Ph3 trial of nerandomilast, the adjusted mean changes in FVC at week 52 were -114.7 ml in the nerandomilast 18-mg group, -138.6 ml in the nerandomilast 9-mg group, and -183.5 ml in the placebo group. Although the Ph2 efficacy of AK3280 appears better than that of rentosertib, we think the existing mechanism of AK3280, an optimized version of pirfenidon, could cap the upside potential of AK3280's Ph3 data. In Ph2, the 400 mg group of AK3280 achieved particularly impressive results, with an FVC increase of 209.4 ml and a 6.4% adjusted percent predicted FVC improvement from baseline.

\- Rentosertib is a selective oral TNIK inhibitor for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) and the world's most clinically advanced AI-discovered, AI-designed drug candidate. The target was identified using PandaOmics, while the molecule was designed using Chemistry42. Following a positive Phase IIa readout, the program has advanced into the pivotal Phase III GENESIS-IPF-3 trial in China, initiated in July 2026, with topline data expected around 2029 per company data.

\- The Phase III study narrows development to the winning 60 mg once-daily (QD) dose and extends treatment duration to 52 weeks, transitioning from proof-of-concept efficacy to registrational confirmation. The earlier Phase IIa (GENESIS-IPF) was a 12-week, 71-patient, 22-site dose-ranging study in China, where the 60 mg QD arm demonstrated +98.4 mL FVC improvement versus -20.3 mL on placebo. We think the Phase III design signals confidence, as patients remain on standard-of-care antifibrotics. As a result, Rentosertib must demonstrate efficacy on top of existing treatment with nintedanib or pirfenidone.

\- IPF remains a high-need and underserved market, with only three drugs approved globally as of July 2026: Pirfenidone (Esbriet, Roche), Nintedanib (OFEV, Boehringer Ingelheim), Nerandomilast (Jascayd, Boehringer Ingelheim PDE4B inhibitor; FDA approval in October 2025). Nintedanib generics are expected to enter China in 2026. Existing therapies primarily slow disease progression and are associated with tolerability limitations, while several high-profile candidates, including pamrevlumab, bexotegrast, and GLPG1690, have failed in development. Rentosertib's novel oral, first-in-class TNIK mechanism is differentiated from the crowded TGF- $\beta$ /kinase landscape. By simultaneously modulating the Wnt, TGF- $\beta$ , and NF- $\kappa$ B pathways, the drug benefits from both Breakthrough Therapy Designation and FDA Orphan Drug Designation, creating a meaningful competitive advantage.

## ISM5411 (Garutadustat, PHD1/2 inhibitor): First-in-Class Gut-Restricted PHD Inhibitor for IBD

\- ISM5411 (garutadustat) is Insilico's wholly-owned, potentially first-in-class oral gut-restricted PHD1/2 inhibitor for inflammatory bowel disease, which one of the platform's clearest demonstrations of AI-driven speed and a key near-term catalyst as its Phase IIa trial progresses. In our model, we forecast its peak sales to reach \~US\$2bn.

\- ISM5411 is an AI-discovered IBD asset, of which Insilico has full economic capture and concentrated upside. Discovered end-to-end on Pharma.AI with its preclinical candidate, was nominated in November 2021, it targets ulcerative colitis (UC) and Crohn's disease. Because no partner economics are attached, the company retains the entire value of the program if it succeeds—making it a high-torque item within the pipeline.

\- ISM5411's differentiation lies in gut-restricted, PHD1/2-selective inhibition that drives local barrier repair while limiting systemic exposure. It inhibits PHD1/2 with IC50 of \~1.9 nM against PHD1 and \~2.5 nM against PHD2 (with minimal PHD3 activity), stabilizing HIF-1α to induce gut-barrier protective genes and anti-inflammation. A high colon-to-plasma ratio concentrates drug exposure at the site of disease, underpinning its potential first-in-class positioning and suitability for combination with existing IBD therapies.

• ISM5411 has advanced from positive Phase I into an actively enrolling

Phase IIa, its critical de-risking readout, with early franchise validation already in hand. Insilico completed two Phase I studies in Australia and China —including a first-in-human Australia study in n=76 healthy volunteers—with positive topline reported in January 2025 showing the drug was safe, well-tolerated and displayed gut-restrictive PK. It has since advanced into the BETHESDA Phase IIa (NCT072655), a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in approximately 80 UC patients led by Prof. Minhu Chen of the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University, with first-patient-first-dose completed on January 12, 2026; endpoints span safety, tolerability, PK, and efficacy via clinical remission/response, endoscopy, histopathology, and biomarkers.

\- Garutadustat's competitive thesis rests entirely on the mechanism and route differentiation, not yet on data. Its non-immunosuppressive, gut-restricted barrier-repair mechanism is orthogonal to the immunosuppressive biologics and JAKs that dominate the IBD market, and its oral, combination-friendly design targets a niche as an add-on rather than a replacement.

\- The BETHESDA Phase IIa readout (\~2027) is the pivotal value-inflection event that could increase the chance of a large global licensing opportunity. Because garutadustat has no disclosed efficacy data yet, its ex-China BD value is currently option-like; a positive 2027 efficacy signal on remission/endoscopic endpoints would materially de-risk it and support a higher-value out-licensing or co-development transaction.
