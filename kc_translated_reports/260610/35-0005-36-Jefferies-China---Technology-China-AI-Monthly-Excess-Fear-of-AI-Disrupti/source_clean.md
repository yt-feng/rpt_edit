## China AI Monthly - Excess Fear of AI Disruption in Software, Esp for China

We view AI's impact on SW as a "natural selection" event, rather than SaaS "apocalypse". Our extensive talks with 7 China SW players on AI reveal likelihood of more resilience to AI disruption than US peers, given China's project-based or module-based SaaS pricing. But IT budget pressure is the key headwind. China SW U/P US peers (7%) and CSI 300 (18%) YTD, driven by earnings D/G and multiple compression. Our top pick in China SW remains Kingdee (KD).

China Software underperforms (U/P) US on earnings downgrade and multiple compression. Since Oct 2025, the US software (SW) sector has sold off sharply on fears that AI will disrupt SaaS. Anthropic's release of Claude Cowork on Jan 12, 2026 intensified the downturn through Mar 31 (\~23% decline), before the market realized that disruption fears were overdone, driving \~21% recovery since then. Our US SW basket (14 stocks): has fallen 6.4% YTD purely due to multiple contraction. Cons 2026 rev profit has risen 2% since Jan 12, while EV/S is down 6.5% YTD from 6.1x to 5.7x. Our China (CN) SW basket (19 stocks): has fallen 13% YTD. Cons 2026 rev has fallen 5% since Jan 12, and EV/S is down 15% YTD from 5.0x to 4.3x, suggesting the sell-off was driven by earnings downgrade (D/G) and multiple contraction.

The rise of LLMs and AI agents is driving a structural shift in SW architecture and economics. We believe AI is NOT a SaaS "apocalypse", but a "natural selection mechanism", as players with defensible moats that proactively embrace AI can evolve into AI-native platforms, while those with weak moats and are slow to adopt AI risk displacement. Three key impacts: 1) Architectural shift. SW will transition from UI-centric, human-operated systems to API-first, agent-executable workflow platforms. Agents can automate tasks autonomously but still rely on SW APIs for workflows, business logic, and data. 2) Pressure on seat-based pricing. As agents replace human operators, per-seat pricing models face structural pressure, pushing vendors toward consumption- and outcome-based pricing. 3) Margin pressure. Unlike traditional SW's near-zero marginal cost, LLM inference is a big usage-based variable cost, which means SW players will have to pass such costs on to customers or be able to charge a healthy margin.

CN SW is less prone to AI disruption, but frail IT budgets remain the headwind. Many CN SW players operate on project-based models, serving large SOEs requiring heavy systems integration and customization. Data security concerns and asset-based mentality have limited public cloud SaaS penetration with SOEs. Moreover, Chinese SaaS players rarely use seat-based pricing; instead, they charge by module. This makes CN SW generally more resilient to AI disruption than its US peers. However, the primary headwind remains IT budget pressure for large enterprises in a weak macro environment.

KD still our top pick in China SW. We believe industrial SW, financial IT, and ERP are the most resilient to AI disruption given deep workflow integration, proprietary data, zero tolerance for hallucination, and strict vendor qualifications. We view KD's recent sell-off as a good entry point: 1) KD's ERP is deeply embedded in clients' workflows as a system of record, and is costly and risky to replace; 2) KD's proprietary data and industry know-how is difficult for AI vendors to replicate; 3) module-based pricing insulates rev from workforce reductions; 4) KD has launched its own AI-native ERP suite and Lingee AIOS, guiding Rmb1.0bn in AI rev for 2026; 5) SaaS now >50% of rev, drives teen rev growth, GPM expansion, and strong op leverage aided by internal AI-driven cost efficiencies; 6) valuation at 0.7x 2026E PEG is highly attractive. ## The Architectural Paradigm Shift: From Software-as-tool to Software-as-digital-labor

Evaluating the structural disruption of generative AI requires understanding the evolution from traditional software/ SaaS to AI agents.
Traditional Software/SaaS functioned primarily as a database-backed system of record, essentially serving as a digital filing cabinet coupled with a manual workflow interface.
Software is designed as a tool for human operators. In this legacy model, the human operator served as the primary executor. Human operator needs to interpret data, navigate the user interface (UI), and manually execute discrete tasks.
We are now transitioning to an AI-native paradigm where software shifts from being a passive tool to an active system of intelligence and action.
Under this new paradigm, AI agents can autonomously navigate interfaces, interpret complex data, and execute entire workflows with minimal human involvement, thereby transforming the software itself into digital labor.
Traditional SW will transition from UI-centric, human-operated systems to API-first, agent-executable workflow platforms. Agents can automate tasks autonomously but still rely on SW APIs for workflows, business logic, and data.

## The "Seat-Based" Biz Model Disruption is More Severe in the US than in China

The convergence of generative AI and agentic execution is fundamentally disrupting the pricing models of the software/SaaS sector.
In the US, legacy SaaS monetization is directly tied to employee headcount as they charge per seat, meaning that as a customer hires more workers, their software spend increases proportionally.
However, when software evolves into an autonomous agent that replaces human workers, the manual headcount required to operate the software collapses.
For example, if a customer support team shrinks from 100 agents to just 10 supervisors managing an AI workforce, a vendor relying purely on seat-based licensing will experience a severe contraction in its addressable revenue base.
In China, however, most of the software companies adopted a project-based business model, as customers are generally large SOEs that demand heavy systems integration and customization services. These customers are also reluctant to adopt SaaS on public cloud due to data security concerns and difficult budget approvals.
Even for Chinese SaaS companies, they seldom use a seat-based pricing model; instead, they tend to price their products on the number of modules or functions (e.g. Kingdee and Yonyou).

## Software Incumbents' Moat to Extend Their AI Transition Window

Deep Integration in Customer's Business Process: 1) Deep integration creates high switching costs, including operational, compliance, legal, and financial risks. Customers must migrate data, rebuild integrations, retrain users, validate controls, and ensure business continuity. 2) Deeply integrated software often serves as the system of record, storing the authoritative source of truth such as customer records, invoices, contracts, and employee data. AI agents may operate on top of these systems, but still need to interact with them.

Proprietary Data: Software vendors that understand their customers' business logic, historical transactions, approval rules, user behaviors, exceptions, and process bottlenecks can leverage this data to build their own AI agents that deliver more accurate and business-specific outcomes. Once a software platform becomes the official system of record for a customer, it becomes very difficult to replace.

Deep Industry Know-how: Software that encapsulates complex industry logic, unwritten regulatory guidelines, specialized terminology, key pain points, and decades of best practices represents a depth of know-how that is difficult for AI companies to replicate or accumulate.

Zero Error Tolerance/ high compliance critical system : Industries like finance, healthcare, and manufacturing demand absolute accuracy and vendor accountability. AI models cannot reliably meet this standard due to hallucination risks. Moreover, regulated systems such as payroll, HR, and ERP require a legally defensible source of truth, strict access controls, and auditor involvement, replacing them with AI poses significant compliance risks.

"Dirty Work" of Implementation and Customization: Enterprise IT environments are complex and different. Delivering IT solution often requires huge amount of consultation, implementation, customization, and secondary development. AI co.s typically lack the engineering capability to handle this "dirty work".

Industry Qualifications: In China, SOE and govt customers tend to select IT vendors with high qualifications and trustworthiness. This creates a moat to fend off competition from AI model co.s and startups.

Economies of Scale: Software co. with strong scale effect could spread the high costs of R&D, regulatory compliance, and infrastructure across a large number of clients, achieving a unit cost that new startups cannot match.

Revenue Model NOT tied to Seat/ User: Software cos. that price their product based on the number of customer seats or users would face severe contraction pressure on software renewals, as enterprise customers' number of employees shrinks in the AI era.

## CN SW is Less Prone to AI Disruption, but IT Budget Pressure is Still the Headwind; KD is Our Top Pick

■ Most of Chinese software companies adopted a project-based business model that requires vendors to provide lots of customization and system integrations to enterprise clients. Meanwhile, their customers are often SOEs and governments, which requires vendors to have high qualifications and are less willing to send data to LLM via cloud API owing to strong compliance.
As a result, we believe Chinese software companies are generally more resilient to AI disruption compared to US software companies. Among our coverage, industrial SW, financial IT, and ERP are most resilient to AI disruption given deep workflow integration, proprietary data, zero tolerance for hallucination, and strict vendor qualifications.
We believe the key problem for Chinese software companies is still customers' shrinking IT budget as enterprises cut their expenses/capex amid a weak macro environment.
We believe the sell-off of Chinese SaaS companies like KD provides a good entry opportunity: 1) KD's ERP is deeply embedded in clients' workflows as a system of record, and is costly and risky to replace; 2) KD's proprietary data and industry know-how difficult for AI vendors to replicate; 3) module-based pricing insulates rev from workforce reductions; 4) KD has launched its own AI-native ERP suite and Lingee AIOS, guiding Rmb1.0bn in AI rev for 2026; 5) SaaS now >50% of rev, drives teen% rev growth, GPM expansion, and strong op leverage aided by internal AI-driven cost efficiencies; and 6) valuation at 0.7x 2026E PEG is cheap.

## We Updated Model API Pricing Methodology Taking Into Consideration of Cache-hit Tokens

In agentic AI use cases, cache-hit tokens are likely to be substantial. Thus, we updated our methodology for calculating the blended model API price, from an input:output ratio of 3:1 to a cache-hit:input:output ratio of 7:2:1. This new methodology has led to decrease in overall model price by c.60%-c70% in both the U.S. and China.
■ We observed model vendors adopted different pricing strategies: Out of the 11 vendors, 5 lowered prices, 3 raised prices, and 3 kept prices unchanged for their new flagship models.
■ Efficiency drives LLM scalability and unit economics: higher efficiency enables comparable performance with fewer resources.
■ Chinese AI players continue to focus on compute and memory efficiency across training and inference

## The Architectural Paradigm Shift: From Software-as-tool to Software-as-digital-labor (1)

Evaluating the structural disruption of generative AI requires understanding the evolution from traditional software/ SaaS to AI agents.
Traditional Software/ SaaS functioned primarily as a database-backed system of record, essentially serving as a digital filing cabinet coupled with a manual workflow interface.
Software are designed as tools for human operators. In this legacy model, the human operator served as the primary executor. Human operator needs to interpret data, navigate the user interface (UI), and manually execute discrete tasks.
We are now transitioning to an AI-native paradigm where software shifts from being a passive tool to an active system of intelligence and action.
Under this new paradigm, AI agents can autonomously navigate interfaces, interpret complex data, and execute entire workflows with minimal human involvement, thereby transforming the software itself into a digital labor.
Traditional SW will transition from UI-centric, human-operated systems to API-first, agent-executable workflow platforms. Agents can automate tasks autonomously but still rely on SW APIs for workflows, business logic, and data.

## The Evolution of AI Integration: From Copilots to Autonomous Agents

The introduction of generative AI copilots marked the first phase of this architectural shift by embedding conversational assistants directly into existing applications.
While copilots successfully streamline information retrieval and content generation, they remain fundamentally passive tools that require continuous human prompting and step-by-step oversight.
The transition to autonomous AI agents represents a far more disruptive paradigm shift because these agents are designed to execute complete, multi-step workflows with minimal human intervention.
Agents achieve this autonomy through dynamic tool-calling and API orchestration, allowing them to interact seamlessly with external databases, web search, computation and analysis tools, enterprise apps, file editing tools, etc.
By leveraging long-context reasoning and state preservation, modern agents can self-correct, handle exceptions, and evaluate intermediate outcomes against a final business objective.
Compared to copilots, agents are more disruptive to software vendors because they directly threatens the manual labor that justifies seat-based pricing.

## UI's Value will be Weakened, But What's Underneath is Still Valuable in Agentic Era

We believe AI will weaken the traditional UI-based software moat because the software user is shifting from human operators to AI agents. Historically, enterprise software was sticky because employees “lived” inside the interface, building habits around specific dashboards, workflows and screens. In an agentic world, this becomes less defensible: agents do not need a browser or a polished dashboard. They need APIs, data access, context, permissions and the ability to execute actions.

As a result, UI is likely to become more of a human control layer rather than the core workflow layer. Humans will still use interfaces to set objectives, approve sensitive actions, monitor exceptions and audit outcomes, but most execution will happen underneath the UI through agent-driven orchestration.

As a result, software architecture will shift from "system of record" to "system of intelligence." Instead of simply storing data and displaying it through UI, AI-native software needs to understand context, coordinate across multiple systems and execute tasks end-to-end.

This requires the business logic layer to shift from human workflow to agent-driven task execution. It also requires open, machine-readable data structures and API-first integration so agents can read, write and act across CRM, ERP, email, payments, documents and internal databases.

Governance becomes a core architectural layer. Software must define which agent can perform which action, on behalf of which user, under what policy, with what approval requirement and audit trail. In this environment, permissioning, and action-level control become more important than UI design.

To sum up, AI reduces the defensibility of UI-centric SaaS, but increases the value of platforms that own business logic, proprietary data, orchestration, governance and the action layer. The future software moat is less about where users click, and more about how agents understand, decide, execute and learn across enterprise workflows.

## The "Seat-Based" Biz Model Disruption Is More Severe In the US than In China

The convergence of generative AI and agentic execution is fundamentally disrupting the pricing models of the software/ SaaS sector.
In the US, legacy SaaS monetization is directly tied to employee headcount as they charge per seat, meaning that as a customer hires more workers, their software spend increases proportionally.
However, when software evolves into an autonomous agent that replaces human workers, the manual headcount required to operate the software collapses.
For example, if a customer support team shrinks from 100 agents to just 10 supervisors managing an AI workforce, a vendor relying purely on seat-based licensing will experience a severe contraction in its addressable revenue base.
In China, however, most of the software companies adopted a project-based business model, as customers are generally large SOEs that demand heavy systems integration and customization services. These customers are also reluctant to adopt SaaS on public cloud due to data security concerns and difficult budget approvals.
Even for Chinese SaaS companies, they seldom use a seat-based pricing model and instead tend to price their products on number of modules or functions (e.g. Kingdee and Yonyou).

## Emerging Software Monetization Frameworks

To escape the seat-based pricing trap, leading enterprise software vendors are actively transitioning toward alternative monetization methods:
1) Consumption-based and flex-credit models: align software billing directly with the volume of work executed by AI agents. Under these credit architectures, standard automated actions consume fewer credits, while complex reasoning tasks consume more, ensuring vendors are compensated for computational effort. In China, many software companies adopt a consumption-based pricing model.
2) Outcome-based or resolution-based pricing: represents the highest value-capture model, where customers are charged strictly based on tasks successfully resolved without human intervention. This outcome-based mechanism fundamentally shifts software value from a simple utility license to a direct, highly scalable substitute for human labor. But in China, this model is still in early testing phase.
3) AI-specific premium SKU bundling: For software suites where seat count remains stable, vendors would offer a premium tier with embedded AI functionality. This would drive higher ARPU to offset potential seat contraction. For example, Microsoft has bundled AI into its Office 365 Suite as copilot and charges a higher-priced seat licenses. In China, Kingsoft Office has followed suite and adopted this pricing strategy.

## AI's Impact on Software Companies' Margins

For software companies, the operational costs of delivering generative AI capabilities could be structurally higher and more capital-intensive than delivering traditional SaaS.
While traditional software features incur negligible marginal costs and yield extremely high gross margins, generative AI generate large inference cost that scales to inference processing and API calls. Thus, model inferencing would place immediate downward pressure on the gross margins of software vendors.
To defend their margin profiles, software companies must ensure they possess pricing power that exceeds their underlying inference costs or they need to transfer the inference cost burden to their customers.
Meanwhile, software companies will also offset these external margin pressures by utilizing AI internally to drive massive corporate cost savings across their own coding, testing, and customer service departments.

## Evaluating Traditional Software Moats in the AI Era

The traditional competitive moats that protect software companies are being tested and reshaped by the capabilities of AI.
The concept of "professional business processes" may become the strongest moat, as embedding complex, industry-specific workflows and unwritten rules into software creates barriers that AI alone cannot easily replicate.
Network effects will not disappear because code becomes cheaper; instead, they will strengthen as AI applications connect more users, agents, and service providers into unified collaborative systems.
However, AI could weaken traditional moats like user-switching cost and scale effects.
User switching costs could weaken as intelligent tools can now automate complex data migrations and accelerate the transition between competing software vendors.
Scale effects may also weaken if the software companies need to bear inference cost for customers to use their products.

## Software Incumbents' Moat to Mitigate AI Disruption (1)

Deep Integration in Customer's Business Process: 1) Deep integration creates high switching costs, including operational, compliance, legal, and financial risks. Customers must migrate data, rebuild integrations, retrain users, validate controls, and ensure business continuity. 2) Deeply integrated software often serves as the system of record, storing the authoritative source of truth such as customer records, invoices, contracts, and employee data. AI agents may operate on top of these systems, but still need to interact with them.

Proprietary Data: Software vendors that understand their customers' business logic, historical transactions, approval rules, user behaviors, exceptions, and process bottlenecks can leverage this data to build their own AI agents that deliver more accurate and business-specific outcomes. Once a software platform becomes the official system of record for a customer, it becomes very difficult to replace.

Deep Industry Know-how: Software that encapsulates complex industry logic, unwritten regulatory guidelines, specialized terminology, key pain points, and decades of best practices represents a depth of know-how that is difficult for AI companies to replicate or accumulate.

Zero Error Tolerance/ high compliance critical system : Enterprise software in industries such as finance, healthcare, and industrial manufacturing demands strict compliance and absolute accuracy. When errors occur, customers need an accountable vendor to take responsibility. AI models alone cannot meet this bar, as they remain prone to hallucination and lack guaranteed reliability. Moreover, systems managing high-stakes, regulated data such as payroll, HR, and ERP, require a legally defensible source of truth, strict access controls, and auditor involvement. Replacing these systems could lead to high compliance risks.

## Software Incumbents' Moat to Mitigate AI Disruption (2)

"Dirty Work" of Implementation and Customization: Enterprise IT environments are complex and different. Delivering IT solution often requires huge amount of consultation, implementation, customization and secondary development. AI co.s typically lack the engineering capability to handle this "dirty work".
Industry Qualifications: In China, SOE and govt customers tend to select IT vendors with high qualifications and trustworthiness. This creates a moat to fend off competition from AI model co.s and startups.
Economies of Scale: Software co. with strong scale effect could spread the high costs of R&D, regulatory compliance, and infrastructure across a large number of clients, achieving a unit cost that new startups cannot match.
Revenue Model NOT tied to Seat/ User: Software cos. that price their product based on the number of customer seats or users would face severe contraction pressure on software renewals, as enterprise customers' number of employees shrinks in AI era.
We believe these moats only buy more time for traditional software companies to make the transition in the AI era. If the software incumbents fail to make the transition, they are still likely to be driven out of the market eventually.

## Metrics to Evaluate Software Companies' AI Revenue Quality

As most of software co. are still undergoing AI transition, we believe several metrics could help us better evaluate whether a software company's AI monetization is high-quality and sustainable, or merely a defensive, short-term packaging exercise. These critical metrics are:

1) Percentage of a company's total sales or annual recurring revenue (ARR) that is directly tied to AI capabilities/products. This is the most direct metric showing software companies' AI transition.
2) Whether AI is priced separately as a premium offering/ add-on, or simply bundled for free. If AI features are offered for free to protect existing renewals and prevent customer churn, it might not be a sustainable solution as R&D and inference costs would be difficult to recover.
3) Pricing model of the AI product. If it is seat-based, it would be prone to value compression as customers' employee number shrinks. If it is usage-based or outcome-based, it will be more align with customer's interest and more scalable.
4) Paid conversion rates to the overall addressable customer base. This will serve as the clearest leading indicator of genuine enterprise adoption and willingness to pay.
5) Tracking shifts in net dollar retention will reveal whether a software company is successfully expanding its value by upselling its AI features and products or slowly bleeding revenue due to structural seat-count compression.
6) Pricing power above its compute and inference costs. Software companies must structure their AI packages via generative credit limits or action based pricing to ensure that customer usage does not cannibalize gross margins.

## Open-Source Models Generally Have More Hallucination but Could be Minimized After Fine-tuning

- AA-Omniscience Index rewards correct answers, penalizes hallucinations, and doesn’t penalize refusals. Its scores range from -100 to 100, with 0 indicating an equal number of correct and incorrect answers, and negatives meaning more wrong than right (e.g. A score of -30 means 65 wrong/35 right answers out of a total of 100 questions).
- The index indicates even the frontier models hallucinate. Therefore, their accuracy would not be acceptable in commercial scenarios that are highly sensitive to hallucination (e.g. finance, healthcare). For now, LLMs need to be fine-tuned or RAG-enhanced to improve accuracy.
- Chinese open-source models are more inclined to hallucinate than U.S. peers. But we believe Chinese models still have an advantage in commercial adoption because open-source models can be easily fine-tuned or RAG-enhanced by third-party, which leads to a stronger ecosystem (similar to Android), whereas closed-sourced models could only be fine-tuned by model vendor (similar to iOS).

## We Updated Model API Pricing Methodology Taking into Consideration Cache-hit Tokens

In agentic AI use cases, cache-hit tokens are likely to be substantial. Thus, we updated our methodology for calculating the blended model API price, from an input:output ratio of 3:1 to a cache-hit:input:output ratio of 7:2:1. This new methodology has led to decrease in overall model price by c.60%-c70% in both the U.S. and China.
■ We observed model vendors adopted different pricing strategies: Out of the 11 vendors, 5 lowered prices, 3 raised prices, and 3 kept prices unchanged for their new flagship models.
■ Efficiency drives LLM scalability and unit economics: higher efficiency enables comparable performance with fewer resources.

## DS V4 is Highly Cost-effective; Co. Still Focuses on AGI Instead of Short-term Monetization (1)

DS V4 demonstrates advanced intelligence and robust coding capabilities, while maintaining a highly competitive price of \$0.17 per million tokens. Several factors contribute to DS' low pricing strategy: 1) DeepSeek V4 leverages a new KV cache compression technique, enhancing cost-effectiveness; 2) it benefits from lower inherent training costs and full compatibility with Huawei's Ascend chips; 3) the company aims to aggressively gain market share and strengthen its ecosystem; and 4) it has received additional cash support from its latest fundraising round.

DS implemented a new KV cache compression technique (Hybrid CAS/HCA mechanism) in its DS V4 that allows it to save memory and compute cost and bring higher inference efficiency and lower margin pressure.

Thanks to several innovations implemented during the training phase, such as Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC), the Muon optimizer, and others, DS V4 achieves a lower inherent training cost compared to its peers. Moreover, its full compatibility with Huawei's Ascend chips gives it a distinct advantage within the domestic chip ecosystem. We believe DS is likely to secure preferential access to Huawei's upcoming Ascend 950 chips.

A low API pricing strategy can help DS gain more market share and help it build a low price + open-sourced ecosystem moat against frontier closed-source models. As more developers use DS' model, it will enhance the data flywheel and help the model to iterate quicker.

A recent news report shows that DS is seeking a massive Rmb50bn fundraising round (source: Reuters), the company's founder Wenfeng Liang will personally invest Rmb20bn and he explicitly stated that 'The fundraising is for pursuing AGI instead of getting listed'. Thus, we believe the company's focus is still pursuing AGI rather than monetization in the short term.

## Competition on AI Assistant Becomes Fiercer as Internet Giants Fight for New Traffic Entrance

## LLM App a Centralized Consumer Gateway That Unifies Fragmented Apps and Fosters AI Agents

■ We believe LLM Apps are important for model vendors mainly because:
- Securing the AI Entrance and Ecosystem: AI vendors' core ambition is to create an omni consumer gateway or "AI OS" (Operating System) of the future, which is expected to replace traditional accesses such as browsers or app stores. The Internet giants aim to build a "Super App" or platform gateway to unify previously fragmented applications. Alibaba, for instance, views its "Qwen" app as a "life service entrance" designed to integrate its vast ecosystem, including commerce, payment, and local services, into a single user entrance.
■ Platform for Agentic AI: The development of large language models has progressed from merely understanding language to being capable of executing complex tasks (known as Agentic AI or AI Agent). AI vendors could provide AI assistants that not only chat with users, but also genuinely "do things" for the user, such as generating elaborate PPTs or completing complex workflows.
- Driving New Revenue and Growth: LLM apps could provide new source of revenue generation. Although unlike US vendors that are already monetizing via subscription on different tier accounts, Chinese vendors could still find ways to monetize via VAS, such as voice recording and transcribing, video creation etc. The AI gateway could enhance an Internet platform's ecosystem by raising the market share of its various Internet services.
