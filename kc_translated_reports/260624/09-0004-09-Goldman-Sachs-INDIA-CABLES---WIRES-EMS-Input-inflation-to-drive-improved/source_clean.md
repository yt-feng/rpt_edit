# Input inflation to drive improved revenue growth in Q1 for C&W and EMS

In this note, we highlight key trends likely to play out for Cables and Wires companies as well as EMS companies in our coverage as they get ready to report Q1 starting mid-July:


Cables and Wires: Key raw materials of Copper and Aluminium have seen a steady increase in prices which should have led to some channel restocking and improved volume growth in Q1FY27E vs Q4FY26. However, we see volume growth still in the mid to high single digits for the quarter. Strong yoy growth in input prices is the key driver supporting revenue.


Dixon: We think an increase in realization due to pass-through of memory pricing inflation should more than offset volume decline and hence growth should improve vs Q4; however, we expect margins to remain under pressure.

Amber: We expect growth to improve in Q1 with normalized inventory in the channel from April onward and AC demand momentum likely extending into Q2 due to delayed monsoons.

For C&W, we raise estimates as we mark-to-market higher input prices and remain Buy rated on KEI, Neutral on Polycab. For Neutral-rated Amber, we raise estimates to reflect lower minority interest post increasing a stake in a subsidiary. For Sell-rated Dixon, we change EPS by -3%/+3% in FY27E/FY28E EPS as we delay some volumes from FY27E to FY28E and increase mobile ASPs on memory cost inflation.

## Cables and Wires

We expect companies to post mid-to high single digit volume growth in Q1FY27, on the back of an expected channel restocking and continuing demand momentum despite such high cost inflation. With copper and aluminium prices increasing $>35\%$ yoy, we think revenue growth for these companies will be largely pricing-driven during the quarter.

Our EPS for FY27-28E increases by 2-3% for both Polycab and KEI driven by MTM on input costs. Our TP increases are slightly higher driven by impact of commodity price increases flowing through the outer years, reflecting in increased EPS. We remain Neutral on Polycab and Buy rated on KEI, with risk-reward remaining well-balanced in Polycab and KEI expected to grow faster in near term post commissioning of new capacities.

## Amber Enterprises

During the quarter, Amber announced a manufacturing collaboration agreement with OPPO Mobile, which marks its entry into smartphone manufacturing and covering 3 brands - Oppo, One Plus and Realme. Amber expects to commence trial production in Q4FY27 and commercial production by Q1FY28, expecting 8mn units in FY28E followed by a 13-15mn ramp up in the 2nd year of operations. It expects to sub-lease existing Oppo facilities in India.

It also expects to begin operations with assembly, following subsequently (in Year 2/3) with increasing value addition through HDI interconnect PCBs, which it aims to manufacture through its JV with Korea Circuit. While we expect initial margins would be lower than its existing businesses, working capital intensity and return ratios are lower and higher respectively. At this juncture, we do not incorporate these into our estimates, and await more clarity on details and timelines.

During the quarter, Amber has increased its stake in Ascent Circuits (from 60%) to 98.5% at a consideration of Rs3.4bn, from its existing shareholders. Mr. Manjunath (erstwhile Promoter of Ascent) has stepped down as MD, and PCB division will now be headed by Mr. Santhosh, (earlier the MD of AT&S India).

This has significantly reduced the minority interest burden for the company and our EPS estimates increase by 11-12% over FY27-28E. As a result our TP increases to Rs7,290 (from Rs6,800) and we remain Neutral.

## Quarter expectations

For Q1FY27, we had earlier estimated consumer durable companies in our coverage to post good growth amid an extended summer and favourable base. We expect Amber's consumer durables growth to improve in Q1 (vs Q4) as well; however the full effect of a prolonged summer and price increases is set to reflect in its Q2 numbers as the industry replenishes inventory for the next summer season. Railway sub-systems segment should improve as execution pace ramps up. Margins however, in our view, would be lower than Q4 due to delayed pass-through of cost increases in PCB business as well as currency depreciation.

## Dixon Technologies

A shortage of mobile memory components, such as DRAM and NAND flash, has increased costs substantially for smartphone manufacturers, and as a result brands have increased prices by \~10-40% over the last 3-6 months. We forecast a \~24% increase in realization in Q1 and a \~18% yoy dip in mobile volumes, and as a result, we expect an overall topline growth of 8% yoy during the quarter.

However, with Dixon getting a fixed profit per unit of smartphone, a higher realization implies margins (as a %) may look optically lower and, coupled with PLI incentives going away FY27 onward, we expect a 30-40bps margin decline yoy in Q1.

With the Vivo JV still awaiting approval, we now expect a slower ramp up in FY27E, and consequently, our EPS changes by -3%/+3% during FY27/28E. We introduce 2029 estimates in this note. Our TP increases to Rs10,025 (from Rs9,790) as a result (all assumptions unchanged). Remain Sell rated.

## Investment Thesis - KEI Industries

KEI Industries is the second-largest cables and wires (C&W) company in India, with 8% market share. In addition to the industry tailwinds, we think KEI is well-placed to continue its strong growth given: (1) it is one of the few companies to have an Extra High Voltage (EHV) cable offering – 220kV+ with the capability to produce 400kV cables. As electricity consumption in India grows, upgrade of cable infrastructure is imminent; (2) increased focus on exports with recent approvals in the US and its offering extending into the EHV segment, driving revenue growth higher than the industry; (3) the upcoming expansion will help de-bottleneck capacity, albeit in phases, with the bulk of expansion in 12-24 months; and (4) with 5%-6% targeted growth in dealers, we expect KEI to grow faster than the industry in the retail segment as it focuses on growing its market share in house wires from 5% to high single digits over the next 2-3 years. We

expect KEI to grow faster than industry in near-term. We are Buy rated.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - KEI Industries

We are Buy rated on KEI with a TP of Rs5,350. Our 12-month price target is based on a 50% P/E and 50% DCF methodology. We assign a P/E multiple of 40x on FY28E earnings and our DCF assumptions are a WACC of 12%, terminal growth rate of 4%.

Risks: Downside: High volatility in commodity prices, significant slowdown in capex, higher-than-expected industry capacity additions.

## Investment Thesis - Polycab India

Polycab has a long runway for growth driven by: (1) the C&W Industry, which we expect to grow at a CAGR of 15% over the next 5 years and for Polycab to grow faster during the initial part of this period due to its available capacity; (2) \~12,000+ SKUs allowing it to find white spaces and creating a sizeable presence; (3) its maiden investment in the Extra High Voltage (EHV) segment, which albeit small, caters to a fast-growing segment; and (4) in the long term, its Fast Moving Electrical Goods (FMEG) business should become an added growth lever, growing on the back of a robust distribution network. However, continuous supply addition and rising competitive intensity is a key monitorable for sustainable profitability. At current levels, we think Polycab is fairly valued and are Neutral-rated. Key Risks: Upside: pickup in renewable capacity addition, improving consumer sentiment aiding FMEG, higher govt capex, faster scale up in exports Downside: High volatility in commodity prices, increasing competitive intensity, slowdown in capex, higher-than-expected industry capacity addition.

## Investment Thesis - Dixon Technologies (India) Ltd.

As India moves away from import of electronics to domestic manufacturing, we anticipate a paradigm shift in the growth of its home-grown EMS (electronics manufacturing services) companies. Dixon is a major beneficiary of the “Make in India” push across products, as it is India’s largest EMS for electronics, lighting, mobile phones, security systems and washing machines. We see concerns on three areas: 1) With Dixon potentially manufacturing a substantial portion of non-Apple phones in India over the next 2–3 years, we think any further increase in this proportion is unlikely, given the preference at OEMs to diversify EMS providers; 2) While backward integration into components should improve margins for the company, we see overall growth moderating; and 3) Other B2C EMS verticals, which used to be a large focus for Dixon in the past, are witnessing intense competition. Consistent downward revisions in estimates on the stock that trades 5X higher than global peers on the basis of P/E

methodology, and 30% higher than other domestic peers, is another concern, and hence, we have a Sell rating.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Dixon Technologies (India) Ltd.

We are Sell rated on Dixon with a 12m TP of Rs10,025. Our price target is based on a combination of DCF (50% weight) and P/E (50%) methodology. We use WACC of 10.3%, terminal growth of 4% and P/E (60X), based on FY28E EPS. Key risks: Regulatory tailwinds, faster customer additions, accelerated execution timelines.

## Investment Thesis - Amber Enterprises India Ltd.

Amber is India's largest Air Conditioner OEM. As the penetration of ACs is lower in India (12% of households as of FY18), we believe the growth runway for Amber is a long one. Import Substitution and Production-Linked Incentives (PLI), coupled with export opportunities, provide strong growth prospects for the company. We expect growth from the following segments: 1) growth in AC components amid import substitution and PLI for next 5 years; and (2) diversified offerings via Railways and PCB manufacturing and assembly in the longer term. We see Amber as a primary beneficiary of the Room Air Conditioner (RAC) story in India. However, it trades at a premium to EMS peers for an inferior return profile, and we are Neutral on the stock. Key upside risks: 1) regulatory tailwinds, 2) accelerated execution timelines, and 3) pick up in railways capex. Key downside risks: seasonality of AC business, slow ramp up of new JVs, lowering of custom duties, competition from foreign EMS players/brands.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Amber Enterprises India Ltd.

We are Neutral-rated on AMBE.BO with a target price of Rs7,290. Our 12-month price target is based on a combination of DCF (50% weight) and P/E (50%) methodology. We use WACC of 9.8%, terminal growth of 4% and P/E (46X), based on FY28E EPS. Key upside risks: 1) regulatory restrictions on imports 2) faster customer additions 3) accelerated execution timelines and 4) pick up in railways capex. Key downside risks: seasonality of AC business, slow ramp up of new JVs, lowering of custom duties, competition from foreign EMS players/brands.
