# Revisiting cyclical framework: industry consolidation unlikely in near term, momentum of earnings cuts accelerating

We revisit our cyclical framework for the China OEM space amid a sharply declining domestic market (volumes -19% YTD), partially offset by a strong acceleration in exports (+70% YTD). Our framework requires three parameters to be hit for industry consolidation to happen: (1) OEMs at cash cost level; (2) management capitulating on profitability & expansion; (3) OEMs' balance sheets turning to net debt.

## Our assessment shows:

Industry consolidation unlikely in the near term. As shown in Exhibit 1, as of 1Q26: (1) The majority (10 out of 14) of OEMs are above cash cost level; (2) Management outlooks are mixed on volume/margin/capacity; (3) Only 1 out of 14 OEMs are in a net debt position. Looking ahead, consensus estimates assume more OEMs will turn EBITDA positive in 2027, likely driven by overseas expansion (which offsets domestic decline) and internal organizational restructuring exercises to cut operating expenses, implying no industry consolidation in the near term.

■ Momentum of earnings cuts accelerating. As illustrated in Exhibit 2, aggregate 2026E/2027E EBITDA consensus estimates for China OEMs have undergone four consecutive rounds of cuts over the last year, but the majority are still above cash cost levels. The momentum of cuts has been accelerating, with the last coming in at -4% post 1Q26 results, or a Rmb1.7bn/1.8bn downward revision to the industry total.

We lower our industry volume forecast by 5% in 2026E to reflect the declining domestic retail sales, despite strong export growth. We now model a -9% yoy domestic retail PV volume decline with +30% yoy export growth in 2026E, amounting to largely stable wholesale and production volume vs. 2025. We forecast 2026E NEV penetration at 60%, implying +1% yoy domestic retail NEV volume growth. Among our coverage, we believe BYD (Buy), Leapmotor (Buy) and XPeng (Buy) are better positioned for both domestic volume growth acceleration as well as higher overseas exposure, with accelerating new export model pipelines and sales network expansion.

## Inflection monitor - 2025/1Q26

Bottom line: Based on our three watch points below, the majority of the OEMs are still above cash cost with overall industry cash profit increasing in 2025/1Q26. On the other hand, the industry’s cash position declined in 2025/1Q26 due to shortening payable cycles under government guidelines. Management teams expect strong overseas demand to mitigate domestic weakness, with mixed views on margin going into the next few quarters. At the same time, more companies are focusing on expanding overseas production. Overall we believe that 1) industry consolidation is yet to come amid a higher industry cash profit level, 2) stabilizing margins, supported by overseas sales and improved product mix, will mitigate domestic competition and cost pressure, and 3) increasing demand for equity issuance could stabilize the industry’s cash position.

1. OEMs at cash cost level: 11 out of 14 OEMs had positive EBITDA in 2025, vs. 10 in 2024. Auto OEM industry EBITDA continues to grow with ongoing cost-cutting measures (today's lowest priced NEV is c.US\$4k) and a profit contribution from strong export growth.

2. Management capitulating on profitability & expansion: Although domestic demand is weaker than initial outlooks at the beginning of this year for most OEMs, strong export volume has supported overall volume growth. Management teams have mixed views on margin with some OEMs expecting lower sequential margins due to ongoing cost pressures, and others anticipating improving margins driven by higher volume and product mix. Also, we note that more OEMs are considering overseas capacity expansion.

3. Calculating potential tipping point: Profitable OEMs on average have room to cut prices by 14% to maintain cash cost break-even, and certain loss-making OEMs could fall into a net debt position in 2028 and beyond without further funding, in our view.

We refresh our inflection point framework for the China autos space post 1Q26 results, following our last edition - China Automobiles: Revisiting inflection framework: Capex expansion & competition amid slowing growth into 2026 (published in Jan 2026).

## 1: OEMs at cash cost level

We believe the OEM industry inflection point will materialize when the majority of companies are at or near cash cost level, i.e. the EBITDA break-even point. This would imply that cash flows for the majority of the industry have deteriorated to the point at which companies are no longer able to add any new capacity and/or have begun to take capacity off-line.

As of 2025/1Q26, 11/9 out of 14 OEMs had positive EBITDA, compared to 10/10 in 2024/1Q25. Meanwhile, OEM aggregate industry EBITDA increased by 6%/11% yoy in 2025/1Q26 (vs. 34%/15% yoy in 2024/1Q25). On the other hand, industry net cash declined by Rmb65bn in 1Q26 vs. 1Q25, mainly due to shortening payable days (especially for BYD). Accordingly, we expect the industry inflection point to be dragged out for a longer period due to strong cash profit generation despite lower net cash positions.

## 2: Management capitulating on profitability & expansion

We believe the industry will start to consolidate when management teams become pessimistic about both the industry and their own company's outlook, which is when we would expect them to start cutting back on capex and opex. Thereafter, industry supply-demand could begin to rebalance with a potential operating leverage turn-around.

Consensus - We note that management teams tend to agree on domestic demand being weak and the export outlook being strong, leading to an industry consensus to cut domestic ICE capacity while seeing more discussions to expand overseas (especially in Europe). Debates - Management teams are different on the timeline for cost pressures to be fully reflected and profit to bottom out, and thus there are mixed views on volume/margin depending on companies' product cycles and overseas exposure.

\- Mixed views on volume guidance: Based on management teams' commentary on the overall industry, they share similar views on weak domestic demand mitigated by a stronger-than-expected export outlook, leading to a mixed volume outlook depending on their domestic model cycles and overseas exposure.

☐ Weaker domestic demand: Management teams are on the same page in anticipating a high-single-digit to low-teens decline in domestic demand this year (vs. -5% to +1% yoy growth per most management teams during our 2026 mgmt outlook call series at the beginning of 2026).

☐ Stronger export outlook: On the other hand, exports continue to beat management expectations, driven especially by high oil prices post the Middle East crisis. Multiple OEMs have raised their export guidance.

## ■ Mixed views on margin outlook:

☐ Competition shifts from price to technology: Within the mass market, BYD aims to improve market share, driven mainly by new technology rather than price competition. Management teams also highlighted more crowded competition within the premium segment (e.g. large 6-seater SUVs). Seres believes Chinese brands will continue to take share from foreign premium brands, and Li Auto/Nio expect potential consolidation on the back of technology (e.g. embodied AI) and increasing brand awareness. Meanwhile, XPeng sees its premiumization as well on track, evidenced by strong orders for GX models.

☐ Different timelines on when cost pressures are reflected...: Some OEMs believe cost pressure was already reflected in earnings in 1Q26, while others see continued pressure into 2Q26/3Q26.

☐ ...resulting in diverging margin trajectories: As a result, some OEMs expect lower sequential margins from ongoing cost pressures, while others look for margin improvement driven by higher volume and product mix.

Consensus view on lowering domestic ICE capacity and expanding overseas: Management teams generally plan to continue reducing ICE capacity domestically, while having more discussions about expanding overseas production capacity, especially in Europe (e.g. BYD, SAIC, Chery, Great Wall, XPeng).

## 3: Calculating the potential tipping point

We carry out an analysis to assess the potential tipping point for different OEMs as to when they could reach EBITDA break-even point and the timing of net cash exhaustion.

For the group of OEMs with positive EBITDA in 1Q26, our analysis suggests that they may have capacity for further 5%-24% price cuts before getting to cash cost (vs. 4%-18% in 1Q25); for the OEMs with EBITDA losses but with continued net cash on hand in 1Q26, our analysis suggests that all would fall into a net debt position from 2028 or beyond (except for GAC, which is already in net debt), vs. tipping points in Aug 2025 for Nio, 2026 for Leapmotor and XPeng, and post-2027 for GAC based on our analysis as of 1Q25.

## For OEMs with positive EBITDA, we try to answer the question: At what price would these OEMs get to EBITDA of zero?

We note that 9 OEMs had positive EBITDA as of 1Q26 (vs. 12/10 in 4Q25/1Q25), and we calculate the unit EBITDA for these companies in Exhibit 14. We assume that unit EBITDA value is equivalent to the magnitude of price cuts it takes to get to an EBITDA of zero. For these 9 OEMs, their 1Q26 blended ASP ex-VAT (calculated by dividing vehicle revenue by vehicle sales volume) ranges from Rmb79k to Rmb347k (vs. Rmb77k to Rmb265k for 9 OEMs with positive EBITDA in 1Q25).

Conclusion: Our calculation implies a price cut range of 5%-24% for these companies (vs. 4%-18% in 1Q25) (Exhibit 15).

## Revising our industry model with lower domestic and higher export volume

We lower our PV retail volume forecasts for the domestic market by 3%-8% over 2020E-2030E, mainly reflecting weaker-than-expected domestic demand in 5M26 driven by 1) a lower trade-in subsidy in 2026 compared to 2025, 2) a higher NEV purchase tax starting in 2026, and 3) the declining effectiveness of subsidies with demand already being pulled forward in 2024 and 2025.

On the other hand, we raise our China passenger vehicle export volume forecast by 6%-11% over 2026E-2030E, expecting 7.8mn units in 2026E rising to 10mn in 2030E (accounting for 17% of market share ex-China/US). Among this, we expect NEV penetration to increase from 49% to 70%, translating to a 26% CAGR over the next five years, underpinned by accelerating NEV penetration from both increased model supply with both improved features and cost competitiveness vs. ICE.

Net-net, we revise our forecast for China passenger vehicle production/wholesale volume by -5% to +1% over 2026E-2030E, primarily due to weak domestic demand offset by strong export momentum.

## Buy BYD, Leapmotor and XPeng on domestic recovery and overseas upside potential

Among our coverage companies, we believe BYD (Buy), Leapmotor (Buy) and XPeng (Buy) are better positioned to benefit from a sequential acceleration in domestic sales, as well accelerating overseas momentum i.e. new export model pipelines and sales network expansion, given their greater overseas exposure.

## BYD (002594.SZ, 1211.HK; Buy) - Domestic recovery set in motion with overseas acceleration

Domestic recovery set in motion: Domestically, we believe BYD's retail sales recovery has been set in motion, as the company launches new models with technology innovation (e.g. 2nd generation blade battery + flash charging ecosystem) to enhance its competitive strengths in China (see our report), as well as most affordable urban NOA model (Rmb78.8k) and 1-year comprehensive safety liability guarantee for its urban NOA (see our report).

For flash-charging capabilities, we believe BYD is now building a comprehensive flash-charging ecosystem from battery, charging, and storage to models, to effectively strengthen its competitive moat, making it difficult for peers to catch up, at least in the short term. For intelligent development, BYD announced that God's Eye B (featuring urban NOA) is now available for all models (package priced at Rmb12k), which effectively makes the entry model price Rmb78.8k, the most affordable in the China market at present; BYD also unveiled its first in-house 4-nm smart driving chip Xuanji A3, which could potentially lower component cost and improve profit levels.

As evidenced by recent strong order intake (e.g. 100k pre-sales orders for Da Tang; 30k orders for Yuan Plus in the first week of facelift launch) and retail numbers (182k units of domestic sales in Apr, improving NEV market share to $21.4\%$ in April from $15.8\%$ in Jan), we believe BYD's domestic sales momentum should continue, demonstrating that a recovery has been set in motion, at least in the following months.
