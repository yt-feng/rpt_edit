# China Consumer
## Tourism: Still-Resilient Leisure Travel Demand During Dragon Boat Festival
## CITI'S TAKE

Despite rainy weather, China domestic tourism demonstrated a resilient trend during the 3-day Dragon Boat Festival (19-21 Jun), with domestic tourist number/tourism revenue growing by 4.4%/4.0% yoy, respectively, according to the Ministry of Culture and Tourism. This came above our expectations given a slight decline in nationwide passenger throughput per Ministry of Transportation. Chinese outbound travel reverted to positive growth during the holiday while inbound travel continued to trend well. While macro and weather uncertainty may linger, we expect that resilient leisure travel demand, solid inbound travel momentum, and normalized industry supply should continue to underpin hotel RevPAR trends in China, especially on ADR strength. Atour remains our top Buy, with its underappreciated earnings resilience against RevPAR volatility and sustained retail strength.

Domestic tourism — According to the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, the domestic tourist number amounted to 124m person times during the 3-day Dragon Boat Festival, up 4.4% yoy. Domestic tourism revenue grew by 4.0% yoy to Rmb44.456bn, with per-capita spend marginally down 0.4% yoy (Figure 1). The discrepancy vs. the traffic momentum illustrated by the Ministry of Transportation, in Citi's view, could be due to less travel demand for non-tourism purposes given the rainy weather while leisure travel demand stayed resilient. According to the Ministry of Transportation, nationwide passenger throughput totaled 648m person times during the holiday, down 0.9% yoy, mainly dragged by -2.1% yoy in self-drive which accounted for 74% of total passenger throughput and -1.8% yoy in waterway. Railway/road/air passenger increased by +2.9%/+2.7%/+0.6% yoy.

Lodging & Hainan Duty Free — 1) Lodging: We observed a divergent RevPAR trend across different regions (i.e., Shanghai saw +1.6% yoy in OCC). We expect Atour to outperform with positive RevPAR growth during the holiday with improvement in both OCC and ADR. 2) Hainan Duty Free: Hainan offshore duty-free sales grew in line, +8.6% yoy to Rmb202m during the Dragon Boat Festival per Haikou Customs, mainly driven by +11.2% yoy in number of buyers while per-capita spend dropped by 2.3% yoy to Rmb5,961 (Figure 6).

Outbound and Inbound tourism — According to the National Immigration Administration, average daily cross-border traffic volume rose by 12.9% yoy to 2.2m person times during Dragon Boat Festival, with +5.5% yoy in mainland Chinese, +18.4% yoy in HK and Macau and Taiwan residents and +23.3% yoy in foreigners (Figure 7). Inbound foreigners under visa-free policy rose 15.2% yoy.

# Atour Lifestyle Holdings Ltd

(ATAT.O; US\$33.29; 1; 18 Jun 26; 16:00)

## Valuation

Our US\$48.0 target price for Atour is based on 14x 26E EV/EBITDA, set at 10% above China's hotel sector average, which we think is justified by the company's faster growth, product competitiveness (particularly in the upper midscale segment), and business resilience.

## Risks

Our quant model rates the Atour stock High Risk for its volatility, but we believe it is unwarranted given the better visibility on recovery after China's reopening, and the company's improving operating capability and earnings resilience.

Key downside risks that could impede the stock from reaching our target price include: 1) travel-related impact from natural disasters or pandemic (e.g., COVID-19); 2) a prolonged economic downturn; 3) intense competition with both domestic and international hotels; 4) failure to expand or delays in new openings; and 5) regulation risks.
