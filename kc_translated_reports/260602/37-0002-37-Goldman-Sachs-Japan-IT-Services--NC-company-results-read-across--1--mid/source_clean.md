# Japan IT Services: NC company results read-across (1) mid-tier SI/NI: IIJ (NW infra/services), Dentsu Soken (SI/financial/manufacturing)

In this report, we consider the potential implications for our IT services sector coverage from visits we made to two Not Covered companies in the mid-tier Sler (system integrator) and Nler (network integrator) space, Internet Initiative Japan (IIJ; network infrastructure/network services/MVNO) and Dentsu Soken (SI/financial/manufacturing). While IIJ's FY3/26 results missed guidance, there were signs in 4Q3/26 (Jan-Mar) that profitability in the network services business is bottoming out, and FY3/27 guidance calls for double-digit (%) profit growth driven by network infrastructure business, for which orders are strong. Dentsu Soken is generally performing well outside of the auto sector, with a strong start in 1Q12/26 (Jan-Mar) including double-digit (%) yoy growth in both operating profits and orders, driven by custom system development and proprietary software. In terms of read-across, we view strong demand for network infrastructure construction as a positive for BIPROGY (Buy), which has a strong presence and a high sales weighting in this field, and as a tailwind for NEC (Buy) and Fujitsu (Buy) as well. Below we outline our key takeaways from NC company commentary. (see Exhibit 1/Exhibit 2/Exhibit 3 for company earnings)

# IIJ (3774.T; NW infrastructure/NW services/MVNO)

(1) Guidance: FY3/27 guidance calls for operating profits of ¥38.5 bn (+11% yoy), with growth expected to accelerate on an underlying basis excluding the dropout of gains from the reversal of retirement benefit provisions (¥1.17 bn). In addition to bottoming profitability in network services, system integration (network infrastructure) is expected to drive growth, backed by strong orders. The company aims to improve profitability in network services by implementing price hikes, mainly for corporate customers (profit impact of just under ¥0.8 bn), and controlling other costs.
(2) System integration (SI) business: 4Q3/26 orders remained strong, rising +51% yoy, with brisk inquiries from government agencies and the financial sector. While existing customers account for the majority, projects in new fields are increasing, and the company's share of wallet (within customer share) is on an uptrend. Demand is strong in network infrastructure construction for structural reasons, and the company expects the trend to continue in FY3/27. It also expects gross margin improvement as the weighting of maintenance business increases.

(3) Network services business: In FY3/27, the company expects stable growth in IP services and outsourcing to be supplemented by work on large-scale WAN projects. Trends in MVNO/MVNE businesses are unchanged, and the company currently has no plans to raise prices (it is gaining share on the back of low prices). It plans to limit deterioration in gross margin to a slight decrease through price hikes outside of MVNO/MVNE and cost controls.

(4) Impact of higher memory prices: The company said components for servers and network equipment have become somewhat difficult to get hold of, and it started front-loading procurement in January. While passing on costs via price increases for products and services in the SI business is relatively straightforward, it takes time to do so in the network services business due to contract timings.

# Dentsu Soken (4812.T; SI/financial/manufacturing)

(1) 1Q12/26 (Jan-Mar) earnings: Operating profits were up +14% yoy in a strong start to the year. While progress in the manufacturing solutions business was somewhat slow, the business solutions and communication IT businesses are tracking above guidance. Gross margin improved on growth in custom system development and proprietary software, and businesses other than manufacturing solutions all saw sharp profit increases.

(2) Order environment: 1Q orders rose +20% yoy, driven by strong inquiries for development for the financial sector (especially megabanks), proprietary software such as STRAVIS consolidation accounting software and POSITIVE human resource management (for trading companies and electric power companies), and development work for the Dentsu Group. Although the manufacturing solutions business won a large, long-term project, investment appetite among automotive customers lacks strength due to factors such as reciprocal tariffs and poor business performance, so the company is shifting its focus from the automotive to the semiconductor sector.

(3) 2Q12/26 outlook: 1H guidance is unchanged, implying a 2Q operating profit outlook of +11%/+¥0.53 bn yoy. The company expects the dropout of a loss on the retirement of POSITIVE software (¥0.31 bn loss) booked in 2Q12/25 as a one-off expense. The order backlog at the end of 1Q was up a robust +33% yoy, and the company said it had greater confidence in achieving guidance. It has not seen any signs of a decline in customer investment appetite due to the situation in the Middle East.

# GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

# M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
