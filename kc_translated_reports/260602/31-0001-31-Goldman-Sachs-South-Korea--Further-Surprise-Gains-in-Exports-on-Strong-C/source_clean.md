# South Korea: Further Surprise Gains in Exports on Strong Chip Exports More Than Offsetting Weak Other Exports
# Bottom line:

Korea's headline exports rose 1.8% mom sa in May, extending a 6-month streak of increase. The outcome was stronger than our above-consensus forecast. Tech exports collectively gained 3.1% mom sa, rising for the 9th consecutive month, led by sustained increases in semiconductor exports. Non-tech exports dropped and partially offset the gains. By destination, exports to the US and EM including China continued to rise. Imports gained 1.3%, driven by sustained increases in energy-related imports. The trade surplus (not seasonally adjusted) narrowed to a new historic high of US\$26.9bn from US\$23.8bn the prior month.

# Key numbers:

May exports: +53.2% year-on-year; Bloomberg consensus: +49.3%; GS forecast: 50.0%; Previous: +48.0%.

May imports: +20.8% year-on-year; Bloomberg consensus: +21.5%; GS forecast: +20.3%; Previous: +16.7%.

May trade balance: US\$26.9bn; Bloomberg consensus: US\$24.1bn; Previous: US\$23.8bn

# Main points:

1. Korea's headline exports rose $1.8\%$ mom sa in May (non-annualized, seasonally adjusted by GS), extending a 6-month streak of increase. In year-on-year terms, growth accelerated further to $53.2\%$ from $48.0\%$ the prior month. The outcome was stronger than our above-consensus forecast.
2. Tech exports collectively gained 3.1% mom sa, rising for the 9th consecutive month, albeit at a moderated pace from 10.5% the previous month. Semiconductor exports sustained a solid momentum of 3.9% (Exhibit 2), whereas other tech exports fell 4.7%, declining for the first time since June 2025 on weakness from computers, handsets and household appliances. Non-tech exports collectively fell 2.8%, with the pace of decline accelerating slightly further from -1.9% the prior month. Weakness was broad across ships, autos, auto parts, machinery and energy-related products.

3. Gains were broad across major destinations except those to the EU which dropped 4.2% mom sa (Exhibit 3). Momentum was the strongest for exports headed to ASEAN, which accelerated to 17.4% and accounted for all the headline sequential increase. Exports to Japan and China also gained robustly, by 6.8% and 5.3% respectively. Exports to the US rose for the 8th consecutive month, at a solid momentum of 3.0%.

4. Imports rose 1.3% mom sa (seasonally adjusted by GS), also slowing from 3.4% the prior month. Energy imports collectively increased 11.3%, sustaining strong momentum after 10.6% the prior month, but the gains were partially offset by a sharper decline in machinery imports, by -5.0%. In year-on-year terms, growth accelerated to 20.8% from 16.7% the prior month, broadly in line with Bloomberg consensus and our forecast. The trade surplus (not seasonally adjusted) widened to a new historical high of US\$26.9bn from US\$23.8bn the prior month.
