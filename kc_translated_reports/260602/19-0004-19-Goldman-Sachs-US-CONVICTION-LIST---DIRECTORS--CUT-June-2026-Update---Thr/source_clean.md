# Directors' Cut - Three Years On

The US Conviction List – Directors’ Cut was first launched on June 1, 2023 with the objective of providing investors with a curated list of the 20-25 highest conviction and most differentiated fundamental Buy ratings from our US stock coverage.

As we do during these anniversary editions, we provide insight into our stock picking process, review performance (including our most frequently requested ‘portfolio’ performance chart) and reflect on what we learned this past year as we continuously tweak our process with the goal of improving outcomes. Additionally, we summarize the 5 themes we are monitoring as we continue to seek alpha across the US equity investment universe and provide write-ups of the thesis for each stock currently on the list.

Since our last annual publication, the S&P 500 is up $29\%$ . As we discuss within, the hit rate on ideas during that time (trailing twelve months) has hovered around $50\%$ against all metrics we track and our equal-weighted indexed return was 180bps below the broader S&P 500 (up 760bps vs the equal weighted index). Consistent with the broader market, the moves under the surface haven't been as smooth as the generally in-line performance would suggest. Our best month was February, where the list benefited from broader participation across market constituents and some strong idiosyncratic alpha on the back of December earnings reports (KEYS, HSY, BRX), while our worst was this past month (May) where the list's underexposure to the technology verticals driving the broader market led to underperformance and a low hit rate.

During the course of the last 12 months, we succeeded in identifying names that showed positive earnings surprises (\~80% of names on the list the last 12 months beat their first quarter expectation after list inclusion), but in a market where themes have often trumped numbers, stocks that were in sectors that, for now, are more of a source of funds underperformed. We show this in more detail on page 12. We did have many idiosyncratic successes, where the key fundamental drivers our analysts were looking for materialized (e.g. WMT, HLI, HSY, COF, WWD, PM) and where market themes supplemented our analysts differentiated fundamental view (e.g. KEYS, GEV). In these cases upward earnings revisions led to higher share prices. There were undoubtedly many others that didn’t work out and we show all adds and removals along with performance of each in exhibit 10.

# Our process continues to evolve

Oversight of the list rests with a subcommittee of our Investment Review Committee — a team of senior research managers who source top ideas from each sector analyst but own the decision to add or remove a name from the list. The list is updated at least once a month in a published note.

As highlighted in each summary write-up, the committee seeks to identify investment views with high conviction, unique points of differentiation, a visible catalyst path and high risk-adjusted return potential. Our goal has been to remain disciplined around our stock selection process, while limiting the list to between 20-25 Buy recommendations and inviting the “competition for capital” among our analysts that many investors have to contemplate.

Since inception, this active discipline has served us well — helping us avoid drawdowns in some names after stated catalysts had passed or market views had become more closely aligned with those of our analysts. But lessons were also learned, as in other instances we would have been better served rotating out of names sooner. As with any investment process we continue to hone our approach, learning from what we got wrong while trying to mimic what we got right.

# Market Commentary - doubt seeds faith

The S&P 500 is now up 84% since Chat GTP was introduced to the world on November 30, 2022. The move has not been straight up. We had to get through the March ‘23 regional banks crisis, the August 2024 Japan taper tantrum, April ‘25’s Liberation Day, and this year’s US-Iran War. But the bounces off of intermittent bottoms have been swift and determined —each time compelling the market back up to new all-time highs.

Underlying this 3½ year rally has, of course, been the AI infrastructure trade compelled by the world's overwhelming hunger for compute power (and everything that goes with it) to run new artificial intelligence models that hold the promise of improving corporate productivity, streamlining almost everything we do, and who knows what else. As with any demand surge, however, investors are left to assess whether the surge has a cyclical component, can actually be fulfilled, and whether the product underlying the demand holds the promise that so many feel it does today (remember iPads, the Metaverse, and ISP's?).

The 20th century Catholic Trappist monk, Thomas Merton, famously said: ‘You cannot be a man of faith unless you know how to doubt.’ And it is this doubt that sometimes extends and fuels stock market rallies like the one we have seen over the past 3+ years.

As for stocks today, Ben Snider points out that, so far, faith has not had to play as big of a role in the rally as you might think (see the May-26 note, “Earnings growth paves a bumpy path to higher returns”). Our recently raised 8000 year-end 2026 S&P 500 target requires no P/E multiple expansion from here — just the realization of the 24% EPS growth rate that we forecast we are on pace to achieve this year. And, indeed, earnings growth has fueled most of the 84% rally we have seen since November 30 2022. In the December 2, 2022 “US Weekly Kickstart,” David Kostin (our strategist at the time) was forecasting 2023 EPS of \$224 for the S&P 500. Fast forward to today, and Ben Snider is forecasting 2026 EPS of \$340 — 52% higher than the forward forecast before ChatGTP was introduced. So almost 2/3s of the gain in the S&P 500 over these past 3½ years is coming from plain old earnings. The rest: faith.

Is this faith sustainable? Well if you are a follower of Thomas Merton, there appears to be just enough doubt to keep fueling the faith. Doubts are likely to persist about how much of an impact AI can have outside of the AI ecosystem itself. Jim Covello, writes in a presentation-style report from Apr-30, “AI Spend vs Benefit,” that the key question that needs to be answered for investors to understand how and where to invest going forward is ‘where is the value going to accrue in the supply chain?’ Will value accrue to the enterprise layer, the model layer, the hyperscaler layer, or the chip layer?

And in a May-5 cross-sector Technology note, Jim Schneider, Eric Sheridan, and Gabriela Borges highlight how the next leg of the AI infrastructure cycle is poised to see an explosion in required compute power in “Decoding the Agentic Economy – The Coming Inflection in AI Usage and Margins.” Agentic AI should drive an increase in token consumption of 24-times the current level. And the types of semis to provide these tokens (a term used to describe what a computer needs to generate or consume the equivalent of 3-4 words of data) is likely to be more CPUs than the GPUs that have dominated the AI infrastructure build-out so far.

Finally, to provide further perspective on how difficult it is for markets to identify structural shifts versus cyclical impulses, the relentless march up in AMZN shares over the past 20 years provides another interesting illustration. On May 14, 2007, our then-internet analyst, Anthony Noto, was forecasting AMZN to generate \$1.44 in EPS for CY2007 (see the May 14, 2007 note, “Noto’s Internet Notables”). Since that note was published, however, AMZN underwent a series of stock splits equating to a 20:1 split, implying that the \$1.44 in 2007 is equivalent to \$0.07 on today’s AMZN share base. Fast forward to today, and Eric Sheridan is forecasting AMZN to generate 2026e EPS of \$9.02 — 129X what it was earning 19 years ago (see Eric Sheridan’s Apr-30 note, “AMZN: Q1’26 Review: Investing Behind Secular Growth Themes Across Compute, Commerce & Advertising”). On May 14, 2007 AMZN shares traded at a current equivalent of \$3.09. Today, the stock trades at \$272 — 88X higher. The stock still hasn’t kept up with the earnings growth it has realized over the past 19 years. Where there is faith, doubt resides.

Where does that leave us today? Ben Snider recognizes that the strength of the recent Momentum-driven rally and midterm election seasonality suggest some tactical hedging may be prudent at this time. But we also think the market moves higher from here. Lean into stocks with the strongest earnings growth momentum, including those exposed to the AI infrastructure build-out (see Schneider and team's note above). And also lean into equities with earnings tailwinds and minimal correlation to the AI trade.

We also sat down with Pete Callahan, the US Technology, Media and Telecommunications sector specialist within GS Global Banking & Markets, last week to discuss how the Tech trade is shaping up as we exited May and looked forward to June (see “The Markets: Why There May Be More Investment Opportunities in Internet Stocks”). Where’s the trade? Callahan likes Internet stocks. DASH is on our Conviction List.

# 10 Themes - Revisited

Investors are looking at the current market as one big trade wrote Ben Snider in a May-15 note, “AI, Momentum, and the One Big Trade.” And if there is any truth to that sentiment, markets perhaps don’t need 10 themes to focus on. So in our half-time update for 2026, we focus on 5 themes for a ‘one big trade’ market.

1. AI & Power. This was the number one theme going into the year, continues to be the number one theme, and is, of course, what investors are referring to when they say the market is ‘one big trade.’ Jim Schneider and team paved a path for where the AI infrastructure theme may go in the years ahead in a May-5 note, “Decoding the Agentic Economy - The Coming Inflection in AI Usage and Margins.” As AI developers shift their attention towards creating agents that carry out tasks on their own, the compute power infrastructure underlying that shift changes as well, in favor of CPU intensity over GPU intensity. The need to electrify massive new data centers, however, does not abate and in February, Carly Davenport raised our US power demand CAGR by 60bp to 3.2% — this is following two decades of no growth at all (see the Feb-23 note, “Power SD Update: Raising US Demand CAGR to 3.2% on Higher Data Center Load, and Introducing Behind the Meter Assumptions”). CL stocks with direct exposure to AI + Power: AVGO, CLS, DUK, and NVT.

2. Productivity & margin gains. Closely associated with the #1 theme is our new #2 theme, as we are seeing increasing interest from investors in uncovering stocks that are boosting margins through all sorts of productivity initiatives, including the deployment of AI. Snider pointed out in a May-26 note, “Earnings growth paves a bumpy path to higher returns,” that the MEDIAN stock in the S&P 500 reported a 14% boost in EPS for 1Q26. There are not 250 stocks participating in AI infrastructure construction. Joseph Briggs writes that companies that more effectively deploy AI agents are likely to pull away from their industry peers and emerge as the next ‘superstar’ firms in a May-12 economics note, “AI Agents, Intangible Capital, and the Next Superstars.” As we continue to cycle through 2026 and into 2027, ‘margin power’ — the ability to improve profitability through more than just topline growth or pricing power — is likely to gain importance. Stocks on the CL that are not part of the AI or Energy trade but where our analysts are still forecasting significant margin expansion in 2026 include: CFG, LOAR, JNJ, UNH, and XYZ.

3. Inflation & rates. In the first 5 months of 2026, the average price of gasoline in the US has risen by over 50%, driving CPI inflation up to 3.8% in April from 2.7% in December. And alongside this inflation surge, yields on 10-year Treasuries have risen over 30bp to 4.48%. Energy stocks on the list that benefit from the underlying inflation surge, include: COP, GLNG, and DUK. Financials stocks that can see higher profits from a steeper rate curve include CFG, IBKR, and XYZ.

4. Consumer Resilience. A companion theme to inflation & rates is the consumer. But what is perhaps most surprising this year is to see how resilient the US consumer has been in the face of such a stiff inflation headwind. Joseph Briggs highlights the resilient consumer in a May-27 note, “US Consumer Dashboard: May 2026: Low

Confidence.” Real PCE growth is tracking at a healthy 2.1% yoy pace through March and April retail sales were strong. But our economists forecast PCE growth to slow sharply from here as the weight of the energy crisis bears down, and the lift from tax cuts fades. Against this backdrop, Kate McShane and team lower our savings rate forecast to make room for lower income growth but still forecast 2026 discretionary cash flow growth to DECELERATE to 4.2% from 5.1% a year ago in an Apr-28 update, “State of the US Consumer: Lowering our DCF outlook based on lower disposable income expectations.” On the CL, we look for idiosyncratic consumer stocks that can still grow earnings in what might become a less attractive consumer environment. CL stocks with exposure to the US consumer: DKS, TSN, BRX, DASH, XYZ, and even CWST (through housing exposure).

5. Geo-political uncertainty & remilitarization. For the remainder of the year, we feel comfortable combining two themes from our January note into one theme today as policy meets military action creating a symbiotic theme for investors. The remilitarization theme that was in full swing in places like Germany and Japan back in January now includes a major remilitarization in the US highlighted by the US-Iran war — an event we did not anticipate. Unanticipated events also underpin the ‘geopolitical uncertainty’ side of this theme for investors. We do not know when the Strait of Hormuz will reopen. That said, although the Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed, physical Brent prices remain well below their early-April highs, driven by substantial OECD strategic reserve releases and a lack of appetite from China refineries writes Yulia Grigsby in a May-20 update, “Oil Tracker: Visible Stock Draws Accelerate on Lower Exports; Weakening China Imports and Demand.” Stocks with exposure to ongoing geopolitical uncertainty and the militarization of the world, include: COP, GLNG, and APD.

# What We Learned

Reflection on what we got right and perhaps more importantly, what we got wrong, is important to our process. Last year, we highlighted in our “lessons learned” that in many cases we would’ve been better served by sticking with winners for longer and removing underperformers earlier. We update this analysis below with that same conclusion, though with improved discipline – the average name that outperformed was on the list for 249 days before being removed vs 194 days for those that underperformed. While we manage this list as a bottom-up list of single stock ideas, agnostic to sector verticals, we did do a better job of ‘respecting what the market told us’ (another lesson learned last year) as we sought to ensure broader representation across market cap and industry groups, though as indicated, our underexposure to the narrow sectors driving the market has hurt overall list performance, as has our attempt to find value in some sectors where business models/exposures are being questioned.

This year we add two new learnings - a refocus on the adage that earnings drive stocks and the challenges one sees when trying to generate alpha when alpha can only be generated when a negative is disproved and when that negative could take years to be proven wrong.

1. Sticking with winners helped overall alpha; we should have cut losses sooner. A repeat from last year, this is core to generating aggregate alpha and is one of the harder disciplines to maintain. We have noted in the past that behavioral biases suggest one may opt to book profits when stocks do well and succumb to thesis drift when a catalyst breaks the wrong way. We attempt to mitigate these behaviors and hold ourselves accountable as we see the analysts' thesis play out in the numbers.

In August of 2014, we published a report that discussed common behavioral biases that can exist in one's investment process (“Mind your Alpha: Case Studies of Behavioral Biases”). One of these was the market's tendency to fade their modeling of companies that have exhibited peer-leading growth — expressing a view that the relative strength of the past cannot last. We used sales growth as the catalyst metric on which we based our analysis. The conclusion: there is a high persistence rate for companies that beat consensus expectations on revenues in a given year that suggests they will beat again in subsequent years.

This bias can be measured in shorter time periods and with a different key performance indicator (KPI). In our June 2025 report, we ran a similar analysis on Directors' Cut stocks we had on the list for at least one earnings report since the inception of the list. In this report, we run this for the 49 stocks that were on the US Conviction List during the trailing 12 months (TTM) and also on the list for at least one earnings report. Key takeaways from this include:

Our analysts have been good at picking fundamental earnings winners. 78% of the TTM new adds (38 stocks) beat consensus EPS estimates in the 1st quarter they reported after being added to the list.
Those stocks outperformed. Those 38 stocks on average outperformed the S&P 500 by 215bps in the 6-day period of -3/+3 days around earnings, with 58% of these stocks outperforming the index.

It paid to hold winners. Of the 38 stocks that beat earnings in that first quarter, 28 remained on the list three months later for another quarter of earnings reports (their 2nd; and another 5 current CL ideas have yet to report their 2nd quarter of earnings). 24 of them (86%) beat consensus EPS for a 2nd time. These stocks, that beat again, on average outperformed the S&P 500 by nearly 500 bps during the -3/+3 day window with 75% of them outperforming the index.
And again. Of the 24 that beat for a 2nd time, 14 remained on the CL for a 3rd quarter of earnings (5 current CL ideas have yet to report their 3rd quarter earnings). 12 of these stocks (or 86%) beat consensus again and, on average, generated another 92 bps of alpha vs the S&P 500 in that same 6-day window.

# 3. Hard to disprove a negative that can't be disproven in the near-term.

It's hard to fight a narrative in the marketplace that question the fundamentals of a business model due to disruptive forces. Particularly when intermediate term fundamentals appear to be fine. We found ourselves doing just that in private credit (ARES), and continuing to do that in software (NOW) – two verticals that have seen multiples contract on the expectation of earnings revisions. In both cases, the moves lower in the stocks have been much more significant than that of the earnings themselves and our analysts have reason to believe the market is pricing in something more sinister than what the numbers will ultimately show. Setting disciplined guideposts on what one needs to see to hold their ground vs cutting losses is critical to risk management as patience is only rewarded if the conclusion is right.

# Stock-specific returns

We recognize a subset of investors focus on stock specific returns; they explicitly neutralize macro/sector/thematic exposures in their investment process. Our investment selection process is most focused on identifying stock-specific characteristics that we expect will result in positive idiosyncratic returns over time. To assess whether we are accomplishing this goal, we isolate and track stock-specific or “idiosyncratic” returns for each name on the list. Specifically, we evaluate the total return of each stock while it is on the list relative to its expected return based on its normal relationship with a seven factor model including beta, sector/size, dividends, growth, value, interest rates and credit.

As we reflect on the past 3 years, we find that the 20-25 names on the Conviction List at any one time in aggregate have generated significant idiosyncratic returns. This is built on a strong foundation of the stock rating process from our analysts which show steady positive idiosyncratic returns for our buy-rated stocks and steady negative idiosyncratic returns on sell rated stocks (both sides have been successful). Alpha trends have not been a straight line. After a challenging 2H2025, 1H2026 has generally been a period of solid idiosyncratic returns.

# Tyson Foods Inc. (TSN) - covered by Leah Jordan

Diversified portfolio with leverage to the hottest area of food

Look for continued earnings growth at TSN — the US’s largest protein processor and marketer — driven by its diversified protein portfolio, plus further margin expansion driven by ongoing operational improvements, strong demand trends, as well as generally better execution across the board.

Chicken strength should drive earnings growth. TSN's portfolio is diversified across protein categories, with exposure to beef (37% of FY24 sales), chicken (30%), prepared foods (18%), pork (11%), and international/other (4%). Jordan expects strong chicken demand to persist across both the retail and foodservice segments as it remains affordable relative to other proteins, driving future earnings growth. She also looks for additional support from recent distribution gains. Additionally, TSN's genetics business has returned to growth after years of investment, and the next leg of the story should ramp over the coming year as this is still a nascent opportunity for the company. Finally, while cattle costs remain an industry headwind amid tight supply, Jordan believes recent capacity closures should drive cost savings for TSN in 2H, especially amid early signs of regional herd rebuilding as rancher economics remain supportive, suggesting further profit recovery over the next few years

\- Prepared foods resiliency. Look for prepared foods volume growth tied to heightened protein demand, distribution-driven market share gains, plus strong innovation. Additionally, TSN should be able to manage potential inflationary cost pressures supported by pricing, innovation/mix, strong volume demand, and partial vertical integration in select commodities, along with value engineering/ supplier programs for packaging costs. Look for further consumption growth ahead, especially in protein-related snacking, amid a sustained structural shift in dietary preferences towards protein-dense foods and an increased focus on nutrition.

Solid balance sheet. TSN's leverage has improved to 2.1x net debt/EBITDA (vs >4x two years ago), nearing its target of 2x or below. Continued FCF growth should support further improvement, which leaves the company plenty of optionality. In addition to a continued focus on maintaining financial strength and investing in the business, Jordan expects to see a step-up in returning cash to shareholders amid this stronger outlook. Of note, TSN restarted open market share repurchases, at a modest pace, in late FY25 (after a hiatus of \~2.5 years).

# UnitedHealth Group (UNH) - covered by Scott Fidel

Shrink to grow insurer on the cusp of a pro-cyclical EPS reacceleration

UNH is on the cusp of seeing a bottom in the underwriting cycle for \~40% of its business — Medicare Advantage (MA) — a down cycle that has weighed significantly on the shares alongside similar downcycles in Medicaid and ACA exchanges. Look for the company to shrink its exposure to the MA end market at the same time as profitability in the market finds a bottom, leading to a sustained positive profit inflection for UNH. For 2026, Scott Fidel forecasts a 4% decline in MA Revenue but a \~50% increase in MA EBIT as the company prices exposures better and cuts costs partly through AI implementation — all helping to drive 15%+ EPS growth on average per year through 2028e.


\- Shrink to grow. In 2026, UNH plans to reduce MA membership (individual + group) by 1.3mn members, reduce its ACA Exchange footprint by \~one-third from a base of \~1.7mn members in 2025, and reduce OptumHealth's VBC membership by \~10%. All of these reductions are part of an effort to upscale the profit per member, drive higher sustained pricing, and improve margins. UNH is also leveraging AI technology in an effort to cut its operating cost ratio in half over time.

The good outweighs the bad. UNH is unlikely to put all of its troubles behind it over the next year. The company's Medicaid business is on pace to see margins decline by 130bp. And regulatory scrutiny around past CMS coding and healthcare access initiatives is ongoing. But the measures it is taking in right sizing its OptumHealth value based reimbursement segment began to yield results in 1Q26. Notably, as MA profitability improves, pressure on OptumHealth from elevated medical costs and operational noise eases, which should aid margin recovery in this segment. Look for notable profit growth as we go through 2026 driving a $13\%$ increase in adjusted EPS after falling by $40\%$ in 2025, with this segment representing an increasingly

important driver of consolidated earnings growth through 2027–28.

# Where we are different.

Fidel's work on the historical underwriting cycles within the US health insurance sector provides a unique lens into how typical the current environment is, even though it feels to some investors like a sea change. Bottom line, Fidel believes UNH represents a high conviction opportunity to own a best in class managed care franchise at the turning point of its earnings cycle. With MA margins bottoming, OptumHealth re accelerating, and cost normalization ahead, Fidel believes a durable, multi year EPS recovery is emerging that is not yet fully appreciated by the market.

# Valuation.

12-month price target of \$435 is based on a target P/E multiple of 19.1x Q5-Q8 estimated EPS. For context, UNH has traded at a 10-year average historical P/E of 18.8x within a range of \~13x-25x. Risks include elevated utilization, regulatory changes and scrutiny, pricing misalignment relative to utilization, and the potential for UNH to take a substantial charge to cover past claims.
