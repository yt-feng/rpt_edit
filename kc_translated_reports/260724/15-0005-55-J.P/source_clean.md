# China: Slow 2Q fiscal deployment leaves more room for 2H
# Near-term focus on execution

\- General budget revenue and spending improved in June, while land sale-dependent managed funds continued to deteriorate.

\- Budget spending rebounded, but infrastructure outlay growth stayed negative, dragging on investment.

\- Despite an accelerated drawdown, the increase in fiscal deposits in 1H remained above recent-year norms (ex. 2025).

\- Slower 2Q fiscal execution leaves more policy space for 2H, with near-term focus on accelerating budget deployment

Fiscal conditions diverged across accounts in June. General budget revenue continued to improve on the back of PPI tailwinds, accompanied by an encouraging rebound in expenditure. In contrast, managed fund revenue and spending contracted more sharply, reflecting the persistent drag from weak land sales. Despite an accelerated drawdown, the increase in fiscal deposits in 1H remained above recent-year norms (excluding 2025). Combined with a slower pace of government bond issuance, this suggests substantial fiscal room left for 2H, underscoring our recent growth forecast upgrade.

General budget revenue growth accelerated while expenditure rebounded in June. Headline general budget revenue picked up to 8.7% oya in June (vs 2.2% full-year budgetary target), the fastest pace since January 2025, lifting the ytd growth to 4.7% ytd. Tax revenue was the main lift with a 10.8% oya gain, led by enterprise income tax (+29.7%), while securities trading stamp duty revenue surged 145.3%. Non-tax revenue growth moderated to 2.9% oya, following a 5.6% rebound in May. General budget expenditure returned to expansion (+4.0% oya) after two consecutive months of contraction, which is encouraging. But even with the June rebound, expenditure only grew 0.2% oya in 2Q, lagging 1Q's 2.6% growth and the 4.4% full-year budgetary target. By category, livelihood spending (related to “investing in people” policy initiative) strengthened to 8.4% oya (vs. 2.5% in May). Infrastructure outlays, however, remained in contraction at -1.8% oya, though the decline narrowed from -12.0% in May. This continued to weigh on infrastructure FAI (-10.6% oya) and public FAI (-7.1%).

Land sales contraction deepened further, dragging on funds account revenue and expenditure. Outside the general budget, government-managed funds, which are now the largest financing source among fund accounts, remain heavily land-sale dependent, while spending is closely tied to land-related expenditures and the deployment of special local government bond proceeds. Revenue weakness was accompanied by a 43.7% decline in fund expenditure, pointing to mounting constraints on fiscal deployment amid strained local-government finances.

Fiscal delivery slowed in 2Q, leaving more budgetary room for 2H. The contraction in 2Q FAI reflects a limited pipeline of eligible projects, greater emphasis on debt repayment, slower government bond issuance and proceeds deployment, and the gradual rollout of policy-bank tools intended to provide seed capital for new projects. Special local government bond issuance slowed again month-to-date in July. While the end-June surge in issuance has created a pool of proceeds for near-term project deployment, it also suggests only a limited acceleration in fiscal execution despite 2Q GDP (4.3% oya) undershooting full-year target (4.5-5%). We believe policymakers remain relatively comfortable with the near-term macro backdrop, as 1H growth of 4.7% is still consistent with the annual target range, reducing the urgency to immediately step up fiscal delivery.

We continue to see a two-stage fiscal path ahead, with the near-term focus remaining on execution. Stage 1 centers on deploying NPC-approved budgets and drawing down fiscal deposits. As project approvals accelerate, government bond issuance picks up, and proceeds are deployed more quickly, infrastructure and public investment growth could strengthen, while base effects turn more favorable in 2H. Stage 2 is more conditional: if 3Q growth falls materially short of the 4.5–5.0% full-year target range again, the case for additional fiscal support before year-end would strengthen. However, our conviction is lower, as the target range provides policymakers with greater flexibility than in previous years. For now, we believe we remain in Stage 1, with a low probability of signaling incremental fiscal support at the July Politburo meeting.
