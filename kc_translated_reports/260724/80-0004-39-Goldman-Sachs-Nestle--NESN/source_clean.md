# Nestle (NESN.S)
# Soft US growth and caution on margin trajectory do not derail company turnaround; Reiterate Buy

Nestlé reported Q2 organic sales growth of 3.7% with +1.8% in RIG which, although in line with consensus, appeared light in the context of the stock's strong run into the print. Within group RIG, we flag the strong performance in AOA, with China yet to contribute. However, North America RIG in Q2 was -0.6% affected by destocking in Petcare and low consumer confidence weighing on performance. Europe RIG of +0.5% in Q2 was held back by delistings which might impact Q3 alongside the ongoing, albeit moderating, drag from the IMF recall. UTOP margin of 16.4% was better than expected despite an increase in marketing, but saw a 30bps one-off benefit from the pension scheme change.

## Guidance unchanged but concerns remain around margins;

Management is expecting H2 UTOP margin to be similar to H1, but as the pension benefit should not benefit H2, it implies a sequential underlying improvement. Despite a volatile input cost environment, management reiterated its 17% mid-term guidance and committed to some margin progression in FY27e albeit not quantified at this stage. We forecast 20bps margin progression per year, assuming that any windfall will be reinvested in marketing to boost future RIG.

## Key highlights from H1 results

We highlight the key positives and negatives from H1, starting with positive fundamentals for Petcare, strong growth in AOA (Asia, Oceania, Africa) and strong returns from cost savings;

I. The Pet food category shows good underlying fundamentals, despite temporary headwinds in the US in Q2 linked to destocking. Management remains confident in the category's medium-term growth outlook. By region, Petcare grew mid-single digit in Europe, while EMs demonstrated good momentum. However, growth was negatively affected by retailer inventory reductions in the US, which was likely caused by the company's improved supply levels reducing the need for retailers to hold onto safety stocks. Management confirmed that US sell-out trends remained strong in Q2 vs Q1, with an acceleration in cat food. Looking forward, Nestlé does not expect further impacts from retailer destocking but cautioned that Q4 growth will be lapping a tougher comp as Q4 25 Petcare sales benefitted from pre-buying ahead of planned price increases.

We see the main negatives from H1 results as the lack of UTOP margin improvement now expected for H2 driving uncertainty around how much margins can expand in FY27, alongside continued headwinds from infant formula...

I. H2 margins are now only expected to be similar to H1 (previously guided for expansion HoH), with management confirming that improving raw material costs for coffee and cocoa are now likely to be at least partially offset by higher transport and energy costs due to the Middle East conflict. While management did confirm that Nestlé's UTOP margin is expected to improve further in FY27, albeit not quantified. Although the H1 26 UTOP margin of 16.4% includes a one-time +30bps benefit, it also includes operating deleverage associated with the infant formula recall (which should mostly normalise by the end of the year) and +40bps in higher marketing costs.

Nestlé's H1 UTOP margin had a one-time 30bps benefit from pension credits. This was due to a curtailment gain arising from Nestlé adjusting its benefits package to better align with employee preferences, by allowing employees to take a lump sum on retirement instead of only offering a regular pension payment. While management does not anticipate a further P&L benefit from these actions, they should provide a small cash flow benefit over time. Our understanding is that it won't benefit H2 26 but will be in the base for H1 27.

II. Infant formula remained a headwind to growth in Q2, despite the initial impact from the recall in Q1 subsiding. However, there was a clear sequential improvement, with infant nutrition sales down mid-teens in Q1, while in Q2 the decline was only mid-single digit. Nestlé expects further improvement throughout the rest of 2026 and for the business to have largely recovered by the end of the year.

III. Mixed bag in coffee with strong Americas and AOA offset by Europe weakness. US coffee RIG slowed as the group increased prices on its Starbucks portfolio, and saw a negative initial RIG reaction, as expected. However, in the rest of Zone Americas RIG strengthened, with double-digit growth in Nescafé across major markets, albeit with Coffee mate declining. AOA and Europe both saw RIG led high-single-digit growth led by Nescafé, as pricing moderated. In Nespresso, the group is seeing new and younger consumers entering the category, and is seeing share gains in the US with Vertuo, albeit with growth moderating. However, Europe remains under pressure driven by the ongoing competitive intensity in its Original line.

## Progress on disposals with waters JV, and strong cash generation

Nestlé is progressing with its disposals, and has announced plans to create Peranel, a 50:50 JV with Platinum Equity, for Nestlé's waters and premium beverages. The transaction is expected to close in H1 27 and assigns an enterprise value of €4.9bn, implying Nestlé will receive CHF 2.8bn in cash proceeds at closing. Nestlé will also classify its mainstream VMS and ice cream businesses as “assets held for sale” as the sales processes progress. On a pro-forma basis, accounting for the waters proceeds, Nestlé would have CHF5.3bn available for debt reduction in FY27 and could reduce net debt to EBITDA to 2.3x (current GSe 2.5x). We expect Nestlé to deliver CHF 9.9bn FCF in FY26e, supported by lower capex and working capital efficiencies, with FCF generation H2 weighted.

## Valuation and risks

Valuation Methodology: Our 12-month price target of CHF95 (unchanged) is derived using an equal blend of DCF and a multiples-based valuation. Our DCF-derived valuation implies an intrinsic value of CHF 92/share (from CHF 93/sh). We use a WACC of 7.4% and a 2.0% terminal growth rate (both unchanged). Our multiples-based valuation methodology implies a value of CHF 99/share (from CHF 97/sh). In our multiples-based valuation analysis, we use a target P/E multiple of 20x (unchanged), which we apply to our Q5-Q8 estimates. We are Buy rated.

Key downside risks to our view and PT: (1) Lower-than-anticipated growth in key categories. (2) Slower-than-anticipated margin recovery. (3) Value-dilutive asset separations or M&A. (4) FX risk given Nestlé reports in CHF. (5) Lower free cash flow generation.
