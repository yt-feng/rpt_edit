# 2Q26 Earnings – Transitory or Prolonged Spending Reprioritization?


Source: Company data, MS

The 2H recovery case is plausible, supported by early deal recapture and stronger productivity, but guidance leaves little room for error and requires a sharp Z rebound. 3Q print/4Q guide should clarify whether 2Q was transitory or a broader reprioritization trend. PT moves lower to \$190. Remain EW.

## Key Takeaways

2H recovery is now the key debate: slipped deals are closing, but guidance leaves little room for incremental conversion pressure or capex deferrals.

Mgmt says 2Q weakness was transitory, with $\sim 1/3$ of delayed ELAs already closed in 3Q, supporting mgmt confidence in a 2H recovery.

Productivity remains the cushion; cost actions offset revenue pressure, supported margin expansion, and drove a higher FY26 PTI margin outlook (now +100bps Y/Y).

Infra guidance requires a sharp 2H rebound, including meaningful Z improvement. Distributed Infra strength alone is unlikely to bridge the gap.

We mark our PT to \$190 (from \$293), based on 11.6x CY26 FCF/share of \$16.43 (vs. prior \$16.55). Remain EW.

Mgmt is banking on a 2H recovery – will it materialize? We believe this is the key debate that will influence Wall Street's assumptions on earnings power, organic growth, and ultimately – valuation – over the next 6 months. The bull take on this debate was presented by mgmt – IBM has already recouped $\frac{1}{3}$ of the pushed deals from 2Q, subscription-based spending is strong, recent acquisitions (HCP/CFLT) are accelerating, Consulting is slowly on the path to recovery, and mainframe will remain core to the IBM customer base and, thus, drive the software flywheel in 2H. This scenario is plausible, but IBM didn't leave much room for error – we estimate the 2H "base case" guide embeds a meaningful Software and z17 recovery that

What we learned from IBM's 2Q26 earnings conference call and IR callback:

(?) Why management believes the 2Q shortfall is transitory vs. the risk to this view. Management's core argument on deal slippage being a transitory issue resides in the details, which was that a low-double-digit number of large capex ELA deals at large clients were redirected in the final weeks of June toward servers, storage, and memory purchases ahead of expected supply-constrained price increases, rather than being a loss of demand or a structural shift away from IBM's platform. The key proof point is that roughly 1/3rd of those slipped deals had already closed within the first three weeks of 3Q, well ahead of the typical two-thirds to three-fourths closure rate management would expect over six months. And IBM's "base case" Software guidance of $6\%$ Y/Y in CY26 assumes the capex-deferral environment continues into 2H, with pipeline yields assumed to remain below historical norms.

That said, the risk to this argument, and guidance, is clear. First, IBM is guiding to relative strength in Servers/Storage persisting in 2H, but also a recovery in Z and parts of software – if the drivers of 2H Distributed Infra strength are the same as 2Q, it's counter-intuitive (to us) to expect the transactional parts of Software/Z that were weak in 2Q to then recover in 2H. Second, in modeling 6% Y/Y CC Software growth for CY26 – which is IBM's base case and embeds further capex deferrals – we still estimate it implies stronger Q/Q dollar revenue growth for Software in

3Q/4Q than any period in the last 5+ years.

(+) Cost savings/efficiencies continue to surprise to the upside. Despite the revenue shortfall (\$17.2B came in \$1B below MSe of \$18.2B) and a gross margin decline of 70 bps Y/Y in the quarter, productivity actions came in ahead of plan, providing enough flexibility to absorb CFLT-related dilution, continue investing for growth, and still expand PTI margins by 30bps Y/Y. Notably, Consulting profit margins expanded 160 bps, and 110 basis points in Software, driven by ongoing productivity actions, despite topline challenges. For FY26, management now expects 100 basis points of operating PTI margin expansion, with productivity more than offsetting revenue-related headwinds. We learned that the savings were both a proactive bias and a reactive response to the quarter, with multiple buckets still in early innings: third-party spend reduction, AI-driven software development efficiency, G&A efficiencies, sales and marketing optimization, supply-chain optimization, and enhanced services delivery. Importantly, no incremental workforce rebalancing charges are assumed.

(?) A 2Q Infra miss but CY26 Infra raise – what's happening under the hood? We already knew from last week's pre-announcement that Infra revenue would fall short of expectations due to mainframe deferrals more than offsetting strength in Servers and Storage. What surprised us this quarter is that on the back of this 2Q miss, IBM raised its Infrastructure revenue guidance for the year – from LSD% Y/Y declines, to LSD% Y/Y growth. We'd note that reaching guidance of +LSD % Infra revenue growth for CY26 requires 2H26 infrastructure revenue to ramp meaningfully, implying the highest Q/Q dollar growth the segment has seen in at least 3 years, even higher than 2H25, after the z17 was just launched. While Distributed Infrastructure's record \$500M+ backlog and 37% 2Q growth are impressive, we don't believe Distributed Infra alone can bridge that gap, as the implied 2H ramp can only realistically work if Z17 has a meaningful recovery in both 3Q and 4Q, in our view.

## EQUAL-WEIGHT THESIS

IBM has gained credit for driving more consistent performance in recent years and shifting its portfolio to more recurring Software and Services. The acquisition of HashiCorp combined with a mainframe/ELA cycle is resulting in growth accelerating in 2025, but also creates risk of a deceleration in 2026. This risk, among others, is what supports our \$190 price target. Yet, the market is clearly also focused on Quantum, and given the re-rating we have seen from pure-play quantum stocks, IBM's early Quantum leadership has upside optionality. Balancing these facts is what drives our EW-rating, with mis-execution the key bear case risk.
