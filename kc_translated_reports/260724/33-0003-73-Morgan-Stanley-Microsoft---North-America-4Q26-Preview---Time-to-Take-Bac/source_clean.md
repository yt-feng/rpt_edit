## 4 Areas We Are Monitoring Into the Print:

## 1) Proving Out the Durability of Azure Growth

MS View: Our confidence in low-40%'s CC Azure growth in F4Q26 reflects Microsoft's continued leadership, reflected in our MS CIO survey work, as well as in constructive partner commentary pointing to durable cloud and AI demand, and incremental AI capacity coming online as datacenter build-outs continue to improve. The 41% cc investor bogey is achievable, and this 1pt beat vs. high-end of guidance would represent a strong outcome for MSFT shares, especially given prior guidance for further modest acceleration in the first half of C2H26 vs. 1H, implying 42%+ cc growth in the following two quarters. We expect Azure growth to remain well supported into FY27 as Microsoft executes against its plan to roughly double its total datacenter footprint over the next two years. We believe this expanding infrastructure footprint should continue easing capacity constraints, allowing Azure to capture robust AI and cloud demand while providing further evidence that Microsoft's significant AI infrastructure investments are translating into durable revenue growth.

Buyside: For 4QFY26, investors are looking for 41%+ growth in constant currency (cc), representing a 1% beat vs. high-end of guidance at 39-40% cc, which is also impacted by a 4% tougher growth comp from last year. Further, expecting management to reiterate prior guidance (from the F3Q26 call) for 'modest acceleration in the second half of the calendar year compared with the first half,' meaning Azure growth of 42%+ in cc in F1H27.

Microsoft Continues to Screen as the Clearest Enterprise AI Winner. Our latest 2Q26 CIO Survey (see Microsoft: 2Q26 CIO Survey Takeaways – GenAI Leadership Position Remains Clear (16 Jul 2026) continues to reinforce Microsoft's leadership across both cloud and GenAI despite a relatively stable enterprise IT spending backdrop. While CIOs expect software spending to accelerate modestly to +4.1% in 2026 from +3.8% in 2025, Microsoft remains the primary beneficiary of that incremental spend, with the highest forward software spending expectations across vendors at +7.6%, improving from +7.3% in our prior survey. Microsoft also continues to lead on a net spending basis, with 72% of CIOs expecting to increase spending on Microsoft solutions over the next year. At the same time, Microsoft's leadership extends well beyond core software spending into enterprise AI adoption. First, Azure remains the primary beneficiary of ongoing cloud migration, screening as the largest expected cloud share gainer over both the next year and the next three years, while Azure spending intentions continued to improve. Second, beyond cloud infrastructure, CIOs again identified Microsoft as the largest expected beneficiary of incremental GenAI spending, while also ranking the company as the preferred platform for both Agentic Automation initiatives and building custom AI applications. Third, at the application layer, Microsoft 365 Copilot remains the flagship AI workload within Microsoft's portfolio, with adoption expectations continuing to improve alongside increasing planned deployment across enterprise M365 users.

Consensus Azure growth expectations call for 40.5% YoY growth (as reported; per guidance given no meaningful FX impact), which implies \~\$3.3bn in QoQ dollars added or \~41% YoY growth in QoQ dollars added. In terms of the expected beat factor for Azure revenue growth, similar to last quarter, we expect expectations calling for \~1pt above the high-end of the CC guidance (39-40% cc guide) this quarter. A few items to keep in mind: 1) Similar to prior quarters, surplus demand signals do not determine realized Azure growth, as management has consistently reiterated that Azure revenue remains constrained by available capacity rather than customer demand. As CFO Amy Hood emphasized on the F3Q26 earnings call, Azure guidance should be viewed as an 'allocated capacity guide,' with the key variables being the timing of datacenter buildouts, GPU deployments, and when newly installed capacity becomes revenue additive. While Azure demand remains robust across both core Azure and Azure AI workloads, reported growth continues to be governed by the pace of incremental infrastructure deployments/monetization. 2) Available capacity must be allocated not only to external Azure customers, but also to Microsoft's rapidly expanding portfolio of first-party AI offerings. As analyzed in our recent Microsoft deep dive (see Microsoft: Battle of the AI Stack: Infrastructure to Knowledge Workers – Azure and Copilot Are Set to Inflect (21 Jul 2026)), incremental AI infrastructure (or new capacity coming online) must be balanced across Azure, 1P applications, internal R&D / training, with

management consistently prioritizing the highest-return opportunities across the Microsoft platform. With that said, Azure growth represents just one output of Microsoft's broader AI infrastructure allocation strategy, though we expect Azure AI to receive an increasing share of incremental capacity as it comes online, reflecting its role as the primary infrastructure layer through which Microsoft monetizes AI demand across both Azure and its first-party AI applications (see Exhibit 9). 3) While Azure AI continues to represent the primary driver of Azure growth, the focus is increasingly shifting to how efficiently Microsoft's expanding AI infrastructure is converted into revenue as capacity constraints gradually ease. As Microsoft executes against its plan to \~2x its datacenter footprint over the next two years, we expect increasing infrastructure yield to support durable Azure growth into FY27 and beyond, though the capacity-driven guidance may continue to limit the magnitude of quarterly upside surprises. In terms of Azure AI in F4Q26, we expect \~100% YoY growth or \~18% QoQ growth vs. an estimated 17% in F3Q26 and 20% QoQ in F4Q25. This implies \~\$1,260MM QoQ dollars added (vs \~\$1,000MM in F3Q) and \~19pts of contribution from Azure AI growth in F4Q (see Exhibit 1), slightly ahead of the estimated contribution in the prior two quarters.

## 2) Microsoft 365 & Copilot: The Next Phase of AI Monetization

MS View: Multiple Monetization Levers Support Durable Commercial Cloud Growth. We continue to view M365 Commercial Cloud as one of Microsoft's most underappreciated AI monetization opportunities, though increasingly believe the focus should go beyond Copilot seat adoption toward the broader evolution of Microsoft's AI business model. As discussed in our recent Microsoft Assumption report (see Microsoft: Battle of the AI Stack: Infrastructure to Knowledge Workers – Azure and Copilot Are Set to Inflect (21 Jul 2026)), Microsoft is transitioning from a traditional SaaS licensing model toward more of a hybrid seat + consumption framework across the application layer. While M365 Copilot seats remain the primary monetization vector today, management has increasingly emphasized broader AI monetization, noting on the F3Q26 earnings call that 'we are seeing a new pattern emerge as customers shift from traditional seat model to seats plus consumption.' GitHub Copilot, Copilot Credits, Dynamics AI agents and broader AI services represent the earliest examples of this evolution. We believe this evolution expands the company's monetization opportunity well-beyond traditional seat-based ARPU growth, allowing AI revenue to scale alongside customer usage while creating multiple independent drivers for M365 Commercial Cloud growth. As these monetization levers (i.e., Copilot seats, E5/E7 upgrades, commercial pricing, and consumption-based components) continue to scale in parallel, we expect M365

Commercial Cloud growth to become increasingly durable throughout FY27 and beyond, supported by a more diversified and resilient ARPU expansion framework.

The Next Phase of Copilot Monetization. We believe the key debate surrounding Copilot is increasingly shifting from proving enterprise adoption toward understanding the long-term monetization opportunity. Over the past year, Microsoft's primary objective was demonstrating product-market fit and driving enterprise deployments. Today, however, the conversation is increasingly shifting toward monetization, adoption velocity and the broader Commercial Cloud opportunity. Importantly, Microsoft's new paid M365 Copilot seat disclosure provides a much more tangible KPI for measuring commercial traction over time. Following the increase to 20MM paid seats in F3Q26 (up from 15MM in F2Q26), we expect paid seat growth to remain one of the key indicators of enterprise adoption and ARPU expansion going forward, with management guiding 'net paid [M365 Copilot] seat adds to increase sequentially,' reinforcing our expectation for continued acceleration in Copilot monetization into FY27. While seats represent only one component of the broader monetization opportunity, they provide the foundation for higher-value E7 adoption and expanding consumption-based AI monetization over time, consistent with our expectation for robust paid seat expansion over the coming years. As such, we continue to believe Copilot-driven ARPU is evolving into a three-headed growth driver consisting of (1) direct Copilot seat adoption, (2) migration toward higher-value M365 E7 subscriptions, and (3) expanding consumption-based AI monetization tied to agents, orchestration and workflow automation. Together with continued product innovation across Copilot, GitHub, Agent365 and workflow automation, we believe these monetization vectors support improving M365 Commercial Cloud growth while making that growth increasingly diversified, ARPU-driven and durable over the long run.

M365 Pricing Increase Represents Another Incremental ARPU Tailwind. Beyond improving Copilot adoption, Microsoft's commercial M365 price increases became effective July 1, 2026, introducing higher pricing across several core SKUs alongside new AI, security and endpoint-management capabilities. As outlined in our initial analysis (see M365 Price Increases – What Is the Tailwind to Growth?), we continue to estimate the pricing changes will contribute approximately \$2bn / \$4bn / \$6bn of incremental M365 revenue across FY27/FY28/FY29, though this is already factored into the stock and estimates. Importantly, while the pricing became effective July 1 for new customers, the existing installed base will not realize the higher pricing until contract renewal, implying the benefit should phase in gradually over roughly three years. Using our bottom-up seat mix assumptions, we see \~\$12 in ARPU uplift once fully reflected across the installed base, translating to an approximate +\$4 / +\$8 / +\$12 ARPU benefit across FY27/FY28/FY29. With the initial pricing increase now behind us, we expect this pricing tailwind to begin flowing into reported results throughout FY27, providing an incremental driver of M365 Commercial Cloud ARPU expansion alongside continued E5/E7 mix shift, Copilot adoption, and expanding AI consumption.

Our CIO Survey Continues to Reinforce Broadening Commercial Cloud Monetization. Our latest 2Q26 CIO Survey continues to reinforce improving Commercial Cloud monetization across multiple growth drivers. First, Microsoft

365 spending intentions improved meaningfully, with 65% of CIOs expecting to increase spending over the next 12 months, up from 55% in our prior survey. Second, premium SKU adoption remains healthy, with 71% of organizations expected to leverage E5 or E7 over the next year, supporting continued ARPU expansion beyond traditional seat growth. Third, M365 Copilot remains Microsoft's flagship AI application, with 88% of CIOs expecting to leverage the product over the next 12 months while planned deployment continues to move higher. Beyond M365 Copilot, adoption expectations also improved across GitHub Copilot, Azure AI Services, Copilot Studio and Azure AI Foundry, reinforcing our view that Microsoft's AI monetization opportunity increasingly extends beyond a single application into a broader enterprise AI platform.

## 3) What is the Capex Trajectory – Does Consensus Capex Need to Move Higher? MS View: Consensus Capex Estimates Could Continue Moving Higher Toward Our Forecasts

The Capex Debate: Street Estimates Still Have Room to Move Higher. We believe consensus FY27 capex estimates remain conservative relative to Microsoft's long-term AI infrastructure buildout and could move higher (mostly in 2HFY27), though unlike prior quarters, we are not currently expecting to materially revise our own forecasts following F4Q26 results (see Exhibit 16). Our model already incorporates a more aggressive multi-year investment cadence than consensus, reflecting management's latest guidance around sustained AI infrastructure investments, continued capacity expansion (mgmt. planning to double total DC footprint over next two years) and a greater mix of short-lived assets. Importantly, on the F3Q26 earnings call, CFO Amy Hood indicated that Microsoft expects \~\$190bn of total capex in CY26, including \~\$25bn of higher component pricing, while reiterating that the company expects to remain capacity constrained through 2026 despite these investments. Further, management noted roughly two-thirds of capex continues to be allocated toward short-lived assets, primarily GPUs and CPUs, reinforcing the direct link between infrastructure investments and future revenue growth. Consistent with this framework, our estimates already imply Microsoft modestly increases quarterly capex sequentially through the F2H27 from \~\$64bn capex estimate for F2Q27. We believe the more likely outcome from here is for Street estimates to move toward our forecasts, rather than our own forecasts moving materially higher post the F4Q26 print.

Our Latest Capex-Implied AI Revenue Analysis: Our capex assumptions have consistently been revised higher. Therefore, we lean on our capex driven forecast model to yield our sustained bullish conclusions about the trajectory of Azure growth, which continues to suggest significant upside to our forward Azure forecasts, despite recently raising our Azure revenue estimates with our recent deep dive. Our capex model sizes the implied contribution from Azure AI based on the capital expenditures dedicated to AI-related initiatives. We compare the capex-implied Azure AI forecasts, assuming various gross margin levels, with the Azure AI estimates in our MS model. As highlighted on the most recent earnings call, and reiterated at our recent campus visit, capex (and corresponding infrastructure) is not just allocated for new Azure demand. Microsoft must support its 1st party applications, internal R&D/Research efforts, internal training, and a replacement capex cycle. Management has also been vocal for some time that a significant portion of capex is for longer duration assets like land and buildings, which could generate revenue for >15 years. Net, in our latest analysis, Azure AI (excluding revenue share) gross margins could already exceed 20%. We still see material upside to our current Azure estimates based on our Capex-Implied framework, reinforcing our view that Microsoft's long-term AI monetization opportunity remains underappreciated.

## 4) What Should Investors Expect for FY27 Guidance?

Looking back (see Exhibit 21) to prior F4Qs, Microsoft has selectively given forward looking commentary across revenue growth, operating income growth, opex growth, operating margins, COGs, capex and/or effective tax rate into the forward fiscal year. Additionally, in F3Qs, the street has also received forward FY commentary on many of these categories, with a reiteration of this initial look given on the FQ4 call with potentially a couple incremental categories, consistently including effective tax rate. In F3Q26, management provided a broader high-level framework for FY27 than in the prior year, including commentary around double-digit revenue and operating income growth, mid- to high-single-digit operating expense growth, continued AI infrastructure investment, the expectation for \~\$190bn of CY26 capex (with a greater mix of short-lived assets), and Azure growth acceleration into C2H26. With much of that framework now already established, we think it is possible the F4Q26 call focuses more on reiterating those expectations rather than introducing materially new forward-looking guidance. We do think it is likely we receive initial commentary pointing to FY27 operating margins 'down slightly' or 'down 1%' YoY, though we see a path to margin expansion in FY27 actual results.
