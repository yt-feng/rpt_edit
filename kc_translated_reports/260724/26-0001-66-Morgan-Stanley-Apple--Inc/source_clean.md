# F3Q26 Earnings Preview – The Market Has Pre-Priced in a Solid June Q/Sept Guide


We expect a small June Q beat and a Sept Q guide that implies rev/EPS upside but margin downside vs. Street, driven by iPhone sell-in and pricing increases, but COGS inflation. Services growth and margin guidance are critical this Q. With shares now at 32x P/E, this setup feels largely priced in.

## Key Takeaways

June-quarter iPhone revenue should modestly exceed consensus forecasts (+22% Y/Y), driven by channel replenishment and 19% Y/Y shipment growth.

\- Sept Q setup is more mixed – Street still under-appreciates Product pricing upside, and therefore we expect a rev guide higher...

... but our September-quarter gross margin of 46.8% (vs. 47.4% consensus) implies a headline GM guide lower (though in line with buyside).

Also, we're 50-130bps below Street on Services growth in June/Sept quarter, embedding conservatism given App Store deceleration.

\- Remain constructive on 12mo view (\$364 PT), but tactically don't expect much ST follow-through post-EPS unless our Services/GM expects are too conservative.

Apple's fundamentals remain very solid, but with shares trading near all-time highs, the tactical setup into earnings is neutral/tougher as it requires zero blemishes across the board. We continue to believe that Apple fundamentals are very strong, with myriad price hikes likely to drive upside to revenue and EPS over the next 6-18 months. Case in point, our new FY27 revenue and EPS of \$563B and \$10.39, respectively, are \~1% higher than our prior forecasts and 7-8% higher than consensus, as we incorporate higher ASPs across the board, partially offset by modestly lower units. However, shares are now trading at 32x our FY27 EPS (and 34x consensus FY27 EPS), just a few turns below Apple's valuation peak, suggesting the market generally has similar elevated expectations. Heading into F3Q earnings next week, we expect June-quarter results and September-quarter guidance to reflect this dynamic – above Street revenue growth, but with mixed margins, though still leading to healthy EPS upside vs. consensus. However, there are 2 areas where we see some near-term risk, namely: (1) June-quarter Services growth, where we are 130bps below consensus due to weak App Store results; and (2) September-quarter gross margin guidance, where we are 60bps below consensus due to a more

First, strong June-quarter iPhone shipments suggest Apple replenished channel inventory, supporting a healthy iPhone backdrop. We expect June-quarter iPhone revenue of more than \$54B (+22% Y/Y), driven by iPhone shipments growing 19% Y/Y as Apple executed a planned channel fill. Recall that Apple exited the March quarter with "very lean channel inventory." Combined with softer China iPhone sell-through during the quarter, we believe channel inventory returned to a healthier level exiting June. Looking into C2H, our supply chain checks have not pointed to any meaningful changes in iPhone builds despite recent headlines suggesting potential cuts, and China sell-through in MTD July has improved meaningfully to double-digit Y/Y growth (Exhibit 1). For the September quarter, iPhone builds of 54M units (+4% Q/Q) are tracking below the typical seasonal increase of +26% Q/Q, which we attribute to: (1) a well-telegraphed slower ramp of the foldable iPhone; and (2) a staggered iPhone launch schedule (Exhibit 2). However, builds still imply >58M iPhone shipments in the September quarter, generally in line with our slightly reduced 58.5M forecast.

Meanwhile, our checks continue to support a largely unchanged CY26 iPhone production outlook, though we modestly reduce shipments to reflect greater pricing-related demand elasticity. Our latest supply chain work continues to point to roughly 265M CY26 iPhone builds, largely unchanged from prior checks, and we have not seen evidence of lower wafer orders at TSMC. That said, Apple typically communicates supplier plans based on a "bull case" demand scenario and often refines orders as early as mid-to-late October once post-launch sell-through trends become visible. Incorporating a more significant pricing increase for the iPhone 18 into our model (\$200 like-for-like starting price increase vs. our prior assumption of \$100), we lower our CY26 iPhone shipment forecast slightly to \~250M units from 252M – below the current consensus estimate of 255M ( Exhibit 3 ), but raise our ASPs by 3%. In our view, consensus in the September quarter – and overall for CY26 – does not appear to fully reflect: (1) a more meaningful like-for-like pricing increase for the iPhone 18; and/or (2) a staggered iPhone launch schedule, with 3 higher-end models launching this September, and 3 lower-end models launching next March. This means that our September-quarter iPhone revenue forecast is well above (8%) Street expectations, but is below Street in December (-5%), as we incorporate lower units, but higher ASPs.

Second, Mac upside appears capped by supply constraints, creating modest downside to our unit estimates but little impact on overall results. Mac lead times lengthened during the June quarter as demand outpaced supply, particularly for Mac mini, Mac Studio, and MacBook Neo, largely due to limited availability of advanced-node SoCs. We therefore reduce our June- and September-quarter Mac shipment forecasts by 11% and 8%, respectively, to reflect both supply bottlenecks and some demand elasticity following Apple's late-June price increases (Exhibit 4). Even so, our checks continue to indicate Apple is ramping MacBook Neo production into C2H26, with output potentially reaching 8-10M units (vs. the original plan of 6-8M). After incorporating higher Mac pricing (along with iPad pricing increases of \$100-150), our September-quarter Mac revenue estimate moves 1% higher despite lower shipment assumptions, reflecting Mac demand elasticity of 0.8 vs. \~1.0 for iPad.

We remain conservative on June-quarter Services growth, but September-quarter growth should find some support from recent pricing actions. We forecast June-quarter Services revenue growth of 13.3% Y/Y, below both the Street at 14.6% and Apple's implied guidance of roughly +13.8% Y/Y ("in line" with March-quarter growth of 16.3% after adjusting for a 2.5-point favorable FX; Exhibit 5). The primary driver is softer App Store performance, with developer revenue up 3.2% Y/Y, representing roughly 460bps of deceleration from the March quarter. That said, slowing App Store growth has been a recurring theme in recent quarters, yet Apple has consistently met or exceeded overall Services guidance. Our tracker also points to an FX benefit largely in line with guidance (+1.1% Y/Y vs. guidance of +1.0%), while Apple recently raised iCloud+ pricing in select international markets, citing FX impacts. Taken together, we leave our June-quarter Services estimate largely unchanged, and see downside risk to consensus.

Looking ahead, we expect September-quarter Services growth to remain resilient despite further App Store deceleration. We forecast Services revenue growth of 12.7% Y/Y, representing a 60bp slowdown from June and roughly 50bps below consensus at 13.2% Y/Y. While App Store trends continue to soften – July MTD developer revenue is tracking at just +1.0% Y/Y, according to Sensor Tower – we estimate that Apple's recent Apple Music price increases (\$1 for individual/student plans and \$3 for family plans) should contribute roughly 1 point of incremental Services growth. As a result, we expect only a 60bp deceleration in September despite a roughly 180bp tougher Y/Y comparison from June, though also remain below Street here as well.

## Finally, we expect the bulk of the debate this quarter to rest with the

September-quarter gross margin guide. We don't see much risk to Apple's June-quarter gross margin and expect results likely come in between the 48% midpoint and 48.5% high-end of the guidance range. This is generally a well-agreed-upon view. However, we do see downside risk to Street September-quarter gross margins – we're at 46.8%, while Street is at 47.4%. Higher component costs, lower low-cost component inventory, and limited price hike protection (primarily on iPhone) serve as the basis for our more conservative Sept Q forecast, which implies that while

historically September-quarter Products gross margins are up 130bps Q/Q, our September '26 quarter Products gross margins fall 70bps Q/Q. Again, we believe our forecasts are largely in line with buyside expectations, but given how critical gross margin are to the Apple debate, a headline margin guide below consensus creates tactical risk for the earnings setup, even if revenue and EPS outperform.

## OVERWEIGHT THESIS

With the most elongated iPhone replacement cycles, new AI features rolling out around the world, and a renewed focus on device form factor changes, we believe Apple can accelerate iPhone growth starting in CY26, with replacement cycles peaking as aged installed base starts to upgrade. When combined with consistent, double digit services growth and moderate operating leverage, we believe Apple can earn \$8.86 in FY26 and \$10.39 by FY27. Memory cost dynamic could create uncertainties in the near term. Longer-term, investments in AI, payments, cloud, health, and home, and long runway to grow spend per user from \$1/day today are key arguments for sustained long-term growth and value creation.
