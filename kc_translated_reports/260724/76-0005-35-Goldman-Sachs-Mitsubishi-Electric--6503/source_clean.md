# Mitsubishi Electric (6503.T): Announces AI sensor JV with Sony; to boost sensor space for physical AI; aligns with FA digital strategy; Buy

Mitsubishi Electric announced on July 22 that it has signed a strategic collaboration agreement with Sony Semiconductor Solutions, a subsidiary of Sony Group (covered by analyst Minami Munakata), which includes the establishment of a joint venture, Advanced Vision Solutions Co., Ltd., to provide AI vision sensor solutions for the manufacturing industry (link). Operations are scheduled to begin in October 2026 (investment ratio: Mitsubishi Electric 60%, Sony Semiconductor Solutions 40%).

Mitsubishi Electric has been driving the digitalization of its FA business, and it has considered the sensor domain to be a missing piece in its strategy. Through the JV, the company aims to develop vision sensor solutions that leverage AI-based analysis of visual data on the image sensor, and enable real-time sensing of the status and positioning of products and components on manufacturing lines. As such, we believe this represents a significant move toward filling in Mitsubishi Electric's strategic gap. Mitsubishi Electric acquired Nozomi Networks in September 2025, opening the way for it to obtain factory-wide data. Integrating these various data streams has the potential to broaden the value it can provide to customers. We look for concrete products and solutions to be presented in the future that will contribute to earnings. We are Buy rated.

## Main contents of the announcement

On July 22, Mitsubishi Electric and Sony Semiconductor Solutions announced the signing of a strategic collaboration agreement, which includes the establishment of a joint venture.

The new company will be named Advanced Vision Solutions Co., Ltd. and is scheduled to begin operations in October 2026.

The investment ratio will be 60% for Mitsubishi Electric and 40% for Sony Semiconductor Solutions. The new company will be consolidated into Mitsubishi Electric.

The new company will develop and deploy vision sensor solutions that utilize cutting-edge technology, enabling AI-based analysis of visual data directly on the image sensor.

By utilizing this sensor, manufacturers will be able to monitor and control production floor conditions, product quality, equipment operating status, and maintenance information without manual intervention, leading to labor savings

and autonomous operations.

According to Sony, there is significant scope for the deployment of image sensors and edge AI, centering on smart factories, against the backdrop of labor shortages stemming from the declining birthrate and aging population. It is in this context that it chose to partner with Mitsubishi Electric, with its established strength in the FA domain.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

## M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
