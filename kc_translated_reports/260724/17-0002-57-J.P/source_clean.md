## Korea Shipbuilding
## Investor Feedback Ahead of 2Q26 Earnings

Korea shipbuilding sentiment remains cautious following a sharp share price correction after the Canada submarine contract loss, leaving investors focused less on near-term upside and more on downside protection and “what can re-rate the group”. Into results, we expect the debate to center on (1) whether core commercial margins can hold up as input costs and wage negotiations evolve, (2) whether engine capacity/backlog constraints are easing (especially at HHI) and what that implies for delivery cadence and order timing, and (3) whether any “option values” (navy, AI data-center engine exposure, FDC) can become tangible enough to be reflected in valuation rather than remaining only as a narrative.

\- Share performance and net buying: Over the past month, shares corrected heavily after the Canada submarine order loss (Hanwha Ocean -34%; HHI -31%; KSOE -15%, Samsung Heavy -21% vs. KOSPI -25%). Foreigners net bought HHI while net selling all other names, with a more constructive tone on potential AIDC engine value-unlocking. Retail investors net bought Hanwha Ocean and Samsung Heavy, while local institutions net bought KSOE.

\- Earnings estimate changes: Hanwha Ocean witnessed the biggest estimate change for 2Q26 OP, up $9\%$ vs. 1Q26 earnings release, HHI/KSOE 2Q OP estimates were mildly lifted by $2\% / 1\%$ , while Samsung Heavy saw 2Q earnings being cut by $8\%$ , largely in-line with our view for the quarter. Negotiations with labor unions and sub-contractors are under way and should be crucial for 2027E/2028E earnings outlook. Another key watch will be Hanwha Ocean/HHI's commercial segment OPM level in 2Q, and guidance on room for further expansion.

\- Investor feedback: Investor sentiment remains negative with a limited near-term catalyst path. More questions are being directed toward identifying the “best short” rather than a high-conviction long. Current valuation (order book at 0.5–0.6x vs. peak \~1.0x) implies mid-cycle expectations and little-to-no premium for option values (navy, AI DC engine, FDC). Investors are watching for an HHI engine capacity addition announcement as the nearest potential sector catalyst. On navy, investors remain reluctant to ascribe value without clearer order visibility and/or tangible legislative changes enabling U.S. battleships to be built in Korea.

\- Key things to watch during 2Q26 earnings: 2Q earnings should again be framed by the core equation of mix/pricing vs. input costs vs/ FX, but with greater attention on negatives - labor negotiations and cost inflation management while protecting margin guidance into 2H. More important catalysts will be for HHI on whether engine capacity/backlog constraints are easing and what tha implies for delivery schedules and order win timeline and for Samsung Heavy on FDC updates and details, while listening for a clean read-through on LNG/LNGC demand and pricing that can improve visibility beyond the near-term margin print.
