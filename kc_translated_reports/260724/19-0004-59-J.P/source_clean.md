# JPM
# Semiconductor/Tech Materials

We visited investors in Asia during the week of July 13. They remained bullish on fundamentals, and discussions centered on where share prices will bottom and identifying relatively resilient stocks. As at the time of our June visits to US and European investors (details here), our focus stocks are, in order, Kioxia (285A; Overweight; covered jointly with Jay Kwon), Tokyo Electron (8035; Overweight), Advantest (6857; Overweight) and SCREEN Holdings (7735; Overweight), Nittobo (3110; Overweight) and JX Advanced Metals (5016; Overweight; covered jointly with Yasuhiro Nakada). Discussion focused on three points: (1) Kioxia's earnings outlook (pricing, long-term agreements [LTAs], and SLC); (2) the feasibility of price revisions at the front-end process companies; and (3) production capacity and pricing power, mainly among the materials names. Among individual names, interest was high in the above stocks plus Disco's (6146; Neutral) growth outlook, Lasertec's (6920; Neutral) order trends, and Rigaku Holdings' (268A; Overweight) medium-term X-ray outlook. Details are as follows.

\- WFE market: Our WFE market forecasts are \$159 billion (+28% YoY) in CY2026, \$205 billion (+29% YoY) in CY2027, and \$237 billion (+16% YoY) in CY2028 (see our June 17 report for details). However, many investors also discussed upside potential, citing (1) a further rise in TSMC's capex in its July 16 results; (2) expectations for higher DRAM-related capex; (3) hopes among some investors that Intel will raise capex at its results due July 24 (Japan time); and (4) expectations for Terafab. The mood was somewhat more subdued than during our June visits to US and European investors, though investors appeared to be looking for a scenario in which the WFE market reaches \$250 billion in CY2028.

\- Kioxia (285A; Overweight; covered jointly with Jay Kwon): The share price declined 32% (while TOPIX dropped by only 3%) during our trip, from ¥77,000 (July 12 close) to ¥52,110 (July 19 close), so discussion centered on the reasons for the decline and where the stock might bottom. The biggest concerns seemed to be (a) whether hyperscalers can sustain their sharp increase in spending, itself driven in part by surging memory prices; and (b) the possibility that the arrival and adoption of the low-cost Kimi K3 model could reduce KV cache demand. The former needs to be confirmed through companies' April-June results, but we think the latter concern eased substantially over the second half of the week given Jevons' paradox (efficiency improvements end up increasing overall consumption) (a proper assessment of the K3 model itself is also warranted, but we leave that aside here). Kimi in fact suspended new sign-ups on July 19 as surging demand for K3 pushed its GPU capacity close to full. On the numbers, investors were somewhat more bullish than our forecasts: they look for FY2027 EPS to clear ¥15,000 (our forecast: ¥14,149) and July-September pricing to be up 25-35% QoQ (our forecast: +20%). As of July 19, the stock had corrected to a P/E of around 3.5x based on our FY2027 estimates, and a growing number of investors appeared to expect a rebound. Detailed comments on LTAs are unlikely at Kioxia's results, but the near-term focus will likely be on hyperscaler capex and management's remarks on its Super High IOPS. \- Front-end process: Even as semiconductor-related stocks corrected broadly, comments on the front-end space overall stayed largely positive, with investors expecting it to benefit from upward WFE revisions. We continue to focus on Tokyo Electron (8035; Overweight) in particular. In addition to its strong presence in types of equipment likely to outpace the WFE market outlook (etchers, deposition, co-development for EUV, etc.), we think its high weighting for DRAM, where growth expectations are strong, is attractive. The company is also trying to improve profitability through price revisions, and we think the tendency for its price changes to be accepted, backed by yen-denominated sales, is a relative advantage. Expectations for higher prices at the front-end names, including Tokyo Electron, likely gained further support from two recent earnings calls: TSMC suggested on July 16 that rising semiconductor-equipment prices were one factor behind its higher capex, and ASML pointed to “more flexible pricing” on July 15.

\- Advantest (6857; Overweight): With the market continuing to favor front-end over back-end names, the share price also struggled to gain ground, weighed down by a lower market outlook for the increase in test times on next-generation GPUs and by concerns about share loss in ASICs. Bullish views still do not appear to dominate, but we maintain our bullish stance. In addition to a revised new/upgrade ratio (a higher ratio of new systems), we also expect opportunities for CPUs and CPOs to expand. In CPUs in particular, where test times are seen as shorter than for GPUs and ASICs, customers that have used in-house testers may shift to outsourcing. As chip structures grow more complex, we expect the company to keep delivering strong results. During the April-June results briefing, we will focus on the latest trends in CPU testers and whether management raises its SoC tester TAM outlook.

\- JX Advanced Metals (5016; Overweight; covered jointly with Yasuhiro Nakada), Nittobo (3110; Overweight): Few investors seemed bullish in the short term, but interest in both was high. At JX Advanced Metals, which announced on June 16 a plan to expand InP substrate capacity by 7-10x, investors were interested in (1) pricing trends and (2) the supply situation. On pricing, our base case had assumed a 2-3x increase, but many investors expected 3x or more. On supply, concerns about phosphorus procurement came up, which we see as a point to watch from April-June results. For Nittobo, discussion centered on concerns about easing supply-demand (intensifying competition) in T-glass. With the spread of multi-core (MLC) and AI-focused DDR potentially expanding the T-glass TAM further, we think Nittobo's stronghold in cutting-edge T-glass will not be easily eroded. We think it may soon announce a large investment to meet demand in FY2028 and beyond (our base case is at July-September earnings). Separately, management has already announced plans to triple T-glass production capacity by end-FY2027 compared with end-FY2023. We expect the announcement to include price hikes to cover the increased investment costs.
