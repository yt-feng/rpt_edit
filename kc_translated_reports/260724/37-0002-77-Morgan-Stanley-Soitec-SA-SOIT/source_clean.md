# The confirmation the market needed


Soitec's F1Q27 beat, guidance for more than $30\%$ F2Q27 growth and expectation for Photonics-SOI revenue to more than double in FY27, confirm the ramp investors had questioned. We move the shares to Top Pick and raise FY28-29 EPS by $43\%$ and $32\%$ .

## Key Takeaways

F1Q27 revenue of €113m beat the Street by 6%, while F2Q27 growth guidance of more than 30% compares with 4% consensus.

■ Photonics-SOI drives both beats, and management now expects FY27 revenue from the product to more than double.

We raise FY27-29 revenue by 8-19% and FY28-29 EPS by 43% and 32%, with almost €8 of FY29 EPS.

Beyond the estimates increase we expect the investor base to broaden as the investment case becomes more accessible on near term momentum.

Soitec moves to Top Pick as we maintain our our €200 PT, using 25x FY29 EPS versus 35x previously.

After yesterday's close, Soitec pre-announced F1Q27 revenue of €113m, 6% above the Street's €107m estimate. Revenue grew 23% y/y in the quarter vs. the company's 15% guide. For F2Q27, Soitec guided to more than 30% y/y growth, compared with 4% expected by the Street. Both beats came from higher Photonics-SOI demand for high-speed optical interconnect in AI data centres. The cherry on the pie was the addition by the company that “Photonics-SOI FY27 revenue is expected to more than double.” We believe this is the catalyst the market has been waiting for and double down on our OW call by moving Soitec to Top Pick.

As optical networking shares sold off, customer revenue targets and capacity plans moved higher. The optical networking sub-sector has been hit particularly hard during this summer's correction as AI capex and CPO (co-packaged optics) timelines were questioned. However, the announcements by companies in the sector told a different story. Soitec's announcement that it expects to "more than double" its Photonics-SOI revenue in FY27 is not surprising in this context but well ahead of market expectations (MSe: +60% y/y, quantitative consensus likely similar).

\- STMicroelectronics guided on 2 June to about \$1bn of data-centre revenue in 2026 and said it could double in 2027. In April, it had guided to nicely above \$500m and well above \$1bn, respectively. PIC100 entered 300mm high-volume manufacturing in March, and ST plans to more than quadruple capacity by 2027.

\- Our Greater China team's supply-chain work points to TSMC, covered by Charlie Chan, ramping photonic integrated circuits from 10kwpm in 2Q26 to 15kwpm in 4Q26 and at least 25kwpm in 2028. At about \$1,000 per wafer, 25kwpm represents roughly \$300m of annual revenue opportunity for Soitec.

We saw the signals but had not yet put them into our numbers. We were close to finalising work on the subject, but had not converted the customer announcements into higher revenue estimates. We appreciate that the market required evidence from Soitec directly to believe that it too could benefit to the same extent. Since our upgrade, a recurring question from investors has been why Soitec was growing only around 40-50% y/y in FY26 while Tower and GlobalFoundries (covered by Joe Moore) were doubling photonics revenue. The new FY27 outlook answers this question as it aligns Soitec more closely with customer demand signals.

This announcement provides the confirmation the market was looking for and works on multiple levels. Most importantly, it confirms that Soitec's near-term revenue trajectory is very strong and aligned with those customers. This should make the stock more accessible, as modelling becomes more straightforward. It also shows that Soitec's photonics revenue is not solely tied to the potential introduction of CPO, a common bear argument. However, we continue to believe that scale-up, even within hybrid multi-rack systems using NPO (near-packaged optics), represents a further 5-10x step-up in Soitec's potential addressable market, which is why we expect very strong growth of a multi-year period

Doubling revenue is the norm in photonics, and we do not expect that to be limited to this year. We expect a material increase in consensus expectations for Soitec's FY27, which ends in March and is therefore best compared with CY26. What the market may still miss is that growth is more likely to accelerate from here than decelerate. Customers such as Tower, GFS and STM have all pointed to multi-year growth trajectories. There is no obvious reason to assume a different path for Soitec, as our checks confirm that Soitec is (almost) always the sole supplier for 300mm wafers, where investment is focused.

We significantly raise our estimates. Our FY27-29 revenue estimates increase by 8-19%, driving earnings upgrades of 43% and 32% for FY28 and FY29, respectively. We now see almost €8 of FY29 earnings power, or €10 in our bull case. FY29 ends in March 2029 and therefore overlaps more closely with calendar 2028. We expect consensus to follow suit, as our EBITDA estimates were already 8% and 16% above the Street before this increase.

Soitec is our New Top Pick. We see no need to raise our PT, for now, given $108\%$ implied upside to yesterday's close. As our estimates move materially higher, we cut our target multiple to 25x FY29e earnings (broadly aligned to calendar 2028), from

35x previously to reflect lower sector valuations and investor pushback on paying a high multiple for outer-year forecasts. That perception could change as the optical networking opportunity becomes better understood. Photonics represented only 8% of Soitec revenue in FY25, but based on our interpretation of yesterday's update, it reaches around one-third of revenue in the current year. Concerns around RF-SOI will continue to linger, which we capture in our bear case, but at around 20% of FY27e revenue, it no longer defines the investment opportunity, in our view.
