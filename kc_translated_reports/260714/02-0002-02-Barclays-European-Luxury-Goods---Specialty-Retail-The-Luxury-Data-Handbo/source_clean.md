## Setting the scene: what are we expecting for Q2?

Looking at 2Q26, we expect our coverage to grow $4.7\%$ on an organic basis, slightly above BBG cons. at $4.3\%$ and accelerating vs 1Q26 at $3.5\%$ . Our estimates range from Richemont Jewellery Maisons at $16\%$ to Gucci at $-5\%$ , highlighting the still dominant polarisation in the sector. We are above consensus on Richemont (which we recently placed on catalyst alert ahead of the print) as we see no reason to expect a sequential deceleration vs calendar Q1. We are below consensus on Gucci, where we expect $-5\%$ organic growth in the second quarter, which, if materialising, would require Gucci to grow at least $6 - 7\%$ in the second half to achieve positive organic growth in the FY.

Talking about the rest of the year, current consensus numbers for 2Q26 imply a c.180bp acceleration vs the first half, with the strongest acceleration implied at Gucci (almost 1,000bp) which is also the main difference vs our implied H2 numbers (we expect 110bp acceleration at the coverage level, 670bp at Gucci). The strongest deceleration, on the other hand, is expected at Richemont Jewellery Maisons, but we think this might change as H2 numbers could be revised upwards should the company beat at its Q1 results.

## What is the data telling us on 2Q so far?

Looking at the data points on 2Q26 published so far, what emerges is a mixed picture across regions, with Korea and the US continuing to accelerate, while ME and domestic Chinese consumption look set to decelerate.

In Asia, we note a sequential slowdown in May on a number of indicators of domestic Chinese consumption, continuing the sequential slowdown we observed already in April. Chinese retail sales further decelerated into negative territory at -0.6% y/y in May, posting the first negative print in two years. By category, Jewellery retail sales in China remained strongly negative in May, down -8.6% y/y, as we were expecting, given the tariff-disrupted comps, but bounced back in HK. Hainan duty-free sales also slowed down to 0.4% y/y, and Macau gross gaming revenue for June fell into properly negative territory, down -12% y/y.

Korea's strength continues to be a popular topic with investors. As a reminder, we estimate that Korea accounts for 5-10% of sales depending on the company, with some (Moncler, Prada) more exposed than others. Korean department store sales for Luxury remained strong in May up 37% y/y, in line with April and above the last two years. This echoed company comments around continued strength in Korea, as well as indications from an expert call we recently hosted, which flagged significant sequential acceleration in Q2 and an outperformance of jewellery brands.

A recurring question in our conversations with investors was whether a sequential acceleration in Korea could be offset by a sequentially weaker Middle East, with the whole of Q2 being affected vs only March (albeit to a much greater extent) in Q1. The first aspect we would point out is that the answer will clearly differ company by company, based on exposures. Given the ME skew at Richemont and Hermes, a deceleration could be felt harder vs Prada or Moncler, which skew to Korea.

A second aspect relates to the sequential dynamics. While ME is arguably sequentially tougher for all players, the sequential acceleration in Korea might have different magnitudes depending on how strong the region was in Q1 already. We think, for example, that Moncler was already very strong in Q1 in Korea, being a key market for them, and would thus err on the cautious side vs the impact that would be implied by the delta in exposures. Similarly, we believe that Richemont will experience a larger sequential improvement in the country, more than offsetting the ME deceleration despite the delta in exposures.

## What are we keeping our eyes on?

## Price increases in the medium term

Following a number of sizeable global price increases between May and June, notably at Cartier watches, Tiffany, LV and Saint Laurent, our tracker did not flag any significant increases in June-July. We would note a small 1% global increase at Prada and a 2% one at Vacheron Constantin, along with regional FX rebalancing at Tiffany, Omega and Ferragamo.

While we monitor price increases at all brands on a monthly basis, we are keeping an especially close eye on Jewellery brands, which have increased prices at a much faster pace than soft luxury brands over the last year. While we think that this was likely driven by the rise in the gold price, which increased the perceived value of gold jewellery to the eyes of the consumer, excessive price taking from jewellery brands could erode the relative value attractiveness that jewellery has acquired since Covid, and could hinder the jewellery supercycle we are currently witnessing.

## Relative valuations

As already flagged in last month's edition, we continue to monitor the relative valuations of Hermes and Richemont, which have continued to diverge. When benchmarked against our coverage, Richemont has never been more expensive over the last ten years. The opposite is true for Hermes, which, excluding a short-lived dip in 2016, has never been cheaper relative to the sector over the last ten years (and on an absolute basis is the cheapest it's ever been over the same period).
