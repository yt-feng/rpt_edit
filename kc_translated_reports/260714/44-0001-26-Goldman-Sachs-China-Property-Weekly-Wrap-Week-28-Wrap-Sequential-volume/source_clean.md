# Week 28 Wrap - Sequential volume weakening with leading indicators trended lower
## Key highlights for the week:

Policy front: The State Council released the “15th Five-Year Plan for Expanding Consumption”. Notably, the plan emphasizes the sustained and healthy development of the real estate market as part of a broader mechanism to increase residents’ property income, shifting from previous notions that focused largely on stabilizing financial assets. Meanwhile, on durable goods consumption, the property-related policy framework remains largely aligned with the “Special Action Plan to Revitalize Consumption” released in March, with key priorities including increasing affordable housing and implementing city-specific policies to expand high-quality housing supply, accelerating the renovation of urban villages, dilapidated housing, and aging residential communities, supporting owner-led initiatives for “intelligent” and “aging-friendly” home improvements, as well as enhancing property management standards and regulating the development of rental markets; on policy frameworks, the plan calls for deepening reforms to the housing provident fund system, specifically expanding the scope of fund usage to better align with the increasingly diverse housing needs of depositors. That said, short-term

supportive measures referenced in March, such as using special-purpose bonds to finance buybacks of unsold completed properties and implementing property-related tax easing are not mentioned in the announced plan.

Market performance: Transaction volumes declined by -25%/-6% wow in primary/secondary, though MTD-July retained yoy improvements in both markets at +5%/+11% yoy, respectively. The sequentially slower transactions were mirrored by secondary visitations and secondary subscription sales (which leads registration-based sales by 1-2 weeks) respectively at -8%/-6% wow, and price appreciation expectations edged lower for both agents and home-sellers. On the brighter side, new home search activities stayed steady wow and new listing supply continued contraction at -9% wow (7% below June average and 18% below July-25 levels).
