# Chinese AI Models Catching Up, Vulnerabilities Accelerating

Adding TENB to Analyst Focus List, Upgrading QLYS to Neutral, CRWD and PANW Remain Well Positioned for Incremental Demand


[[KC_IMAGE_001]]


Chinese models are catching up; demand poised to accelerate. In our April note on Project Glasswing, we walked through Anthropic's frontier-AI vulnerability discovery launch and concluded the resilience factors across our preferred names remained intact, with CRWD and PANW best positioned as essential defensive layers rather than disruption targets. The picture has moved since. Open-weight Chinese AI models are closing the gap with frontier models in vulnerability discovery capabilities. Meanwhile, we already see evidence of vulnerability acceleration, illustrated by CVEs tracked for the first quarter of the year (Figure 1). This comes at a time when enterprises are preparing to assess and manage a 'tsunami of vulnerabilities' expected across their IT estates, and the progress of the Chinese models likely accelerates levels of urgency. Accordingly, we are adding TENB to our Analyst Focus List as we view TENB as the best positioned exposure management vendor to help organizations face this threat. We are also upgrading QLYS from Underweight to Neutral as we think the company will also benefit across its core installed base, and we could see upside to growth expectations in spite of the execution issues that the company has been managing through. Outside of the exposure management vendors, we anticipate that the collapse of mean time to exploit will also accelerate demand across the rest of our coverage universe as the number of vulnerabilities (and likely exploits) accelerate. We continue to view consolidators that have effectively invested in their platforms as best positioned to benefit from incremental demand with PANW and CRWD being best positioned.

Tenable: Best positioned to capture acceleration of exposure management demand. We think an acceleration in vulnerability volumes will likely lift exposure management vendors with VM exposure, and we view TENB as best positioned to benefit. In addition to greater asset coverage under the TENB platform, acceleration of the vulnerability landscape could drive better new product adoption across the company's exposure management platform, in our view. Hexa AI went GA on May 20, 2026 inside Tenable One Foundation and Advanced and is not available to standalone VM customers, making the product a concrete reason in the cycle to upgrade off legacy scanning. Hexa is Claude-powered per the joint Anthropic-Tenable announcement, and the Exposure Data Fabric, built on two decades of platform telemetry, is the trusted-execution layer that lets the agent act without operational disruption. Roughly 30% of customers are on Tenable One today, leaving two-thirds of a 40K-plus customer footprint as upsell runway, with 2,000-plus customers at over \$100k in ACV anchoring the migration economics. Tenable One was 41% of new business in 1Q26 with ASP uplifts in the 6% to 60% range, and Hexa's agentic engine resolves non-CVE exposures, where Tenable's own telemetry attributes 63% of breach entry points despite their making up only 37% of findings. TENB trades at 12.8x EV/FCF on CY27 against an FY29 framework targeting HSD/LDD revenue growth, 28% operating margin, 31% unlevered FCF margin, and Rule of 40, a multiple that does not yet reflect the category re-rate. We see the setup as favorable and the risk-reward asymmetric. We are adding Tenable to the Focus List with a Dec 2027 price target of \$40.

Qualys: A rising tide could lift this boat too, in spite of execution challenges. The same vulnerability explosion that lifts the VM category lifts QLYS off the UW floor, even with execution lagging. The company is a Project Glasswing and Daybreak participant, runs over 200mm agents worldwide, and has shipped a multi-agent platform with Agent Val GA for autonomous patch orchestration, Agent Nyra for threat-informed prioritization, Agent Sid for exposure and asset context, Agent Sara for risk elimination, and ROCky as the conversational layer on top of the Risk Operations Center. Agent Grant is emerging as an identity play, with the company citing "over 74% of breaches" involving a compromised identity. The ROC has been operational with the customer base for over a year and is the 100% sales focus for 2026. The margin profile offers valuation support: adjusted EBITDA margin of 47% has held flat from 2022 through 1Q26, Rule of 40 is 57%, and FCF margin is 45% against a peer-median EBITDA margin of 26%. However, bookings mix has been a headwind. ETM and CSAM are 11% of LTM bookings, up only 3 points in two years, with VMDR still 50% of mix, and the absence of a multi-year financial framework leaves the platform-value question unanswered. Until that mix shifts and a long-term framework is disclosed, we think QLYS captures less of the vulnerability tailwind than Tenable. We are moving Qualys to Neutral from Underweight, and establish a Dec 2027 price target of \$139.

Share consolidators with favorable AI exposure; CRWD and PANW remain Overweight. We continue to read the Mythos moment as a structural shift, and CRWD and PANW are the cleanest beneficiaries, with the demand-side acceleration running through vendors that were already winning on coalition standing, platform consolidation, and AI-native execution. CRWD is the only vendor with standing across all three frontier-AI security coalitions, as a Glasswing launch partner, an OpenAI Daybreak partner, and the founder of Project QuiltWorks. AIDR has emerged as the new platform pillar, with management indicating the market could prove larger than EDR because it covers seven attack surfaces against EDR's one. The company cited "\$2.5tn+ in forecasted worldwide AI spending against only a low single-digit percentage of organizations with an advanced AI security strategy" as the gap AIDR converts. Falcon is the runtime defender for AI infrastructure across GPUs, neoclouds, token factories, and agentic applications. PANW was another of the first 12 named Glasswing partners, which our channel work indicates was an initial CIO procurement filter as enterprises shortlisted vendors. The data flywheel can help close the false-positive and last-mile gaps frontier models alone cannot solve, and Mythos-related consulting revenue is already accelerating for GSIs. Cortex XSIAM remains the consolidation engine, with Cyberark and Chronosphere extending the platform into identity and observability. We remain Overweight on both, with an upward bias to estimates from here.

# Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks

Qualys (Neutral; Price Target: \$139.00)

## Investment Thesis

We believe that top-line growth for Qualys is currently reliant on existing customer expansion rather than new customer additions, and consequently view the detection and remediation product adjacencies as key to successful revenue expansion beyond core VA/VM offerings. However, we remain cautious as valuation remains elevated for a company with single-digit growth, contracting margins, and declining FCF. We see better risk/reward elsewhere in our coverage.

## Valuation

We are establishing a Dec 2027 price target of \$139, which is derived from a 13x EV/CY27E FCF multiple (FCF of \$276mm for CY27), a substantial discount to our low growth coverage, which we believe is warranted due to execution risk.

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

(+) Accelerated adoption of Cloud Agent Platform, traction with platform approach, rate of SMB market penetration. (−) Sales execution headwinds, slower bookings mix shift toward ETM/CSAM, margin contraction from accelerating S&M investment.

# Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks

Tenable (Overweight; Price Target: \$40.00)

## Investment Thesis

We believe Tenable's growth expectations are reasonable and see potential for upside as the company continues to execute as a best-of-breed exposure management provider. Looking at the demand environment as a whole, we believe there remain plenty of greenfield opportunities as enterprises shift towards adoption of formal VA/VM programs in an elevated threat environment.

## Valuation

We are establishing a Dec 2027 price target of \$40, which is derived from a 14x EV/CY27E FCF multiple (FCF of \$306.6mm for CY27) and a 3.7x EV/Sales multiple (sales of \$1,168.2mm), a substantial discount to our low growth coverage, which reflects the company’s exposure to lower growth market segments, namely VM.

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

(-) Federal exposure, exposure to lower priority spend, new management team.
