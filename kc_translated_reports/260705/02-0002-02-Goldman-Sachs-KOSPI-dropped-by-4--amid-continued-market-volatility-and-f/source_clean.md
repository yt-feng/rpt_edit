# KOSPI dropped by $4\%$ amid continued market volatility and foreign outflows; fP/E fell to the lowest level since the G.F.C

KOSPI dropped by 4% amid continued market volatility and foreign outflows while NTM P/E fell to the lowest level since the G.F.C. The Banking, Securities and Leisure sectors outperformed this week, while Tech, Insurance and Retail sectors underperformed the most (Exhibit 8).

■ Foreign investors continued to sell the KOSPI market, driven by outflows for KOSPI Tech (Exhibit 35).

KOSPI 12m-forward EPS was revised up by $+4.8\%$ . The Leisure sector saw the strongest upward earnings revisions, while the Chemicals sector was revised down the most this week (Exhibit 22).

The KRW strengthened $0.3\%$ vs. USD this week. However, it weakened by $0.2\%$ vs. both JPY and EUR.

The latest Korea Equity Risk Barometer (GSSRKERB Index) is at -1.5, falling into a risk-adverse territory.

## Charts of the Week: KOSPI Sell-off Pulse Check

KOSPI Sell-off Pulse Check: Amid continued market volatility partially caused by Meta's news, KOSPI 12m forward P/E hit the lowest level since the G.F.C amid the recent market volatility. Even assuming significant earnings downgrades (-33%) and valuation at historical earnings trough, KOSPI has upside potential versus the current level. Earnings upgrades have continued this week despite the market weakness and earnings have been upgraded broadly across most sectors. In the meantime, from a market technical perspective, RSI Index for KOSPI has also fallen to the lowest level since the end of March, 2026 as KOSPI has broken below 50day moving average. The latest Korea Equity Risk Barometer (GSSRKERB Index) is at -1.5, falling into a risk-adverse territory. Our Korea equity risk barometer is a contrarian indicator especially under the conditions where significant market moves have already taken place; it indicates more constructive equity market returns on a forward-looking basis.
