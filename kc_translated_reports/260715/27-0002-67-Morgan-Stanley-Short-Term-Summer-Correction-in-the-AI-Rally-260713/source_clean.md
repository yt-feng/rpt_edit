# Short Term Summer Correction in the AI Rally

We do not believe the medium-term AI rally has ended. However, deteriorating foreign investor flows and summer position adjustments are likely to sustain near-term reversal pressure. While we do not recommend fully exiting AI-related positions, we tactically favor domestic demand value stocks.

## Key Takeaways

The correction since late June may reflect a combination of seasonality and position adjustments.

■ Foreign investors bought a net ¥10trn of Japanese cash equities in 1H26, so the pullback could be meaningful.

We do not believe the AI rally has ended, but we should be mindful of a near-term momentum reversal.

Tactically, we prefer domestic demand value stocks, which tend to be relatively resilient to higher interest rates.

## Short term summer correction in the AI rally

The AI rally has been correcting since late June. We believe the recent correction reflects the combination of retracement following strong foreign investor buying and momentum gains through June, together with the seasonal pattern of summer flows. While correction pressure may persist in the near term, we do not believe the AI rally itself has ended (see Exploring the Turning Point of the AI Rally (6 Jul 2026)).

The most notable seasonal factor between July and September is foreign investor flow. Foreign investor cash equity flows exhibit a clear seasonal pattern, with net selling typically occurring during August and September, followed by net buying in October (Exhibit 1 and Exhibit 2). Consistent with this pattern, equity indices also tend to underperform in August (Exhibit 3).

Looking more closely at foreign investor cash equity flows on a weekly basis, outflows have historically tended to occur from late August through the end of September (Exhibit 4), a period of roughly six weeks.

This year, following a historically strong rally, it is reasonable to assume investors have accelerated profit taking and position adjustments. Foreign investors purchased a net ¥10trn of Japanese cash equities during the first six months of 2026 (Exhibit 4), and the resulting retracement is unlikely to be insignificant.

From a factor perspective, there is normally little notable seasonality during Jul-Sep (Exhibit 5. August composite momentum appears weak, but this is partly driven by a small number of outliers). However, foreign investors currently appear to have high exposures to AI and semiconductor-related stocks and, from a factor standpoint, to momentum, growth, large-cap, and high-volatility. Consequently, any foreign investor position adjustment would likely trigger a reversal in these factors (Exhibit 6).

That said, we view this as a temporary correction driven by seasonality and position adjustments. Even during the dot-com bubble, technology stocks did not rise uninterrupted. While it would be inappropriate to mechanically apply the experience of that period, it is worth noting that performance stalled between July and September 1999 despite the broader bubble-driven advance (Exhibit 8). Accordingly, we expect the AI rally to resume following the short-term correction. In terms of timing, we expect around early September, given that foreign investor position adjustments appear to be occurring earlier than usual this year.

As such, we do not believe investors necessarily need to unwind their medium-term positions in AI and semiconductor-related stocks. From a factor perspective, we maintain our constructive medium-term view on the Revision factor. At the same time, as a tactical allocation to weather weaker summer foreign investor flows and a reversal in momentum, we prefer domestic demand value stocks. Unlike the broader market, which includes foreign demand-oriented stocks, the value factor has continued to work within the domestic demand universe throughout 2026 (Exhibit 9). In addition, the recent rise in interest rates (Exhibit 10) should be relatively supportive for shorter-duration value stocks.
