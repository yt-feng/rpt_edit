# The Cardiology Renaissance: Post CARDIO-TTRansform, two top-of-mind catalysts remain for 3Q

Following the surprise failure of AZN/IONS' CARDIO-TTRansform, a highly-watched event for the ATTR-cardiomyopathy field, and subsequent volatility (AZN -10%, IONS -33%, ALNY -11%, BBIO +6% from the July 8 close, before the announcement), we look to two remaining key cardiovascular disease (CVD) catalysts likely in 3Q that could result in significant stock reactions and inform the opportunity for a number of novel approaches:

1. NVS/IONS' HORIZON trial of pelacarsen to read through to the Lp(a) class. Investor expectations appear low given the aforementioned delays, Lp(a) as an unproven target, and NVS' commentary - per our statistical analysis, significance would be achievable in HORIZON's overall population and the high-risk subgroup on a 12% and 14% risk reduction, respectively. Nonetheless, we believe a trend in efficacy would provide positive readthrough for the field, and if HORIZON is successful, we expect commercial uptake to remain debated in the context of Lp(a) testing rates/payor dynamics, noting the emerging longer-acting or oral options (e.g., AMGN with data in 2027/28, LLY, among others).

2. NVO' ZEUS study of ziltivekimab (IL-6), which could validate that inflammation reduction as measured through lowering of high sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP), translates to a reduction to CV risk reduction in ASCVD - a 15%-20% benefit would read favorably to BIOA's oral NLRP3 inhibitor with proof-of-concept data in YE.

We recognize there could be heightened caution into these events as they are perceived by investors as higher-risk than CARDIO-TTRansform. Within, we frame the set-up, including benchmarks for success, stock moves in the illustrative bull/bear scenarios - we calculate +2%/-3% and +7%/-5% for NVS and NVO, respectively - alongside options commentary, potential sympathy reactions from affected companies (e.g., IONS, AMGN, NAMS, BIOA), and broader CV catalysts in 2H26/2027.

Broadly, we remain positive on AMGN, ALNY and MRK as names best positioned to experience upside from large CV product cycles.

## Framing two top-of-mind cardiology catalysts

Recall, AZN and IONS announced that the Phase 3 CARDIO-TTRansform study (n=1,432) of antisense oligonucleotide Wainua in ATTR-cardiomyopathy (CM) missed its primary endpoint of the composite outcome of cardiovascular (CV) mortality and recurrent CV clinical events assessed up to week 140 – while there was a nominally significant hazard ratio of 0.71 in the monotherapy arm, there was no evidence of benefit in combination with stabilizer therapy (e.g., PFE's tafamidis), and tafamidis-treated patients performed better-than-expected in the milder population.

The update was a negative surprise, as success on the primary endpoint was expected per our investor discussions and the key debate centered on whether the stabilizer combination group - 57% of patients with an additional 24% drop-in rate - would achieve significance. We await full results at the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Congress in August.

After the CARDIO-TTRansform news, significant stock moves transpired for AZN (-10%), IONS (-33%), and affected companies ALNY (-11%) and BBIO (+6%). On the forward, we look to two other Phase 3 cardiovascular catalysts this quarter that could result in volatility for the subject companies as well as wide-reaching effects for the field: (1) NVS/IONS' HORIZON trial of Lp(a)-targeting pelacarsen; and (2) NVO's ZEUS assessing ziltivekimab (IL-6). Below, we provide the set-up into the catalysts, including benchmarks for success, readthrough to other affected companies, our implied up/down moves, and options commentary.

## NVS/IONS: HORIZON - pelacarsen (Lp(a)) in cardiovascular disease

Background/Market Opportunity. We see Lp(a)-targeting drugs as a potentially transformative, representing perhaps the last known significant cardiovascular target that has not yet been drugged despite being a risk factor for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) that impacts \~20% of the general population (\~1.4 billion people globally, 60mn+ people in the US) - see our deep dive here. Emerging Lp(a) directed therapies (see below) have reduced Lp(a) by up to \~80-90%+ in prior trials, albeit the potential causal relationship of lowering Lp(a) to improve CV outcomes is awaiting validation per topline data from NVS/IONS' HORIZON trial evaluating pelacarsen likely in 3Q (per NVS' guidance of an early 2H release). For pelacarsen, we model for \$2bn peak sales at a 60% PoS in our NVS model. We note key debates into HORIZON'S data have included:

☐ The translation of Lp(a) lowering to CV risk reduction;

☐ Whether greater Lp(a) reduction drives superior CV outcomes, which would benefit AMGN;

☐ The commercial outlook and readthrough to other studies if the primary endpoint is achieved only in the high-risk subgroup OF horizon and not the overall;

☐ The lack of benefit on high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, an inflammatory marker; and

□ AMGN's and NVS' trial design and baseline characteristics, particularly the isolation of Lp(a) via the optimal treatment of other risk factors (e.g., LDL-C), which is logical from a regulatory/payor perspective but likely impacted event rates.

Catalyst outlook. The Ph3 HORIZON trial of monthly subcutaneous antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) pelacarsen vs. placebo is the first large-scale (n=8,323) pivotal study evaluating the effect of lowering Lp(a) on cardiovascular risk reduction (on expanded MACE; cardiovascular death, non-fatal MI, non-fatal stroke and urgent coronary re-vascularization requiring hospitalization) - thus representing a key catalyst for the field. NVS is testing the primary endpoint in the overall population (Lp(a) ≥70mg/dL) and the high-risk subgroup (Lp(a)≥90mg/dL), where the events-driven study has >90% power to detect a hazard ratio (HR) of approximately 0.80 and 0.75, respectively.

Our analysis of the trial statistics suggests that the $\geq70mg/dL$ could achieve significance with a 12% benefit and the $\geq90mg/dL$ significance could be hit with a 14% benefit, noting management anticipates achieving statistical significance in the 13-15% range and believes that while such a range might be considered modest for LDL lowering, it could be meaningful for Lp(a). That said, uptake is a key area of debate, including Lp(a) testing rates and emerging competition with longer-acting and oral options – NVS is also investigating its next-generation, long acting DII235, which could move to Ph3 if proof-of-concept is achieved with pelacarsen in HORIZON.

■ Options commentary. Assuming that the HARBOR trial for del-desiran and the REMODEL trials for remibrutinib in MS are expected to come in late September/ early October (base don clinicaltrials.gov primary completions), the options market (Sept expiry) is pricing in a $+/-9.2\%$ move for Novartis into HORIZON.

## NVO: ZEUS - ziltivekimab (IL-6) in cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease and systemic inflammation

Background/ Market Opportunity. Ziltivekimab is a humanized mAb to IL-6, which in turn is often a key factor in inflammatory diseases. The primary readout from the Phase 3 ZEUS trial of ziltivekimab in ASCVD is expected by management in 3Q26.

Catalyst outlook. ZEUS is a Phase 3, randomized, quadruple-masked 48 month study of ziltivekimab vs placebo (1:1 randomisation) in 6,200 patients with ASCVD (atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease). Given there are no previous studies of ziltivekimab in cardiovascular indications, it is difficult to set an efficacy bar for success. However, in our experience, the industry standard for a cardiovascular medication to be deemed useful is showing a 15-20% efficacy benefit. In the ZEUS trial, this would translate to a 15-20% increase in the time to first occurrence of 3-point Major Adverse Cardiovascular Event (MACE). However, based on our calculations, we expect that a 12% MACE benefit should be enough to show statistical significance in the trial.

We currently model ziltivekimab across all indications at a 40% PoS, with launch in 2028E and rising to peak risk-adjusted US sales of c.DKK 10.5bn/\$1.6bn in 2037E. Adjusting the PoS to 100% would increase our peak sales estimate to c.DKK 26.2bn/\$4bn, and imply c.7% upside potential to our DCF valuation; 0% PoS would imply c.5% downside risk to our DCF valuation.

Options commentary. With the trial expected to come in 3Q'26 (Sept expiry), the options market is pricing in a $+/-14.3\%$ move for the ZEUS trial for Novo shares.

\- Affected companies.

☐ Novo: As noted above, our illustrative analysis implies +7%/-5% impact on the shares, all else equal, on success/failure of the ZEUS trial. In addition, and perhaps more importantly, a successful ZEUS trial could give Novo an anchor asset for its obesity/diabetes/CV adjacent portfolio, which could be a first step towards diversifying the portfolio/reducing some of the concentration risk that comes with the long term semagltuide patent expirations.

BIOA: BIOA is advancing its oral, small molecule NLRP3 inhibitor BGE-102 in cardiovascular risk, following positive Ph1 MAD data which demonstrated rapid (within 2-3 weeks) and competitive (86%) reduction in hsCRP, a marker of CV inflammation, in patients with obesity and baseline inflammation. IL-6 is downstream to NLRP3 in the inflammasome pathway, and we expect 15-20% efficacy benefit on 3-point MACE to read favorably BIOA's approach in CV risk, further validating hsCRP as a surrogate for MACE post NVS' Ph3 CANTOS data for canakinumab (IL-1β) which demonstrated compelling MACE benefit in a post-hoc analysis of patients who achieved hsCRP normalization (<2mg/L). BIOA is running a Ph2 proof-of-concept study for BGE-102 in obese patients with elevated CV risk, assessing % reduction in hsCRP from baseline as the primary endpoint, with data anticipated in YE.

□ LLY: In March, LLY announced the completion of the acquisition of Ventyx Biosciences, where the deal was announced post positive Ph2 data for oral, once-daily NLRP3 inhibitor VTX3232 in patients with obesity and CV risk

factors. VTX3232 demonstrated 78% reduction in hsCRP vs. 3% increase in placebo at week 12, with 69% patients achieving hsCRP normalization. Additionally, in combination with semaglutide, VTYX reported significant reduction in liver inflammation. Similar to BIOA, we expect a 15-20% 3-point MACE benefit to read-through favorably to VTYX's approach in CV risk.

☐ NOVN: Back in October 2025, Novartis closed the acquisition of Tourmaline Bio for \$1.4bn, which brought in pacibekitug, an IL-6 antibody with quarterly dosing intervals (which led to hsCRP reductions of 86%). We model \$2bn sales in 2037, which we include at a 40% PoS, following launch in 2031. If ZEUS is negative, leading to the market losing confidence in the IL-6 thesis, and moving to 0% PoS, implies a c.2% negative impact on our DCF. Conversely, a positive result, leading to greater confidence in the IL-6 thesis, and moving to a c.80% PoS, implies c.2% addition to our DCF.
