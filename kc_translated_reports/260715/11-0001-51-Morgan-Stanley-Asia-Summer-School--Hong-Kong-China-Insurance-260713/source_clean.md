## Asia Summer School 2026

1. Industry Growth Potential

## 1.1 Solid household wealth growth; insurance could outperform

\- China's household financial assets reached Rmb320tn as of 1Q26 and have delivered double-digit growth over the past decade, despite Covid-related disruptions and a weaker macro backdrop.

\- Insurance products remain the only principal-guaranteed financial products in China that also offer reasonable yield and value-added services. As a result, insurance has continued to gain share within household financial assets in recent years.

\- We expect this trend to persist, with household financial assets growing at an 8% CAGR over 2024-30E to reach Rmb435tn by 2030E, versus Rmb310tn in 2025. We believe insurance products are well positioned to capture a growing share of incremental household savings.

## 1.2 Inelastic and high demand for senior care

\- The percentage of China's population aged 65+ reached 16% in 2025, and this will accelerate when the over 300mn people born in the 1970-80s reach retirement age in the next 5-10 years.

\- We see significant demand for prudent financial management, wealth succession and senior care & healthcare demand.

\- We believe insurers are the best play to leverage off this dynamic, as there are few players servicing the sector and they consolidate market resources and bundle product offerings to meet this demand.

## 1.3 Rising demand for premium healthcare services

\- China's government has undergone healthcare insurance reform, which introduced standardized medical insurance, and launched commercial insurance directories.

\- We think insurers can play a more important role in personal health expenditure, and provide customers with better access to new drugs and premium healthcare services.

2. Key Industry Trends

## 2.1 Insurers have largely completed shift to par products over the past two years

\- Declining long-term interest rates in recent years, together with regulatory guidance, have driven China insurers' product strategy shift from traditional to participating products.

\- Listed insurers have accelerated par product roll-outs since 2H24 and largely completed the transition from traditional to participating products across both agency and banca channels in 2026.

\- In 1Q26, most listed insurers recorded a \~90% par product mix in regular FYP (first-year premiums), up from <50% in 1H25.

## 2.1 Insurers have largely completed shift to par products over the past two years

\- Most unlisted insurers have delivered similar trends. They posted positive YoY operating cash flow growth in 1Q26 with an \~11% median.

\- We see a material increase in participating products' contribution to operating cash flow in 1Q26 vs. 1Q25 across most names, making par products a key driver of liability-side growth and near-term cash flow improvement.

\- However, it remains debatable whether par products are the optimal offering in a de-risking environment, esp. for smaller players. We expect product strategies to become increasingly differentiated going forward.

## 2.2 Effective action has been taken to address interest rate spread loss risks

\- With continued declining pricing interest rates and further expense restriction, new inflows' guaranteed liability cost has begun to fall meaningfully across insurers.

\- High-quality insurers had already started to see a downward inflection point in liability costs of in-force business by end-2025, while for more companies, such inflection points may not emerge until 2026-27 or later.

\- Yield of new assets is notably higher in 2026, vs. 2025 due to stable interest rate, while insurers' NIY is still declining.

\- We expect the interest spread to remain under pressure over the next 2-3 years, but believe the industry is approaching an inflection point.

## 2.3 Clear opportunities in banca channel, with large insurers maintaining their edge

\- Banca rebounded with $>15\%$ new premium growth in 2025 after a brief adjustment due to tighter expense regulation (“Bao Xing He Yi”) in late 2023 and early 2024.

\- At the industry level, channel structures have diverged between large insurers and mid-to-small players, with SME insurers relying heavily on the banca channel.

\- Most insurers with Rmb10bn-Rmb1tn assets derive 50-100% of gross premiums from banca, while insurers with >Rmb1tn assets maintain a more balanced mix – the agency channel contributing \~60-80%

## 2.3 Clear opportunities in banca channel, with large insurers maintaining their edge

## 2.4 Gradually stabilized long-end interest rate and steeper yield curve

\- China's 10Y government bond yield rebounded to $\sim 1.8\%$ in 2H25 after bottoming at $\sim 1.6\%$ in early 2025 and has remained broadly stable at this level in 2026 YTD.

\- The yield curve has steepened, with long-end rates (30-year) rising more notably over the past year, which should prove more supportive for insurers over the longer term.

\- Listed insurers' overall bond allocation slightly declined in 2025 while government bond allocation increased.

## 2.5 Notably higher impact from equity volatility under IFRS 9

\- As most non-listed insurers also adopted IFRS 9 from early 2026, equity asset classification and its impact on reported earnings have increased significantly.

\- Leading insurers have materially raised equity allocations to \~15%-20% of total invested assets (including equities and funds), with limited room for further increases, in our view. However, equity investments can continue to grow alongside the expansion of overall insurance funds.

\- We see divergent allocation strategies across insurers, including high-dividend, growth-oriented, and blended approaches. While FVOCI equity exposure differs meaningfully, insurers with lower FVOCI allocations still show an incentive to gradually increase exposure to high-dividend stocks over time.

\- Earnings could be highly sensitive to equity market movements if there is high FVTPL stock allocation.

## 2.6 Listed insurers have increased equity allocations, esp. in par accounts under IFRS 9

\- Different investment strategy in par and traditional accounts – the former could be more aggressive.

• Over the past three years, we have seen higher FVTPL allocations in par accounts (i.e., more assets held for trading gains, including equities, bonds, and others), while overall FVTPL allocations have remained relatively stable.

\- Trends in par accounts diverge from non-par accounts: Ping An has maintained a broadly stable FVOCI equity mix in par accounts, while CPIC Life and China Life have continued to reduce this mix (indicating higher FVOCI equity allocations in non-par accounts). ## 2.7 Leading insurers are exploring innovative and alternative asset investment

\- Listed insurers' net investment yields have continuously declined over the past few years.

\- In 1Q26, industry median investment was around 0.65% while median comprehensive investment yield was only about 0.31%

\- Leading insurers are adopting more diversified allocation strategies and have expanded into a broader range of asset classes, including ABS, public and private REITs, and private equity funds.

\- SME insurers have obstacles in these investments, and many focus on equity investment amid this backdrop.

## 2.8 Solvency remains key constraint on insurers' ALM

\- The full rollout of the C-ROSS Phase II framework has tightened capital requirements. Solvency ratio pressure could continue to weigh on some insurers' asset allocation and liability growth.

• We see limited likelihood of launching C-ROSS Phase III in 2026, with implementation more likely in 2027.

\- Lag effect of interest rates could continue to weigh on capital in 2026, while we believe large insurers could maintain the advantage - regulators have continued to ease solvency rules and risk factors in recent years, and business quality continues to improve.

3. HK Life Insurance Market

## 3.1 HK market sales trends – continued strong momentum

\- HK saw APE at HK\$35.4bn in 4Q25, up 21% y-y, moderating from a strong 2Q and 3Q (+57% and +52% y-y). This still marks a historical high 4Q (over the past decade) despite continued regulation tightening in the year (see page 23 for details).

\- MCV growth at double digits remains strong, and we expect the onshore and offshore contribution mix to remain relatively stable.

\- Even considering the tighter regulations in HK's insurance market since 2025 and amid concerns on a cross-broker clampdown on MCV business, we expect HK to maintain its robust premium growth this year.

## 3.2 Regulatory movement in the Hong Kong market - stricter oversight but not a ban

\- Regulatory tightening in Hong Kong has mainly focused on high projected returns, elevated upfront commissions, and irregular mainland Chinese customer referral practices.

\- We do not see a risk of MCV business being suspended, although tighter compliance and more standardized operating practices will likely be required.
