# Kingmed (603882.SS): 1H26 prelim NP beat; Launching new equity incentive plan; Buy

Kingmed released its 1H26 preliminary results on July 13, 2026. Its net profit ranged from Rmb170mn to 220mn (vs -85mn net loss in 1H25), which beat GSe (Rmb156mn). Per the company, the better-than-expected earnings were mainly driven by continued cost control and progress in digital transformation, which helped improve operating efficiency. On the same day, the company announced its 2026 equity incentive scheme. Full vesting requires revenue growth of no less than 6%/14.5%/26% in 2026/27/28 versus the 2025 base, implying annual revenue growth of c.6%/8%/10%, respectively. The corresponding net profit excluding non-recurring items targets are Rmb390mn/500mn/600mn.

We believe the incentive targets have likely already factored in the potential decline in testing volumes following the implementation of DRG 3.0, as well as possible price reductions arising from the nationwide standardization of medical service pricing. On the profit side, however, the company expects to mitigate the impact through tighter cost and expense control, including passing part of the cost pressure upstream to suppliers, expanding its laboratory center network, and further improving operational efficiency through digitalization and intelligent management. As such, management does not expect these policy headwinds to have a material impact on earnings. Based on this prelim results as well as the equity incentive plan, we have revised our 26-28E estimates. Maintain Buy.

## Investment thesis

Kingmed is the leading player in China's ICL (independent clinical laboratory) industry. While investor concerns have focused on post-Covid margin compression and accounts receivable risks, we believe industry competition is now easing as supply-side rationalization accelerates under tighter compliance and DRG/DIP reforms. Despite near-term weakness in high-end testing, Kingmed is well positioned to offset this headwind through rising outsourcing penetration and market share gains from industry consolidation. Our proprietary adjusted margin indicator suggests normalized profitability is stabilizing and approaching an inflection point. With improving earnings visibility and valuation still reasonable relative to the recovery outlook, we are Buy rated on Kingmed. Key events to watch: 1) pace of industry consolidation and share gains; 2) trajectory of margin recovery; 3) progress in accounts receivable collection.

## Price Target Risks & Methodology

We are Buy-rated on Kingmed with a 12-month target price of Rmb36. Our valuation is based on a 5-year exit P/E methodology with: 1) a discount rate of 9.5%; 2) global peers P/E at 21.8x. Key risks: 1) Slower-than-expected recovery in industry outsourcing demand; 2) Prolonged weakness in high-end test demand; 3) Intensified pricing pressure under VBP and local procurement policies; 4) Slower pace of industry consolidation.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

## M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
