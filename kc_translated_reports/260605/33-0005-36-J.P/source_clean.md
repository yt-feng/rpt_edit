# Clean Energy

We attended the CleanPower 2026 Conference in Houston, Texas this week and spoke with several renewable developers, EPCs, and equipment manufacturers. Key themes from the conference included: 1) generational levels of power demand driven by AI, industrialization and electrification, 2) continued momentum in battery energy storage, 3) the increasing role of robotics in EPC and O&M, and 4) continued industry consolidation towards the best in class companies. We came away from the conference positive on the outlook for utility scale solar & storage growth in 2H 2026 and beyond. The outlook for wind remains relatively murky given what some view to be market saturation of the technology as well as federal permitting and political challenges. Against this backdrop, we believe top picks NXT and MTZ remain well positioned, and view Overweight-rated PWR and MWH as additional beneficiaries of the same demand and consolidation dynamics.

\- Demand backdrop remains profoundly robust. Practically every conversation at the conference began with talk about the unbelievable amount of power demand in the market, with most expressing conviction that solar and wind will be just as impactful as fossil fuels in meeting new generation needs. However, the economic and practical feasibility of pairing solar + storage with dispatchable resources to directly meet behind-the-meter needs of a hyperscaler data center remains a point of debate amongst the industry, with many pointing to the need for continued advancements in energy storage technology before renewables can fully meet the reliability requirements of data centers. Nevertheless, multiple industry participants pointed to insatiable demand for renewable generation from hyperscalers, even if the mega projects in question are not directly powering data centers. Developers we spoke with noted that interconnection queues — while still congested — are increasingly populated by high-quality, creditworthy offtakers with long-dated power purchase agreements, a meaningful shift from the speculative queue composition of prior project development cycles. Texas, California, and Arizona remain the hottest markets for solar & storage projects of scale.

\- Momentum in battery energy storage is real. Storage is increasingly being co-developed and co-located with solar as a default configuration rather than an optional add-on. Conference participants noted that standalone storage projects are currently being underwritten very favorably on a merchant basis in key markets including ERCOT, CAISO, and PJM, driven by widening energy arbitrage spreads and capacity market revenues. BESS for data center backup and load management was a hot topic, particularly in light of the recently announced FLNC partnership with Nvidia and Siemens. However, the overall size of the data center driven BESS market remains a key question for many conference participants, including storage-focused developers who described the current levels of data center BESS demand as “off the charts”. \- Robotics is becoming increasingly important for EPC & O&M. Skilled labor shortages for both EPC and O&M are pushing developers and contractors to adopt robotic solutions to fill the gap. We spoke with several robotics companies at the conference, which specialize in automated panel cleaning, aerial drone inspection, vegetation management, cable management and solar panel installation. Many of the technologies seem to now have reached levels of significant maturity and industry adoption, with many solutions citing double-digit GWs deployed or under management through their solutions. Robotic panel cleaning and drone-based thermal inspection now seem to be fairly standard practice on utility-scale projects above a certain size threshold. Additionally, case studies from the conference show AI-enabled monitoring platforms combined with autonomous inspection robotics can significantly compress O&M cost per megawatt-hour in ways that are beginning to show up meaningfully in project-level returns.

\- Scale and balance sheet matter more than ever. The combination of interconnection complexity, equipment procurement scale, and the capital intensity of co-located solar-plus-storage is creating a natural selection dynamic that favors larger, better-capitalized operators. Smaller developers without established utility relationships, permitted pipelines, or access to low-cost capital are finding it increasingly difficult to compete on project economics. This dynamic is being further reinforced by hyperscalers, who are increasingly directing their procurement toward large, creditworthy counterparties capable of delivering gigawatt-scale pipelines with execution certainty. Project sizes are also trending materially larger across the board, which compounds this advantage. Utility-scale projects that were once underwritten at 100-200 MW are increasingly being developed at 500 MW to 1+ GW, a scale that demands sophisticated EPC capabilities, deep equipment manufacturer relationships, and the procurement leverage to secure favorable pricing and delivery commitments.
