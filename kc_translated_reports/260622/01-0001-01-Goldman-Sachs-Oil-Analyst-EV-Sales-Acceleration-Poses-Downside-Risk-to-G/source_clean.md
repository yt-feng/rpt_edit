# EV Sales Acceleration Poses Downside Risk to Global Oil Demand

\- Our new nowcast $^{2}$ shows a broad-based acceleration in EV car sales as a percent of total car sales (i.e., seasonally adjusted EV penetration) since February:

☐ Global EV car sales penetration has increased by 3.4pp and reached its all-time high last month of 26.1%, excluding September 2025 (when US EV sales surged in advance of a tax credit expiry) (Exhibit 1).

☐ China has experienced the largest gains, with the EV penetration rate up 11.4pp (Exhibit 2).

☐ Of the 15 largest EV markets, 12 have experienced an increase in EV penetration (Exhibit 4).

We estimate the potential hit to global oil demand in December 2027 from the cumulative increase in EV sales under two scenarios $^{3}$ .

☐ We apply our rule-of-thumb that every 1 million shift in sales from ICE to EV cars $^{4}$ lowers road oil demand by 30kb/d in the US and 20kb/d elsewhere.

☐ Under the “Temporary Acceleration” scenario, which assumes that regional EV penetration rates remain flat at their May 2026 levels, we estimate a

global demand hit of 0.13mb/d (Exhibit 5).

☐ Under the “Persistent Acceleration” scenario, which assumes that regional EV penetration rates grow linearly according to their February 2026 – May 2026 trends (through August 2026 before gradually slowing for China, and through December 2027 for world ex China), we estimate a demand hit of 0.32mb/d (Exhibit 5).

This acceleration in global EV car sales suggests our downside oil price scenario is plausible (although potentially more persistent constraints on Hormuz flows pose significant upside price risks, too). In this downside scenario, global oil demand experiences a 1.5mb/d Hormuz-related persistent loss (vs 0.5mb/d in our base case), and Brent declines to the mid-\$50s/bbl in late 2027 (with supply beats also weighing on prices). Higher global EV passenger car sales is just one channel via which the Hormuz shock may weigh on long-term oil demand, even if oil products prices moderate further, for the following reasons:

☐ Sales vs Stock: We are only considering additions to the stock of passenger cars, but substitution is already occurring within the existing stock in response to high fuel prices $^{5}$ , as evidenced by the just over 20% year-over-year drop in China gasoline and related products sales volumes and the surge in EV charging volumes. $^{6}$

☐ Beyond Passenger Cars: We omit from this analysis all non-passenger car EVs, and the around 55% of oil demand not related to road fuels (e.g., petrochemicals, for which coal might be an alternative). Most notably, two-/three-wheeler EVs comprise a majority of total EV sales in India (92% in 2025), Vietnam (80%), and China (35%) and can displace a sizable one-third to one-half of the fuel that a passenger car EV can.
