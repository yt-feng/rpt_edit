# China Internet: Is bad news now... good news?

A bounce... or something more? In this note we outline some latest thoughts on the China Internet sector, taking stock of the latest developments and data points across the industry, recent discussions with the large caps we cover, and investor feedback we'd heard in a few dozen meetings with investors on the road in North America.

If it can't get worse... We started 2026 with a cautious top-down view. Some concerns on macro and regulation persist, though compared with December, investor perceptions and sector valuations have caught down in recent months. Some of the narratives we'd heard on AI costs (e.g. unconstrained inference costs even while agentic AI engagement remains low) had become misinformed and overly negative. At the risk of sounding wishful (consumption remains anemic, but non-growth in Q2 was at least partly influenced by high 6.18 comps), we do wonder if the NBS' willingness to report below-target GDP growth might precede a period of less repressive macro policy.

Is AI capex good again? The combination of Tencent's Hy3 release and Alicloud growth remaining on track (plus e-commerce profits proving better than feared) likely catalysed the recent bounce in sector sentiment. For Tencent, Xiaowei feedback and the timing of the next Hy4 pre-train represent the next data points to watch. Alibaba moving T-head into its Cloud segment felt a little cute, but should contribute to revenue growth. Chip sales to neocloud businesses (who then lease back compute?) feels circular, but are probably necessary on the path to a possible spin-off T-head IPO.

Beyond tokenmaxxing... the next leg of AI adoption. More cost-efficient use of AI should ultimately be bullish for adoption. Open source progress is bullish for everyone... except closed source AI labs rushing to IPO. That said, the transition between the top 1% of power users spending heavily, and more broad-based adoption, bears monitoring. In contrast with Western markets where investors have largely abandoned hope for consumer AI monetisation, trillions of RMB of Weixin Mini Program GMV represent the obvious opportunity for Tencent, with monetisation happening via merchants.

Beneficiaries of cheaper, more capable AI. Google Gemini using Capcom to justify the usefulness of its AI product felt like a useful data point in the gaming disruption debate. We remain of the view that AI solves for production, but not IP credibility or multiplayer network effects. Some give-back is likely in NetEase's shares after Sea of Remnants launches, but low H2 comps, higher buybacks, likely southbound inclusion, and eventual Ananta news remains an attractive catalyst runway in our view. AI enabling better matching should help Boss Zhipin improve ARPJ and enterprise ARPU.

Tactical stock thoughts. Tencent remains modestly valued in our view. The “all-clear” for shorter-term investors will likely require operating profit revisions to bottom, a blockbuster new game (Delta Force continues to perform strongly), or perhaps Hy4 to truly settle the AI debate. But we doubt the stock stays on 11-12x forward PE as these things play out. We like NetEase, and JD and BZ feel too cheap to us, given double-digit profit growth. Meanwhile, we won’t have been the only ones to notice the seasonal trade in Alibaba’s shares into the Apsara cloud conference… or the value of its CXMT and Kimi stakes.

## INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS


Tactically, the set-up for the next few months feels constructive to us. Q2 reporting should be somewhat de-risked at this point. Tencent's Xiaowei roll-out will be gradual, but early feedback has been solid, and it's not hard to see how the functionality can dovetail with agent2agent agreements signed with the main Android smartphone makers. The full Hy3 release has helped to improve perceptions of the company's model-building credentials. For Alibaba, the decision to move Freshippo and T-head into China E-commerce and Cloud segments should help to boost reported growth in these segments, on top of ongoing capex and potentially more price increases for compute supporting the latter. The CXMT IPO and Apsara cloud conference line the catalyst runway in the next few months.

Elsewhere, some give-back is likely in NetEase's shares after Sea of Remnants launches (the domestic mobile game launches Jul 23), but low H2 revenue comps, accelerating buybacks, southbound inclusion, and eventual Ananta news flow remains an attractive path forward in our view. We continue to like JD and Boss Zhipin too - trading on mid-single digit forward PE multiples (the latter 4x ex-cash and investments) in contrast with double-digit earnings growth.

## DETAILS

## TAKING STOCK OF THE CHINA INTERNET SECTOR


Fundamentally, some of the discourse around AI spend (e.g. the idea the platforms will need to sponsor inference cost ad infinitum) had become extreme in our view. The debate around AI capex and ROI will continue, but with Xiaowei (Weixin agent) now rolling out to users, and Alibaba's Apsara cloud conference on the horizon, we're hopeful the debate will at least partly shift to what can go right.

## AI inference costs only scale if transactions and GMV follow

Aside from the ongoing global debate about the magnitude of AI capex, and the ROI that results, one of the core bearish arguments we've heard related to Tencent and Alibaba has centred around earnings revisions, and the risk the AI roll-out for these companies means they'll need to carry significant losses related to inference costs - while consumer AI monetization remains minimal.

Relative to operating cash flow, Tencent's capex (10.9% of LTM revenue) remains modestly lower than Alibaba levels (12.3% on the same basis). Compared on the basis of gross profit and operating cash flow, Tencent's capacity for capex raises remains far greater. Going forward, both Tencent's cash flow headroom and management commentary point to sequential capex increases in the next few quarters. The debate on long-term ROIC won't be settled anytime soon. Near term though we note Alicloud saw revenue accelerate to the mid-40% range in the June quarter, while low-teens margins likely implied 35-40% incremental cash margin. Alibaba signaled for compute price increases to continue in the rest of the year.

On inference costs, some spend is likely, we'd note that chatbot-style conversations typically consume hundreds of tokens, while agentic transactions require tens of thousands. Said differently, Tencent's token consumption only goes parabolic if agentic transaction volume and GMV take off. A time lag is likely between engagement and monetisation growth, but history suggests the latter is a matter of when not if. Alibaba notably now calls Qwen app engagement "less of a priority". That puts the billions of RMB spent on bubble tea subsidies into perspective... but more focused capital allocation is probably a good thing.

## Food delivery losses are coming down faster than expected, but...

Overall, we thought Alibaba's tone on the June quarter sounded largely in-line with expectations, with the notable exception of quick commerce where losses are clearly coming down faster than we and the Street had modeled. We'd previously argued (and we think most buyside investors had anticipated) that Alibaba was likely to cut spend faster than guided.

Overall though we stand by our view that e-commerce, food delivery, and quick commerce had become a single market where online penetration had reached fairly high levels, and competition is likely to remain fierce and largely fungible. While non-growth in retail consumption in May was likely a function of high 6.18 comps from 2025, our 2.5% growth trend-line remains base case for retail consumption SAAR growth. In Q2, NBS data pointed to restaurant industry revenues reaching 12.9% of aggregate retail consumption, close to the 13% long-term average.

6-7x forward PE is probably a low-water mark even for e-commerce valuations. But even more generous multiples leave us with mixed feelings on Meituan upside after the rebound in recent weeks... especially if one assumes that LongCat 2.0 was closer to the beginning than the end of Meituan's AI investments (the ongoing will-they-won't-they in the Middle East is presumably not great for business growth, though lower order volumes likely still imply lower losses).

Reports that China's CSRC has approved a Shein IPO in Hong Kong might refocus investors on the question of what Temu should be worth - we don't begrudge tactical investors for trying to see the bright side. But PDD management's continued disinterest in engaging with investors remains the bigger issue for longer-term focused investors. What is the sum of discounted future cash flows for a stock, when management consistently refuse to entertain the concept of capital returns?

## Further thoughts on Tencent's AI efforts

In recent weeks, the combination of Hy3 showing impressive gains from the April preview - and Xiaowei starting to be rolled out for testing - has driven a bounce in Tencent sentiment. Our recent meetings with investors in North America made us feel these developments remain under-appreciated among folks further from home. On the other hand, our observation that the company's Hy and Weixin teams had continued to work separately struck a nerve among investors.

In our discussions with the company, management noted that the decision to keep the work streams separate reflected the desire to avoid interdependence, and the risk that delays on one front might propagate to the other. While Hy3 may now be coming out of what's been a retooling phase, the Weixin team was able to leverage models at the China frontier (e.g. DeepSeek). Management pointed to Yuanbao and more recently Workbuddy engagement (8-9mn monthly users, compared with Z.ai management noting there were 8-10mn software engineers in China) as sources of user feedback for the Hy3 team... and noted that the smaller WeLM model should help to keep inference spend in check as consumer engagement ramps up.

Our updated base case is that over time the degree of co-design between the Hy and Weixin teams does ultimately increase, as Hy4 and subsequent models deliver stronger performance. Applying the “average” China AI lab of a 2.0-2.5x increase in parameter scale to the Hy3 team would imply a Hy4 preview landing sometime around year-end. In general our sense is that most things Tencent hopes to achieve within Weixin are relatively straightforward by broader agentic AI standards… meaning the in-house model becomes “good enough” relatively quickly.

This may be premature, but it increasingly feels like the makings of a coherent AI strategy are starting to come into view, consisting of a light WeLM model serving as the orchestration service for Mini Programs and various other parts of Weixin, and inference compute delegated to the edge over time. Recent innovations like 1-bit quantizations of Bonsai 27B and Tencent's Hy3 model strike us as important but easy to miss developments related to the latter.

## Broader stock views and tactical perspectives

Our sense is that Tencent's low valuation reflects a collection of concerns that are sensible in the moment (e.g. gaming slowdown given the lack of an obvious “next big game”, AI investments driving operating expense headwinds)... but are ultimately temporary. It’s easier to say now after a 15% bounce that 12x trailing PE in June was too low, and had perhaps priced in fears of perpetual non-growth in earnings. As some of these headwinds dissipate we’d expect Tencent’s shares to re-rate back near its post-2021 average in the 17-18x forward PE range. New game announcements are hard to preempt, but over long horizons we’d expect Tencent to outperform a consolidating video gaming industry. Delta Force and broader PC growth likely continued to significantly outperform mobile growth in Q2.

Some of Alibaba's recent business realignments struck us as... cute, but ultimately probably share price positive. The company pointed to further cloud growth acceleration in the September quarter, while moving Hema from All Other to China E-commerce Group also helps to boost earnings in the parts of Alibaba investors value in an SOTP. Selling T-head chips to neocloud customers and then leasing back compute should help to reduce Alicloud's reported capital intensity, while feeding the narrative of external chip sales revenues. We won't be the only ones who've noticed that the September Apsara cloud conference represented the high-water mark for AI sentiment in both 2024 and 2025... or that the $5\%$ or so of CXMT that Alibaba owns now stands to be worth a pretty penny.

After a period of meaningful relative outperformance (+15% versus KWEB -5% since NetEase beat Q1), we'd expect some give-back in NetEase's shares after the Sea of Remnants release (the mobile game releases domestically on Jul 23). But low H2 revenue comps (reflecting the slowdown in the company's deferred revenue recognition last year), accelerating buybacks, southbound inclusion, and eventual Ananta news flow remains an attractive catalyst runway in our view. Back-testing NetEase buybacks in the last 10 years points to a very high correlation between accelerating buybacks and subsequent share price performance.

## UPDATING OUR ESTIMATES

With this note we’ve updated our estimates for Tencent, Alibaba, and JD, to reflect recent discussions with the companies. Our Tencent estimate changes are modest and chiefly reflect slower top-line growth in Business Services revenues.

Our Alibaba estimate changes chiefly reflect the company's segment reporting adjustments, faster-than-expected loss reduction in quick commerce, and higher “All Other” losses in the June quarter. Overall our estimates for the company are modestly lower.

Our JD estimate changes chiefly reflect slower top-line growth, lower food delivery losses, and higher R&D spending. On balance, our bottom-line estimates are higher, reflecting the company's continued focus on profitability amid macro uncertainty.
