# Base Metals Tracker: Copper: Physical Tightness vs. Fading Macro

Copper's price action last week has captured the tension between tightening physical fundamentals and a less supportive macro backdrop. The LME copper price rose to a weekly high of around \$13,640/t as signs of physical tightening strengthened, with Chinese inventories drawing sharply and LME cancelled warrants rising. It then briefly fell towards \$13,400/t on Friday as broader risk-off sentiment intensified amid renewed Middle East tensions, more hawkish Fed repricing and Friday's sell-off in AI-linked chip stocks, before recovering to close at \$13,526/t on Friday.

\- Our price decomposition shows that the AI complex has become an increasingly important driver of copper prices (Exhibit 1), less because current datacenter copper demand is large in absolute terms (\~1% of total copper demand), and more because AI has become a key narrative shaping expectations for future power, grid and copper demand (Exhibit 2).

Against a weaker macro backdrop, China's physical market has continued to tighten. China visible copper cathode inventories fell to 167kt, leaving stocks at the bottom of their seasonal range (Exhibit 3). Typhoon Bavi-related stockpiling and delays to cathode arrivals amplified the latest draw, but the disruption hit a market that already had little inventory cushion.

We attribute the recent tightening mostly to substitution from scrap into cathode rather than to end demand. Tighter VAT enforcement on scrap in China has constrained domestic scrap circulation and kept secondary rod output unusually weak, shifting fabricators towards cathode-based rod (Exhibit 4). Concentrated smelter maintenance had also weighed on domestic cathode availability, with output falling month on month in June and remaining broadly flat YoY (Exhibit 5).

We expect the ex-US copper market to remain tight near term, with the continued US import pull on tariff expectations drawing units away from an already thin ex-US market, and low Chinese inventories and limited scrap substitution cushioning the downside. We see near-term downside risk to copper prices if the Middle East conflict escalates further and adds to inflation and rate hike concerns, though renewed Fed hikes are not our economists' base case.

## Methodology Notes for GS High Frequency Industrial Metals Indicators

1. Global copper inventory: US unreported stocks increase/decrease when monthly US seaborne cathode imports are over/under 50,000t, adjusted for the change in US exchange inventories. China copper smelter revenue chart:

2. Breakeven costs for a low-cost China smelter: TCRC weighted 50% Benchmark and 50% Spot in 2025. Import arbitrage is the difference in the price paid for all contained metal, less selling price, less VAT. We assume payable/recovery: copper 96%/98.5%, gold and silver 90%/98%.

3. China Growth Factor (ex-copper): The first principal component of PC1 of A- and H- shares, and PC1 of short- and long-term Chinese rates. The methodology is laid out in our cross asset strategists' note: https://publishing.gs.com/content/research/en/reports/2020/05/13/6a00d7af-7f5a-4a91-993f-41aa73963068.html.

4. Copper to aluminium switching: Switching index is derived from the value of low-voltage cable exports, relative to copper and aluminium prices. A switch to aluminium, is where we see a higher implied share of aluminium low-voltage cable in the export data. We show the absolute difference between copper and aluminium rather than the typical ratio of copper to aluminium, as our analysis suggests there is more sensitivity from buyers to the price difference rather than the price ratio.
