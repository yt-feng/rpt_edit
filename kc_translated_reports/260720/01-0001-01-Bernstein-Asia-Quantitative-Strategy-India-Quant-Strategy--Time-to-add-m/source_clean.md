# India Quant Strategy: Time to add more India exposure - The opportunity set widens

We remain neutral on India. However, with the momentum (tech/Al) trade under-pressure led by record high valuations, earnings expectations and crowding; we believe the case for India is improving. While the recovery remains selective rather than broad-based, the combination of cheaper valuations, emerging earnings support for large-caps and potential foreign flow normalization creates a more constructive outlook. We prefer large-caps over SMIDs and recommend adding momentum names while maintaining some high-quality defensive exposure; especially stocks where earnings momentum is improving.

India valuations have reset. Pockets of earnings support emerging: MSCI India has fallen from peak valuations of 23.2x fwd. PE in Q424 to 18.6x fwd. ie. below 10yr avg, after three years. India's premium to EM has fallen to 10yr levels on fwd. PE and at COVID lows on PB. Most sectors are now trading at or below 10yr average, with Financials and Tech trading near COVID-era PEs, creating a much better entry point. While an average stock is still seeing higher pace of downgrades, there are early signs of earnings recovery led by large-caps and certain sectors like Materials and Consumer. The long-term earnings growth expectations have also fallen to $10.6\%$ which is way below historical averages; indicating positive returns over the next 12m.

Return of foreign investor interest and the growing opportunity set: We believe the opportunity set has increased in India, even for global investors; who have been using India as a funding source for the regional AI/tech trade, resulting in record outflows of -US\$29bn. This has started to come back in July (+US\$1.8bn) and even active GEM funds have reduced their UW to -2.1% from -2.7%. Some sectors such as Staples, Tech, Energy, Real Estate present a trade due to record low crowding while above-average dispersion in Utilities, Real Estate, Tech and Materials is presenting active stock picking opportunities. Financials and Tech are extremely cheap while Materials and Consumer could find support from earnings recovery.

Factor positioning: We have been positive on quality since the beginning of the year and the style has been the best in 1H26, down -0.4% vs. market down -4.5%. India quality comes at a price but given earnings upgrades are intact and geopolitical uncertainty remains high, we believe keeping this defensive exposure on is prudent. Since April, we added more risk in India through momentum stocks, which are up 15% since then. We believe momentum rally in India is likely to strengthen from here given near record low valuations and inflection on earnings/crowding cycle. We also prefer large-cap exposure vs. SMIDs given very attractive valuations and better earnings revisions. Across the styles, we focus on stocks where earnings momentum trends have been showing an improvement in the last 6 months.

## DETAILS

Indian market has been under pressure since Q425 and in the first half of 2026, MSCI India is down -10% in dollar terms while KR was up 112% and TW 64%. Since June, as expected, momentum trend has started seeing a reversal in turn improving sentiment towards the anti-momentum/anti-AI market ie. India. Since April, we added more risk back in India, and we believe there are ample opportunities for investors to look at. We discuss them below.

## INDIA IS NOW CHEAP - FINANCIALS AND TECH NEAR COVID LOW MULTIPLES

Indian market has de-rated to below 10yr average valuations after 3yrs; now trading at 18.6x fwd. PE ie. -0.7SD below 10yr avg. On book-value it is still slightly above average levels at 3.3x PB. Relative to EM, valuations seem to have bottomed on 12m fwd. PE while on PB the relative valuation has now dropped to COVID lows. Most sectors are now trading at or below 10yr average, with Financials (-1.9SD) and Tech (-1.5SD) being the cheapest sectors trading at COVID level valuations. Healthcare, Industrial, and Utilities are relatively expensive, trading above 10yr average, but within +1SD. Staples, Financials, Technology, Energy are trading at COVID lows even on PB. Hence, Financials, Technology, Energy are quite attractively valued now. (Exhibit 1-Exhibit 3)

## INDIA SEEING EARLY SIGNS OF EARNINGS RECOVERY LED BY LARGE-CAPS AND MATERIALS/CONSUMER

Disappointing earnings has been a key drag for Indian equities as an average stock continued to see higher pace of downgrades. Unfortunately, the downgrade cycle for India is still not showing a clear bottom; however the large-caps seem to be getting back some earnings support. India is seeing a broad-based earnings down cycle visible across all sectors except Materials and Consumer ex Autos which is showing some earnings recovery. In terms of crowding, we see extreme dispersion in investor interest - Energy, Staples, Tech, Real Estate are at record low crowding while large Utilities and average Financial stock looks crowded.(Exhibit 4-Exhibit 7) Hence, India is showing early signs of recovery led by large-caps and certain sectors like Materials, consumer while some sectors look attractive on valuations (Financials/Tech) or record low crowding (Stapes/Tech/Energy/Real Estate). We believe the opportunity set for investors in Indian markets is incrementally becoming more attractive.

## LOW LONG-TERM EARNINGS EXPECTATIONS=POSITIVE RETURN OUTLOOK FOR NEXT 12MONTHS

The long-term earnings growth expectations for India again edged down from recent peak of 12.9% as of Jan 2026 to 10.6% as of Jun 2026 ie. below long-term average. Historically, low long-term growth expectations have resulted in positive next 12month fwd. returns for market. Using historical trends, while it is difficult to call the magnitude of next 12m returns, directionally, we are most likely headed towards positive returns over the next 12m. (Exhibit 8). From a market-structure perspective, the average stock dispersion in India is still making it difficult for active managers to pick stocks. However, above average dispersion in Utilities, Real Estate, Tech and Materials is presenting some active stock picking opportunities. (Exhibit 9-Exhibit 10)

## FII SENTIMENT IMPROVING

In 1H 2026, India market continued to see strong domestic institutional fund at US\$49bn which was +19% higher than US \$40bn seen in 1H 2025. However, Flls pulled out record -US\$29bn which was much larger than the -US\$8bn in first half of previous year. Since June, Fll outflow has been slowing down and MTD in July, there has been net inflow of +1.8bn USD while Dll inflow has been slowing down. SIP 3m trend wise showed some improvement in May but again slowed down, not giving any positive signal. While India was the funding source for the AI/tech trade in the region, since June as AI/tech has seen more volatility, India seem to be getting back some foreign investor interest - we believe this is likely to increase further. Even GEM active funds have reduced their UW on India to -2.1% by May, up from -2.7% as of Feb. (Exhibit 11-Exhibit 12)

## FACTOR OUTLOOK - PREFER LARGE-CAPS OVER SMIDS, ADD MOMENTUM+MAINTAIN DEFENSIVE EXPOSURE

Since April 2026, we added back some risk in India through momentum exposure while recommended to still have some defensive exposure. We maintain this barbell, and we believe the momentum uptick that started since April (up 15%) is likely to strengthen from here. However, given the geopolitical and earnings uncertainty we still maintain some defensive exposure. However, we focus on companies where earnings momentum has been improving.

In 1H26, India quality/high vol (-0.8%) has been most resilient, while low vol (-7.2%) has underperformed most vs. market (-4.5%). FY0FY3 Growth (-1.6%) and momentum (-4.0%) also did better than market. However, we note that since April, factors in India market started coming back – high vol/SMIDs(+20%), FY0FY3 growth (+18%), quality (+17%) and momentum (+15%) have been outperforming the market (+14%). (Exhibit 13)

We started 2026 with all styles stretched on valuation, except momentum which was trading closer to 10-year average. In first half of 2026, all factors have gradually de-rated. Now, only high FCF yield and quality remain relatively expensive, near +1SD above the 10-year average while Price momentum and large caps are the cheapest, trading at -1SD below the 10-year average. Relative to market also, momentum and large-caps are the cheapest, trading at -1SD level, while quality/value/SMIDs are still expensive.

Looking at earnings revisions for factors, we note that by 2Q26, all factors have been in net downgrades, except momentum which remains in upgrades. Only momentum and quality are seeing early signs of earnings recovery while all other factors are still seeing increased pace of downgrades. On relative-basis, except mid-caps/high vol/value, all other styles are showing better than market earnings revisions. (Exhibit 14-Exhibit 16) Overall, momentum, large-caps and quality look most consistent where relative valuation and earnings advantage looks strong.
