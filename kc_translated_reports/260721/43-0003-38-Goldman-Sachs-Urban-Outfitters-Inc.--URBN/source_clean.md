Upgrade to Buy. We upgrade shares of URBN to Buy from Neutral with a 12-month price target of \$93. With Anthropologie brand execution risk now well-appreciated, we believe management actions to reset the assortment will continue to build into 2H, driving stronger consistency of comp and profit execution. More importantly, Anthropologie uncertainty is now embedded in expectations, and we thus see upside to shares as UO brand recovery bolsters profitability. Emerging growth drivers are also scaling, led by Free People Movement and Nuuly. Relative to other specialty softlines peers within our coverage universe, we believe URBN offers a stronger risk/reward.

## Urban Outfitters brand recovery a key profit tailwind

Following several years of weak comp and profitability trends, the UO brand inflected positively in FY26 and is now showing signs of profit recovery. Our brand momentum trackers suggest continued improvement, which we believe will lead to stronger profit delivery as a result of markdown recapture and sales leverage. On our math, a recovery to historical levels of \~MSD% segment margins presents significant upside to consensus estimates by FY27.

## Valuation and Key Risks

We upgrade URBN to Buy from Neutral and raise our 12-month price target to \$93 from \$76 prior. We raise our FY27/FY28/FY29 adjusted EPS to \$6.31/\$6.90/\$7.49 from \$6.12/\$6.65/\$7.18 prior to reflect improved Urban Outfitters comp trends and margin recovery, continued growth and profitability gains at Nuuly, and more favorable trends at FP Movement. We assume Anthropologie brand stabilizes. Our price target increases to \$93 on our updated estimates, and as we now apply an 8.0x Q5-Q8 EV/EBITDA target valuation multiple vs. 6.75x prior, with the increase largely reflecting stronger visibility to growth and margin improvement.

Our estimates vs. consensus: We model FY27/FY28/FY29 adjusted EPS of \$6.31/\$6.90/\$7.49, reflecting 2.1%/2.1%/5.5% upside relative to FactSet consensus of \$6.17/\$6.73/\$7.08.

Key downside risks include: (1) Weaker brand momentum across any of URBN's core brands; (2) Product and merchandising execution amidst a backdrop of shifting fashion trends; (3) A weaker US consumer.
