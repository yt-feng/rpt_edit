## Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation

## Investment Thesis

We expect TSMC's structural growth drivers to remain very strong as leading-edge supply (N4, N3, and N2) stays tight well into 2027 or early 2028, given accelerating AI demand, continued growth in N3, N2 and advanced back-end revenues, and long lead time for capacity build despite accelerated capex. Near-term, we believe GM is also likely to remain strong, nudging $70\%$ levels, given N3 improvement, high UTR and decent pricing, despite N2 and overseas dilution. We expect broader price hikes in 2027, given the widening supply-demand gap and a stronger Datacenter AI CAGR (we now estimate $69\%$ CAGR from 2024-29E, driven by both higher accelerator estimates and a surge in AI CPU numbers).

As a result, we expect TSMC's blended ASPs to grow \~26% YoY in 2027, helping to drive another year of 30+% YoY revenue growth.

## Strong near-term Gross Margins: N3 improvement, high UTR and pricing should offset N2 and overseas dilution

We expect TSMC's 2Q26 GMs to approach 70% (JPMe 69.5%, up 3+ppts QoQ), supported by: 1) better utilization (N5 and below continuing to run at 100%+ UTR); 2) higher ASPs from expedited wafers (hot/super-hot runs carrying 50%/100% price premiums); and 3) improved overall efficiency.

Looking into 2H26, while the company has previously flagged margin dilution from the N2 ramp and overseas fab expansion, we expect GMs to hold in the high-60% range through 2H26 and into 1H27. Key supports include: N3 profitability is likely to surpass the corporate-average GM by 2H26, and better pricing in 2027 (we expect 8–10% hike for N3/N2 pricing and mild increase in N5/N7/CoWoS) should help sustain healthy margins as newly ramped capacity at Fab 18 P9 and AZ comes online. Overall, we expect TSMC to maintain GMs in the high-60s to \~70% range over the next few quarters.

## Capacity plans becoming more aggressive, raise FY26/27/28 capex to \$58/\$78/\$84bn

Our checks indicate TSMC has turned more aggressive on capacity buildout since early 2026, continuing to accelerate both N2 and N3 expansion plans. Multiple new fab shells began construction in 1H26, with increased equipment orders from 2Q26 onward. As a result, we are raising our capex estimates and now forecast \$58bn/\$78bn/\$84bn for 2026/27/28, respectively.

At the last earnings call, the company modestly revised up 2026 capex guidance from \$52–56bn to the upper end of the same range, while indicating that capex over the following three years would be significantly higher than the prior three-year period. For the upcoming call, we expect TSMC to raise its 2026 capex range modestly, but believe that the company is unlikely to provide a numerical three-year capex target (JPMe\~\$219bn cumulative capex for 2026–28, vs. \$101bn in 2023–25).

## N2 ramp looks very strong, with further upside from Fab 22 P7-9 buildout

We believe TSMC appeared very keen on ramping up N2 capacity build out and encouraging HPC customers to shift to N2. Based on our checks, we see a robust N2 project pipeline ahead with 1) all Apple iPhone models (A20/A20 Pro), 2) AMD Venice and MI450, 3) flagship SoCs from MediaTek, and 4) flagship SoCs from Qualcomm. From late 2027-28, major AI accelerators (such as Google TPU, NVIDIA Feynman, and Trainium 4) are also likely to join the camp. Overall, we believe that TSMC's N2 market share is likely to stay well above $90\%$ into 2028-29.

To meet the strong demand ahead, TSMC has planned to ramp up N2/A16 in the next 3 years with up to 9 Fab phases:

• Fab 20: 1-2 phases in Baoshan

• Fab 22: 5 phases in Kaohsiung

• Fab 22: recently added P7, out of P7-P9, in Tainan

\- AZ P3: 1 phase

Thus, we now raise our TSMC's 2027 N2 node capacity to 170k in 2028 (vs. 150k wfpm) to reflect further upside from Fab 22 P7-9 buildout, with a much faster ramp than any prior process nodes and eventual capacity reaching 240-250k wfpm by 2029-30.

We believe that the pressure from competition should be relieved if the customers consider supply to be sufficient by 2028, given sticking with TSMC likely to be simpler for their high volume AI chip designs. In addition, we believe the faster adoption cadence is empowered by the improved yields and better performance, with yields already reaching 75-80% iPhone processors and achieving defect density goals two quarters earlier than prior nodes.

## No mid-year price hike in 2H26, but bigger and broader price increases in 2027

TSMC raised prices by 6–10% for advanced nodes (N5 and below) in early 2026 (with negotiations having started in 2H25). However, though 2027 negotiations may already be under way, contrary to certain press reports, we do not expect TSMC to raise baseline pricing for leading-edge nodes in 2H26. ASP improvement in 2H26 should instead be driven primarily by N3 mix enrichment, as HPC customers become the majority at that node.

For 2027, we expect \~8–10% price increases for N3 and N2, while other nodes (N5, N7) and CoWoS could also see mild increases. Even mature nodes are likely to see flattish pricing, as slack capacity has been increasingly absorbed by cross-fab operations to accommodate leading-edge demand.

As a result, we expect TSMC's blended ASPs to grow 26% YoY in 2027, helping drive another year of 30%+ revenue growth. We also believe more customers are likely to agree to multi-year price contracts given continued capacity tightness into 2027.

## Market share and competitive status remains intact; expect management to be confident on competitive position

As we noted previously (see link), we expect increased noise around leading-edge competition during a period of severe TSMC wafer shortage, amplified by geopolitical considerations (particularly around Intel Foundry). However, we believe TSMC's technology leadership remains robust.

Intel's 18A process is roughly equivalent to TSMC's N3 baseline process (which began ramping in 2023), while the revamped 18AP should benchmark against TSMC's N3X (high-performance N3 variant). For Intel 14A—while we view as comparable to A16 (with back-side power delivery) within TSMC's N2P family, TSMC's N2P should enter production well ahead. Intel's 14A is expected to reach risk production in 2028 with high-volume ramp in 2029, versus TSMC's N2 family ramping in volume starting 2026.

Accelerated N2 capacity additions should also address the supply challenges customers may face in the 2028–29 timeframe—the earliest window when meaningful capacity from competitors (Intel, Samsung Foundry, TeraFab, etc.) could become available. We expect TSMC to retain 95%+ market share for the first two waves of N2 products, consistent with its N3 and N5 experience, and to continue gaining overall foundry share.

Beyond node leadership, TSMC is deepening its competitive moats through COUPE for CPO, SoIC for 3D packaging, and CoWoS for 2.5D packaging.
