# Game on – not game over

YTD sell-off reflects market concerns over structural order losses as major customers shift in-house. Following our meeting with management, we see the setup the other way: where the market sees a threat, we see an opening.

BYD represents a platform migration, not an exit: The recent sell-off reflected concerns that BYD's in-house chips and NVIDIA solutions would relegate Horizon to lower-compute ADAS functions. However, at the recent management meeting we hosted, Horizon is confident that BYD's God's Eye 5.0 upgrade creates a platform refresh opportunity, allowing it to expand share across both God's Eye C and B through broad hardware and software product offerings.

Horizon's moat lies in its comprehensive product portfolio, spanning BPU, NPU Toolchain, SoC, and HSD, which gives OEMs greater flexibility and optionality. Even highly self-reliant OEMs can leverage Horizon's hardware and software architecture in their own in-house solutions.

Horizon now offers a full suite of products, covering J6M for the mass market, J6B for overseas markets, and J6H/P and HSD for the high-end segment. This positions the company to participate in both mass-market and premium AD adoption trends. According to management, HSD 2.0, which began rolling out on 30 June, could prompt OEM customers to reassess their in-house solutions.

Semi-localization is second order trade, in our view: The larger theme is localization, with domestic platforms increasingly displacing global players across China's automotive technology stack on the basis of cost, efficiency, and policy support. Management believes both in-house and third-party domestic players will continue to gain share from global incumbents in the AD market through 2026–27. We expect market dynamics to remain fluid with a more balanced industry structure rather than a winner-takes-all outcome.

Major OEMs will maintain back-up sourcing given pace of silicon iteration: Rapid advances in silicon development, together with the associated thermal, power, and software engineering requirements, make it difficult for mainstream automakers to fund development independently. Historical experience from the smartphone industry supports this view. Even when customers fully insource development, Horizon continues to receive royalty income, similar to ARM's business model.

Stay OW; worst is priced in with wins a free option: Horizon's share price has declined approximately 50% YTD. At a market cap broadly in line with Mobileye, the market appears to value Horizon as an ADAS provider while assigning little value to its AD optionality. With weak 1H performance and market share concerns largely priced in, potential platform wins and HSD 2.0 rollout offer upside not reflected in current valuation, in our view. We expect the debate to continue, but the risk-reward skew has shifted toward upside on any confirmed design win.

## Estimate Changes

We revise our 2026 ADAS/AD chip shipment estimate down 9% to 5.1mn units to reflect macro-related vehicle volume pressure this year, although the forward order pipeline remains robust. Holding ASP and other revenue assumptions unchanged, our 2026 revenue estimate declines by 4%.

Reflecting macro headwinds and customers' in-house strategies, we lower our GPM assumptions by 5.9ppt/3.8ppt/2.5ppt in 2026–28, respectively. That said, we expect group GPM to remain above approximately 55% over the next three years, with disciplined opex helping offset part of the impact. Consequently, we now forecast a net loss of Rmb2.8bn in 2026 and Rmb1.1bn in 2027, while still expecting the company to reach net profit breakeven by 2028.

Reflecting these earnings estimate revisions, we lower our price target by 13% to HK\$8.7.

## Valuation Methodology

We continue to apply a probability-weighted DCF valuation to derive our price target for Horizon Robotics, assigning 25%/50%/25% weightings to our bull, base, and bear cases, respectively, to reflect elevated stock-specific and industry volatility. The balanced weighting between the bull and bear cases reflects our constructive view on Horizon's ability to gain market share among ADAS and AD suppliers in China and secure additional global OEM customers for its licensing and services business, while recognizing intensifying competition and potential geopolitical headwinds in smart driving. We believe DCF remains the most appropriate valuation methodology to capture Horizon's long-term growth profile, given the likely volatility in earnings and cash flow over the near term.

Base case HK\$8.2 (from HK\$9.5 previously): Our DCF-derived base-case value implies 18x 2026E P/sales based on our base-case revenue estimates. Under this scenario, we expect total ADAS and AD shipment volume to reach 5.1mn units in 2026 and 7.1mn units in 2027. Horizon reaches net profit breakeven by 2028.

Bull case HK\$15.0 (from HK\$16.0 previously): Our DCF-derived bull-case value implies 30x 2026E P/sales based on our bull-case revenue estimates. Under this scenario, we expect total ADAS and AD shipment volume to exceed 5.5mn units in 2026 and 8.0mn units in 2027.

Bear case HK\$3.5 (from HK\$5.0 previously): Our DCF-derived bear-case value implies 9x 2026E P/sales based on our bear-case revenue estimates. Under this scenario, we expect total ADAS and AD shipment volume to remain below 5.0mn units in 2026 and 6.0mn units in 2027 due to order losses as OEMs shift toward in-house solutions. Horizon remains loss-making in 2028.

Our new probability-weighted DCF-derived price target of HK\$8.7 (down from HK\$10.0 previously) implies 19x 2026E P/sales, which we believe is justified. We expect Horizon to gain further market share among ADAS and AD suppliers in China following the launch of J6P and HSD, while retaining the potential to convert existing licensing and services revenue from global OEMs (Carizon) into product solution sales over the longer term. We also expect Horizon to generate greater operating leverage as J6P scales up and the J7 platform launches in late 2026 to early 2027.
