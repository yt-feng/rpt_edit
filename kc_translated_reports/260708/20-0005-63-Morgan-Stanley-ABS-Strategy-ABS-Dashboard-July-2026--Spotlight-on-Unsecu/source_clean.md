# ABS Dashboard July 2026: Spotlight on Unsecured Consumer Vintage Performance

Over 2Q26, \$117bn of ABS was issued, a new record. Consumer ABS spreads finally softened in June, even as supply eased marginally m/m to \$37bn.


## Key Takeaways

Prime auto DQs held steady in June at 0.48%. In subprime, DQs ticked up to 5.47% in line with historical seasonal behavior.

In Consumer MPL ABS, the 2025 vintage continues to be the strongest performing and aggregate delinquencies continue to soften.

We dig into this asset class in our Spotlight, finding that this strength in aggregate performance has less to do with individual performance improvements.

Rather, we find that the introduction of new, lower-than-average delinquency shelves since 2021 is meaningfully contributing to the aggregate's improvements.

## Spotlight on Unsecured Consumer Vintage Performance

The performance data of unsecured consumer/MPL ABS looks strong this year, a different story than in auto ABS which continues to show weakness vs prior years. Aggregate delinquencies in MPL have been falling for a number of years, with 60+ DQs now well below prior years' highs. Easing of lending standards during Covid saw performance deteriorate rapidly. A common heuristic has been that lenders rapidly tightened standards in the aftermath, and to be sure that is indeed what reporting suggests (see Exhibit 7). However, we find that improved 2025 vintage performance is a more nuanced story, with changes in the universe as the MPL ABS market has grown contributing meaningfully as well.

Like with subprime autos, the improved MPL performance is not just due to individual lender behavior changes, but also due to a change in the universe composition. Unlike autos, however, this universe mix works in the favor of the aggregate. Of the largest 25 shelves today, ten are new since 2021, accounting for 22% of the total universe, and all of those ten have lower-than-average delinquencies.

Overall, shelves that launched since the beginning of 2021 account now for just over a quarter of the outstanding balance of unsecured consumer, with a real acceleration of market share acquisition since the start of 2024. (We base our analysis on the top 50 MPL shelves by current balance). At the same time, the share of shelves with lower-than-average 60+ delinquencies has been steadily increasing. We define lower-than-average shelves as those for whom their average DQ rate over the past 12 months is below the average DQ rate of the universe over the same time period, and the higher-than-average shelves as the converse.

## New Issuance, Outstanding Balance, and Trading Volumes

ABS issuance volumes set a new record in 2Q26 with \$117bn issued, +6% over 1Q26, bringing the YTD totals to \$228bn (+26% vs 2025 YTD). June was a slightly slower month than May, with \$37bn issued, but still 20% higher than June 2025 volumes and with a decent mix of both consumer (\$24.5bn) and commercial supply (\$12.5bn). A handful of digital infrastructure deals also priced in June for July settle, ending a 2-month drought in data centers with new deals from QTS and Aligned and bringing fiber YTD totals to nearly \$10bn with deals from Uniti and FirstLight. New issue spreads in both data centers and fiber tightened vs previous deals across the stack, with demand enthusiastic.

Consumer ABS spreads finally halted their rally with prime auto AAA widening \~8bps vs the May tights and subprime auto AAA \~13bps wider. We were surprised last month that spreads continued to hit new YTD tights despite the very heavy supply; the ongoing heavy issuance, not just in consumer ABS but in resi, helped to finally push spreads slightly wider across the consumer sec credit complex.

# Performance of Auto Loans (Prime vs. Subprime)

Prime delinquencies held steady in June at 0.48%, while subprime delinquencies rose 20bp to 5.47%, in line with typical seasonal trends for June. Cumulative default rates (CDR), on the other hand, saw improvements, with prime falling 1bp to 1.46% and subprime down 7bp to 12.67%. Losses remained stable in June, falling 1bp in both prime and subprime to 0.58% and 7.97%, respectively.

# Performance of Marketplace Lending Deals

In June, MPL 60+ DQs saw a 7bp decrease to 2.66%, with the decline driven across high-FICO shelves which were down 13bp on the month to 4.15%. Low-FICO shelves, in contrast, rose 4bp to 2.05%. 3mo CDR slightly increased 17bp on the month from 8.94% to 9.11%. Prepayment speeds were up in June to 34.81%, from 34.50% in May.

# Performance of Credit Card Loans

Credit card ABS 60+ delinquencies declined 9bps m/m to 1.17%. Charge-offs also declined in June, down 7bp to 3.01%. The monthly payment rate remained stable in June, decreasing 2bp to 41.53% from 41.55% in May.

## Solar Loan ABS

30-day delinquencies ticked up 4bp to 0.91%, returning to the level seen in April. Defaults and losses continue to climb, with 3mo CDR increasing 33bp to 4.59% from 4.26%. The 2024 and 2025 vintages continue to underperform the prior vintages. Annual net losses rose to 5.30%, up from 4.52% in May.
