# China FX/Rates Monitor: Resilient CNY amid Dollar Strength, Low Rates amid Growth Weakness

Our monitor tracks the latest developments and identifies key indicators for China FX and rates, including valuations/policy stance, technicals, flows, and fundamentals. Below we summarize our key takeaways on the China FX and rates markets.

■ Growth worries have increased, but expectations for broad easing remain low. May activity data suggests domestic demand has weakened visibly. Retail sales fell 0.6% yoy in May, and the single-month FAI declined 10.6% yoy. Retail sales weakness partly reflected the fading boost from trade-in subsidies and a high base, while FAI weakness was mainly driven by slower fiscal policy implementation and still-weak property investment. Our growth decomposition suggests domestic demand grew only by 1-2% yoy in April-May. While nominal export growth remained strong, real export growth slowed to around 4.5% yoy in the recent two months, down from 15.2% yoy in Q1. Taken together, these point to risks of below 4.5% yoy real GDP growth in Q2, in line with clients' growing concerns over slower economic activities. That said, expectations for broad easing remain muted: the 4.5-5.0% growth target range gives policymakers more tolerance for temporary growth softness, and China's policy reaction function has remained reactive rather than proactive. In our view, the near-term policy response is more likely to come through faster fiscal policy implementation, ample interbank liquidity and targeted credit easing. The July Politburo meeting will be the next key window to watch for policy signals.

CNY resilience remains in place despite a stronger Dollar. USD/CNY spot moved down to the 6.75 handle in the first half of June, but moved back to the 6.79 handle after the June FOMC meeting, leaving June largely a round trip for CNY against the Dollar. The renewed depreciation pressure mainly comes from wider US-China rate differentials, reflecting higher US front-end rates. That said, DXY rose roughly $2\%$ in June, while CNY depreciation against the Dollar was limited, suggesting the policy anchor behind RMB resilience remains in place. As a result, the CFETS RMB index rose around $2\%$ in June. In the near term, we expect USD/CNY to stay range-bound through the summer: the pace of fixing decline may not be fast enough to offset the positive carry from long USD/CNY positions, but increased exporter conversion on higher USD/CNY should cap upside. The policy preference, and market expectation, for gradual CNY appreciation against the Dollar remain intact. A potential visit by President Xi to the US in late September and a fading of Fed hike expectations could be the next catalysts for renewed CNY strength against the Dollar. Rates should stay low, but the bond yield curve may remain steep. Cash yields and swap rates remained low in June despite limited expectations for policy rate cuts and quarter-end liquidity demand. On fundamentals, weak domestic demand and lower oil prices argue for interest rates to stay at relatively low levels, especially for front-end rates. Although June PPI inflation likely increased further in year-over-year terms, it was likely near a peak as oil prices declined materially, while core CPI inflation remained soft due to lower gold and tourism-related services prices. On flows, soft loan demand suggests that banks may continue to allocate more funds to bond investment. In addition, the PBOC's introduction of overnight reverse repos, with the reported operation rate below the policy rate, points to a policy preference for keeping interbank liquidity ample. These factors should anchor front-end rates at low levels. Further out the curve, faster fiscal policy implementation with more ultra-long term CGB auctions in Q3 could limit the downside in longer-tenor yields. We therefore expect 10y CGB yields to remain low but range-bound, while the 10s30s slope could stay relatively steep.
