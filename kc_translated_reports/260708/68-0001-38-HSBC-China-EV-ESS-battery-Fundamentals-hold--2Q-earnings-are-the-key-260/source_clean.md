# China EV/ESS battery

Fundamentals hold; 2Q earnings are the key

\- Recent share price weakness has diverged from resilient fundamentals. 2Q earnings should be next key catalyst

Second half of the upcycle is where earnings quality and next growth driver visibility matter more than beta exposure

♦ CATL (H/A Buy/Buy; TP HKD790/RMB547) is our preferred pick in the battery sector

## What happened? Price correction has outpaced fundamental changes.

The battery sector has materially underperformed despite resilient operating fundamentals. Over the past two months, the China battery index has declined 13%, while CATL A-shares have fallen 17%. In contrast, industry demand has remained robust, with China's battery sales (including exports) growing 43% YoY in both April and May. Production schedules for June and July continue to point to sequential growth, while lithium carbonate prices have stabilised within the RMB150k–200k/t range rather than moving into another extreme cycle. We therefore believe the recent correction reflects profit-taking after the sector's strong rally in the first five months (+21% vs. CSI300 +4% in the first five months of the year) and market rotation, rather than weakening fundamentals. Against a backdrop of resilient demand and stabilising costs, we see the recent sell-off as excessive.

Where are we? The upcycle remains intact, but stock selection matters more. The sector is entering the second half of the current upcycle. As the cyclical recovery becomes increasingly priced in, investors are shifting their focus towards earnings sustainability and the visibility of the next leg of growth. We expect share price performance to become increasingly differentiated, with companies offering superior profitability, stronger execution and clearer structural growth drivers outperforming the broader sector.

What's next? 2Q earnings should be the next major catalyst. The upcoming 2Q earnings season could serve as the next key catalyst for the sector. Given the low base and, in many cases, losses in 2Q25, we expect most battery companies to deliver a meaningful YoY earnings recovery in 2Q26. Positive earnings pre-announcements and reported results should help improve sector sentiment. Beyond the earnings season, however, we believe market attention will increasingly shift from the magnitude of earnings recovery to the quality and sustainability of profits.

## What will drive the next leg? Three variables will shape sector performance.

Demand, costs and technology are becoming the key differentiators. Demand remains supported by both EVs and ESS, while ESS's weight is rising and AI-driven power consumption is creating a new medium-term growth narrative through AIDC energy storage deployment (see our China EV/ESS battery: From EVs to AI data centres: The next wave of energy storage demand, 11 June 2026]). Meanwhile, lithium price volatility is increasing the value of scale, diversified customer exposure and pricing power, favouring industry leaders that can protect margins through raw material cycles. Finally, technological leadership - including sodium-ion batteries, solid-state batteries and ultra-fast charging - is becoming an increasingly important source of competitive differentiation as commercialisation gradually accelerates. Against this backdrop, CATL remains our preferred sector exposure, supported by its unmatched scale, diversified end-market exposure and industry-leading R&D capabilities.

## Battery production schedules remain healthy

Battery demand remains robust. China's battery sales (including exports) reached 783GWh during 5M26, up $47\%$ YoY, comprising 528GWh of EV batteries $(+45\%$ YoY) and 256GWh of energy storage batteries $(+88\%$ YoY). Energy storage has become the primary incremental growth driver for the battery industry.

Despite the slowdown in domestic EV sales, China's EV battery installations were up $7\%$ y-o-y in 5M26, mainly contributed by rising battery capacity per vehicle and a surging commercial EV penetration rate.
