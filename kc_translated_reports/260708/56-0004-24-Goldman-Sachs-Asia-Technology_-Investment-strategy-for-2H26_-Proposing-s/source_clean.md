## 2H26: Three key messages and stock recommendations

In this report, we update our investment strategy, focus areas, and stock recommendations across the Asia technology team coverage for 2H26.

In our January 12 report, Asia Tech Outlook & Resurgent Japan - Seizing the Global Growth Opportunity: A broader and deeper AI presence in the Asian supply chain, we highlighted five themes we believe are under the market's radar in the AI space:

■ Extensive business opportunities driven by soaring power consumption

■ Extensive business opportunities driven by faster large-scale data transmission

■ Tightening supply/demand for niche components/materials

■ Acceleration of physical AI

AI disruption

While these five focus areas remain unchanged, given the significant share price fluctuations YTD we now present three perspectives for 2H26 and update our stock recommendations.

## Three perspectives into 2H26

1. We remain bullish on fundamentals/share prices in the Asia AI supply chain (hardware). The two bottom-up trends we monitor for AI server/data center (DC) applications driving the cycle—namely (1) signs of semiconductor/component device supply exceeding demand, and (2) signs of a shift toward lower cost trends over higher value-added technology (i.e., a slowdown in technological evolution)—have yet to materialize. Furthermore, beyond server (infrastructure) investment, the continued penetration of edge devices such as physical AI and edge AI could serve as the next driver. While share prices in the Asia AI supply chain (hardware) have risen significantly YTD, we maintain an overall bullish stance for 2H26, as we believe the AI cycle is still large and long.

2. Selecting AI stocks not only requires assessing expansion into sub-segments; well-regarded names require a closer examination of their individual risk-returns. Compared to previous expectations, we expect extended tightness in major sub-segments like memory. In addition, valuations of niche electronic components (such as MLCCs) and electronic materials rose in 1H 2026, and we see potential for supply/demand tightening to spill over into silicon wafers and analog/power semiconductors (excluding China) in 2H26. Shin-Etsu Chemical and SUMCO are key players in silicon wafers, alongside Renesas Electronics and Rohm in analog/power semiconductors.

Meanwhile, for sub-segments/companies where supply/demand is already tight (and share price valuations have advanced), we think assessing the risk-return of individual companies becomes even more crucial. Factors for analysis include: (1) significant benefits from higher prices/ASPs, (2) the ability to unlock capacity constraints through timely investments, (3) valuations lagging despite future profit contributions from AI, and (4) idiosyncratic drivers not priced into consensus. Accordingly, examining individual stock risk-returns, news flows, and timing in greater detail is critical. Such stocks may be oversold and could attract attention as investment candidates if the main AI hardware names were to face a risk-off phase for any reason. ## Maintaining an overall bullish stance on the Asia AI supply chain (hardware) in 2H26

In our June 8 report (Japan Electronic Components/Semiconductors: Potential onset of one of the largest cycles in history), we highlighted the possibility that the sector is entering one of its largest cycles ever, driven by AI impacts. We also argued that we are likely currently in the early stages of this cycle.

The timing with which investors have begun to price the cycle into share valuations in the Japan electronic components/semiconductors sector has arrived about three years later than for the main Asia AI hardware sectors such as foundries, memory, and SPE (hence our use of “early stages”). However, even in the leading sectors, we see absolutely no sign of a peaking-out at present. Rather, over the past six months, our analysts covering Asia technology (hardware) have become increasingly bullish on the outlook.

To detect a cycle peak-out, we will monitor for the following two phenomena, which have yet to emerge:

1. Signs of semiconductor/component device supply exceeding demand: currently we see no such signs (rather, our view is that an increasing number of segments are experiencing stronger supply/demand tightness and a continued favorable pricing environment)

2. Signs of a shift toward lower-cost trends from higher value-added technology (i.e., a slowdown in technological evolution): currently we see no such signs (rather, our view is that the pace of technological evolution remains significant and the trend toward higher value-add will continue for some time)

Furthermore, as also noted in our June 8 report, we think it is highly likely that after the demand growth/technological evolution for AI servers, the penetration of edge devices such as physical AI and edge AI will continue as the next driver of hardware demand. The possibility that this cycle will be one of the biggest and longest ever, combined with the lack of any signs of a peak-out, underpins our continued overall bullish stance on the Asia AI supply chain (hardware) for 2H26.

## Japan Industrial Electronics (Ryo Harada)

Stock preferences: First, we select stocks from the perspective of growing profit exposure to AI-related products and valuations, highlighting Panasonic HD and Sumitomo Electric. For Panasonic Holdings, the proportion of profits from AI/DC-related products, including BBUs for generative AI data centers where it holds around an 80% global market share, is expected to expand from 27% in FY25 to 47% in FY28. Meanwhile, the company's FY2E P/E (LSEG basis) is 22X, making it undervalued for an AI stock in our view. Similarly, Sumitomo Electric looks set to see its proportion of profits from AI/DC-related products expand from 15% in FY25 to 42% in FY28, driven by expanded sales of optical fiber cables, increased production of optical connectors, and contributions from optical devices and InP substrates with high marginal profit ratios, and it also looks undervalued at an FY2E P/E of 23X. As earnings results ahead confirm a rising proportion of AI/DC-related profits, we expect the stock to trade at a higher multiple. Next, in optical products where supply/demand is tight, we maintain our bullish view on Fujikura, which has ample scope to invest in capacity expansion. Global supply/demand for optical fiber cables is tight, but Fujikura has already announced large-scale capital investments in Japan and the US, and has the strong financial base with which to support them. In order to retain flexibility in its capital investments, the company's policy is to avoid relying on subsidies that would impose restrictions on its investments.

Heading into the second half of the year, we believe the spotlight could gradually shift to stocks related to 800V DC power supply systems. Thus far, power equipment such as transformers has garnered attention from the perspective of supplying power to data centers (outside the data center), but at present, rapid technological changes are occurring inside the data center, and a shift to direct current and higher voltages is being considered to accommodate the increased power consumption of computing chips while suppressing the increase in current. Under this new architecture, products such as SSTs (Solid State Transformers) and rectifiers are gaining importance, and companies such as Mitsubishi Electric, Hitachi, and Fuji Electric could benefit. If plans for these architectures firm up in the second half of the year, this will likely become a key investment theme.

## Could stocks perceived negatively by investors due to AI disruption fears become good investment candidates during a risk-off phase?

Here we introduce our views on companies that we feel have been perceived negatively by the equity market relative to their underlying capabilities, including those for which AI disruption is now conversely starting to open up new business opportunities. These stocks could gain attention as investment candidates if major AI hardware stocks were to face a risk-off phase for any reason.

While the degree of AI disruption varies across tech subsectors, on the flipside it is important to highlight that threats from advanced AI are starting to unearth new demand for software/system construction, including in cybersecurity applications.

## Japan IT services sector (Chikai Tanaka)

While concerns over AI disruption linger, we are starting to see signs of recovery in the US in investor sentiment and US software stock indices, spurred by strong earnings from Snowflake and comments from Capgemini during its IR Day. Stock prices in the Japan IT services sector remain soft, likely facing headwinds from sector rotation. However, in light of the difference in customer in-sourcing rates between the Japanese and US markets, the view is spreading that major Japanese system integrators are less likely to be disrupted by AI. In recent discussions with investors, our impression is that excessive concerns over AI disruption are gradually receding. Headwinds from sector rotation could continue, but based on strong fundamentals, we watch closely for the timing of a stock price reversal. Following Accenture's earnings (June 18; see report), share prices across the entire sector fell the next day, and investor sentiment worsened further. However, the company's sluggish earnings were primarily driven by client investment restraint due to the situation in the Middle East, which has little impact on the demand environment for IT services in Japan, and we believe stocks are now looking even more undervalued. From the following two perspectives, we recommend NEC, Fujitsu, NOM Institute, and BayCurrent as Buy-rated stocks with the potential to benefit from the spread of generative AI.

## AI-related business

In the short term, trends in AI-related business sales will likely gain attention as a catalyst for the IT services sector. The market consensus is that the spread of generative AI is a headwind for the IT services demand environment, but similar to the on-premise vs. cloud transition, generative AI is also creating new IT services demand. We believe that if this becomes visible in companies' earnings results, it will serve as a positive catalyst, increasing the possibility of a share price reversal for groups of stocks that benefit from AI.

According to NRI and Baycurrent (major consulting firms), AI-related business is beginning to take off as corporate appetite for AI investment grows. Inquiries are mainly strong for consulting/SI demand in the AI implementation support space, construction of AI data infrastructure, and security measures. In particular, there is a strong need for AI data infrastructure currently, with projects on the scale of several billions of yen beginning to increase depending on the project. Also, while the sales scale for consulting is still small, demand is rising rapidly, including for security solutions. In our view, this is

likely to lead to demand for revamping aging systems, from the perspective of linking them to AI and strengthening security (accelerating the modernization trend).

With the emergence of frontier AI models, significant security issues are coming to light, but similar to DX, a vertically integrated model of consulting followed by system development is likely to be effective in AI transformation (AX) as well. When companies make full-scale AI investments, the decision-making authority lies with management rather than the IT department. Thus, NRI, which has deep ties with customers' C-suite executives through its consulting business, is more likely to be able to win over customers than its competitors, in our view, and we consider it to be in an advantageous position. Baycurrent, as a pure-play consulting firm, should also benefit most from the expansion of consulting demand for AI implementation support and security solutions. Similar to NRI, NEC and Fujitsu operate across all areas, including consulting, SI, network construction, and security, so they could also see expanded demand for AI-related business.

## AI-driven development

The next catalyst to watch is profitability improvement through AI. As a measure to improve profitability, companies have indicated plans to focus on AI-driven development, which applies generative AI to system development, testing, and other functions. NEC, Fujitsu, and NRI have all entered into business alliances with Anthropic and expressed their intention to utilize its AI tool (Claude Code) for AI-driven development. At present, Fujitsu and Hitachi are leading the way in AI-driven development efforts, and this is already having a positive impact on their company-wide profits. Going forward, we expect NRI, which is proceeding with the full-scale roll-out of its proprietary AI-driven development platform, and NEC, which is advancing the internal use of Claude Code, to start to see AI-driven development benefits emerge.

NEC sees operational efficiency improvements via generative AI (including AI-driven development) as the biggest driver of profitability improvement in its IT services business. Fujitsu expects its gross margin to continue improving by +2 pp or more per year from FY25 through FY35, driven by the full-scale roll-out of AI-driven development and personnel optimization. We believe both companies will see a significant profit uplift from expansion in the scope of AI-driven development, given the large scale of their IT services businesses. NRI's medium-term plan also assumes a profit increase of c.¥15 bn over the next three years from operational efficiency improvements, including AI-driven development. According to the company, development automation by AI agents is progressing ahead of its expectations, boosting the possibility of further benefits.

## Japan game & internet sector (Minami Munakata)

Regarding the impact of generative AI on Japan's game & internet sector, we get the impression that objective scrutiny is progressing away from one-sided discussions of AI disruption. We are starting to see a gradation of effects: (1) Generative AI has a positive impact: Recruit Holdings (Buy); (2) Limited negative impact from generative AI from a creative perspective, with potential benefits in terms of production efficiency: Game sector; (3) Generative AI could have a negative impact and requires close monitoring: Internet advertising space and ad agencies. We believe we are entering a phase of further stock differentiation. We highlight Recruit Holdings, Capcom, and Sony Group as Buy-rated beneficiaries.

## Internet sector

Compared with the entertainment sector, the impact of generative AI on Japan's internet sector is becoming event in the underlying business environment and earnings in a shorter timespan, and both positives and negatives have come into sharper focus. Representative of the positive side is Recruit Holdings' Indeed business. The use of AI to enable rapid and advanced matching of companies and job seekers (expanded use of Premium Sponsored Jobs and more advanced screening) is driving monetization, resulting in higher US ARPJ (US Average Revenue Per Job posting: up +25% yoy in 4Q3/26, and we estimate +21% yoy in FY3/27). Recognition of Indeed being well-placed to benefit from AI is spreading in the equity market, and we expect Recruit Holdings to continue to be preferred within the sector.

At the same time, LY Corp (Not Rated) could be susceptible to negative impacts from generative AI. While the company is actively utilizing generative AI, including the introduction of an AI agent named Agent i, it could face direct competition from generative AI in the search domain. Indeed, its daily unique browser count (quarterly average) declined by -3.5% yoy in 4Q3/26. In addition, ad placement on generative AI platforms is starting gradually in various countries, and ad placement on ChatGPT was recently launched in Japan as well. Three companies—CyberAgent, Dentsu, and Hakuhodo—are serving as agencies for this ad placement, and for ad agencies, we believe now is a time where their ability to adapt business models to the generative AI era will be tested.

## Game sector

Investor concerns that generative AI will impair content scarcity and creative value have largely receded, in our view. Rather, the positive aspects of generative AI are becoming evident. These include SIE (Sony Interactive Entertainment) utilizing its internally developed AI tool Mockingbird (which automatically generates high-quality 3D facial animations from performance capture data) to achieve operational efficiencies in the creation of Horizon Zero Dawn Remastered, and Sony Group seeking out potential in utilizing AI for monetization, such as in payment processing fraud detection and user data analysis in its PlayStation Store.

Meanwhile, ongoing capital inflows into AI-related stocks—amid further outflows from Japan’s game & internet sector—remain a headwind for supply/demand. Under such conditions, we think stocks with strong earnings momentum which have undergone some adjustment in valuation look attractive. Among platformers, we highlight Sony Group (Buy; we estimate FY3/27 OP growth of +14% yoy, while FY3/27E P/E of 16.8X is near the bottom of its 5-year range). Among publishers, we highlight Capcom (Buy; we estimate FY3/27 OP growth of +16% yoy, while FY3/27E P/E has fallen back in line with the sector average at 21.3X despite the company’s commitment to double-digit (%) OP growth each year).
