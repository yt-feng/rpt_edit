# China Aluminum Sector Excessive pessimism creates opportunities to accumulate
## China: aluminium capacity limit intact; valuation reset creates opportunities

China's 2017 supply-side reforms capped approved nameplate aluminium capacity at 45.43mt. While record margins have driven modest overproduction via potline upgrades, deferred maintenance and parallel production, we expect output to rise to 45.6mt in 2026E and 46.0mt in 2027E (vs. 44.5mt in 2025), slightly above nameplate capacity. However, we do not expect China to abandon the capacity cap and revert to the unconstrained smelter expansion of 2009-17. Reflecting our moderately softer aluminium price outlook and a less tight market, we cut our sector earnings forecasts 10-30% for 2026-27 and our PTs c20%, based on unchanged target PE and dividend yield assumptions. However, we think valuations remain compelling with our new PTs implying 60-80% upside. Upside catalysts include ongoing inspections of overproduction and potential closures of non-compliant capacity, Indonesian policy uncertainty and limits to further nickel power diversion, and delayed Middle East restarts. These could support aluminium prices and earnings.

## Ex China: more supply and less tightness, but no material surplus

We forecast a 1.1mt global aluminium deficit in 2026 before the market returns to balance in 2027E, supported by >3mt supply growth. In Indonesia, we expect output growth of 1.0mt in 2026E and 1.7mt in 2027E. We flag c3.0mt of supply has been affected by Middle East tensions and supported the 2026E deficit, but gradual restarts and the release of 0.3-0.4mt of trapped inventory should ease tightness in Europe and the US. However, we do not expect a material surplus over the next two to three years. Indonesian policy uncertainty, recovering traditional demand and a supportive copper-aluminium ratio (>4x) should help aluminium prices rebase above the cost curve even as market tightness eases.

## China demand looks weak in 2026E, but supported by exports

China's property/construction activities and solar are weighing heavily on aluminum demand in 2026, offset by robust aluminium net exports of c40% YoY in the first five months. Earlier price hikes amid the Middle East disruption have pressured downstream demand. In 2027E, property and solar should be less of a drag. Structural demand from vehicle lightweighting, resilient machinery sales and electrical transmission growth, supported by some copper to aluminium substitution, suggest total China demand should stay flat in 2026E and increase 2% in 2027E. Recent price moderation may support some demand improvement/restocking.

## Excessive pessimism creates attractive valuations; maintain Buy ratings

UBS now forecasts global aluminium prices of US\$1.50/lb in 2026 and US\$1.42/lb in 2027. Factoring in a 9%/5% SHFE/LME discount, we lower our China aluminium price forecasts to Rmb23,000/t/Rmb22,500/t for 2026/27, and cut 2026E/27E earnings for Tianshan, Chalco and Hongqiao 9-15%/24-33%, respectively. We roll forward to 2027E and maintain our target valuations (10x PE for Tianshan and Chalco; 6.5% dividend yield for Hongqiao), and cut our PTs 19-21%.

We think market concerns around overproduction appear overstated.

\- Capacity replacement may temporarily allow parallel production. In 2026, total capacity swaps are \~2.6mt, including \~0.85mt cross-province relocations and \~1.76mt upgrades. While overlapping operations during transitions can in theory lift output above nameplate capacity, the impact is limited at the aggregate monthly level.

\- Non-compliant legacy capacity (c.\~0.6-0.8mt) is already captured in installed capacity. Its potential formalisation therefore does not represent net additions and has no incremental impact on supply. However, permanent shutdown would lead to a net reduction in both nameplate capacity and production.

\- Potline upgrades can lift effective output via gains in efficiency or amperage. For example, according to Aladdiny, a 1ppt improvement in current efficiency (e.g. from 93% to 94%) lifts annual output by \~1.1% (c.147kt for a 500ka potline), while a 10ka increase (500ka to 510ka) raises output by \~2.0% (c.73.5kt at 94% efficiency). In practice, current efficiency gains are slow and incremental. Aladdiny estimates annual improvement of \~0.5ppt, and only \~30% of capacity may achieve this in the near term. Amperage increases offer more headroom (theoretically up to \~10%), but technical constraints mean even a sustained 5% uplift takes time and is not nationally feasible. If we assume an idealised scenario of 100% utilisation, with nationwide efficiency improving by 0.2ppt and amperage rising 1\~3%, effective annualised capacity could reach \~46.8mt. However, producers remain reluctant to maximise short-term output, given concerns over profit sustainability and potline lifespan. Meanwhile, rotating maintenance cycles (requiring overhaul every 4-8 years) leave a structural utilisation buffer of \~2%.

In reality, despite theoretical and anecdotal instances of parallel operations and output gains from pot upgrades, China's nationwide monthly installed-capacity utilisation has not exceeded 100% from 2021 through YTD26. Effective utilisation has remained tightly anchored around 100% ( $\pm2\%$ ), with a peak of +1.6% in Feb-23. Even at margin peaks of >Rmb8,000/t, operational constraints continue to cap supply, with 5M26 effective utilisation staying within +1%. Notably, while some market participants use annualized April 2026 NBS output (>47mt) as a baseline proxy for 26E production, we continue to track and base our estimates on Aladdiny and Mysteel operating data.

Overall, we do not expect the speculated overproduction to materially lift utilisation or supply, and continue to view the market as well balanced rather than structurally oversupplied.

## Indonesia: to add 1.5/1.9mt capacity and 1.0/1.7mt output in 2026/27E

We raise our Indonesia aluminium output outlook for 2026-27, driven by both new projects and accelerated ramp-ups. Key additions include Tsingshan's Taiyun Morowali project (600kt, 27E) and Weda Bay expansion (800kt; 400kt in 26E, 400kt in 27E). Several projects are also tracking ahead of schedule: Harita's 100kt has been brought forward from 2028–29 to 2027–28; Bosai's 600kt similarly moves forward; and ramp-up is accelerating across Tsingshan's projects (Juwan, Taijing, Xianfeng; 1.2mt capacity).

\- For 2026, we see high visibility for \~1.0mt of incremental output. Existing projects are progressing smoothly. 1) Tsingshan/Xinfa are set to add \~0.5mt, with no near-term power constraints. According to UBS's Indonesia analyst, NPI and nickel matte exports at Morowali dropped to near zero in May and early June, potentially freeing \~1GW of power capacity, equivalent to \~670kt of aluminium capacity, sufficient to support Taijing's 600kt ramp-up. Shipments have begun, with \~60kt expected in June (primarily exported to Europe and the US on LME-linked pricing), followed by 240–280kt over July–December. 3) Nanshan-A project (125kt) is expected to complete construction by end-2026, with initial output ramping gradually.

\- Looking to 2027, we estimate realised incremental output of \~1.7mt, though execution risks rise. The Taiyun Morowali project (600kt) carries the highest uncertainty; we assume only \~40% capacity contribution in 2027E, despite a construction target of 4Q26, as power remains the key bottleneck: 1) Existing nickel power diversion at Morowali may only support partial ramp-up (\~10% of Taiyun's capacity). Relative aluminium vs. nickel profitability could further impact power allocation if nickel margins recover. 2) Beyond nickel NPI and matte power, additional capacity headroom at Tsingshan's park (\~4–5GW) is unlikely to be released given potential employment and trade balance implications. 3) Thermal power remains a viable solution: while lead times for Chinese equipment exports are long, industry checks suggest coal-fired unit exports remain practically feasible. Tsingshan has secured approvals and land for new captive coal-fired plants, according to Aladdiny, though commissioning is only expected by end-2028 to 2029.

\- By 2028, we estimate \~1.4mt of incremental output. Execution risks vary by project: Bosai's 600kt, if reliant on grid power, faces reliability constraints and domestic coal supply challenges, while Harita's 800kt appears relatively more certain, with both smelter and captive power construction progressing on track.

Overall, while 2026 ramp-up looks well supported by nickel power flexibility, the mid-term trajectory appears constrained by power availability and project execution risks.

## Demand

## China total demand flat/+2% with direct net exports +18%/+6% in 2026/27E

We expect China aluminium demand to soften modestly in 2026E, reflecting contractions in solar, home appliances and a moderation in auto demand growth. Offsetting this, tight overseas markets and a wider LME-China arbitrage window should continue to support aluminium exports.

\- Property (-9% 26E): We forecast property-related aluminium demand to decline 9% YoY, driven by persistently weak construction activity. The UBS Property team expects new housing starts to fall 9% in 2026E before stabilising in 2027E. While infrastructure and special-bond-funded resettlement housing provides some support, commercial residential construction remains under pressure. Higher cost sensitivity among developers is also likely to weigh on aluminium intensity.

\- Transport (+2% 26E): We expect transport demand to grow 2% YoY, supported by resilient vehicle exports. Domestic demand is likely to moderate as subsidies for lower-end models are phased out and VAT incentives are reduced. Continued EV penetration should largely offset weakness in the ICE segment.

\- Electrical (-2% 26E): We forecast electrical demand to decline 2% YoY. Grid investment and energy storage systems (ESS) should remain key growth drivers; however, we expect it to be more than offset by a sharp correction in solar demand following the strong installation cycle during 2023–25. New solar installations totalled only 60GW in 5M26, down 70% YoY, with UBS's Utilities team expecting a recovery from Q326E.

\- Packaging (+1% 26E): We expect packaging demand to increase 1% YoY, supported by stable consumption trends, albeit with some moderation in retail sales growth.

\- Appliances (-3% 26E): We forecast appliance demand to decline 3% YoY as government subsidy support fades. UBS's Consumers team notes the entry of a post-subsidy downcycle, which they expect to persist through 2025–27E.

\- Machinery (+6% 26E): We forecast machinery demand to grow 6% YoY. Construction machinery entered a replacement upcycle from 2H25, supported by export demand, with momentum likely to extend through 2027E. Machine tools should deliver modest growth in 2026–27E before slowing beyond 2028E.

Exports. The widening LME-China spread has supported a steady increase in Chinese aluminium exports in 5M26, with unwrought aluminium and product exports reaching 1.23mt in Apr-May (+16% YoY), providing some relief to domestic pressure. However, we expect export growth to remain gradual rather than high, despite the attractive headline arbitrage. Industry participants highlighted that realized export margins are substantially below theoretical spreads. 1) Export tariffs remain a major constraint, with primary aluminium subject to a 30% duty and aluminium products facing tariffs of 10-30%, absorbing a significant portion of the arbitrage gain. 2) Strong trader bargaining power and growing demand from overseas buyers for a share of the widening spread are diluting arbitrage gains and limiting producers' realized margins. 3) Logistics costs and execution risks have also tempered producers' willingness to aggressively pursue exports. 4) For key arbitrage products such as aluminium wire rod, rising export volumes have driven premium compression, with overseas wire rod premiums lagging primary aluminium premiums amid intensifying competition among exporters.

Overall, while a wider LME-China spread should continue to support export growth, we expect the response to remain incremental rather than exponential, constrained by tariffs, profit-sharing across the value chain, logistics costs and intensifying competitive pressures.

## Valuation

China aluminium price steady at Rmb23,000/t in 2026E, easing to Rmb22,500/t in 2027E

UBS Global mining team maintains its 2026 aluminium price forecast at US\$1.50/lb and lowers 2027E to US\$1.42/lb. Assuming a 9%/5% SHFE-LME discount in 2026E/27E, we trim our China aluminium price forecasts to Rmb23,000/t in 2026E (from Rmb23,500/t) and Rmb22,500/t in 2027E (from Rmb25,000/t). Despite the lower price outlook, industry profitability should remain healthy, with China aluminium cash spreads averaging above Rmb6,000/t in 2026E and above Rmb5,000/t in 2027E.

The price changes reflect the following factors: Resumption of supply from the Middle East (ME) will take time and we continue to forecast a global deficit in 2026. Recent supply developments in Indonesia combined with notably weak demand (due in part to -ve impact of substitution) result in a less supportive S&D outlook over the next 2-3 years. We expect tightness to ease over the next 6-12 months: (1) as\~3mt of ME supply returns to the market; and (2) given increased supply expectations from Indonesia, resulting in supply growth of \~5%/3% in 2026/27E vs demand growth of 2.5-3.0%.

However: (1) we forecast a return to a balanced market in 2027/28 not a material surplus; (2) traditional demand drivers should improve with prices moderating; (3) Indonesian policy remains highly unpredictable; (4) we expect price support from the copper (aluminium ratio above 4x); and (5) stale extended speculative positioning is normalised. In our view the fundamental outlook has deteriorated, but prices are likely to rebase above the cost curve.

More detail can be found in our latest UBS Q226 price review note.

## UBS THESIS MAP a guide to our thinking and what's where in this report

## Q: Will China's aluminium industry enter oversupply in 2026-27E?

No. While record margins are encouraging producers to optimise output via potline upgrades, delayed maintenance and temporary parallel operations, we expect production growth to remain modest and reach 45.6mt in 2026E and 46.0mt in 2027E versus 44.5mt in 2025. Importantly, we do not expect China to relax its 45.43mt capacity cap established in the 2017 supply-side reforms. Although capacity swaps, legacy capacity and operating efficiency improvements could temporarily lift effective output, nationwide utilisation has remained tightly anchored at c100% in recent years despite elevated profitability. In our view, operational constraints and policy discipline should continue to contain supply growth, leaving the market broadly balanced rather than structurally oversupplied.

## Q: Will the global aluminium market enter oversupply in 2026-27E?

No, but 2027E should be less balanced than in 2026E. With China nearing the capacity ceiling, Indonesia remains the key source of incremental supply; however, power constraints and policy uncertainty should limit the pace of capacity additions. We forecast Indonesian output growth of 1.0mt in 2026 and 1.7mt in 2027, supported by power reallocation from nickel producers and ongoing smelter construction. At the same time, a significant portion of Middle East smelter capacity remains offline, and any restart is likely to be gradual given the need to rebuild facilities and alumina inventories, and ramp up operations. We forecast global primary aluminium supply to decline by over 0.6mt in 2026, followed by over 3mt of supply growth in 2027E, implying a less balanced market in 2027E but not one that is materially oversupplied.

Company. We believe Hongqiao will re-rate further driven by: 1) its consistent delivery of solid results; 2) disciplined 10-year track record of raising the dividend payout, which currently stands at a generous 63%+; and 3) our expectation of deleveraging starting in 2026E as capex peaks and as the core business outlook looks positive. Industry. UBS remains constructive on the global aluminium market outlook, driven by balanced supply-demand and sustained industry profitability. We reiterate our Buy rating on Hongqiao.

Company. Hongqiao continues to deliver strong earnings and shareholder returns, supported by a decade-long record of dividend increases and ongoing buybacks. Industry. China's aluminium inventory continued to decline in May-June 2026, with robust exports offsetting weaker domestic demand. Middle East disruption has removed c3mt of operating capacity from the market. While Indonesia's aluminium expansion is accelerating in 2026E, supported by power reallocation from nickel producers, power constraints and policy uncertainty should limit supply growth beyond 2026E.

Hongqiao trades at an 11.5% 2027E dividend yield, which we view as undemanding compared with its A-share aluminium peer average of 6.7% and H-share peer Chalco's 7.4%. We believe the valuation is attractive at a target dividend yield of 6.5% with a strong dividend payout.

## UBS THESIS MAP a guide to our thinking and what's where in this report

## Q: Will China's aluminium industry enter oversupply in 2026-27E?

No. While record margins are encouraging producers to optimise output via potline upgrades, delayed maintenance and temporary parallel operations, we expect production growth to remain modest and reach 45.6mt in 2026E and 46.0mt in 2027E versus 44.5mt in 2025. Importantly, we do not expect China to relax its 45.43mt capacity cap established in the 2017 supply-side reforms. Although capacity swaps, legacy capacity and operating efficiency improvements could temporarily lift effective output, nationwide utilisation has remained tightly anchored at c100% in recent years despite elevated profitability. In our view, operational constraints and policy discipline should continue to contain supply growth, leaving the market broadly balanced rather than structurally oversupplied.

## Q: Will the global aluminium market enter oversupply in 2026-27E?

No, but 2027E should be less balanced than in 2026E. With China nearing the capacity ceiling, Indonesia remains the key source of incremental supply; however, power constraints and policy uncertainty should limit the pace of capacity additions. We forecast Indonesian output growth of 1.0mt in 2026 and 1.7mt in 2027, supported by power reallocation from nickel producers and ongoing smelter construction. At the same time, a significant portion of Middle East smelter capacity remains offline, and any restart is likely to be gradual given the need to rebuild facilities and alumina inventories, and ramp up operations. We forecast global primary aluminium supply to decline by over 0.6mt in 2026, followed by over 3mt of supply growth in 2027E, implying a less balanced market in 2027E but not one that is materially oversupplied.

## Valuation Method and Risk Statement

We use a PE multiple to set our price targets for Chalco-A, Chalco-H, and Tianshan. We use a dividend yield methodology to set our price target for Hongqiao.

Key risks to the companies we cover in the aluminium sector coverage include economic conditions and policies that could result in aluminium price volatility. Supply plays as important a role as demand, together shaping the supply/demand balance and determining aluminium prices. Risks also include: 1) ramp-up progress and government control of new aluminium/alumina capacity; 2) demand growth in property completions; 3) FAI growth in infrastructure activity; 4) government environmental regulations; 5) potential SOE reforms; and 6) prices of raw materials.
