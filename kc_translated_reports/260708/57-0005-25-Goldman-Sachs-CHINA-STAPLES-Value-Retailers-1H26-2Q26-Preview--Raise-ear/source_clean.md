# Value Retailers 1H26/2Q26 Preview: Raise earnings on robust store open pace and resilient SSSG; Reiterate Buy

Our value retailer coverage (Busy Ming/Wanchen) share prices have rebounded 29%/18% from recent lows, but still c.15% below the previous peak in mid-May. We see upside to 1H26 earnings driven by faster-than-expected store expansion (c.4,500+ gross opens for each in 1H26 on GSe, see tracker in Exhibit 2) while we expect per-store average GMV to remain largely resilient in 1H26 (with help from LNY seasonality, despite weather impact in 2Q) and healthy SSSG. We currently expect 54%/98% 1H26 sales/adjusted NP growth for Busy Ming and 41%/102% 2Q26 sales/pre-MI NP growth for Wanchen, respectively. We reiterate that the TAM remains vast with substantial room for further penetration and category expansion (in our view, industry-wide stores have the potential to reach up to c.100k in the long term vs. c.68k by end of 2026E on GSE).

1) Faster-than-expected store network scaling by the two leaders: Store openings accelerated YoY with Wanchen gross open above 2025 full year (c.4.7k) and Busy Ming also at 4.5k+ per our tracker vs 1.5k/2.5k gross open in 1H25, with accelerated monthly opening (monthly gross open sped up to 1k+ in May/Jun) ahead of upcoming summer school holiday season. The store open is mainly from cultivating stronghold markets (differentiation for leaders), exploring white space in low tier/under-penetrated area as well as taking market share from smaller regional players, ie. Wanchen emphasized that store-opening locations in 1H26 remains differentiated vs. competitors with 70% of openings concentrated in its stronghold markets (Yangtze River Delta/Shanhe Four Provinces). We noted that both industry leaders remain rational and disciplined over store-open subsidies, with special subsidies only approved for special location/regions. Moving forward, we expect both players to continue prioritizing health per-store GMV performance, but still looking for sufficient space to sustain the store opening pace into 2H26.

Our long-term view of still substantial industry store runway intact: We project the industry store network to expand to 68k by end-2026 and c.80k outlets by 2028E, representing a +15% CAGR from 2025-28E, with we reiterate a long-term addressable space exceeding 100k stores. Meanwhile, we also expect leaders Busy Ming/Wanchen to grow the combined market share to 80% in 2026 (up from 70%+ in 2024-25) leveraging advantages in network scale/store mgmt expertise to gain share from smaller regional players (our tracker showed very limited store expansion in 2026YTD for smaller chains).

## 2) Resilient SSSG trends in1H26/2Q26 on concrete growth

initiatives/better-than-feared traffic dilution: We expect 1H26 avg. per-store GMV still positive in both companies supported by: i) strong Jan-Feb CNY trading with HSD% per-store GMV growth per mgmt; ii) continued ramp up in SSSG of existing stores; iii) improved site selection quality in a more rational competitive environment; and iv) an easy comp, given per-store GMV declined MSD%/teens% in 1H25 for Busy Ming/Wanchen. That said, both players noted unfavorable impact on per-store GMV from weather/pax mobility especially in June/Late 2Q.

■ Into 2H, we expect avg. per-store GMV growth to moderate sequentially vs. 1H26 on a higher comp base (Busy Ming/Wanchen returning to positive per store GMV in 3Q/4Q25), while we expect both companies to continue prioritizing store health and franchisee margins, and category expansion/portfolio upgrades to support per-store GMV (ie. both successfully added cold-chain and chilled/frozen categories, ie. at c.3% of total GMV for Wanchen).

3) Store UE remains healthy (key support to store open acceleration) while margin expansion runway intact: we highlight that store-level franchisee GPM has stayed stable or improved under disciplined pricing, while payback periods remain attractive (e.g., c.2 years). At the franchiser level, we continue to expect procurement scale and logistics optimization to further support GPM expansion, while allowing franchisees to preserve attractive returns by sharing the gains in a more efficient industry value chain (F&B brands offering attractive pricings to value retailers channels to enjoy scalability/high channel growth/opex saving). We now forecast 4.6%/5.7% adj. NPM/pre-MI NPM for Busy Ming/Wanchen in 2026E, up 0.5pp/1.0pp YoY, while 1H26 margin will likely enjoy more operating leveraging from LNY peak season and 2H25 new store ramp-up.

Earnings revision: We revise up Busy Ming/Wanchen 2026-28E sales by 2\~10%/up to 9% and NP by 6\~13%/2\~11% on faster store expansion and operating leveraging. We reiterate Buy on Busy Ming/Wanchen given attractive risk/reward considering that the stocks are trading at 17x/14x 2026E P/E vs. 25%/21% NP CAGR in 2026-28E, respectively.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Busy Ming Group

Valuation: Our 12-month target price for Busy Ming is HK\$581, based on a 21x target P/E applied to our 2027E EPS and discounted back to mid-2027, using a COE of 9.4%, referencing the average NTM P/E of global value-focused retail peers including Dollar General and Dollar Tree in the US, and Ryohin Keikaku and Pan Pacific International Holdings (operates Donki) in Japan, as they are scaled, price-led operators with broad, defensible customer reach similar to Busy Ming.

Key risks: 1) Intensifying competition and price investment; 2) Network density and cannibalization; 3) Franchise model risk and scaling complexity; 4) Supply chain, food safety, and logistics.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Fujian Wanchen Food

Valuation: Our 12m TP is Rmb324, based on a target 2027E P/E at 19.6x discounted back to mid-2027 at an 8.2% COE. The target P/E is at a HSD% valuation discount to Busy Ming, as we reference the average gap of DLTR's NTM P/E vs. DG's in the past two years, factoring in the relatively slower network scaling momentum and less favorable UE (lower per-store GMV but profits driven by high mix of smaller brands/white labels).

Key risks: 1) intensifying market competition and price sustainability; 2) franchise model risk and scaling complexity; 3) M&A risks; 4) supply chain, food safety and logistics.
