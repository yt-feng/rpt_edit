# 2Q26 EPS preview: We expect generally better guidance from industrial tech companies, and in line outlooks in autos

Heading into 2Q26 earnings the median stock in our Autos & Industrial Tech coverage is about flat since reporting 1Q26 results and up 3% YTD (with Auto exposed stocks up 7% since earnings and up 3% YTD, and Industrial Tech stocks down 5% at the median since reporting 1Q results and up 33% YTD).

With this backdrop, we expect 2Q26 results and forward guidance to generally be better than consensus from the Industrial Tech companies in our coverage, and we expect more in line outlooks from auto OEMs and suppliers. In Industrial Tech, this is due to what we think is ongoing strong hyperscale/AI capex spending, and a recovering industrial market more generally (e.g. the ISM index has been >50 the last six months). In autos, we expect modestly lower yoy global auto production and US auto sales, but this is consistent with what auto suppliers and OEMs commonly assumed in guidance.

## In terms of potential stock moves this earnings season:

For Industrial Tech, there has been a high bar for datacenter exposed Industrial Tech stocks to trade up in the first session post reporting over the last several earnings seasons. If that trend continues, there could be more upside specifically around earnings for stocks that have been more debated recently in this cohort (we highlight APH in particular as a name we're positive on).

For core autos, with both US SAAR and global production near historical averages, we think the focus will be on idiosyncratic growth drivers and EPS sustainability. This has been partly from energy and datacenter opportunities, which has helped BWA, APTV, and F to trade higher intra-quarter (as detailed in our Platforms & Power III report, we see BWA as best positioned to benefit from this theme). We also see upside to TSLA Street (FactSet) estimates, consistent with the stronger 2Q deliveries. In terms of stocks we'd be more cautious on specifically around earnings, we believe 2026/2027 estimates could be revised lower on MBLY to reflect costs for its robotaxi effort, as we think the company will need to increase its investments before its direct approach could lead to incremental earnings growth (and we believe that new design-win traction and/or wins would be needed for the stock to work around the quarter after the recent move higher in shares).

Key topics and themes we expect more generally this quarter include:

1) Trends by end market including auto sales and product cycles, industrial demand, and datacenter capex

2) China OEM exposure for auto suppliers, including risks from softer China auto sales YTD (which are -19%) but also the impact of growing exports and share gains, including in Europe.

3) The USMCA and how/if trade rules will change especially for autos as the White House has sought higher US specific content rules (per media reports). We expect companies in general to point to an ability to partner with suppliers/customers and price-cost discipline. However, the USMCA will remain in effect for another decade provided no country decides to exit per Bloomberg, so we do not expect any incremental actions to be taken unless companies have visibility into rules changing.

4) The degree of input cost inflation and ability for companies to offset this. Some costs have continued to rise, including copper and memory. We expect suppliers to continue to take steps to offset this. With memory costs likely to rise again in 2027, we expect the ability for this to continue next year to be a debate amongst investors. Our base case view is that the supply chain can pass on these costs over time, albeit at times on a lag. Conversely, aluminum and crude prices have moderated from recent highs, which could help lower resin/metals costs if this is sustained (which would be a positive for auto OEMs including limiting how much yoy input/material cost headwind GM and Ford may see in 2027).

5) AI related efforts including manufacturing efficiency, automation/robotics, and AV/robotaxi efforts. For Tesla, we believe FSD adoption/penetration, the pace of its robotaxi roll-out, and how Optimus development is going will be key topics on its earnings call.

In this report, we detail estimate revisions, share performance, and valuation scenarios by stock, and adjust estimates and price targets for LEA (reflecting company updates at an investor conference) and BDC (driven by a recently completed acquisition).

## EV and hybrid sales by region

EV growth has varied by region.

EV sales in the US were down 24% yoy in 2Q and are down 25% yoy YTD per data from Motor Intelligence. Hybrid sales in the US were up 30% yoy in 2Q and are up 21% yoy YTD, and PHEV sales were down 37%/48% in the same timeframe.

In major European markets (UK, Germany, France, Italy, and Spain), EV sales were up 55% yoy in 2Q and are up 45% yoy YTD, with PHEV sales up 24%/up 33% over the same timeframe. In China, BEV and PHEV sales are up 10%/down 29% in 2Q through May and down 8%/down 25% YTD through May.

EV sales as a percent of new vehicles sold in the US were 6% in 2Q per Motor Intelligence, in line with 4Q25 and 1Q26 but down from 10-12% in 3Q due to the IRA credit expiration (and pull-in demand in 3Q25). This is below both Europe and China.

## Policy in focus: USMCA negotiations began July 1, impact to autos and tier 1s in focus

The USMCA trade agreement formally began negotiations, on July 1st, with the US declining to renew the agreement for a 16-year term, which will result in annual reviews (as reported by the media). The USMCA will remain in effect for another decade provided no country decides to exit, per Bloomberg. Per media reports, the US administration is pushing for increased content to come from the US to receive preferential tariff treatment for autos. Recall, currently, all vehicles imported into the US are subject to an up to 25% sectoral tariff (including Canada and Mexico), with exceptions for trade deals (e.g. UK at 10%, EU at 15%). We highlight industry imports of vehicles from Canada and Mexico, and OEM and regional exposure for tier 1s in Exhibit 17 to Exhibit 20. We also note that NHTSA also has data available (linked here) on the percent of content from the US and Canada, and up to 2 countries with at least 15% of the car's content, location of final assembly, and origin of the engine and transmission. We note that the data in Exhibit 18 reflects 2025 production, and GM has since announced and been working towards adding 400K vehicles of capacity to the US which could increase its US production to \~2 mn units including vehicles for export (and both Ford and GM have at times added extra shifts to US plants to increase production).

## Price target and estimate changes, and key risks

We update our estimates for Lear to reflect company comments on financials for 2Q and FY26. We now model EPS of \$15.00/\$16.85/\$18.40 for 2026/27/28, from \$14.95/\$16.40/\$18.00 prior, reflecting better margins in 2026 and better revenue in 27/28 (reflecting recent wins).

We raise our 12-month price target to \$138 from \$131, which is based on 8X (unchanged) our normalized EPS estimate of \$17.20 (from \$16.40, reflecting our updated estimates). Key downside/upside risks to our thesis include: The auto cycle being more positive or negative than we expect; Lear outgrowing/undergrowing underlying auto production more than we expect; Lear gaining/losing market share in Seating and/or E-systems; larger/smaller than expected tariff impacts; and upside/downside to margins (wages, supply chain issues, and competition could negatively impact Lear's margin targets and cost actions including increased automation could positively impact margins).

We also update our estimates for Belden to reflect the announced closure of its RUCKUS acquisition on July 1st. Recall management expects RUCKUS revenue to grow high single digits, with adjusted gross margins over 60%. Further recall management expects RUCKUS adjusted EBITDA margins to be 20%+ in the first full year after closing. We now model EPS of \$8.40/\$9.65/\$10.75 in 2026/27/28 respectively, from \$8.05/\$8.75/\$9.45.

We adjust our 12-month price target to \$152 from \$153, which is based on 15X (from 17X prior, reflecting the increased leverage) our normalized EPS estimate of \$10.15 (from \$9.00 prior, reflecting our updated estimates). Key downside risks to our view relate to: 1) Our revenue outlook, including in industrial (revenue and orders trends have historically been correlated with broader industrial indicators such as the ISM index, and some industrial datapoints have been weak), Enterprise (given sluggish commercial

office/smart building and telecom capex trends), overall industrial softness due to tariffs; 2) Margins (we expect mix shift to full solutions sales to help drive margins higher, but there could be downside risk from pricing pressure and execution risk); 3) Belden's ability to continue to grow through M&A and inorganic investments in areas with higher growth profiles, such as fiber connectivity and wireless; and 4) The extent to which government stimulus programs drive incremental demand, and there could be downside risk depending on the allocation of funding.
