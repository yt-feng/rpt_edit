# Robotaxi Launches in Miami; Model YL Debuts in the US

Tesla launched robotaxi in Miami over the weekend. Tesla launched robotaxi Miami (including unsupervised vehicles) on Friday, July 3rd. Miami was listed as "preparations underway" in the 1Q26 slide deck, along with Phoenix, Orlando, Tampa, and Las Vegas. We expect Tesla to launch in all of these metro areas by year-end. Additionally, there have been some sightings of presumed robotaxi test vehicles in New Orleans (not confirmed by the company), which was not one of the cities listed as being in the preparation stage in the last earnings presentation. We believe this news will be well received by the market after what had felt like a deceleration with the Robotaxi Tracker reflecting a decline in active vehicles in fleet since late April (although this data is not validated by the company).

July 7th Catalyst? We are watching for an update tomorrow on Cybercab production following an interview last week with Lars Moravy (VP of Vehicle Engineering at Tesla) where it was mentioned that there will be an announcement at Giga Texas: "a week from Tuesday there will be some cool news about things happening around Giga Texas as part of the scaling effort".

Early Signals from Austin. We wrote last month that our Alphawise data showed increased hiring in northern metro areas for 'AI Safety Operators' - a sign that Tesla is gearing up to expand robotaxi operations, along with FSD usage continuing to accelerate, reaching 10bn cumulative miles traveled in early May. We also built out a tracker normalizing NHTSA data from the Austin fleet, which reflected significant improvement in miles/accident since launch. With Tesla's approach to autonomy still heavily debated, investors are closely watching rate of change in monthly safety


data. Tesla's ability to grow its robotaxi fleet while concurrently improving incident rates (miles between accident) is a key measure that we expect will dominate investors perception of "success" for its robotaxi business. For further reading, see: Our Guide to Robotaxi Safety Data: Early Signals from Austin.

Our expectations. We forecast 1,500 robotaxis (supervised + unsupervised) in the fleet by year-end, ramping to 30,000 by 2030. While the absolute number of robotaxis is likely immaterial to earnings this year, the rate of change in the rollout provides more clarity for investors who may be skeptical about Tesla's autonomous technology at scale. Outside of robotaxi, Tesla also launched Model YL in the US for deliveries in October. After a blowout 2Q delivery beat last week, Tesla announced the launch of the larger Model YL in the US, starting with a Limited Edition Premium AWD Launch Series Trim. The starting price is \$61,990, which includes 1 year of FSD, Supercharging, and Premium Connectivity, and color choices at no additional cost. The US launch follows the Model YL's successful debut in China, where the six-seat, long-wheelbase SUV was introduced last year at a \~\$4,000 premium to the standard Model Y. This compares to the US Model YL, which is at a \$12k premium to the MY Premium AWD and a \$22k premium to the MY RWD. Strong initial demand in China demonstrated consumer appetite for a roomier three-row Tesla, likely underpinning the decision to expand the model globally. US customer deliveries are expected to begin in October, positioning the model to contribute more meaningfully in 4Q26 and 2027. Longer term, the Model YL broadens Tesla's addressable market by targeting larger families and partially filling the gap left by the discontinued Model X, supporting mix and ASPs while helping sustain Model Y demand in key markets.

## Tesla Inc (TSLA.O)
