# Equity Thematic Strategy

For the first time since 1994, the FIFA World Cup returns to North America as the United States, Canada and Mexico jointly host the 2026 tournament. This is likely to be the largest single-sport event in history, with 48 nations competing in 104 matches across 16 cities, of which the majority (78 matches and 11 cities) will be in the United States. Much of this is driven by the expansion of the tournament from 32 to 48 teams and from 64 to 104 matches. The tournament expansion is expected to drive higher fan participation, travel flows and global media engagement. Early indications are positive as evidenced by the 500M ticket requests for \~7M available seats or an oversubscription ratio of \~70x (see Report by our Internet Research team). According to FIFA, 6.5M people are expected to attend matches, with \~\$14B of event-related expenditures (see Report) driving some \$17.2B of GDP for the US alone.


Sectors and Industries. In the US, value-added gains are expected to be led by Accommodation & Food (\$2.4B), Real Estate (\$2.0B), and Wholesale & Retail (\~\$1.5B, see Report). Therefore, we expect the most upside capture across segments to be Secondary Ticketing, Online Travel and Lodging, Digital Advertising, Rideshare and Food Delivery, Advertising and Media, Airports, Car Rentals, Restaurants and Consumer. Our Lodging Research team estimates \$910M in incremental US hotel room revenue connected to the World Cup, translating to 30-40bps of RevPAR growth. Further, host city RevPAR should increase 7-25% in June-July 2026 (see Report). For Advertising, as of our Internet team's report on the World Cup, they expect \$5B of incremental global advertising spending, of which \$4B should flow to digital channels (73% digital penetration). Host Country Performance. During the 2022 World Cup, Qatar had a mixed experience given Covid-era travel hesitancy, limited hotel infrastructure, geographic location/lack of tourism multiplier. US/Canada/Mexico are better positioned to benefit from the event given a more developed ecosystem to absorb tourists. From an equity performance perspective, host-country equity returns around recent World Cups have been mixed: Qatar +10% (2022), Russia +5.4% (2018), and Brazil -19.5% (2014), all in USD and proxied off MSCI country indices relative to MSCI ACWI (see Figure 5). That said, the median host-country return sits at roughly +10% the year they host, likely underpinned by persistent tourism effects, stronger consumer sentiment, and scope for higher FDI/investment inflows.
