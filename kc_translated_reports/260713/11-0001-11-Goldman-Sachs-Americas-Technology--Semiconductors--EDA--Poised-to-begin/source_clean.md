## Chip design labor shortage could drive a material EDA revenue opportunity through 2030

Bottom Line: The semiconductor industry has a long-standing labor problem, with the world producing new chip designers at just 2%-3% a year—yielding \~255k engineers by 2030—relative to the 325k design engineers we estimate the industry will require to service surging AI-driven demand.

According to a 2022 industry workforce study by the Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA) and Boston Consulting Group (BCG), as of 2021 the industry already expected a shortfall of roughly 23,000 chip designers by 2030. Since that study (which predates the 2023 generative AI boom), hyperscalers and AI labs have quickly built their own chip programs, while merchant chipmakers have cut design cycles from two years to one. Layering in that incremental demand implies a global shortage of \~70k designers by 2030 based on our estimates. As AI design tools are the only supply response available in the short term, our estimates suggest they could augment the industry by \~48k engineer-equivalents by 2030—enough to cover the shortage the industry had five years ago, but not the demand the AI boom has since created. We believe Cadence and Synopsys—the two market leaders in EDA tools—are best positioned to convert a structural labor deficit into a new revenue stream—roughly \~\$3.7bn of incremental, consumption-priced AI software by 2030 (\~\$11bn in cumulative revenue in 2026-30), on top of a \~\$14bn base of core EDA revenues, none of which is reflected in Street numbers today.

We expect a material AI revenue opportunity for EDA software, even on conservative assumptions

Bottom Line: Our model assumptions are deliberately conservative despite the significant upside potential offered by AI tools in an industry constrained by labor supply. Even so, our estimates equate to \$3.7bn of incremental annualized AI revenue by 2030.

Our analysis begins with the global population of chip designers, where we isolate the roles AI tools can already help augment (such as verification, physical design, RTL) and exclude what they still cannot. We quantify two distinct revenue layers: (1) AI augmentation or copilot-style tools for existing human engineers (adoption starts at 20%-40% of leading-edge designs where AI is already used today, priced at \~\$25k per engineer, or a \~20% pricing uplift on AI-enhanced renewals already observed); (2) Autonomous AI agents that compensate for the engineers who cannot be hired (we assume agents perform just 30% of that unfilled work by 2030, priced at \~\$50k per “virtual engineer,” or less than half of the implied value capture). Our forecast relies on what we believe are relatively modest, real-world assumptions (e.g., partial adoption, value-price pricing) yet comfortably compounds to a \~\$3.7B annual market by 2030.

## Still priced for pre-AI growth — despite the AI inflection now in sight

Bottom Line: We believe the market has largely treated EDA companies as bystanders in the AI trade—as defensive, stable growers at best and as victims of AI disruption at worst—with the SOXX delivering a \~140% TTM return while CDNS gained \~20% and SNPS traded sideways. We now see a major catalyst on the horizon as agentic AI products enter the market in the coming quarters.

According to Street estimates (Visible Alpha), Cadence is seemingly modeled on the same growth algorithm it has been—\~8% volume growth plus \~6% pricing, decelerating to \~13% by 2028—with AI apparently driving zero contribution (Exhibit 9). Layering in our AI upside estimates could accelerate revenue growth to the mid-to-high 20% range for CDNS and \~20% for SNPS, driving incremental EPS of \~\$2.30 and \~\$2.20 by 2030, respectively. Critically, this revenue would reflect sustainable, high-quality growth from consumption-priced software amid a structural labor shortage, delivered through the same incumbent tools the industry already uses on a daily basis. In addition, we believe agents would only work in conjunction with the vendors' own simulation and signoff engines. With CDNS trading at \~45X NTM EPS (and SNPS at \~35X) on the assumption that neither grows significantly faster than mid-double digits in the medium term, we expect significant upside to both names if the first evidence of AI adoption and monetization is demonstrated in the coming quarters.
