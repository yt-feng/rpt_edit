# China Restaurants: Luckin/Chagee initiation feedback; addressing key investor questions

Following our initiations on the two freshly made drink players Luckin and Chagee on Jun 22, we held numerous marketing meetings with investors. Investors generally agreed on the growth opportunity in China's freshly made drink sector where penetration remains low, and the DD% CAGR makes it an outperforming sector in the consumer space. For our Buy-rated name Luckin, investors also view the margin turnaround story as an attractive catalyst for 2H26; we also saw investor interest on Chagee if the SSSG improvement continues. The key investor push-back centers on the weak overall consumption, near-term SSSG pressure from last year's high base, competition and pricing risks, especially in the freshly made coffee category. Meanwhile, raw material cost and by-tier-of-city opportunity were also among the frequently asked questions. In this report, we provide analysis on these investor questions.

While we acknowledge freshly made drink companies face tough SSSG bases in 2Q-3Q, multiple brands have managed to deliver relatively resilient SSSG performance into high bases (e.g. ChaPanda Jun SSSG improved to slight LSD% decline, Guming SSSG in Jun was also resilient at slightly LSD% decline), vs. market expectation already lowered. 3Q remained a high base quarter so investors may still stay at sidelines in the near-term to see the sustainability of the resilient performance from 2Q and competition landscape. For Luckin, we expect SSSG of -5% in 2Q26 (vs. market expectation skewed towards HSD% decline); pricing competition remains rational and 2H margin set-up is favorable (delivery mix decline from 35%-45% last 2H, more favorable coffee bean price impact kicking in), supporting the company to deliver earnings growth outperformance in 2H. ## Competition in coffee market

With multiple leading freshly made tea players entering the fast-growing freshly made coffee market, we are seeing investor concerns on stepped-up competition. However, we note that: 1) the expansion and pricing remains largely rational, and coffee is a supplement to the stores instead of the major contributor. For Guming, which stands out in coffee roll out, the company targets for $20\% / 25\%$ coffee contribution in 2026-27E; the coffee coupons offered by the brand are also disciplined, i.e. one Rmb9.9 coupon per week, compared to Cotti's aggressive full menu Rmb8.8-9.9 campaign in 2023-early 2026; 2) coffee scale of freshly made tea players remains relatively small, compared to when Cotti entered the market (aggressive store expansion, and Luckin's scale is smaller compared to today). Based on our estimation, if we assume $20\%$ coffee mix (for stores introducing coffee machines) for Guming in 2026E, it translates to Rmb8bn coffee GMV, or low teens of Luckin's scale (vs. Cotti's GMV reached $17\%$ of Luckin's as of 2023 per CIC). The progress of coffee roll out for other freshly made tea brands remains meaningfully behind that of Guming. 3) Store location wise, the competition between Luckin and freshly made tea brands is not head-to-head, given Luckin's stores are high tier city skewed ( $16\% / 44\%$ stores located in tier 1/tier 1+new tier 1 cities, compared to Guming at $3\% / 16\%$ ).

Meanwhile, we note Cotti, which had fierce competition in store expansion and pricing with Luckin in 2023-24, has seen pressure YTD. On store expansion, Cotti has turned to net closures in some recent months, and gross openings were also meaningfully slower compared to last year when delivery subsidy boosted sales, according to Hongcan (Exhibit 2). News reported that Cotti removed some of the franchisee supporting policies YTD. Given the weaker store UE (according to channel checks, around half of Cotti's stores didn't generate profit in 2024 before the delivery subsidy), if Cotti turns to continuous store net closures, this would create more opportunities for other players such as Luckin and Guming.

## Pricing trend

With the goal to educate customers, we note freshly made tea brands have been offering attractive prices for coffee products, and investors are concerned that there will be further price competition emerging in the sector. While we acknowledge that the favorable prices are likely to last to acquire customers, the magnitude of promotion remains disciplined — e.g. for Guming, Rmb9.9 coupon is offered on weekly basis; for Luckin, the two week Rmb9.9 campaign covers select SKUs. The overall ASP trend for most of the leading freshly made drink brands is also largely stable according to Zhaimen (Exhibit 4).

We view possibility for large scale price competition is relatively low, given: 1) The past experience suggests such competition is not sustainable and could pressure SSSG performance due to lower ASP (e.g. in 2024, Luckin's SSSG decline was mainly driven by ASP). 2) Product quality/differentiation matters more for freshly made drink players to gain share, instead of purely low price. In fact, ingredient/product upgrade is among the focus areas for leading players this year. 3) With primary market investment trending down (total amount of financing was at less than Rmb2bn/1bn in 2023/24, compared to Rmb8bn in 2021 per ITJuzi/Yilantop), the resources for new players to enter with

aggressive pricing is limited. That said, if irrational pricing competition emerges and players follow to defend market share, we see downside risk to our forecasts.

## Q2: Near term SSSG amid tough base

The near term SSSG pressure and lower visibility from last year's high base is among the key pushbacks from investors to position in freshly made drink names. We acknowledge that the high base from late Apr brings SSSG yoy pressure in the near term, but investor expectations have also turned conservative (based on our marketing feedback, for Luckin, investors look at MSD-HSD% SSSG decline in 2Q vs. GSe of -5%, and HSD%-low teens% SSSG decline in 3Q vs. GSe of -8%). Despite the high base and weather headwind in Jun, multiple freshly made drink brands saw resilient performance — ChaPanda SSSG improved in Jun to LSD% yoy decline supported by better-than-expected roll out of coffee along with marketing campaigns; similarly, Guming also had resilient SSSG at LSD% yoy decline performance in Jun partially supported by promotion calendar shift. For Luckin, while channel checks suggest SSSG in Jun was lower than Apr-May which in our view was due to higher base and slightly lower ASP from the Rmb9.9 campaign, volume boost from the campaign and new products (e.g. new drinking method for butter latte) should provide some support. For Chagee, new products supported sequentially better performance in Jun, in terms of both GMV and SSSG; the company has low base in 2Q-3Q given it refrained from participating in delivery subsidy, and new product launch is muted last year vs. rich pipeline for this year's peak season.

Looking ahead, while Jul-Aug continues to see tough base for most FMD brands from last year's delivery subsidy, for players able to deliver resilient SSSG and earnings performance in 1H/2Q26, it would give better earnings visibility to 2H especially after Jul-Aug when base peaks out. We believe category expansion and successful new products would be drivers for SSSG resiliency amid high base. Meanwhile, into May-Jun, multiple brands noted sequential and yoy delivery subsidy mix decline. Lower delivery mix is a tailwind for store level profitability, and DTC-skewed player Luckin would see higher margin sensitivity to delivery mix change.

## Q3: Opportunity by tier of cities

Regarding Luckin's 55k store upside in ML China (based on $15\%$ market share in FMD), we received investors' questions on the by city tier opportunities. We see penetration opportunity in both high tier and low tier cities:

In tier 1 cities where Luckin reached high density level, Luckin has \~9 annual cup consumption per capita in 2025. Affordability in high tier cities is high at \~0.2x hourly wage, a comparable level to CVS coffee in Taiwan and Starbucks in US. Using this as benchmark (high teens-low 20s cups per capita consumption), we think Luckin still has room to double the store count in tier 1 cities. We also view the competitive landscape with other freshly made drink players as relatively favorable in tier 1 cities, where the leading freshly made tea brands have relatively small exposure. In new tier 1 and tier 2 cities, annual per capita consumption is at 6/9 cups, and we also see meaningful growth opportunity with coffee consumption habit established.

In lower tier cities, the penetration is at low level, i.e. 2 cups annual consumption for Luckin. It is a bigger penetration opportunity in the longer term; that said, pace of store expansion is subject to the progress of customer education on coffee consumption. Competition wise, leading freshly made tea brands (e.g. Mixue, Guming) stores are skewed towards lower tier cities. But Luckin's diversified coffee offerings and brand awareness in coffee would enable it to capture the lower tier city coffee penetration opportunity; meanwhile, the non-coffee category expansion helps to attract customers that haven't established coffee consumption habit.

## Q4: Raw material price trend and impact

Raw material price trend is relatively favorable yoy, or stable (e.g. milk which is a major raw material for Luckin and Chagee) except for packaging-related oil by-products. For Luckin, coffee beans take the largest portion in raw material cost at 30%-40% mix. Coffee bean price started to drop in late 2025 (while it has picked up recently, the spot price is \~20% below the level in 2025), with \~6 months inventory, cost tailwind would be more meaningful in 2H if coffee bean price does not further pick up. For the cost inflation in PET, we expect the impact to be relatively small on Luckin as total packaging material takes up 5D% in raw material cost; sugar mix in raw materials is also small at LSD%. For Chagee, milk and tea are the major raw materials; PET cost mix is small with the brand's relatively high price, and sugar mix is also relatively low. However, we need to watch the jasmine tea price (the key production base is located in Guangxi which was impacted by floods), which is a key raw material for its star product Boya jasmine tea (over 40% GMV contribution earlier this year).

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Luckin Coffee

We are Buy rated on Luckin with a 12-m TP of HK\$49, based on 21x 2026 P/E. Key risks include: 1) Data privacy risks, 2) Market competition, 3) Weaker-than-expected pricing, 4) Raw material cost inflation, 5) Delivery mix impact on margin, 6) SSSG dilution with store network expansion, 7) Food safety.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Chagee Holdings

We are Neutral-rated on Chagee with a 12-month target price of US\$13.6, based on 10x 2026E P/E. Our target P/E multiple of 10x is benchmarked against the trading P/E multiples of Jiumaojiu and Helens, both of which have experienced earnings downcycles as their brand upcycles moderated. Key risks include: 1) stronger- or weaker-than-expected product cycle and innovation; 2) faster- or slower-than-expected store network expansion in China and overseas; 3) delivery subsidies; 4) margin and profitability; and 5) brand reputation and food safety risks.
