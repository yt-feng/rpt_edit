# JPM
# Copper Dashboard

Global inventory eases on China destocking; tariff front running continues as US stocks continue to build; remain constructive on most copper miners

Key takeaways: (1) Global visible copper inventories have fallen to 1.05Mt from 1.37Mt in March - SHFE + China bonded warehouse destocking has been the main driver (down 450kt to \~120kt), while US located LME & COMEX inventory has continued to rise on tariff front-running (now at \~725kt or \~70% of the global total). Overall, ex-US-based inventory has normalized back to 2025 levels. (2) China demand was flat YTD through May, with higher refined production offset by lower imports; global mined production was up a modest 1.4% YTD through April. (3) High-frequency indicators appear positive with China cathode premia strong and LME canceled warrants elevated. Copper prices have remained resilient at around \$6-6.2/lb (+8% YTD), with China dip-buying and US tariff front running appearing to be stronger price drivers than fundamental demand. Price risks going forward seem to hinge on tariff outcomes (certainty vs higher tariffs in the future that could drive further US inventory build or de-stocking). We are OW on CSC/SFR/BHP/RIO/S32 in Australia and prefer Freeport (OW) in North America. Within EMEA, we upgraded Antofagasta to OW, owing to its peer-leading copper volume growth (\~30% to 2028E), FCF and ROCE inflection, as well as improving valuation (link). We are UW on Anglo American and recently placed it on Negative Catalyst Watch for 2Q production results (23 July) as we see the greatest cost-driven risks due to its unique exposure to high Brazil and South Africa iron ore freight rates and weakness in diamonds (link). For both Anglo American and Glencore (44%/44% owners of Collahuasi), an update on the suspension of Collahuasi's desalination plant carries tail risks, as we believe a prolonged suspension could have implications for copper recoveries in H2'26 or 2027.

JPM's Head of Base & Precious Metals Research, Greg Shearer, noted that while tight mine supply and structurally supported demand keep the medium-term backdrop supportive, at \~\$13,600/mt, up more than 40% YoY and against a surplus global refined market of over 500 kmt in 2025, it is hard to argue copper prices look cheap. The key 2H26 catalyst is policy, not balances: the market is effectively in a US-China tug of war, with the US having pulled metal via an attractive COMEX/LME arb since early 2025, building a \~1.2 mmt stockpile in 18 months - tightening the ex-US market and forcing China's buying floor materially higher toward \$13,500/mt. On tariffs, the team believes the Trump administration will pursue a phased, escalating Section 232 tariff on refined copper cathode imports, likely 15% starting January 2027, escalating to 30% in January 2028 - designed to keep imported copper onshore as a critical reserve. As this tug of war continues, the team expects persistently higher floors and ceilings on a push towards \$15,000/mt, with risks skewed towards a very bullish pinch point driven by rapidly dwindling ex-US inventories. See Greg and the team's latest note here: Copper: Where we're going, we don't need balances and Base Metals Supply Demand Tracker.
