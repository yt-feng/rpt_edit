# June 2026: Domestic showing early bottoming out signs with easing comps, resilient overseas on share gains

Domestic appliances data started to show sequential improvements in June on a lower base: 1) NBS home appliances and electronics retail sales fell by 9% yoy in Jun (vs. -16% yoy in May), showing sequential improvements as a lower comp base kicks in; 2) AC domestic ex-factory shipment came in better than feared at -7% yoy (vs. -23% in the previous production plan as compiled by IOL) aided by sequentially better pricing. Going forward, we expect domestic demand growth to gradually recover with diminishing drags from front-loaded demand against easing comps (retail sales +15%/-18% yoy in 3Q25/4Q25 vs. +40% yoy in 2Q25 per NBS). That said, high frequency data suggests it remains difficult to pass through all the cost inflation pressure through price hikes, in the absence of a strong demand recovery.

Appliances exports remained resilient and beat IOL and market expectations in June. Overall appliances exports growth further accelerated to DD% growth in Jun vs. HSD% in May. Though AC exports decline widened slightly mom, the magnitude came narrower than previous production plan. Consumer sentiment in the US/Europe and US housing sales data also demonstrated sequential improvements recently, consistent with a narrower appliances' shipment decline in the US in 2Q compiled by AHAM. Going forward, we expect continued robust growth momentum on the back of continued market share gains via innovative/competitive products, as shown from the recent popularity of Chinese ACs amid Europe's heatwave.

On cyclical factors, shipping rates further increased in the past month. Key commodity costs pulled back in the past month. Regarding stock ideas, we continue to favor Buy-rated Midea (A/H) as we see limited downside risk protected by shareholder returns and upside potential from overseas markets and emerging business. We continue to hold a constructive view on Ninebot for its growth potential from a comprehensive product line-up, including share gains in the domestic E2W market, structural growth of robotic lawn mowers and overseas E2W potential, against a near historical low valuation. We turned more constructive on Roborock as we expect faster revenue growth driven by continuous global market share gains, new product expansion esp. wet dry vacuums and margin recovery after business strategy adjustment esp. in China on the back of sequentially easing competition.

## Ex-factory shipments decline came better than expected in June

According to China Industry Online (IOL), AC total ex-factory shipments decline narrowed to -7% yoy in Jun (vs. -11% yoy in May), with narrower decline from domestic (-7% yoy in Jun vs. -15% yoy in May) but weaker growth from exports (-7% yoy in Jun vs. -4% yoy in May). Compared to previous production plan, both domestic and exports came above (vs. -23% and -21% yoy of previous production plan respectively). Similar to previous months, we note that overall exports by Chinese players should be greater than that indicated by IOL export data as leading companies such as Midea also export from their overseas factories.

Domestic shipment came in better than expected by IOL in Jun, likely supported by better demand as we noticed sequentially higher AC discounts during 618. Going forward, IOL production plan suggests SD% yoy decline in 3Q26, a narrower pace vs. 2Q26.

Exports remained resilient and came in better than expected in Jun, driven by Chinese players' continued share gains in our view. In particular, the surging demand for Chinese ACs amid the current heatwave in Europe has been a key investor focus, contributing to company sales in both 2Q-3Q. For example, Midea mentioned $70\%+$ yoy growth for its AC sales in France, Spain, Germany and the United Kingdom in 1H26 was driven by the blockbuster product PortaSplit. Property sales data (Exhibit 12) in DM and consumer sentiment (Exhibit 11) both showed sequential improvements in the past month. US appliances shipment decline also narrowed in 2Q26 (-3% yoy vs. -8% yoy in 1Q per AHAM). Going forward, the current IOL production plan forecasts a similar exports growth in 3Q26 as 2Q.

By brand, Haier and Hisense outpaced the industry in terms of total shipments growth, while Gree and Midea lagged. Haier's total AC ex-factory shipments were up by 8% yoy, underpinned by +4%/+23% domestic/exports growth. Hisense's total AC ex-factory shipments decreased by 3% yoy, underpinned by +12%/-17% domestic/exports growth. Midea's total AC ex-factory shipments decreased by 11% yoy, with larger decline of both domestic and exports (-11% and -10% yoy). Gree's total AC ex-factory shipments decreased by 13% yoy, with above-industry domestic and exports (-26% and -25% yoy).

Total ex-factory shipment volume growth for washing machines/refrigerators demonstrated mixed trends in May.

## Retail sales started to show sequential improvements with easing comps

Retail sales decline narrowed mom in Jun on a lower base. NBS home appliances and electronics retail sales decreased by 9% yoy in Jun, improving from -16% yoy in May.

Product wise, while most categories continued to record yoy declines in June, RVC relatively outperformed with positive growth in both online and offline channels. Channel wise, more categories showed better online growth vs. offline in June, similar to previous months.

Regarding pricing, ASP growth continued to show mixed trends in June. Select small appliances such as pressure cooker showed positive ASP growth in both online and offline channels, while select white goods such as AC and washing machine exhibited ASP growth pressure yoy.

Brand-wise, Midea, Xiaomi and Haier showed mom market share increase in the AC market in Jun, while Gree lost share mom. In cleaning appliances, Roborock maintained market leadership, and Ecovacs gained share mom.

## Overseas RVC and robotic lawn mower growth remained robust

RVC industry app downloads growth (a proxy of retail demand growth) in overseas markets accelerated to +56% yoy in Jun (vs. +38% yoy in May). By region, Americas, Europe and APAC all showed accelerated growth (Americas/Europe/APAC +76%/+57%/+30% yoy in Jun vs. +36%/+43%/+30% yoy in May). Roborock overseas downloads growth accelerated to +64% yoy in Jun (vs. +54% yoy in May), still faster than the industry average. By region, Roborock gained shares in the Americas and Europe (+6ppt/+1ppt yoy to 25%/30%), while its shares in APAC pulled back but remained the market leader (-2ppt yoy to 33%).

Amazon US RVC sales data showed notable mom acceleration in June (+131% yoy in June vs. -16% yoy in May) likely supported by earlier Prime Day (in June this year vs. July last year). Roborock, Ecovacs, Dreame and iRobot gained share, while Eufy and Shark relatively underperformed.

Based on overseas lawn mowers app download data (tracked by Sensor Tower), robotic lawn mower demand remained solid with DD% growth and Ninebot's continued market share gains with 200%+ growth in Jun.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology

## Midea

Valuation: Our 12-m TPs (A/H-share) of Rmb98/HK\$113 are based on a 16x exit multiple applied to our 2028E EPS, discounted back to 2027E using a 9.5% cost of equity.

Key risks: 1) Worse-than-expected disruption on white goods demand from weaker macro globally; 2) rising material costs affecting product margins; 3) execution risk of its premiumization strategy; and 4) rising competition in the low-to-mid-end segment.

## Ninebot

Valuation: Our 12-m target price of Rmb62 is based on a 16x exit P/E multiple applied to our 2028E EPS and discounted back to 2027E using a 9.5% cost of equity.

Key risks: 1) Decline in disposable income and consumer confidence from weaker macro conditions; 2) slower-than-expected product launches or new category expansion; 3) intensifying competition in domestic/overseas markets; 4) potential tariffs/anti-dumping duties may reduce profitability; and 5) higher-than-expected raw materials cost.

## Roborock

Valuation: Our 12-m TP of Rmb170 is based on a 17x exit P/E multiple applied to our 2028E EPS and discounted back to 2027E using a 9.5% cost of equity.

Key risks: 1) Faster demand recovery supported by better-than-expected macro conditions; 2) better-than-expected product development/expansion; and 3) easing competition.
