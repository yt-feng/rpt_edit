# Kweichow Moutai Co - A

# Second price hike in 2026 and our take

What happened: Kweichow Moutai announced a price increase for Feitian Moutai 53% vol 500ml (2026 vintage) over the weekend, effective July 18, 2026. The ex-factory price will rise from Rmb1,269 to Rmb1,369 per bottle (+Rmb100/+7.9%), and the self-operated retail price on iMoutai will also increase from Rmb1,539 to Rmb1,639 (+Rmb100/+6.5%). This is the second price hike in 2026, following the March 30 announcement of a Rmb100/8.5% ex-factory increase and a Rmb40/+2.7% iMoutai increase (see our note).

\- Pricing power on display. Moutai is raising prices for the second time during the off-season—a contrarian move that underscores its exceptional control over demand-supply dynamics and pricing autonomy. The new pricing is broadly in line with prevailing distribution levels, reflecting a calibrated “market-following” approach. With Feitian consistently selling out on iMoutai even at the old price, we view this as a structural signal that gradual, sustained price increases are likely to continue. Moutai’s pricing power is reinforced by disciplined volume control and the ongoing build-out of DTC channels. The message is clear: pricing will likely move in measured, steady increments, supported by the company’s ability to manage supply proactively.

\- Ripple effects across the value chain. The increase should lift Moutai's margins from 3Q26, while also boosting the embedded value of aged inventory as older vintages reprice upward in tandem. Notably, this hike is symmetric (both ex-factory and iMoutai prices +Rmb100), versus the March adjustment, which was asymmetric (ex-factory up more than self-operated retail). Price adjustments also appear to be coming at a faster cadence; as a result, hoarding becomes more about timing the market and absorbing higher volatility - harder, riskier, and less appealing. This may discourage speculative behavior, improve inventory health, and support more demand driven by consumption rather than trading.

\- Implications for the sector and other baijiu brands. The distribution price for Feitian Moutai improved to Rmb1,700 (20 Jul) from Rmb1,660 (17 Jul, ahead of the announcement). Moutai's share price rose $6\%$ on the first trading day after the announcement, while Wuliangye, Luzhou Laojiao, Fen Wine, and Yanghe gained $3\%-6\%$ . As the industry leader, Feitian serves as the anchor for baijiu pricing: in practice, other distilleries typically gain room to reprice only after Feitian moves up. If the anchor price is supported by real demand, it also reduces the risk of renewed, cascading price pressure on sub-premium categories. That said, this is a necessary but not sufficient condition - over the weekend, the distribution prices of Wuliangye and Laojiao did not rise in tandem with Moutai. We believe other brands will need more time to work through inventory, rebuild pricing power, and re-accelerate growth, as overall demand remains lukewarm.

# Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks

Kweichow Moutai Co - A (Neutral; Price Target: Rmb1,420.00)

## Investment Thesis

We are Neutral on Moutai. Moutai is leading the baijiu sector through a pivotal shift from cyclical contraction to stabilization. Its direct-to-consumer (DTC) transformation has broadened mass-premium reach, improved pricing transparency, and reinforced brand equity. Current valuation appears fair against a soft macro backdrop and China consumer staples'\~15x average P/E. A sustainable re-rating likely requires: 1) continued DTC traction (next key demand window: Mid-Autumn Festival and National Day holidays) and 2) clearer evidence of a household wealth recovery, particularly linked to property and equity markets.

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

Downside risks to our rating and price target include: 1) worse-than-expected market demand for premium baijiu due to regulatory tightening/a consumption slowdown; 2) slower-than-expected ex-factory price hikes for Feitian; 3) a potential hike in the liquor tax rate; and 4) a potential social responsibility burden levied by local government on large SOEs.

Upside risks include: 1) further DTC penetration; 2) a stronger-than-expected recovery in household wealth spurring premium demand; and 3) a recovery in spending on premium baijiu.
