# Canada Economics Comment: New US Tariff Threats Raise Downside Growth Risks, Strengthen Case for BoC to Remain on Hold
## MAIN POINTS:

1. The White House announced new tariffs on \$20bn worth of Canadian goods yesterday, which are due to take effect after a 30-day window on August 19 (Exhibit 1). The announced tariffs would raise the effective tariff rate on Canadian imports by around $2 \frac{1}{2} \mathrm{pp}$ .

2. Applying our estimated impulse from the 6pp increase in the effective tariff rate in 2025 to the announced tariffs, and adjusting for more limited retaliation, implies a 0.2-0.3pp drag on real GDP growth over the next 12 months. Our estimates incorporate a 0.2pp hit from weaker export growth and a 0.2pp drag on investment from heightened uncertainty, which are partly offset by Canadian consumers continuing to shift from US goods toward domestically-produced alternatives. Assuming Canadian retaliation remains limited, we see little upside risk to inflation. However, if Canada were to retaliate, our previous analysis suggests tariff increases would pass through to consumer prices within a few months.

3. It remains unclear whether the Trump administration will implement these tariffs at the end of the 30-day window (either in full or in part), especially given ongoing USMCA negotiations. In a Tuesday morning interview, US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer described the tariffs as a “targeted measure” in response to Canadian trade barriers and said USMCA negotiations would continue. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada “stands ready to intensify” negotiations in a prepared statement posted Monday evening. Carney did not signal plans to retaliate, marking a departure from Canada’s response to US tariff announcements last year.

4. The main implication for the Canadian outlook is that the escalation and added uncertainty are likely to weigh on investment and growth—whether or not the tariffs are implemented—thereby increasing dovish risks to the policy outlook.
