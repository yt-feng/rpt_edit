## Japan Aerospace & Defense: Basic policy framework for 2026 approved; focus on revision of three strategy documents toward year-end

On July 21, the Japanese government approved the Basic Policy on Economic and Fiscal Management and Reform 2026 (Honebuto 2026) via a Cabinet decision (Cabinet Office; in Japanese only). The framework is underpinned by the concept of responsible and proactive fiscal management, with key pillars including: advancing crisis management investment and growth investment to strengthen medium- to long-term growth potential; improving the budget formulation process and enhancing the predictability of the government budget to induce private investment; and building both a strong economy and fiscal sustainability. The policy outline includes the creation of a framework for growth investment based on 17 strategic investment areas, including AI and semiconductors, the defense industry and aerospace, and shipbuilding; the utilization of clear multi-year investment support measures; and the promotion of large-scale investment, R&D, and regulatory reforms that are not constrained by the principle of single-year budgets or supplementary budgets, but instead conceived and executed from a medium- to long-term perspective.

Particularly regarding national security, the policy explicitly states that deliberations on Japan's three security strategy documents (scheduled for revision by the end of 2026) will be accelerated to realize a transformation of defense capabilities within five years. To adapt to new forms of warfare, the policy outline also includes measures to advance the introduction of unmanned assets, stand-off defense capabilities, and enhanced utilization of data and AI. From the perspective of ensuring sustainable response capabilities, the policy states that considerations will be made for government involvement via GOCO (Government-Owned, Contractor-Operated) arrangements and other means for critical equipment that is difficult to procure stably, as well as to strengthening production capacity. The policy also touches on a framework in which the government takes a leading role in equipment transfers (exports), and the utilization of a Ministry of Defense version of the SBIR (Small Business Innovation Research) program. Additionally, it outlines plans to work on constructing a satellite constellation to strengthen space security.

This policy outline does not include any specific quantitative targets for defense spending. The focus ahead will be on the scale and funding sources for defense spending under the next five-year plan, to be incorporated into the three security strategy documents scheduled for revisions within 2026. ## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

## M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
