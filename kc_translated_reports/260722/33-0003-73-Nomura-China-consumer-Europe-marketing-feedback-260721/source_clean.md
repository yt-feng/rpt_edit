# Europe marketing feedback

European investors are selectively interested in luxury, shopping mall and sportswear names in China

China consumer segments still offer structural growth despite weak beta We met c.30 institutional investors across Europe over the past two weeks to discuss key sector trends and investment ideas in the China consumer space. Europe-based investors' interest in China consumer sectors remains relatively strong despite the mediocre fundamentals of general consumption in the country, which came as a slightly positive surprise to us; investors are especially interested in segments with structural growth opportunities such as luxury, shopping malls and sportswear.

\- European investors we talked to largely agree that China's weakening economic and consumption trends are concerning, limiting the upside of most consumer companies' 1H26 & FY26 results. Investors are interested in the potential consumption-related policy easing to be introduced, as the $15^{\text{th}}$ Five-year Plan for consumption expansion contains limited details on near-term support for the consumer sector.

\- European investors are also interested in the implications of China's property market on consumption, given the sector's early signals of stabilization (e.g., recovery in secondary housing transaction volume in Shanghai and Shenzhen, as well as comeback in the sales of luxury apartments in leading tier-1&2 cities); investors wonder if the negative wealth impact from the property market's five-year downcycle has finally come to an end.

\- For the luxury sector, European investors track the sales of European luxury companies' sales in China closely, thus our routine channel checks through leading Chinese shopping malls have been helpful. Laopu (6181 HK, Buy) is one name attracting most debate. Most investors are concerned about the drag of the decline in gold prices on Laopu's sales (see our report); some investors are also not convinced about Laopu's long-term strategy to become a legitimate Chinese premium brand given China's sluggish domestic demand growth. On the other hand, some investors are interested in Laopu's unique approach in penetrating into high-end shopping malls in China and the brand's growing presence in occupying Chinese wealthy/elite consumers' mind share; they are also looking for Laopu to open more boutiques in overseas markets for further brand-recognition building.

\- For sportswear brands, ANTA (2020 HK) was one of the names attracting the most interest of European investors. Some investors like the name for its solid on-the-ground channel operations, while some question ANTA's diversification efforts of owning too many brands, which they believe will not necessarily generate meaningful synergy and also lead to sales cannibalization across brands. Meanwhile, most investors we met hold a neutral-to-negative view towards the Puma acquisition, as they fear the deal will need a prolonged process to close, and that ANTA also does not have the operational know-how to defend/expand Puma's European and US businesses.

\- European investors we talked to generally have a negative view towards other consumption subsectors under our coverage including retail, catering and brewery; the major concern on the home appliance subsector comes from the payback of the trade-in programs in the domestic market.

## Preferred names in the consumer space

We maintain our preference for companies that demonstrate structural development strategies, visible margin trends, and attractive valuations (especially after the recent sector-wide share price weakness).

We continue to prefer ANTA in the sportswear sector and we like Laopu in the retail space, as we believe Laopu has attractive risk-reward, especially if spot gold prices stabilize in a more accommodative geopolitical environment.
