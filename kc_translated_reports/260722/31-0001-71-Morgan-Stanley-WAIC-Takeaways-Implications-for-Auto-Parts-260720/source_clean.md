# WAIC Takeaways – Implications for Auto Parts

WAIC 2026 presents opportunities for auto suppliers to enter the humanoid robot realm. Meanwhile, as competition intensifies, it's getting harder for suppliers to select customers, differentiate products and retain R&D talent.

The Shanghai World Artificial Intelligence Conference (WAIC) 2026, held on 17-20 July, was even more crowded than 2025's conference, with $300+$ humanoid robots on show this year vs. $150+$ last year. Here, we focus on implications for auto suppliers and challenges we foresee.

Challenge #1: Customer selection becomes more difficult. With the rising number of humanoid robots, it's getting harder to gauge which robot integrators will emerge as clear leaders. Winning orders from leading customers such as Tesla (TSLA.O, covered by Andrew Percoco) and Unitree remains tough, while for small-volume customers, suppliers' upfront R&D and capacity may be more profitably utilized elsewhere. We think auto parts suppliers are more favorably positioned – they can leverage existing R&D resources, CNC machining equipment, and material sourcing channels to serve robot customers, and thus incur less upfront investment.

Challenge #2: Product offerings are converging. Last year, humanoid suppliers tended to have an area of focus, but now they tend to offer as many products as possible. For example, Joyson (600699.SS) has introduced dexterous hands (TeleHand), in addition to AI head assembly, electronic skin, brain domain controllers, and solid liquid hybrid batteries. Sanhua (002050.SZ) has extended its product portfolio from actuators to hand modules and sensors, per management guidance. Zhongding (000887.SS, NC), Zhenyu (300953.SZ, NC), and Lens (300433.SZ, covered by Derrick Yang), for example, all make joints, hands, limbs, and sensors. The convergence of product offerings may intensify competition.

Challenge #3: R&D talent requires incentives to stay. For most listed auto parts suppliers, earnings and share prices came under pressure in 1H26 because of weak domestic auto demand, ongoing price competition, and rising material costs. However, on the opposite side, humanoid start-ups are attracting VC/PE investors at substantial valuations. This presents challenges for auto parts companies to retain talent, and we think it could also spur them to consider spinning off in-house humanoid teams and provide stock incentives.

Connecting earnings with dreams: For 2Q/1H26, OEM price cuts, material cost hikes, higher social insurance contributions, and FX losses could pressure earnings at many auto parts suppliers we cover. Investors appear attracted by the humanoid angle, but they are also concerned about near-term earnings. We prefer Minth (0425.HK; OW) to play the humanoid story, as it also has a solid earnings outlook (double-digit YoY growth for FY26, per guidance) to support valuation.

## Valuation Methodology and Risks

## Minth Group Limited (0425.HK)

Base case, derived from DCF valuation. Our key assumptions include a 13% WACC (15.3% cost of equity and 6.3% cost of debt), a 9% medium-term growth rate, and a 3% terminal growth rate.

## Risks to Upside

■ More new project/client wins than expected for its aluminium battery housing products

■ Faster-than-expected overseas market expansion

■ Humanoid project wins

## Risks to Downside

■ Significant long-term slowdown in China's PV market

■ Sluggish overseas market sales growth

■ Labor cost increases
