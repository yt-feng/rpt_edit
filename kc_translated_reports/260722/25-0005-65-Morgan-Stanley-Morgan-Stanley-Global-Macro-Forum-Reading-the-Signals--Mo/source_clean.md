June Inflation: Not a Messi Print

We forecast headline and core June PCE inflation of -0.08% and 0.17% m/m, respectively.

If so, the three- and six-month annualized rates of core PCE inflation rates as of June would be $3.00\%$ and $3.82\%$ , respectively, compared with $3.52\%$ and $4.14\%$ in May.

We maintain our out-of-consensus call for the Fed. Since the conflict in the Middle East began, financial markets have been pricing between one and two 25bp rate hikes by year-end. In our view, the Fed will see inflation running closer to $2.0\%$ on an annualized basis in the second half of the year, allowing them to stand pat. We maintain 50bp of cuts in 2027.

That said, renewed conflict in the Middle East in recent days has pushed WTI back toward \$80/bbl and remains a risk to our baseline outlook for further disinflation.

Section 232 and 301 tariff reviews remain ongoing.

AI-related demand effects could offset tariff disinflation.

## Key Takeaways

\- Early thoughts on Moonshot: Last week, Chinese AI startup Moonshot released Kimi K3, an open-weight large language model that the company claims delivers frontier-level performance at 40–70% lower cost than comparable US models. While these claims need to be validated and geopolitical, regulatory, and strategic considerations surrounding adoption of Chinese-developed models need to be kept in mind, this underscores the rapid pace of innovation outside the US and raises the possibility that leading-edge AI capabilities may become increasingly accessible at lower cost. The broader read-through is that continued improvements in model efficiency have the potential to reduce the cost of using AI to perform a wide range of economic activity, leading to the classic "Jevons Paradox" that can drive even greater demand for compute (and associated AI infrastructure).

\- Macro takeaways from the strong Q2 bank earnings: Economic and macro sentiment remains constructive. US consumer continues to be resilient – spending and credit are running better than expected. Commercial loan growth is broadening; consumer loan growth is positive but increasingly selective and overall credit trends remain benign with problem assets declining rather than migrating into losses. The AI investment cycle is still accelerating and the opportunity is spreading across the economy

\- Macro data takeaways – not a “Messi” print: June inflation is a clear signal about disinflation. We maintain our out-of-consensus call that the Fed will remain on hold this year and cutting twice next year. The Fed will see inflation running closer to 2.0% on an annualized basis in 2H26, allowing them to stand pat.

\- Treasury market view - Odyssey toward 2 percent inflation: The market-implied path for Fed policy prices is just under 50bp of rate hikes by March 2027 – in essence, putting over a 50% probability on a scenario that our economists think is a tail risk. Less tightening supports yield curve steepeners.
