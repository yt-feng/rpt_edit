## China Bubble & Brew
## 1H26 Preview: Tea brands hit the brakes, Luckin hits the gas

Markets proved perceptive again. Earlier this summer, even as the industry's same-store sales (SSS) only just edged into negative territory, valuations were already sliding sharply (Mixue/Guming down 23%/25% in 2QTD, versus the HSI down 1%). The market sniffed out two things early: sticky consumer habits and rising franchisee anxiety on weaker foot traffic. The market was right: Mixue's slowdown was more evident (link), and while we anticipated a guidance cut from Guming, the magnitude still surprised (FY26 net openings cut by 43%). Luckin was the only name that kept sprinting (JPMe: 4.7k net openings in 1H26) - surprised on the upside. We recommend investors revisit Guming towards late 2026 as visibility improves in 4Q (on a cleaner base), given its growth potential and competitiveness remain intact. New pecking order: Luckin (OW)> Chagee (OW) > Guming (OW)> Mixue (UW).

\- From rising tide to split screen. Unlike last year's “all boats rise” dynamic, we have observed a clear divergence YTD and a slower expansion pace of the “wave riders” - Guming and Mixue; we cut FY26 earnings by 5% and 11%, respectively - now 2% and 13% below consensus. We expect 2H to trend weaker than 1H on tougher comps (3Q the toughest) for both (Figure 1). To meet our FY26 estimates, Guming needs to net add another 1.2k stores in 2H26 (vs. +800 in 1H26), and SSS needs to stop getting worse in 3Q (JPMe: MSD deline) and stabilize in 4Q (JPMe: flat).

\- What's changed in consumer habits and franchisee mindset? Even as Alibaba and Meituan have cut subsidies sharply YTD, consumers are seemingly habituated to ordering via delivery apps. To steer orders to their own channels, brands are pulling multiple levers, including WeChat mini-program discounts, BOGO deals and “dine-in only” offerings. Don’t forget the franchisees - the real canaries in the coal mine - the topline pressure typically matters far more than any margin lift from a healthier order mix. We got the impression that existing franchisees are less willing to open a second store and refurbishing older stores feels less urgent - and this might explain the aggressive guidance cut from Guming, in our view.

## Luckin Coffee

## 2Q26 preview: Running through the rain

2Q26 may mark an earnings inflection for Luckin. Recall that last year, delivery subsidies boosted Luckin's revenue scale but significantly diluted margins. In 2026 YTD, third-party delivery subsidies appear to be unwinding faster than the industry had expected. To retain customers and migrate them back to 1P channels, Luckin has stepped up dine-in offerings and promotions, including “a whole coconut”, and has expanded the availability of specialty coffee across more stores. Luckin maintained a fast expansion pace during the quarter (JPMe: +2.2k to a total of 35.8k), while key competitors such as Cotti, Lucky Cup, Mixue and Guming have started to slow down. Our positive thesis remains anchored by (1) an early-mover advantage and leading coffee market share, (2) a structural cost advantage, and (3) multi-engine growth potential across premium/specialty coffee, RTD, and overseas markets. Luckin remains one of our top picks in the China FMD space.

\- What's new in 2Q26. In April, Luckin's smart roasting center in Qingdao commenced operations, adding annual roasting capacity of $55\mathrm{k}+$ tons with total investment of $\sim$ Rmb3bn. In the same month, Luckin officially announced Yibo Wang as the global brand ambassador for Luckin Coffee Instant (its RTD business). In May, Luckin launched a special Americano nationwide, signaling the shift of its fresh fruit-based beverage business from small-scale testing to a national rollout; the company also launched two specialty alcoholic drinks nationwide, marking its entry into the "slight buzz" segment. Luckin also partnered with Team Spain and Team Portugal for the 2026 FIFA World Cup.

\- Competition is easing, not intensifying. Among coffee retailers, Cotti stopped accepting franchise applications in provincial capitals starting in March, and Manner raised its SOE espresso drink prices by Rmb5 (20–25%) in April, from Rmb20–25 to Rmb25–30. Even as more bubble tea brands introduce coffee offerings, we believe Luckin is still gaining share - not only within coffee, but also across the broader freshly-made beverage category. As of May 31, 2026, cumulative sales of Luckin's non-coffee beverages exceeded Rmb20bn, with 22 star products each surpassing 100mn cups sold - among which its light Jasmine Milk Tea saw sales of more than 400mn cups.

\- 2Q26 preview and valuation. Store expansion pace remained fast at +2.2k (JPMe) in 2Q26 (vs 2.5k in 1Q26). We forecast 2Q26 revenue at Rmb15.6bn (+26% yoy) and gross margin at 61.8% (-1.0pp yoy). With the delivery mix normalizing to above 30% (slightly higher yoy), we estimate Luckin's 2Q26 non-GAAP net earnings to be Rmb1.4bn, +3% yoy. Our PT of US\$43 is based on a target 2027E P/E of 15x, in line with the target we use for Luckin's key competitor Guming. The stock has traded at a 16x 12-month rolling P/E on average since 2022.

## 2Q preview: SSS recovery on track; risk-reward attractive

Chagee has shown signs of stabilization YTD, with the domestic same-store sales (SSS) decline narrowing for a second consecutive quarter to LDD% in 2Q26 (JPMe). Since the beginning of the year, management has prioritized membership engagement and SSS improvement over expansion. Accelerated new-product launches and category expansion into gelato have added meaningful “newness” to the offering. Further product innovation (including higher gelato penetration) and a successful South Korea rollout create additional runway for growth both in China and overseas. With SSS recovery on track, a US\$150mn share repurchase program through Jun 2027 (c.7% of market cap) and potential cash dividends from 2H26 (JPMe: 6–7% yield), and the stock trading at just 8x FY26E P/E, we see an attractive risk-reward. Chagee remains one of our China FMD top picks.

\- Domestic same-store sales recovery on track. Chagee's domestic SSS decline narrowed to 16.1% yoy in 1Q26 from 25.5% in 4Q25, and momentum appears to have extended into 2Q26; we expect an LDD% decline, with average monthly GMV per store above RMB350k. An accelerated launch cadence of 1–2 new SKUs per month, together with the gelato launch in May, has supported SSS trends. The gelato business carries a similar gross margin versus drinks and has contributed up to 20% of total GMV in some pilot stores. While a nationwide rollout has not yet occurred due to higher equipment capex and ongoing unit economics validation, a broader rollout could drive an additional leg of growth if lower-cost equipment becomes available.

\- Improving franchisor–franchisee dynamics should support future growth. YTD, franchise store unit economics (UE) are generally healthy, with 12%–14% EBITDA margins and an \~20-month payback period, according to the company. Franchisees have continued to open new stores YTD after transitioning to a profit-sharing model in early 2026. Meanwhile, Chagee has been more cautious on expansion and has prioritized SSS recovery and member engagement, which we view as critical to a potential turnaround. One summer-season risk to flag: under the new scheme, Chagee guarantees a maximum 10% discount for franchise stores, so more aggressive subsidies from third-party delivery platforms could create downside risk to Chagee's revenue and gross margin.

\- 2Q26 preview and earnings revisions. Store openings pace has decelerated in the domestic market, from 318 in 2Q25 to fewer than 50 (JPMe) in 2Q26 as the company prioritizes SSS recovery. We forecast 2Q26 revenue at Rmb3.6bn, +8% yoy, and gross margin at 55%, +1.1pp yoy. We estimate Chagee's 2Q26 core net earnings at Rmb 401mn, -36% yoy. Our PT of US\$16.5 (from US\$15) is based on 10x 2027E P/E, around 0.5STD below its historical P/E mean. The stock has been trading at a 12x 12-month rolling P/E on average since listing (Mar 2025). The 2Q and 3Q25 results miss and guidance cut during 2025 triggered a de-rating cycle from over 20x to the current \~7-8x 12-month rolling P/E on consensus EPS. The current P/E looks attractive to us compared with Chagee's China consumer peers.

## 1H26 preview: 1H earnings safe; 2H to be slower

Investors have questioned Guming's 3.5k net opening target since the start of the quarter, and the share price fell 22% in 2Q26 (vs HSI -8%). As bears expected, management turned more cautious in mid-July and cut the full-year net opening target by c.40% to \~2k from 3.5k. With c.800 net openings in 1H, 2H net openings are now implied at \~1.2k. YTD, third-party delivery subsidies appear to be unwinding faster than the industry previously expected, and we suspect consumer behavior is relatively sticky - i.e., customers may not immediately shift back to dine-in even as couponing on Meituan and Taobao eases. To retain customers, brands may need to fund more offline discounts, which could pressure both topline growth and margins. That said, Guming remains the peer with better growth runway and visibility, in our view, supported by its regional densification strategy and stronger franchisor-franchisee relationship. That said, investors may continue to stay on the sidelines until the SSS decline ends (against a tough base), likely during or after 4Q26.

\- FY26 store expansion target cut to \~2k (from 3.5k). Management reduced FY26 net store addition guidance by c.40% to \~2k from the initial \~3.5k ±500. While the cut had largely been anticipated by the market, the magnitude was still a negative surprise. 1H openings were slower as the focus shifted toward upgrading and refreshing existing stores. In addition, franchisees' willingness to open new stores has been dampened by headwinds from the unwinding of third-party delivery subsidies. Nevertheless, same-store sales have remained relatively resilient at +15% in 1Q26 and a <5% decline in 2Q26 despite a tough comparison base. Management continues to guide to FY26 SSSG of -5% to +5%, in line with the initial outlook; we think this is achievable, but on its own may be insufficient to trigger a meaningful re-rating.

\- Product innovation and new categories remain key SSS growth drivers. Guming remains competitive on new product launches, with new SKUs introduced every 1–2 weeks. Coffee continues to drive organic growth, and management expects daily coffee volume to reach c.120 cups by year-end without incremental promotional support. Beyond beverages, the breakfast daypart and bakery are also contributing incremental SSS: the coffee + bakery combo generates \~Rmb300–400 during morning hours, and bakery alone adds \~Rmb100–200 per store per day. If bakery/roaster penetration accelerates from Aug, this could provide an additional LSD–MSD uplift to 2H26 SSS.

\- 1H26 preview and earnings revisions. We estimate 1H26 core net earnings at Rmb1.5bn (+32% yoy). With \~800 net store additions (+28% yoy) and relatively healthy SSSG (+15% in 1Q and <5% decline in 2Q), we forecast 1H26 revenue at Rmb7.3bn (+28% yoy). We cut our FY26 net store addition forecast from 3.5k to \~2k and also cautiously revise down 2027–2028 net store additions from 3.4k–3.5k to \~3.0k. As a result, our 2026–2028E EPS estimates are lowered by 5%–9%. Our new Jun-27 PT of HK\$26 (from HK\$38) is based on 15x 2027E P/E (previously 20x), in line with the multiple we apply to key competitor Luckin.

## Mixue Group - H

## 2026 a classic squeeze

As delivery subsidies taper off in 2026, Mixue Group has launched multiple initiatives, including (1) expanding its category offering (e.g., introducing coffee machines into Mixue stores), (2) upgrading ingredients (higher penetration of fresh milk), and (3) elevating brand positioning (e.g., Lucky Cup ambassadors). However, as we flagged on the cover page, listed companies typically face a classic squeeze when topline GMV softens, franchisees require greater support, and corporate spending rises simultaneously. A return to a higher-growth trajectory ultimately hinges on SSS recovery, which in turn depends on sustained product innovation, category expansion, and/or improved pricing power - none of which can materialize overnight. Adding to the challenge, the increasing convergence between coffee and tea may lead to internal dilution and intensify competition across banners; this is why we expect Lucky Cup's store network growth to stall in 2026. In our view, investors will likely want to see clearer mitigation of cannibalization risk, along with early signs of SSS and unit economics improvement, before revisiting the name.

\- Store expansion taking a breather. As third-party delivery subsidies unwind, franchisees are feeling the pain. Mixue's ability to expand is not in doubt, but the cost of doing so is the key question. Faced with the trade-off between store count growth and SSS, we believe management is likely to prioritize healthier SSS during the downcycle. We conservatively assume the Mixue brand will deliver \~3k net store openings in FY26, heavily front-loaded in 1H26. Average store count growth should moderate to the high teens in 2026 from 30% in 2025. The SSS needs to pick up or stabilize before the market can forecast faster expansion. Freshly brewed coffee is expected to be a core SSS driver, but YTD penetration appears to be tracking below expectations (JPMe: 2-3k stores out of 43k Mixue stores).

\- Cannibalization risk appears unavoidable. Our channel checks suggest franchisees of both Mixue and Lucky Cup are puzzled about Mixue's move into freshly brewed coffee. Both Mixue and Lucky Cup are heavily skewed towards lower-tier cities and counties in China, where consumer education around coffee remains well behind that of top-tier cities. Incremental capex for coffee machines (Rmb30–60k) could lengthen payback periods for Mixue stores. At the same time, Lucky Cup's unit economics could deteriorate if Mixue offers similar freshly brewed coffee in close proximity, particularly given Lucky Cup's less established brand perception among these two. In our view, the market will need to see a clearer and more differentiated strategy to alleviate cannibalization concerns.

\- 1H26 preview and valuation. We estimate a total net opening of 3,150 stores in 1H26 (much slower from 6,535 in 1H25). We forecast 1H26 revenue at Rmb16.5bn, +11% yoy, and gross margin at 29.3%, -2.3pp yoy. We estimate Mixue's 1H26 core net earnings to slightly decline by 2% yoy to Rmb2.7bn. Our PT of HK\$200 is based on a target 2027E P/E of 12x, in line with the China F&B peers' average. The stock trades at 12x 2026E P/E now (Jul 2026; vs. avg of 21x since IPO).

## Mixue Group - H (Underweight; Price Target: HK\$200.00)

## Investment Thesis

Mixue has a distinctive position in the China FMD sector – a combination of value proposition + low-tier penetration + scalability + logistics + IP (Snow King). However, Mixue's multi-year hyper-growth phase is ending, in our view, and the stock now faces a sharp slowdown as management signals a recalibration in 2026. Mixue will be in a growth to value transition in the next 12 months, facing both external headwinds (subsidy unwind) and internal structural headaches. We are Underweight on Mixue, supported by three key pillars: (1) muted growth (LMSD revenue and earnings CAGRs for 2025-28E), (2) margin squeeze, and (3) cannibalization risks.

## Valuation

Our Jun-27 PT of HK\$200 is based on a target 2027E P/E of 12x, in line with the China F&B peers' average. The stock trades at 12x 2026E P/E (Jul 2026; vs. avg of 21x since IPO).

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

Key upside risks to our rating and price target include: 1) new product launches and supply chain efforts to gain users and support store UE; 2) successful pass-through cost inflation to franchisees; 3) higher-than-expected revenue contribution from FULU Fresh Beer.

Downside risks include: 1) further margin pressure due to cost inflation; 2) higher-than-expected store closure rate of Lucky Cup due to cannibalization risk; 3) later turnaround of international markets.
