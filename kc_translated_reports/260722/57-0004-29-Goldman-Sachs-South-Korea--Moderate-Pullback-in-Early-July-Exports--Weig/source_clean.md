# South Korea: Moderate Pullback in Early July Exports, Weighed Down by Ex-chip Demand
## Bottom line:

Korea's per-workday exports in the first 20 days of July fell $2.3\%$ mom sa, pulling back after two months of gains. Total exports during the 20 days fell $5.4\%$ mom sa. While semiconductor exports stayed almost flat after nine months of strong gains, other tech exports dropped $11.2\%$ and non-tech exports declined $5.6\%$ . Exports to the US and Taiwan fell $10.4\%$ and $28.4\%$ respectively and together accounted for around two-thirds of the headline sequential declines. Total imports declined $4.9\%$ , and the trade surplus (not seasonally adjusted) narrowed to US\$12.2bn from last month's historical high of US\$17.4bn.

## Main points:

1. Korea's per-workday exports in the first 20 days of July fell $2.3\%$ mom sa, pulling back after two months of gains (Exhibit 1). Total exports during the 20 days fell $5.4\%$ mom sa, down from $+5.2\%$ the prior month. On a year-on-year basis, total exports during the 20 days grew $52.4\%$ , moderating for the second month from $60.2\%$ the prior month.

2. Semiconductor exports stayed almost flat (-0.3% mom sa) after nine months of strong gains. However, other tech exports dropped 11.2%, weighed down by a sharp contraction in computer exports by 23.1%. Non-tech exports declined 5.6%, on broad declines across major products including petroleum products, steel, autos and auto parts.

3. Exports to the US and Taiwan fell 10.4% and 28.4% mom sa respectively and together accounted for around two-thirds of the headline sequential declines. Exports to other Asian economies including Hong Kong, Malaysia and Japan were broadly weak, whereas exports to mainland China extended a 9-month increase at a moderate pace of 3.4%.

4. Total imports declined 4.9%, also pulling back after two months of gains. Imports of machinery and semiconductor manufacturing equipment rose, but the gains were offset by contraction in semiconductors, handsets and energy-related imports. The trade surplus (not seasonally adjusted) narrowed to US\$12.2bn from last month's historical high of US\$17.4bn.
