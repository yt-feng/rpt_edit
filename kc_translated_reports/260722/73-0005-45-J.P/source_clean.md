# Weichai Power - A/H

## Fireside chat and management update support the AIDC thesis; price reset creates an attractive entry point; 1H26 preview

Recent investor engagement around Weichai Power remains high despite recent price swings, as reflected in strong participation at the fireside chat with Phil Buller (JPM European Capital Goods Analyst, covering Siemens). The session, which drew over 130 sign-ups, focused on the global AIDC cycle, architecture shifts, and sector risk/reward—key themes directly relevant to Weichai's positioning and outlook. In addition, we highlight our update call with Weichai's management that provided incremental color on AIDC execution, orderbook trends, and margin outlook. Weichai's US market entry is progressing on track, as the 2.5MW gas engine product for AIDC was officially launched at the end of June, with customer validation and US EPA certification now underway and expected to take 2-3 months, i.e., by August. Initial shipments will follow certification, and Weichai is already engaging directly with major US data center customers. Weichai's flexible go-to-market approach – including both direct sales and partnerships – positions the company to capture incremental demand as US supply constraints persist. Last but not least, concerns about weak 1H26 and 2Q26 delivery have weighed on the share price in light of the soft domestic ICE truck sales in May/June. Against this backdrop, Weichai's parent company's 1H26 beat provides positive read-throughs as we note Weichai Group's revenue rose 22% Y/Y to over Rmb200B and data center product revenue was up 140% Y/Y.

Stock view: Since the early May peak, Weichai has corrected by c. 20-25%, underperforming MSCI China (-7%) over the same period. This pullback has been driven in part by investor concerns around 1H26 delivery and the risk of a miss versus expectations, as well as broader debate on cycle sustainability and overcapacity. Despite the recent pullback, Weichai has still recorded c. 50% gains on average YTD (A/H), outperforming MSCI China (-12%) and ranking as a top performer in the China industrials universe. At current levels, our price target of Rmb49 for the A shares and HK\$52 for the H shares implies 80% and 75% upside, respectively. The stock trades at c. 12x FY27E P/E with a current yield of c. 4%. We view the current reset as an opportunity, with upside to our price target supported by a more resilient delivery profile, margin discipline, and a multi-year growth runway.

He pointed to persistent supply bottlenecks, robust order growth, and the structural nature of the data center power upcycle as evidence that the risk of true overcapacity remains a post-2030 issue. \- The architecture shift in data centers is driving a structural demand upcycle. Phil's analysis points to a step-change in data center design, with rack power density expected to rise from \~10kW historically to 50kW today and potentially 600kW+ by decade-end. This transformation is creating hardware bottlenecks and sharply higher power requirements, forcing hyperscalers to rethink capex and power planning. The long-term US target of 320GW (nearly one-third of current US electricity consumption) underpins a multi-year demand cycle for power equipment, with implications for both global and China-based suppliers. Phil highlighted that order growth remains robust, with European equipment firms reporting strong data center-related order momentum and fundamentals outpacing market pricing.

\- Order backlogs and pricing power are holding up, with overcapacity risk a post-2030 issue. US data center power demand is poised to grow at a c.22% CAGR, with supply still lagging orders—last year saw c55GW delivered vs. c100GW new orders, and this year 110–120GW new orders vs. 64–65GW deliveries. Phil’s view is that backlogs will likely keep expanding, supporting pricing power for leading suppliers. The overcapacity debate is nuanced: while market volatility reflects concerns about supply catching up, the risk of true overcapacity is more of a post-2030 issue, especially for high-value large-frame turbines and engines.

Phil argued that the combination of architecture-driven demand, grid bottlenecks, and hyperscaler capex cycles will support continued growth and pricing power for leading suppliers. ## Weichai's latest update demonstrates that strategic progress and execution remain on track

The most recent management call suggests continued progress on AIDC execution, with shipment guidance reaffirmed and the US gas engine certification process advancing as planned. The update also highlighted Weichai's flexible go-to-market strategy in the US, ongoing capacity expansion, and a robust order pipeline with top-tier data center customers.

\- AIDC execution and product roadmap are supporting robust growth and order visibility. Weichai is tracking in line with its 2026 AIDC shipment guidance of 3,500–4,000 units, with 1H26 deliveries and a back-half weighted ramp supported by a strong order pipeline. Diesel backup engines remain the near-term volume anchor, while gas engines are driving higher profitability (GPM 40–50%) and have already secured significant 2027 orders with leading cloud customers. The 2.5MW gas engine is now positioned as the mainstream data center solution, with US certification underway, and the 5.5MW model is set to scale from 2027.

\- Capacity expansion and flexible manufacturing are positioning Weichai to capture incremental demand. The company is on track to reach 8,000-10,000 units of AIDC-related capacity by end-2027, with flexible lines supporting both diesel and gas products. SOFC capacity is also ramping, with 30MW in 2026 and 200MW in 2027, and management is prepared to accelerate investment if market demand warrants. This manufacturing agility underpins Weichai's ability to respond quickly to shifts in customer requirements and market opportunities.

\- Technology leadership, supply chain control, and new energy initiatives are strengthening Weichai's competitive edge. Weichai's integrated approach – leveraging mature platforms, rapid capacity buildout, and a predominantly domestic supply chain – continues to deliver cost and service advantages over global peers. The company's direct engagement in North America and exclusive diesel partnership with Generac support both margin capture and aftermarket monetization, with recurring service revenue poised to become a larger share of the profit pool. SOFC and new energy initiatives are expanding the long-term growth runway, while the legacy engine business remains a stable profit base as the profit mix shifts toward AIDC and new energy solutions.

\- Margin trends remain robust, with mix shift and scale supporting profitability. Diesel backup engine GPM is holding at c. 35-40%, while gas engines are delivering 40-50% GPMs, driving blended margin improvement. SOFC is not poised as a material profit contributor in coming years, but management sees breakeven at 100MW and GPM potential of 30–40%+ at scale. Aftermarket revenue is poised to rise as the installed base grows, with management targeting 20%-30% of lifecycle value from services and parts.

## 1H26 preview; positive read-throughs from the parent-co earnings beat

Recent investor concerns have centered on a weaker 2Q26 print, driven by the sluggish domestic ICE HDT sales in May-June – particularly soft LNG truck volumes – which has weighed on Weichai's share price in recent months. However, we believe these concerns are overdone, as we believe Weichai's AIDC business fundamentals remain robust and continue to anchor the company's growth and margin profile into 2H26. Furthermore, we see positive read-throughs from the better-than-expected parent-co's 1H26 print, released on July 18 (link).

\- 1H26 earnings preview. We expect Weichai's 2Q and 1H26 profit to rise by $12\% - 15\%$ Y/Y. This is underpinned by broad-based sector momentum, with excavator sales up $26\%$ Y/Y, loader sales up $27\%$ Y/Y, and HDT volumes up $21\%$ Y/Y in 1H26. Market expectations are muted and we see scope for an earnings beat, mainly on the margin front.

\- Parent company's 1H26 beat provide positive read-throughs. Weichai Group reported headline revenue surpassing Rmb200B for the first time in 1H26, up $22\%$ Y/Y (an acceleration from the $14\%$ Y/Y rise in 2025), with all major operating metrics at record highs. Power and energy revenue rose $30\%$ Y/Y, and data center product revenue grew by $140\%$ Y/Y, highlighting the acceleration in strategic growth areas. Engine market share increased across core segments, export mix improved, and the new energy business is gaining traction, supporting the group's resilience and long-term growth curve. Management reaffirmed the full-year Rmb400B revenue target (JPM notes this refers to the group level) and outlined priorities for accelerating new energy launches, and expanding high-end power markets. While the exact breakdown is not disclosed, Weichai Power's revenue typically forms around $65\%$ of the group total, and the listed company's earnings trajectory remains the key read-through for overall group performance.

## Weichai Power - A (Overweight; Price Target: Rmb49.00)

## Investment Thesis

Weichai is a leading HDT engine provider, with a \~40% domestic market share. Investors have started to recognize the company's transition from a cyclical, legacy engine story to a multi-year growth platform anchored by AIDC and new energy solutions. The re-rating is still at an early stage, with the market's attention so far focused on the strong momentum in AIDC backup engines, but not yet fully reflecting the scale of the aftermarket opportunity, which could potentially grow to 5x the size of new product sales over the coming years and is not yet modeled in consensus. More importantly, Weichai is moving into the AIDC primary power segment for overseas markets, leveraging its SOFC and gas engine technology to address the next phase of global data center investment. We maintain our bullish view on Weichai.

## Valuation

Our Jun-27 PT of Rmb49 is based on our DCF valuation. Key assumptions include:

\- A WACC of $8.1\%$ , driven by a cost of equity of $8.6\%$ and a beta of 0.9x (latest Bloomberg data).

\- Cost of debt at 5% and a tax rate of 25%.

• Target debt-to-capital of 10% and terminal growth of 3%.

\- A $5\%$ A/H discount to value the A-shares.

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

Key downside risks include: (1) a slower-than-expected recovery in heavy-duty truck sales; (2) weaker-than-expected market-share gains; and (3) weaker-than-expected performance at Kion.
