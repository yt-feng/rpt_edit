# China and the Miners

Our monthly report summarizes key data/charts from China to give investors quick insights into China's macroenvironment and its demand for mineral resources.

China view – Growth softens on weak domestic demand: Our China economics team notes that May activity reinforces a more entrenched two-speed economy, with export-led production holding up while domestic consumption and investment weaken. Retail sales turned negative and the property sector remains fragile. Weaker govt. bond issuance and infrastructure FAI slump reflect a project-pipeline lull post-1Q front-loading. They see 2Q GDP tracking at 4.4% YoY, and expect policy to step up capex-focused fiscal rollout from 3Q, particularly support for AI computing networks, data centers, and smart grids under the "Six Networks" initiative as geopolitical tensions reinforce the imperative of energy security and tech self-sufficiency (see here).

Macro – Property outlook weakens: China’s IP was 4.5% YoY in May (April 4.1%); May PMI was 50 (April 50.3). May CPI was 1.2% YoY (April 1.2%). New property starts fell 24.7% YoY in May (April -27.1% YoY) and GFA sold fell 14.1% YoY in May (April -10.3% YoY). Our China property team expects a weaker 3Q. Secondary real-time home sales in high-tier cities started weakening in mid-May, and have decelerated even faster in recent weeks. Considering still-fragile resident sentiment, diminishing policy effects/pent-up demand, and reduced new saleable resources, the team estimates secondary home sales will turn negative y-y and primary home sales will drop further y-y in 3Q, despite the low base. FAI was down 12.5% YoY in May (April -9.4% YoY). May aluminum production was up 1.7% YoY to 3.9mnt, reflecting capacity resumption in Liaoning and new capacity in Inner Mongolia.

Commodities data – Steel activity slowed: Steel exports in May decreased 2% YoY to 10.3mnt and crude steel output was down 2.7% YoY, while domestic steel apparent consumption fell 8.5% YoY. China's iron ore imports were flat YoY in May at 98mnt. Port inventories began to draw down gradually during the month but remain elevated.

How are we positioned? BHP.AX remains our preferred diversified exposure, supported by low-cost WAIO cash generation with accretive 330Mtpa expansion optionality, Copper SA valuation upside, potential for further asset crystallisation, and stronger long term growth profile than peers (see our recent insight note here). Our iron ore order of preference: BHP.AX (OW), DRR.AX (OW), RIO.AX (EW), FMG.AX (UW). OW WHC.AX for coal exposure, potentially benefitting from reduced NSW/QLD logistics disruptions, and weaker SE Asia hydropower supporting thermal coal demand under a strong El Niño scenario. For lithium, we see balanced risk-reward currently; we are EW PLS, still preferred over IGO (UW) with the Greenbushes LOM plan still a key uncertainty.

# Industrial Production Data and Trade Data

## IP data:

New starts were down 24.7% YoY in May, and GFA sold fell 14.1% YoY, while property completions were down 19.6% YoY. Our China property team expects a weaker 3Q. Secondary home sales in high-tier cities started weakening in mid-May and are likely to turn negative y-y, while primary sales also appear set to decline further y-y despite a low base, driven by fragile resident sentiment, diminishing policy effects/pent-up demand, and fewer new saleable resources.

Domestic steel apparent consumption decreased 8.5% YoY in May, on apparent inventory buildup amid a 2% slowdown in net exports, together with a 9% decline in net exports while key steel mills production was down 4.4% YoY.

May aluminum production was up 1.7% YoY, to 3.9mnt, driven by capacity resumption in Liaoning and new capacity in Inner Mongolia. As operating capacity has reached a ceiling, we expect overall aluminum production to stay at an elevated level for the remainder of the year given solid industry profitability.

May coal production was down 1.7% YoY but up 3% MoM to 397.2mnt. The MoM recovery in coal production follows the seasonal trend, as restocking demand from power plants should gradually increase to prepare for the summer peak consumption season. However, a mine accident and tighter safety controls could cause domestic coal production to remain constrained in the near term. Thermal power generation rose 2.1% YoY in May to 472.6bn kWh, accounting for 60% of total power generation, vs. 62% in Apr-26.

## Trade data:

Iron ore imports were flat YoY in May to 98Mt. Port inventories in China started to draw down gradually during the month but remain elevated. Pig iron production at steel mills is down 2% YTD.

Coal imports decreased 8% YoY but rose 1% MoM in May to 33Mt. Total YTD coal imports reached 183Mt, down 3% YoY. The decline reflected seasonal weakness in demand during a slower consumption period, along with higher imported coal prices. As domestic coal prices increased, the import arbitrage reopened, which we expect to support a MoM rebound in coal imports in June.

Steel exports in May decreased 2% YoY, but improved 9% MoM to 10.3Mt, likely reflecting a low base in April. Exports for 5M26 totaled 44.6Mt, -8% YoY. Daily production at CISA member mills declined 4.4% YoY in May (vs -5.7% YoY in April).

China's imports of copper and copper products (refined + alloys) totaled 446kt in May, down $1\%$ MoM but up $4\%$ YoY. The import arbitrage opened intermittently during April and May despite rising copper prices, with net refined imports in April reaching the highest level since September 2025. Inventory draws slowed in May. The Yangshan premium ranged between US\$60 and US\$75/t, indicating continued solid physical demand. Copper ore and concentrate imports declined 1% MoM and were flat YoY at 2.35Mt, while treatment charges continued to fall as strong sulphuric acid prices supported smelter margins. Chinese refined copper output growth also slowed in April.

Aluminium and product exports rose 6% MoM and 16% YoY to 632kt in May, the highest level since November 2024. Supply disruptions in the Middle East tightened ex-China markets and lifted LME prices, widening the export arbitrage. Chinese inventories have begun to decline, particularly as semis exports increase.

China's iron ore imports totalled 98Mt in May, down $6\%$ MoM and flat YoY. Port inventories in China began to draw down gradually during the month but remain elevated. Pig iron production at steel mills is down $2\%$ YTD, compared with a $4\%$ YTD decline in crude steel output.

Coal imports declined 8% YoY but rose by 1% MoM in May to 33Mt. Total coal imports YTD reached 183Mt, down 3% YoY. The decline reflected seasonal weakness in demand during a slower consumption period, along with higher imported coal prices. As domestic coal prices increased, the import arbitrage reopened, which we expect to support a MoM rebound in coal imports in June.

## Major Theme of the Month

## Metals in the Eye of El Niño

NOAA has confirmed an El Niño for this year and raised the probability of a very strong event to 63%. Implications for metals include risks to copper production in Chile and Zambia, and potential impacts on some aluminium, zinc and lithium supply. Thermal coal would likely see higher demand.

For further details, see metal&ROCK: Metals in the Eye of El Niño (17 Jun 2026)

El Niño raises copper supply risks: El Niño has officially emerged and is expected to strengthen, with NOAA confirming that Pacific Ocean surface temperatures have warmed sufficiently to trigger an atmospheric response. It has assigned a 63% probability of a very strong event, potentially the strongest on record. This poses notable supply risks for metals, particularly copper, as El Niño could disrupt Chilean production through floods and infrastructure damage, while drought conditions in Zambia may strain its hydropower-dependent grid and impact output.

Other metals at risk too: El Niño poses broader risks across metals markets, with drought threatening hydropower supply for aluminium and zinc smelters in China's Yunnan, while heavier rainfall in Peru and Chile could disrupt zinc and lithium production. Iron ore impacts are likely mixed as favourable conditions in Australia and northern Brazil may support output, but excess rain could hinder logistics in southern Brazil. Meanwhile, thermal coal demand is expected to rise, as hotter Asian summers increase power consumption and drought reduces hydropower reliability, driving greater reliance on coal, albeit unevenly across regions.

MS outlook: With a more mixed macro backdrop as interest rate uncertainty builds, metals have become more linked to idiosyncratic drivers, making disruptions more important. All else equal, the El Niño event should bring upside risks to prices, particularly if more severe weather is observed. We watch copper in particular where supply has already faced significant disruptions YTD (see note), and remain constructive on thermal coal into the summer, with El Niño adding upside risk to demand.
