## Key thesis

China Consumer Top Picks: (1) Nongfu (OW): strong brand momentum and margin buffer; (2) Anta (OW): well-executed multi-brand portfolio and overseas upside; (3) Guming (OW): continued runway for network growth and improving brand equity.

What if the Iran conflict ended now? Packaging and industrial inputs (e.g. PE/PET) are up 15-40% vs the 2025 average. Alpha comes from operator quality, not beta. Demand may stay headline-noisy, but the real story is the split underneath. Operator quality – not broad category beta – should be the primary source of alpha. In our coverage, we like Anta, Pop Mart, and Midea.
Catalysts to watch and strategy: Cost inflation hitting from 3Q26 is a headwind for beverages. Mid-Autumn Festival and October Golden Week are key read-throughs for baijiu and gold/jewelry.

Positioning: add FMD after June (Guming, Luckin, Chagee); avoid beverages (especially crowded dividend trades) in 3Q26. Comps get friendlier over time: Moutai (N) from 3Q26, FMD and Pop Mart (N) from 4Q26, and beverages from 2Q27.

- Beer – embracing new channels. CR Beer is best positioned with 70% off-trade mix. An early and hot summer could act as catalysts.

## Beer – Right side of the channel migration

## YTD performance: Diverging trajectories among the big three

The channel migration has produced differentiated performance among the major brewers YTD. All three brewers are converging on a common playbook: optimizing product portfolios, extending strength in their respective advantaged segments, and building capabilities for the off-trade channel.

Bud China, historically over-indexed to nightlife and high-end dining channels, has been undergoing a strategic pivot since 2025 - refocusing on the Rmb 6–8 price band with core SKUs including Classic Budweiser, Hoegaarden, and Corona, while accelerating the recovery of Harbin Beer. This transition has come at a cost: organic sales declined 11% in 2025 and remained negative at around -4% in 1Q26.
CR Beer continues to deliver steady, positive momentum, leveraging its structural advantage in off-trade channels (70% off-trade mix - the highest among major players). The company is driving growth through its core brands - Brave the World, Pure Draft, Marrs Green - and the premium Heineken series. Sales grew \~1% in 1Q26 and accelerated to \~2% from April onward, with volume growth of 2–3%, reflecting continued progress on premiumization upgrades.
Tsingtao, with a higher on-trade skew (60% on-trade / 40% off-trade), is navigating the channel shift by centering its strategy on Draft Beer as the hero SKU, targeting the Rmb 6–8 and Rmb 8+ craft segments, and expanding 1-liter formats tailored to family and home-banquet occasions. April sales grew 2.4% with volume up 3–4%, indicating that its product structure optimization and format innovation are gaining traction.

Cost resilience: Beer margins better insulated than RTD beverages; YTD beer cost index only up 3.9%

## Baijiu – Approaching the trough

## Where are we in the cycle?

The baijiu sector's prosperity is shaped by the convergence of three overlapping cycles:

1) Macro cycle; 2) Supply-demand and pricing cycle; 3) Policy cycle.

## Macro cycle: Baijiu growth decoupling from a slowing economy

With fixed asset investment growth now hovering in the low single digits and GDP growth stabilizing around 4–5%, the macro tailwinds that once propelled the industry have largely dissipated. We expect industry revenue to begin stabilizing in 2026E, though the recovery trajectory will be more muted than in prior cycles given the structurally lower growth backdrop.

## Household balance sheets recovering: Property prices showing early signs of stabilization

JPM forecasts that tier-1 city home prices will see a soft form of stabilization in 2026 (average -1% to 0% YoY). Beyond tier-1 cities, the broader national picture remains more cautious but is approaching a floor.
For baijiu, household balance sheet repair should begin to alleviate the wealth destruction effect that has weighed on high-end consumption and banquet spending over the past two years.

## Supply-demand & policy cycle: Mapping 2024-to-date downcycle against 2012-16

In the prior cycle, the trigger was a sudden policy shock, sending industry revenue into decline for seven consecutive quarters before an inflection in 2H15. The current cycle, began with a more gradual deterioration, and the “alcohol ban (禁酒令)” arrived in 2Q25 added further downward pressure.
If the 2012–2016 playbook is any guide, the industry may face several more quarters of contraction before bottoming. However, the current downcycle entered its demand weakness phase earlier, suggesting the industry may already be further along in its adjustment process than headline revenue declines imply.

# Baijiu – Approaching the trough

Where are we in the cycle?

## Our assessment: Approaching the trough, but recovery will be gradual

Synthesizing across all three cycles, we believe the baijiu sector is in the early innings of recovery.

- On the macro front, GDP growth has stabilized and property prices are showing tentative signs of bottoming – laying the groundwork for a gradual recovery in household wealth confidence.
On the supply-demand and policy front, the industry has already absorbed five quarters of revenue decline and the incremental policy shock of the alcohol ban; if the 2012–2016 analog holds, the trough may arrive within the next two to three quarters.

However, unlike the V-shaped recovery of 2016–2017, we expect the rebound to be more L-shaped - constrained by structurally softer GDP growth, a still-fragile property market outside tier-1 cities, and a more cautious consumer mindset. The setup favors patient positioning in quality names with pricing power and DTC optionality, rather than aggressive cyclical bets on a sharp snapback.
