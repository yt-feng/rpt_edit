# Indonesia: Competition Expected to Improve in Java, but Rise ex-Java

## Number of industry participants (consolidated to three)

• Consolidated from 10 players in 2008 to three today.
- Tsel is Indonesia’s market leader with \~45% subs market share.
- ISAT and H3I merged to form Indonesia's #2 mobile player in 2022.
• XLSmart was formed following the EXCL+FREN merger in 2025.

## Industry structure:

\- Largely prepaid subs; mobile revenue driven by data revenue (legacy voice/SMS has fallen from $>60\%$ of revenue in 2015 to $<10\%$ today).

## Recent developments:

- Competition has improved since 2H25.
- We expect network coverage/quality to improve ex-Java now with ISAT and EXCL bigger/stronger post their respective mergers.
• ARPU converging towards Rp45k between all three operators.

## Philippines: Third Player is Still Hanging Around

## Number of industry participants (up from 2 to 3)

\- Historically a two-player market, with a new entrant (Dito) launched in March 2021.

## Industry structure:

\- Largely prepaid subs; data revenues driving growth and replacing legacy voice/SMS revenues (this has fallen from \~80% of revenue in 2013 to \~6% in 2026e).

## Broadband – lower pricing arising from affordability issues

- Broadband penetration to rise \~2ppt p.a. over 2025-28e to 37%; this is still lower than the ASEAN average of \~50%.
- Affordability is an issue, with broadband ARPU as a percentage of household income at one of the highest levels in ASEAN: \~2.5% vs. the ASEAN average of 1.6%.

## Indonesia: Capex has Peaked, but 5G Poses Downside Risk

## Technology upgrade:

- ISAT and EXCL have hiked their capex over the past few years to expand their network coverage ex-Java.
- Indonesia telcos have passed their peak capex, with capex at $< 30\%$ of sales.
- 5G – Indonesia launched 700MHz and 2.6GHz spectrum auction in April 2026.

## Network expansion:

• All operators have phased out 3G BTS.
• EXCL had 12k 5G BTS as of 1Q26
• Tsel had 5.4k 5G BTS as of 1Q26
• ISAT had 10k 5G BTS as of 1Q26

## Fixed network investment:

- Fixed broadband household penetration in Indonesia was 21% in 2025, and we forecast this to rise to 24% by 2028e.
• TLKM is spinning off its fiber assets.
• ISAT targets 8-10% subscriber market share by 2028.

## Thailand: Capex has Peaked

## Technology upgrade:

- 5G spectrum auction was held in Feb-20; prices were reasonable.
- Operators launched 5G plans at end-2020.
• Another round of spectrum auctions held in June 2025.

## Network expansion:

- The operators showed a significant increase in capex in 2015, as they aggressively rolled out 4G.
- All operators have finished their 4G rollouts and have begun their 5G rollouts.
- We saw small capex increases for Thailand through 2023 and 2024, as 5G technology was being deployed, but not at the steep increase as seen during the 4G upgrade.
- We expect capex as a percentage of sales to trend downward, closer to 13%, as operators have passed their peak 5G investment.
- AIS covers $>95\%$ of Thailand's population; $39\%$ of its subs are on 5G.
• TRUE has 98.9% nationwide coverage; 38% of its subs are on 5G.

## Fixed network investment:

- Fixed broadband household penetration in Thailand was 55% in 2025, and we forecast this to rise to 58% by 2028e.
- Growth spiked in 2019/20 due to increased demand from the work-from-home Covid-19 situation.
• We expect telcos to continue to roll out fibre across Thailand.
- AIS' acquisition of Triple T Broadband was completed in 2023.

## Singapore: Capex has Peaked; Cybersecurity Risks are Rising

## Fixed network investment:

- Fixed network investment in Singapore is modest for all players because of NetLink NBN Trust.
- 10Gbps broadband is becoming increasingly available, part of a nationwide effort to upgrade broadband infrastructure from the current 1Gbps speed.

## Indonesia: Regulator has Moderate Influence, and we Expect This to Sustain

## Regulator/Govt

- Has a strong influence on the telco market, as TLKM is 52% owned by the government
- Set-up of Danatara sovereign wealth fund
• Key initiatives:
• Streamlining
• Infra monetization
• Higher dividends

• Spectrum sharing allowed
• Approved sharing of telecom infrastructure
• Foreign ownership limits removed (previously 67%)
- Mandated transition from analog to digital broadcasting to free up spectrum for 5G

• EXCL merged with FREN in April 2025

## Philippines: Regulation has Increased

## New Entrants

- July 2019, Dito received a license to operate as the country's third telco operator.
- Dito launched commercially in 2021 and had acquired 17mn subs as of July 2023 (\~10% subscriber market share).
• Starlink was given the green light to operate in 2022.

## Common Tower Policy

• The government aims to improve service quality by encouraging the construction of tens of thousands of new towers.
- The Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) said the Philippines needs another 50k cell towers on top of the \~18k that PLDT/Globe have built.
• Tower-sharing regime will aid Dito's rollout.
- PLDT and Globe have sold \~15k towers.

## Foreign Ownership Limit

• In March 2022, the government removed the 40% foreign ownership cap in industries such as telcos, airlines, domestic shipping, and railways.
• Foreign nationals can now own up to 100% of capital in telcos.
- A reciprocity clause prohibits foreign citizens from owning $>40\%$ of critical infrastructure, unless their country provides similar access to Filipino investors.

## Others

• The SIM Registration Act was enacted December 2022 – to curb cybercriminal activities.
- Mobile Number Portability of 2019 allowed subs to change providers without changing their number.

## Knektadong Pinoy Act

• No congressional franchise needed
- Open Infrastructure sharing
• Accelerated deployment

## Malaysia: Regulatory Uncertainty

## Mobile consolidation:

- Celcom + Digi's proposed merger in 2019 did not go through
• Celcom/Digi merger talks were re-initiated in April 2021
- April 2022 – MCMC laid out concerns over the merger
- June 2022 – MCMC approved the merger
- December 2022 – The merger was completed

## 5G rollout plans:

- Initial plan was to award the 5G spectrum to a consortium
- May 2020 – 5G spectrum awarded without open tender
- June 2020 – 5G spectrum awarded was revoked
- March 2021 – DNB deployed 5G through a Single Wholesale Network
- December 2021 - Malaysia reconsidered a plan for a single 5G network amid impasse with telcos
• March 2022 - DNB offered 70% stake to telcos
- May 2022 – Big 4 MNOs rejected minority stake in DNB
• June 2022 – 6 telcos took a 70% stake in DNB
- December 2022 – Operators started rolling 5G plans except MXSC

## 5G rollout plans:

- May 2023 – Malaysia transitioned towards a Dual Wholesale Network
• August 2023 – MXSC launched 5G
- June 2024 – CELC, MXSC, U Mobile, YTL subscribed to DNB
• July 2024 – Tender process for second 5G network
- November 2024 – U Mobile awarded the opportunity to build the country's second 5G network.
- May 2025 – U Mobile sold its 17% stake in DNB to fellow shareholders; targets 80% 5G population coverage within 12 months, 90% in the subsequent 12 months
- Early 2026 – CelcomDigi & Maxis each inject additional RM202mn into DNB
- Early 2026 – TM in dispute with DNB after issuing notice to terminate wholesale access agreement; TM switched to U Mobile under a new three-year agreement
