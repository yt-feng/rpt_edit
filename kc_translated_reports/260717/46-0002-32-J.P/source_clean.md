# China Consumer Strategy

## June retail sales return to growth on easier comps; 15 $^{th}$ FYP sets the goal, not the how

China's retail sales moved back into positive territory in June, rising $1.0\%$ YoY after a $0.6\%$ YoY decline in May, ahead of Bloomberg consensus $(-0.1\%$ YoY), though the rebound was helped by an easier comparison base; on a two-year stacked basis, June retail sales were up $5.8\%$ , roughly in line with May. By category, consumer electronics led the improvement $(+13 - 17\%$ YoY), followed by cosmetics $(+13\%)$ and food & beverage $(+8\%)$ , while home durables and autos continued to weigh on the headline with HSD-DD declines. For 1H26, total retail sales increased $1.3\%$ YoY, but momentum cooled meaningfully in 2Q $(+0.2\%$ vs 1Q at $+2.4\%)$ . With 6.18 campaigns in the quarter, 2Q online retail sales $(+2.6\%)$ further outperformed offline $(-0.6\%)$ . The recently announced 'Expanding Consumption in the 15th FYP' implies a $3.7\%$ CAGR target – encouraging in principle, but still light on the 'how', suggesting a back-end-loaded pickup and/or additional consumption stimulus may be needed. Looking ahead, with demand still soft and higher input costs starting to bite, we expect more companies to cut FY26 guidance during the 2Q26 reporting season; while commodity costs have largely peaked, we believe cost pressure will likely persist through 2H26.

\- Sector performance recap – another month of underperformance. China consumer staples/discretionary sector share prices are down 5%/4% over the past month (vs MSCI China/HSI Index -3%/-2%). Their forward P/E has de-rated 4%/3% to 15x/12x (vs MSCI China/HSI Index down 4%/2% to 11x/11x).

\- CPI-PPI spread widening. CPI was up 1.0% YoY in Jun (vs 1.2% in May), with food CPI down 1.6% YoY and non-food CPI up 1.5% YoY. Core CPI was up 1.0% YoY (vs 1.1% in May). PPI in Jun jumped to 4.1% in the month, which means the CPI-PPI spread has now further widened to -3.1%. Companies that are not able to bargain with their raw material suppliers and/or pass through the cost hike to the downstream will see meaningful margin squeeze from here.

\- Downside risks to unemployment. The unemployment rate was 5.0% in Jun, flattish YoY and down 0.1ppt MoM. With the first round of AI adoption in more industries in China and higher input costs biting from 3Q26, there is a higher chance that more companies will seek “cost reduction” in 2H26 – headcount cuts seem one of the most practical measures.

\- Jun 2026 retail sales breakdown. By channel: online retail sales grew $3.9\%$ YoY, while offline retail sales were flattish YoY. Top 5 YoY performing categories: telecom products (up $17\%$ YoY), office supplies (such as laptops; $+13\%$ ), cosmetics $(+13\%)$ , liquor/tobacco $(+12\%)$ and staple foods $(+8\%)$ . Bottom 5 YoY performing categories: auto (down $16\%$ YoY), building materials $(-11\%)$ , home appliances $(-9\%)$ , furniture $(-7\%)$ , and fuel $(-5\%)$ .

\- Stock calls. We believe our barbell strategy would continue to work into 2H26: (I) Quality names with limited downside and earlier earnings stabilization – Anta (OW) for a well-executed multi-brand portfolio and overseas upside; Nongfu (OW) for strong brand momentum and margin buffer; Midea (OW) for a balanced revenue mix and B2B optionality; and (II) Turnaround stories – Chagee (OW) for China turnaround and upside in shareholder return;
