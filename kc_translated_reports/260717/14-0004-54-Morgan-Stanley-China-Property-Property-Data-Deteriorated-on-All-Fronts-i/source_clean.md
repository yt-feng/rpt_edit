# Property Data Deteriorated on All Fronts in June

Home sales declined further in June, after reversing the uptrend since May, amid weaker home prices and construction activity. We expect the continued slowdown in secondary home sales to accelerate home price declines in 3Q, while developers' still-prudent view on landbanking could weigh on REI.

Sales declines deepened in June, in line with our expectations. Rebased national sales fell 13.9% y-y by value and 14.3% y-y by volume (May: -9.3% and -13.2%), bringing 6M26 YTD sales declines to 13.6% y-y by value and 11.6% by volume. The NBS 70-city home price index continued to edge lower at a steady pace, with primary market prices down 0.2% m-m and secondary market prices down 0.3% m-m in June. Tier-1 cities recorded milder home price gains, +0.1% in primary and +0.3% in secondary (May: +0.2 and +0.4%) amid softening secondary sales.

Construction activity weakened further: New starts fell 26% y-y and completions fell 25% y-y in June, widening the 6M26 declines to 23% and 24%, respectively. Combined with the smaller construction scale, the decline in real estate investment (REI) deepened to 18.0% y-y in 6M26 (5M26: -16.2%). We expect construction activity to remain subdued in 2H, as developers remain cautious on landbanking (land purchase by the top 100 developers fell \~40% y-y in 6M26), posing downside risk to our full-year forecasts.

Weaker home sales likely in 3Q: Real-time secondary home sales in higher-tier cities remain weak, reaching MSD% y-y in the latest week (May: 26%; June: 10%). Given still-muted home purchase intentions, fading policy effect/pent-up demand, and reduced pipeline of saleable inventory, we expect secondary home sales to turn negative y-y, and primary home sales to decline further y-y in 3Q despite a low base. This could prolong the K-shaped home price trend, with prices continuing to decline m-m across most markets, potentially at a faster pace in 3Q, while some Tier-1 cities may sustain modest price gains given better demand/supply dynamics.

Stay selective: Considering the near-term headwinds, limited policy upside from July's Politburo meeting, and continued fund flow disruption, we recommend staying prudent, and continue to favor quality players with credible self-help alpha. We think CR Land (1109.HK), C&D (1908.HK), and Seazen (601155.SS/1030.HK) offer more attractive risk-reward at current valuations.
