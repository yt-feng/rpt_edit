## China Healthcare
## 2-day Chengdu tour takeaways: Rising innovation, partnerships and global execution

Our two-day Chengdu healthcare tour on 14–15 July was well attended by both generalist and specialist investors and covered a broad range of biotech/pharma, CRO and medtech companies (in total 9 companies: Kelun Pharma, Kelun Biotech, Hinova, Haisco Pharma, Baili Pharma, Zenitar, Keymed, Hitgen, and HxMedbot). One of the clearest takeaways was the broad push to adopt AI: many biotech companies are already applying it across drug discovery and development, while others are investing heavily to embed it into their R&D workflows. More broadly, the company meetings reinforced our constructive view on China healthcare innovation, with leading companies translating stronger R&D platforms into clinical validation and global development opportunities, while recent policy tightening on drug sales representatives has not seen much impact in 2Q26. We continue to like biotech and CXO subsectors the most. In biotech, we like Kelun Biotech and Innovent. In CXO, we like WuXi Apptec and WuXi Bio. In addition, we also like Medbot.

\- AI application is improving R&D efficiency, but proprietary data and wet-lab capabilities remain the real barriers to entry. HitGen is combining AI with DEL screening, structural biology and automated DMTA cycles, while Hinova and Zenitar are applying AI to molecular design, target structure analysis and lead optimization. Kelun Biotech also highlighted the importance of access to high-quality physical and private datasets for AIDD (AI for drug discovery) models. The common message is that AI can reduce the number of molecules synthesized and shorten discovery timelines, but it does not replace experimental validation. Companies with integrated data, biology and laboratory platforms should benefit more than standalone AI providers.

\- Out-licensing should remain robust as global pharma companies continue to seek differentiated China-origin assets. Kelun Biotech's partnership with MSD, Biokin's collaboration with BMS and Gilead's acquisition of Ouro (main asset from Keymed) demonstrate continued MNC appetite for Chinese innovation across ADCs and immunology. Earlier-stage companies such as Hinova and Zenitar also reported significant interest from overseas partners in molecular glues, metabolic assets and autoimmune programmes. Partnerships provide Chinese companies with non-dilutive funding and global development expertise, while helping MNCs replenish pipelines. Strong clinical differentiation for unmet clinical needs—not simply lower development cost—will remain the main driver of deal value. We don't see any companies expressing concerns about geopolitics impacting licensing activities. Keymed expressed the intention to do more deals with MNCs instead of Newcos.

\- The anti-corruption campaign appears to have had limited impact on compliant companies, although it will accelerate industry polarization. Kelun Biotech and KeyMed and other companies selling innovative drugs indicated that recent policy tightening had not materially disrupted sales progress in recent months. Newly commercialized products with compliant promotion systems and clear clinical value appear less exposed to tighter sales channel practices. Meanwhile, Kelun Pharma expected a sharp divergence between clinically necessary drugs and promotion-driven auxiliary products, and expect selling expense to drop for many companies in 2Q26. \- Improved investor sentiment coupled with clinical catalysts in 2H26 could continue to help China healthcare. Though some investors expressed a little concern that whether HARMONi-3 data in 2H26 will impact sentiments and how long the current healthcare rebound will continue, most investors agree that China healthcare fundamentals remain solid and there would be buying opportunities in the event healthcare stock prices see a dip.
