# China: Housing activity index trended lower further

\- JPM's housing activity index fell further, with declines in starts, sales, completions and developer funding deepening.

\- Price stabilization remains tentative, as tier-1 home price gains moderated and secondary-home price declines widened nationally.

\- The Consumption 15th FYP redefines housing as a consumption enabler, shifting the focus to improving affordability, supporting upgrade demand etc.

\- Policy priorities have shifted from expansion to quality and services, but the market remains in a prolonged quantity adjustment.

JPM's housing activity index continued to trend lower amid a slower recovery in tier-1 city home prices. The contraction in major housing activity indicators widened in June, including residential floor space starts (-26.6% oya in June vs. -22.9% in May), completions, sales (-15.3% oya in June vs. -12.4% in May), and developers' funding sources. The NBS 70-city new-home price decline narrowed modestly to -0.15% m/m nsa in June (vs. -0.20% in May), while the decline in secondary home prices widened to -0.32% (vs. -0.26% in May). Tier-1 city home price recovery also moderated, with new-home prices rising 0.1% m/m nsa and secondary home prices up 0.3%, both 0.1% pt slower than in May. New-home prices are now down 13.9% from their 2021 peak, while secondary home prices have fallen 22.8%. Centraline's sales manager confidence index gave back its early-July gains last week, while the secondary home asking price index continued to decline, suggesting limited room for further recovery following the 1Q green shoots.

Following the Urban Renewal 15 $^{th}$ FYP, the State Council recently released the Boosting Consumption 15th Five-Year Plan, in which the housing sector features prominently. Its role has shifted from that of a traditional investment engine to a facilitator of household consumption. Rather than focusing on boosting home sales, policymakers emphasized better meeting housing demand through affordable housing, increased supply for upgrading demand, and a more developed rental market. Housing is grouped alongside other durable goods such as automobiles and home appliances, underscoring its continued importance in the household consumption basket.

A key theme is the transition from “buying homes” to “improving living quality”. The plan calls for a housing quality improvement program centered on building safer, greener, smarter “good houses”, while accelerating urban village redevelopment, dilapidated housing renovation, elderly-friendly retrofits, and smart-home upgrades. Housing-related consumption has broadened from new-home purchases to renovation, furnishings, home appliances, building materials, and housing services. Property management is also explicitly identified as a consumption segment, with plans to upgrade service quality and expand community-based services.

In the section on promoting household income growth through multiple channels, the plan recognizes the role of housing in supporting household confidence and wealth. Measures to promote the “healthy development” of the property market are included under policies to raise household property income, while local governments are encouraged to further optimize housing policies and reform the housing provident fund system to better meet both first-home and upgrade demand. Overall, housing is positioned as a platform for consumption upgrading and service-sector growth, rather than a driver of property-led investment expansion.

China's housing market remains caught in a policy time-inconsistency trap. While policymakers aim to improve affordability and reduce the economy's dependence on property, concerns over household wealth and local government finances have limited the pace of price adjustment, pushing the correction primarily through quantities. As a result, housing starts, sales, land purchases, and real estate FAI remain under pressure. Improvements in tier-1 secondary markets were encouraging, supported by lower inventories, stronger transaction volumes, improved conversion rates, selective price gains, and potential wealth effects from IPO activity and firmer equity markets. However, the recovery remains narrow and increasingly selective. Lower-tier cities remain broadly subdued, and the recovery in tier-1 cities and the upgrade/luxury segments also seems to be losing steam. Elevated inventories, weak household credit demand, and ongoing declines in real estate investment suggest that the broader adjustment is still incomplete. Overall, recent developments appear more consistent with a K-shaped stabilization within a prolonged quantity adjustment than the beginning of a broad-based and self-sustaining nationwide recovery.
