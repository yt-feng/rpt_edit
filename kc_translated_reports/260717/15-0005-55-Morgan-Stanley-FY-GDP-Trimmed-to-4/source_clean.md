# FY GDP Trimmed to 4.6%; Faster Budget Rollout to Lift 2H Growth
## Key Takeaways

We revise down our full-year GDP forecast by 20bps to 4.6%, in view of below-target 4.3%Y 2Q growth (Consensus: 4.5%Y)

K-shape deepened further in June: High-tech production firmed on export strength, while domestic demand softened again.

GDP deflator YoY rose to 1.5% in 2Q, led by selected export sectors and oil-intensive services, but may rollover on weak demand and fading oil impulse.

Below-target 2Q growth may spur faster infrastructure rollout from 3Q, yet budget expansion remains unlikely given \~Rmb2trn in-budget fiscal impulse for 2H.

2H GDP YoY will likely rebound to \~4.6%: Besides faster budget rollout, lower oil prices would bring firmer global capex cycle and reduced upstream disruptions.

2Q GDP undershot at 4.3%, missing consensus and Beijing's target: Key drags were: (1) slower infrastructure push after 1Q front-loading, (2) oil drag on refining and petrochemical production, and (3) softer consumption on faded trade-in effects, a weak job market, and lingering property drag — outweighing broadening export strength. June activity data further underscored a deepening K-shape: resilient high-tech production and exports vs. sluggish domestic demand and investment.

Higher pressure on policy fine-turning, not budget expansion: The July Politburo will likely urge faster budget rollout, given the risk of full-year growth slipping below the 4.5–5.0% target. Policy focus should stay on AI and energy infrastructure, not consumption, amid intensifying US-China tech rivalry. A supplementary budget looks unlikely, with \~Rmb2tn of in-budget fiscal impulse still available for 2H.

20bps marked-to-market cut on our FY2026 GDP forecast, to 4.6%: We expect modestly firmer sequential growth in 2H, supported by: (1) faster budget deployment and (2) normalizing oil prices, which would lift external demand and ease drags on refining & petrochemicals. Combined with a low base, 2H YoY could reach 4.6%. Still, the weaker starting point warrants a modest full-year downgrade.
