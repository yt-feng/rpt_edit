# Zoom Communications

We hosted a meeting with CFO Michelle Chang, along with Charles Eveslage and Carsten Beckwith from Investor Relations, where we had the opportunity to discuss revenue drivers in detail for both the Online and Enterprise businesses, in addition to the margin outlook. Key takeaways from the meeting included:

\- ZCX growth pinned at higher-end of double-digits; crossed \$100 mn ARR five quarters ago and accelerating. The long-standing “high double digit” growth characterization for Zoom Customer Experience was sharpened, with growth described as being in the higher-end of the double digit percentage range, off a base that crossed \$100 mn ARR roughly five quarters ago (around F1Q26) - implying, by our math, a \~\$200 mn+ ARR business today. Importantly, growth was noted to have accelerated in each of the last two quarters, attributed to portfolio expansion, most notably ZVA voice (added in F3Q26), with differentiation anchored in the one-platform handoff between virtual agents, human agents, and the broader Zoom estate. Deal texture remained consistent with recent prints (nine of the top ten deals competitive displacements; nine of ten attaching paid AI), while pricing continues to migrate toward consumption and, prospectively, outcome-based constructs.

\- Zoom Phone gaining share and representing a significant portion of company revenues / ARR. Management reiterated already disclosed milestones for Zoom Phone, including \~\$500 mn of ARR in F2Q24 and mid-teens ARR growth y/y for the last five-plus quarters, and crossed >10 mn paid seats in F3Q26. We estimate the above implies Zoom Phone is at \~\$750-800 mn ARR today, by our math. The mid-teens growth for Zoom Phone implies share gains for the company, with the management team highlighting share gains relative to RingCentral and 8x8, with top wins split roughly evenly between on-prem displacements and cloud takeouts against a \~280 mn seat market framed as approximately half cloud, half on-prem. From a pricing perspective, there are several tiering options with per-seat pricing from \~\$6-7 at the low end and extending to \~\$16-17 at the high end.

\- Focused on both Horizontal and Vertical levers for Enterprise growth; Common Room complementary to portfolio while BrightHire adds to Vertical depth. The Common Room acquisition was framed as extending upstream of ZRA - from in-call coaching / deal intelligence to knowing who to have the conversation with in the first place - creating an end-to-end revenue platform, with Clay referenced as the primary competitor in the space. Management characterized sales-oriented AI as complementary to the portfolio and a horizontal sale with an easier ROI roadmap. Even so, the Vertical strategy remains crucial to Zoom's opportunity to gain wallet share of customers, while acquisitions like BrightHire have a comparatively niche addressable market.

\- Long-term GM target of \~80% intact; FY27 operating margins are a better reference point for the company. Management clarified the legacy 33-36% operating margin range was constructed to preserve acquisition capacity and is “a bit stale,” stating margins would not be taken toward that zone absent “a serious inflection in revenue beyond what we’ve seen.” Instead, the FY27 operating margin guide is more reflective of where the company will be. Management remains confident in its ability to hold to the 79%-80% gross margins helped by scaling AI usage through a federated model where internal SLMs are leveraged for dominant scenarios like ASR and balanced against Anthropic and OpenAI for cost / quality, as well as incremental efficiency harvested in the core meeting stack. Importantly, management noted margins have been held while AI is monetized only indirectly in the core and ZCX, with the “third piece” (horizontal paid AI) not yet contributing - positioning the monetization ramp as margin accretive.
