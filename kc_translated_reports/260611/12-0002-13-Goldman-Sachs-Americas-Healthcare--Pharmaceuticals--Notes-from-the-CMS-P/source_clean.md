In conjunction with our conference, we are conducting our customary mid-year investor sentiment survey

At our 47th Annual Global Healthcare Conference, we hosted a discussion with Stephanie Carlton, CMS Deputy Administrator and Rebekah Armstrong, CMS Chief of Staff and Director, Office of Legislation.

The panel framed their perspectives on key healthcare issues, with a focus on most favored nation (MFN) drug pricing, and actions to target waste fraud and abuse including through changes to State Directed Payments and within the Health Insurance Exchanges.

We discuss key takeaways within.

## Key Takeaways

High level framing of CMS Priorities: CMS leadership framed its near-term agenda around four core priorities anchored in affordability, access, and system modernization. First, the agency aims to operate as a patient-first payer, including expanding use of technology to improve access to health data and care delivery. Second, CMS is advancing a prevention-focused strategy (MAHA), alongside investments in access models and rural health, to shift the system towards better outcomes and more proactive care. Third, the agency is intensifying efforts to combat fraud, waste, and abuse, with a particular focus on oversight to combat fraudulent operators, with the panelists highlighting the Medicaid enrollment freeze on home health and hospice providers. Finally, CMS is prioritizing affordability, most notably through MFN drug price negotiations with pharmaceutical manufacturers to lower costs across federal healthcare programs.

State Directed Payment and Medicaid Funding: On the recent changes to State directed payments and shifting liability to state Medicaid programs, the panelists noted they are focused on tightening program integrity and rebalancing cost-sharing. The agency indicated a shift in the federal share of Medicaid funding to \~70% of overall funding, up from \~60% at inception. Programs like provider tax mechanisms, and the Medicaid Expansion population were highlighted as areas that are drawing more federal dollars, which CMS views as contributing to excess federal spend. CMS is also moving to align Medicaid reimbursement more closely with Medicare benchmarks through the introduction of payment caps over time. The policy direction emphasizes increased financial participation and accountability from states within the program structure.

Drug pricing: The agency highlighted how the initial round of IRA negotiations has delivered \~20% average net savings, compared to the discounts of \~40% for the second round under the current administration. MFN-related efforts were framed as introducing broader pricing pressure globally, including through trade dynamics, with CMS viewing these mechanisms within a broader toolkit aimed at systematically lowering drug costs. The panelists did not comment on plans to expand MFN beyond the current 17 biopharma manufacturers.

■ ACA Exchanges: On the Exchanges, the agency highlighted concerns around significant fraud and improper enrollment in the program and indicated a clear focus on strengthening program integrity by removing ineligible participants. CMS pointed to new payment rules that incorporate a range of integrity measures, alongside broader efforts to improve competition across plans and ensure individuals can access coverage that meets their needs.
