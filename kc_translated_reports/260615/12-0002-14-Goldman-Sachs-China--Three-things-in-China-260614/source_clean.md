# China: Three things in China

Trade data beat expectations: China's exports and imports increased $19.4\%$ yoy and $27.5\%$ yoy, respectively, in May, both above market expectations. The ongoing AI capex boom has been a major contributor to China's strong trade growth. Semiconductors and automatic data processing machines (including data center equipment) together accounted for approximately half of export and import growth in May.

PPI inflation climbed but CPI inflation unchanged: PPI inflation increased from $2.8\%$ yoy in April to $3.9\%$ yoy in May. However, upstream price increases did not translate into higher consumer prices: CPI inflation stayed unchanged at $1.2\%$ yoy in May. The lack of transmission points to weak end-user demand. Credit data show that loan demand is indeed soft. For the May activity data to be released on Tuesday, June 16, we expect both retail sales and fixed asset investment to decline year-on-year.

Policy chatter: A recent Bloomberg article on the Chinese government's plan to spend RMB2tn on data center construction has attracted market attention. However, this is not new and was part of the “Six Networks” laid out in the 15th Five-Year Plan in March: water networks, new-type power grids, computing power networks (including data centers), next-generation communication networks, urban underground pipeline networks, and logistics networks. These areas will be a key focus of investment over the next few years. For example, at the March “Two Sessions”, NDRC head Zheng Shanjie noted that China’s investment in the “Six Networks” could exceed RMB7tn (5% of GDP) this year.
