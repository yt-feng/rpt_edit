India has long prioritised energy efficiency to strengthen energy security, improve productivity, and expand access to affordable energy for all. Key initiatives such as the Perform, Achieve and Trade scheme, Building Energy Codes, and the Ujala programme have helped drive progress across sectors.

In 2025, India was among the global leaders in energy efficiency, with more than $4\%$ improvement in primary energy intensity. This aligns with the pace needed to meet global efficiency goals by 2030 and supports India's updated NDC, which targets a $47\%$ reduction in GDP emission intensity by 2035 and $60\%$ non-fossil installed power capacity by 2035. India has already achieved a $37.38\%$ reduction in emission intensity by 2022 and $53.21\%$ non-fossil installed power capacity by March 2026, underscoring the central role of energy efficiency in its clean energy transition.

The Bureau of Energy Efficiency is strengthening the energy efficiency market through innovative financing mechanisms. Its ADEETIE scheme is helping MSMEs adopt efficient technologies through interest subvention and technical support, while also improving project bankability.

Financial de-risking instruments such as energy savings insurance (ESI) have emerged as a promising tool to unlock energy efficiency investment. With a view to de-risk the financing for energy efficiency, BEE has been working with the OECD CEFIM Programme, to develop a roadmap for the design and implementation of ESI in India.

I believe this roadmap will guide stakeholders in designing and implementing an insurance-backed mechanism to support financing, unlock investment, accelerate deployment and support India's broader energy and climate goals.

## Editorial

Energy efficiency is a cornerstone of sustainable economic development. In India, it plays a pivotal role in reducing the energy intensity of a rapidly expanding economy, while enhancing affordability and energy security, boosting industrial competitiveness and mitigating emissions. Improving the efficiency of buildings, industry and transport is one of the fastest and most cost-effective ways to reduce energy demand and exposure to external shocks, while enhancing energy sovereignty and economic resilience. Over the past decades, India has made remarkable progress in strengthening its policy framework for energy efficiency. Numerous flagship initiatives led by the Indian Bureau of Energy Efficiency have progressively raised efficiency standards across end-use sectors. Micro, small and medium-sized enterprises, which form the backbone of India's economy, possess untapped potential for achieving energy savings, however barriers prevent higher investment in efficiency improvements.

In this context, the launch of the Energy Savings Insurance (ESI) Implementation Roadmap of India, developed in close collaboration between the Indian Bureau of Energy Efficiency of the Ministry of Power and the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, marks an important milestone in unlocking finance and investment for energy efficiency in India. But, of course, it does not stop there. Implementing the roadmap requires collaboration between a multitude of national and international actors in raising awareness, strengthening capacities, piloting and technically supporting the rollout of the ESI scheme.

The OECD, through its Clean Energy Finance and Investment Mobilisation Programme, has worked with the BEE to strengthen India's enabling environment for energy efficiency financing and overcome persisting barriers. This roadmap emerges from a sustained and inclusive collaborative process. The 2022 Clean Energy Finance and Investment Roadmap of India identified Energy Savings Insurance schemes as a key instrument, following extensive cross-ministerial and multi-stakeholder consultations. In the following, the BEE tasked the OECD with developing a dedicated needs assessment and ESI implementation roadmap in India. A jointly organised series of workshops and consultations in 2024-2025 and international lessons from ESI deployment, confirmed the potential of energy savings insurance to substantially reduce the perceived risks of lenders and investors, thereby unlocking private capital for energy efficiency in India's MSMEs and beyond. This roadmap provides a step-by-step guide to tapping into the energy efficiency potential in high priority sectors of the Indian economy through ESI.

We extend our appreciation to the BEE for its leadership and commitment to this initiative. We equally thank the Asian Development Bank, the Ministry of MSMEs, the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India, the International Institute for Energy Conservation, the many financial institutions, insurers, energy service companies, energy auditors and other experts who contributed to this work. We look forward to continuing our excellent collaboration with the BEE and all partners in supporting the mobilisation of clean energy finance and investment as we move from roadmap to implementation.

## Case study B3: Energy Efficiency Insurance in a hospital facility - United States

Location: United States

Program: HSB Energy Efficiency Insurance

Partners: HSB (Munich Re), Energy Services Company (ESCO)

Background: the hospital facility, originally built in 1970, spans 580 000 square feet. With an annual energy cost baseline of USD 430 000, the hospital recognised the need for significant energy upgrades to reduce costs, enhance operational efficiency and meet sustainability goals. The hospital engaged with an Energy Services Company (ESCO) for an energy efficiency retrofit, which included comprehensive upgrades to the building's infrastructure (Hemic, 2016[30]).

Project description:

\- Technology implemented: the project included 12 energy conservation measures, which were designed to reduce energy usage and improve operational efficiency. These measures included:

HVAC upgrades:

\- Lighting replacements with energy-efficient LEDs

○ Installation of variable frequency drives (VFDs) for motors

\- Water conservation measures (such as low-flow fixtures and systems) (Hemic, 2016[30])

Building type: hospital facility (multi-story, 580 000 sq. ft.)

Total investment (CapEx): USD 2.4 million

## Achieved benefits:

\- Annual energy savings: the project was projected to achieve USD 260 000 in annual energy savings.

\- Insured energy savings: HSB's energy efficiency insurance covered 90% of the guaranteed annual energy savings, which amounts to USD 234 000 per year (Hemic, 2016[30]).

\- Insurance coverage: the policy covered shortfalls in annual aggregate efficiency savings as a result of deficiencies in design, implementation and/or performance. Losses were verified via annual measurement and verification reports.

Insurance and loan term: the project was financed with a 10-year loan.

\- Risk mitigation: the energy efficiency insurance covered risks related to the shortfall in energy savings and provided coverage against losses. Additionally, the credit enhancement provided by HSB's solution allowed the ESCO to show significant improvement in the credit rating of the project, which helped reduce the overall cost of the project loan.

\- Financial outcome: With the insurance coverage in place, the hospital was able to reduce financial risks associated with the project, making the large-scale energy improvements more feasible.

## Impact:

\- Risk reduction for project stakeholders: the energy efficiency insurance mitigated financial risks, protecting the project owner, contractors and stakeholders from potential performance shortfalls and failures to meet energy savings targets.

\- Improved project financing: the credit enhancement feature helped improve the credit rating of the project, which reduced the overall financing cost and made the investment in energy-saving technologies more financially attractive.

Operational efficiency: with the annual energy savings and improved systems in place, the hospital significantly reduced operational costs while improving energy efficiency across its facility, aligning with sustainability and financial goals.

## Case study B4: Performance Insurance for fuel cell technology

Background: in 2013, XL Group's Complex Accounts unit, Munich Re's Green Tech Solutions team, and New Energy Risk (NER) collaborated to offer an innovative performance insurance policy for a portfolio of fuel cell servers installed across the U.S.

Challenge: fuel cell technology manufacturers faced challenges in meeting performance expectations, which could hinder financing and investor confidence.

Solution: The insurance cover provided critical support for project financing by insuring the fuel cells' performance over 15 years, ensuring reliability even if manufacturers couldn't meet warranty obligations. The USD 99 million bond issued to finance the portfolio received an investment-grade rating, enhancing its attractiveness to investors (New Energy Risk, 2014[31]).

Outcome: this collaboration marked the first time that insurance coverage provided by XL Group and Munich Re contributed to a financing bond receiving an investment-grade rating, broadening its appeal to a wider range of investors. The initiative demonstrated the importance of insurance in making clean technology projects more attractive and financially viable (New Energy Risk, 2014[31]).
