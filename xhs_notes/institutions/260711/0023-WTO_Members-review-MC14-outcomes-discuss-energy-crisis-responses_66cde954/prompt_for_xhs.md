## ANNEX II

# GUIDELINES FOR DESIGNING TRANSPARENT, TARGETED AND TEMPORARY FOSSIL FUEL SUBSIDY MEASURES IN RESPONSE TO ENERGY CRISES

## Transparent

\- Follow good regulatory practice in the development and publication of measures, including through use of existing transparency mechanisms, and with due regard to relevant WTO notification requirements.

\- Communicate clearly the rationale, approach and nature of such measures - including that they are intended to be temporary only - to all interested stakeholders.

## Targeted

\- Ensure a clear focus on most affected stakeholders (e.g. poorest or most vulnerable households).

\- Avoid untargeted fossil fuel subsidies that distort the cost of fossil fuels in comparison to alternatives, are economically inefficient and impose high fiscal costs.

\- Consider alternative measures, including support for the just transition and clean energy sources, income support and other energy-neutral support measures, to deliver most efficient and effective targeting of temporary measures.

\- Examine ways to boost the energy transition, including through support for clean energy sources promoting energy efficiency and conservation, to enhance longer-term energy security, including for the most vulnerable population groups.

## Temporary

• Make such crisis support measures temporary, including by:

\- setting limited timeframes, with clear end-dates (these could be supplemented, if needed, by clear criteria for consideration of any extension to these timeframes);

• building in regular review clauses and/or sunset clauses, unless already provided;

• incorporating built-in transition mechanisms to ensure smooth and progressive adaptation if applicable.

\- Consider the most efficient and effective ways to phase out such temporary measures, including starting with the least targeted ones, and continuing to refine the targeting of any remaining measures to facilitate their subsequent removal as well.
