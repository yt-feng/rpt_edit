## ABSTRACT

This paper examines the impact of corporate digital transformation on environmental performance and the underlying mechanisms. Utilizing textual analysis of annual reports, we develop a four-dimensional measure of digital transformation comprising technological, business, manufacturing, and operational aspects. Analyzing data from Chinese A-share listed firms between 2009 and 2022, we demonstrate that corporate digital transformation significantly enhances environmental performance. While all dimensions of digital transformation contribute positively, manufacturing digital transformation exhibits the most substantial impact, followed by operational and technological digital transformation. We identify that green innovation, strengthened environmental monitoring and communication, increased environmental information transparency, and greater external attention are pivotal mechanisms through which digital transformation affects environmental performance, with transparency emerging as the strongest factor in this context. This paper offers new insights into the role of digital transformation in advancing sustainability.

## I. INTRODUCTION

In the digital era, the digital revolution emerged as a critical driver for achieving the Sustainable Development Goals (United Nations 2020). Countries are increasingly formulating policies that promote the interplay between digitalization and sustainability, both of which are among the most powerful market influences in today's corporate landscape. However, diverse practices lead to varying outcomes, and the effectiveness of this synergy remains a subject of active debate and research.

A growing body of academic literature has explored the impact of digitalization on sustainability. Numerous studies indicate that the digital economy enhances resource efficiency (Dubey et al. 2019, Ren et al. 2021, Wang et al. 2022), improves green total factor productivity (Lee et al. 2023), reduces carbon emissions (Wang et al. 2022, Kopp et al. 2023), drives high-quality green development (ElMassah and Mohieldin 2020, Ma and Zhu 2022), increases resilience (Copestake et al. 2024), and fosters green economic growth (Hao et al. 2023). Recently, a new stream of research has begun to investigate the real effects of digitalization on corporate sustainable development, such as carbon emissions reduction (Zhou and Liu 2024, Liu et al. 2024), environmental performance improvement (Xu et al. 2023, Song et al. 2024), and environmental, social, and governance (ESG) ratings enhancement (Zhao and Cai 2023, Li et al. 2024). Our study will enrich this line of literature by examining whether and how corporate digital transformation influences environmental performance.

Theoretically, the relationship between corporate digital transformation and environmental performance is ambiguous. On the one hand, the application of digital technologies enables firms to reduce the search, replication, transportation, tracking, and verification costs of data (Borgatti and Cross 2003, Goldfarb and Tucker 2019); overcome knowledge localization barriers (Jaffe et al. 1993, Forman and Van Zeebroeck 2019); enhance communication (Nguyen et al. 2020); support the development of new offerings, processes, or solutions (Tortora et al. 2021); strengthen the collection and monitoring of environmental data (Chapron 2017); and thereby improve management capabilities across all aspects of corporate operations (Graetz and Michaels 2018, Fromhold-Eisebith et al. 2021, Chen and Jiang 2024).

On the other hand, it is important to acknowledge the potential downsides of digitalization. First, corporate digital transformation entails significant upfront investment and maintenance costs, and it can increase energy consumption and e-waste generation (Ren et al. 2021). Technology misalignment may lead to ineffective or even failed digital investments. Additionally, political, social, and psychological concerns surrounding digitalization are increasingly highlighted (Trittin-Ulbrich et al. 2021), such as inequality, employment displacement, psychological dependence, and technological reliance. Nevertheless, there remains widespread optimism about digital transformation, with ongoing efforts to explore its potential to enhance environmental performance.

Identifying corporate digital transformation levels accurately is challenging because of the absence of a clearly defined or universally accepted evaluation standard. Verhoef et al. (2021) propose that firms can be categorized into three stages: (i) digitization, (ii) digitalization, and (iii) digital transformation, based on their organizational structure, digital assets, and capabilities. They define digital transformation as the process through which enterprises leverage advanced digital technologies, such as big data, artificial intelligence (AI), and industrial robotics, to transform production processes, business models, internal controls, and organizational culture, thereby adapting to technological advancements and achieving competitive advantage. Inspired by Verhoef et al. (2021), we focus on “the application of digital technologies in enterprises” and aim to construct a more comprehensive and structured measure of digital transformation. Drawing from classical functional domain theories in management, we develop a four-dimensional digital transformation measure, including technological, business, manufacturing, and operational aspects. Using textual analysis, we analyze the annual reports of listed companies to extract keyword frequencies corresponding to each dimension, thereby obtaining both individual and overall digital transformation levels. This structured approach provides a clear foundation for subsequent empirical analysis based on the defined dimensions.

Our primary analysis investigates the impact of corporate digital transformation on environmental performance using a large sample of Chinese A-share listed firms from 2009 to 2022. This study employs the environmental performance score (EScore) developed by the Sino-Securities Index (SNSI) ESG ratings as the dependent variable, with the constructed digital transformation levels serving as the core independent variable. Our baseline results indicate that an increase in digital transformation exerts a positive and statistically significant effect on environmental performance, after controlling for firm-specific characteristics and incorporating firm and year fixed effects. Specifically, all else being equal, a one standard deviation increase in corporate digital transformation corresponds to a 1.34% enhancement in environmental performance in this context.

To address the issue of endogeneity, we first utilize instrumental variable (IV) estimations constructed from the digital transformation levels of peer firms within the same industry, employing a two-stage least squares (TSLS) method. Additionally, we conduct a difference-in-differences (DID) analysis, using the “Broadband China” strategy as an exogenous shock (Tang et al. 2021). Launched in 2013, this initiative by the Government of the People’s Republic of China (PRC) aims to develop a nationwide high-speed broadband network and foster widespread adoption of information technology. The strategy was implemented in phases across 120 pilot cities in 2014, 2015, and 2016. The evidence suggests that city-level digitalization improves corporate environmental performance, helping establish a causal relationship between digital transformation and environmental performance.

The results remain robust when employing alternative environmental performance measures, including the EScore from the Wind ESG ratings and corporate environmental protection tax. Further validation using a change-on-change model specification, as well as concurrent and lagged digital transformation measures, confirm the consistency of our findings. Moreover, excluding financial, information services, and special treatment-listed firms does not affect our results.

Corporate digital transformation can influence environmental performance through several plausible channels. First of all, the most well-documented channel is green innovation. Numerous studies indicate that digitalization facilitates green innovation (e.g., Horbach 2008, Tang et al. 2023, Du et al. 2023), which in turn enhances environmental performance (Singh et al. 2020). On the one hand, digital technologies improve individual research and development (R&D) capabilities and promote knowledge spillover (Trantopoulos et al. 2017, Zhuo and Chen 2023). For instance, AI and big data enhance analytical capabilities (Dubey et al. 2019), while industrial internet and blockchain provide accurate and timely data (Chapron 2017). On the other hand, digital transformation significantly alters communication and cooperation models within and outside organizations, promoting R&D collaboration across the value chain (Benzidia et al. 2021). Wu et al. (2019) show that data analytics facilitate decentralized innovation. De Marchi (2012) highlights that R&D cooperation with suppliers, knowledge-intensive business services, and universities is essential for firms aiming to achieve environmental innovation. We term this channel as the green innovation channel.

The second plausible channel through which digital transformation enhances environmental performance is suggested by few theoretical models on environmental management (e.g., Klassen and McLaughlin 1996, Melnyk et al. 2003). In these models, environmental management—efforts to minimize the negative environmental impacts of a firm's products throughout their life cycle—has the potential to significantly influence environmental performance. The operations strategy literature indicates that both structural (plant and equipment) and infrastructural (production planning, performance management, and product design) choices require effective environmental monitoring and communication to support sound decision-making (Vogler and Eisenegger 2021).

Importantly, information is a prerequisite for assessing environmental conditions and making informed decisions to address identified challenges (Gholami et al. 2016). As extreme weather events increase, firms increasingly require timely responses to climate risks and uncertainties in environmental policy, necessitating adjustments in management strategies, such as investment and financing decisions (Benincasa et al. 2024). Additionally, changes in work practices brought about by digitalization, like virtual and hybrid conferences (Tao et al. 2021), facilitate more effective climate change mitigation efforts. We refer to this channel as the environmental monitoring and communication channel.

The third plausible channel is related to the information asymmetry problem and discretionary disclosure documented in the literature on transparency, with a specific focus on environmental information transparency. Numerous studies have confirmed that environmental information disclosure significantly affects firms' environmental performance (e.g., Patten 2002, Clarkson et al. 2008, Shi et al. 2021), primarily by strengthening external governance. Wang et al. (2023) demonstrate that information transmission channels affect firms' disclosure behaviors. As societal awareness and preferences for environmental sustainability grow, there is an increasing demand for greater transparency in environmental information across various industries. Hence, we term this channel as the environmental information transparency channel.

The fourth plausible channel at play involves external attention, complementing the third mechanism, as both promote the implementation of external oversight. The intensity of this oversight is closely linked to the level of attention firms receive, and external attention has been shown to be a powerful force driving firms to go green. Irani and Oesch (2013) demonstrate that analyst monitoring complements corporate governance and impacts financial reporting quality. Buntaine et al. (2024) show that public appeals through social media significantly reduce firms' pollution emissions.

A firm's ability to attract attention from local governments, analysts, media, investors, and the public is linked to its visibility and accessibility. Further, the effectiveness of external monitoring is closely related to the availability and quality of information. Liu et al. (2024) argue that governments and investors play a critical oversight role through which corporate digital transformation reduces carbon emissions. In addition to publicly disclosed information, nonpublic information that stakeholders can obtain through personal relationships is also crucial and can be enhanced by corporate digital transformation (Altschuler et al. 2015). We refer to this channel as the external attention channel.

Additionally, we evaluate the relative importance of these mechanisms using the horse race approach. We find that environmental information transparency has the strongest impact in this context, followed by green innovation and external attention. Although environmental monitoring and communication lacks significance in this analysis, this may be primarily attributed to our choice of measuring it through the establishment of environmental emergency response mechanisms; exploring a more comprehensive proxy could yield further insights.

We further analyze whether the sub-dimensions of digital transformation exert differing impacts on environmental performance and assess their relative importance. The findings show that all digital transformation dimensions significantly improve environmental performance. Notably, under the horse race approach, manufacturing digital transformation demonstrates the strongest impact, followed by operational digital transformation and technological digital transformation, while business digital transformation does not show significance. Our results indicate that, although all forms of digital transformation contribute to promoting sustainability, the benefits derived from specific leverage points vary. Merely altering business models yields limited advantages; effectively applying digital transformation to production and operational management, along with the appropriate utilization of digital technologies, is essential.

Lastly, we conduct a heterogeneity analysis on firm characteristics to investigate whether the relationship between digital transformation and environmental performance is driven by specific groups, thereby addressing concerns related to the digital divide. Our findings indicate that the positive impact of digital transformation on environmental performance holds across various firm sizes, ownership types, and regional economic development levels. However, the economic impact varies across the four sub-dimensions of digital transformation for firms with different characteristics. Therefore, both managers and governments should create tailored digital strategies or policies.

Overall, all of these findings suggest that there exists a positive, causal effect of corporate digital transformation on environmental performance. ## Corporate Digital Transformation and Environmental Performance Evidence from the People's Republic of China

This paper examines how digital transformation influences corporate environmental performance. Using firm-level data from the People's Republic of China between 2009 and 2022, the study finds that firms' digital transformation significantly improves their environmental performance scores. Improved green innovation, enhanced environmental monitoring and transparency, and increased external attention are identified as possible channels underlying this relationship. The results offer policy insights on accelerating digital transformation to support the green transition.
