【金字塔原理写作原则】
1. 结论先行：文章开头先回答“这份报告最值得看的判断是什么”，而不是介绍背景。
2. 统领思想：全文只能服务一个主判断，避免变成摘要合集。
3. 纵向回答：每一层都要回答上一层提出的“为什么”或“所以呢”。
4. 横向 MECE：每个一级小节必须彼此独立、共同支撑主判断，避免重叠。
5. Synthesis over summary：不要复述报告段落，要提炼“这些事实合在一起意味着什么”。
6. So what：每个小节末尾必须落到对行业、公司、竞争格局、资产定价或读者观察框架的含义。
7. Action title：所有 `##` 小标题必须是“直接讲述洞察的完整句子”，不能是目录标签。

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Heat waves are intensifying. Heat stress is one of the deadliest yet least understood consequences of increasing climate volatility. Heat is no longer a seasonal discomfort that passes by. It has become an intense, relentless, and all-pervading crisis that threatens lives, livelihoods, ecosystems, and our future existence. From scorching urban centers to parched rural landscapes, and from melting glaciers to warmer oceans, heat waves have impacted all geographies and sectors. Heat waves and extreme heat events have intensified in frequency and severity, adversely impacting infrastructure, widening social inequities, and driving up mortality rates. The time to act on this issue has come.

Temperatures are rising fast. The year 2024 was the hottest on record in human history, with temperatures ranging from 1.45°C to 1.60°C, higher than the average in 1850–1900. By 2028, annual global temperatures are projected to rise further to 1.90°C above pre-industrial levels, putting over a quarter of the world's population at risk from heat waves. The effects of a Oceans are already evident: Oceans are getting warmer, ice sheets are shrinking, glaciers are retreating, snow cover is decreasing, sea levels are rising, extreme events like droughts and heat waves are becoming more frequent, and ocean acidification is increasing. All these changes have a profound impact on human well-being, ecosystems, and economies.

Heat stress impacts lives. The most severe impact of extreme heat is on human health, especially the most vulnerable groups, including children and older people, as well as outdoor workers who are most exposed to its debilitating effects. Exposure to high temperatures leads to heat exhaustion and heatstroke and exacerbates chronic conditions like cardiovascular and respiratory diseases. Higher mortality rates due to heat-induced causes, workplace heat injuries, and a rise in infectious diseases are straining public health care services in low-income countries lacking access to reliable electricity and basic water facilities. Extreme heat disproportionately affects the most vulnerable, low-income, and marginalized communities who lack adequate housing, cooling, health care, and emergency support. It has led to widening learning gaps, lower human capital development and productivity, migration, and increased food insecurity.

Extreme heat has multifaceted adverse consequences. Extreme heat is disrupting food systems, straining water supplies, increasing economic losses, reducing labor productivity, and compounding health risks. The global economic and productivity losses due to extreme heat are now at levels that insurance companies no longer consider it as an “invisible peril” and are forced to evaluate the associated investment risks and cascading costs. Extreme heat is now a direct threat to economic competitiveness, depressing outdoor labor output and increasing business costs. For countries reliant on informal workforces, productivity losses could become structurally embedded. By 2025, heat stress was projected to cause \$2.4 trillion in productivity losses and \$448 billion in annual fixed asset losses for publicly listed companies.

Excessive heat has led to infrastructure vulnerability and compromised the efficiency, integrity, and safety of vital utility services, resulting in frequent power outages, disrupted transportation networks, reduced water availability, and increased maintenance and replacement costs. A warmer planet has caused devastating losses across the entire biodiversity spectrum, from forests to terrestrial and marine organisms and ecosystems—the combined effect of which has triggered multidimensional challenges leading to health deterioration, economic strain, and ecological decline.

Thailand is highly vulnerable. As one of Southeast Asia's most vulnerable countries, Thailand stands on the front lines of climate volatility, with projections indicating a potential loss in gross domestic product of up to $45\%$ by 2048. Agriculture, fisheries, and tourism—the core pillars of the Thai economy and livelihoods—are threatened, as extreme heat, coastal erosion, and flooding intensify. Bangkok and the coastal regions, which are major tourist destinations, are all at high risk from heat-related health, infrastructure, and economic impacts. The Gulf of Thailand, which shares a marine ecosystem with neighboring countries, is under severe ecological stress because of overfishing, marine heat waves, coral bleaching, and pollution. There is an urgent need to reduce the degradation of this shallow, semi-enclosed body of water that provides food security and livelihoods to coastal communities and rest and recreation for tourists.

Scaling up action requires an integrated approach. Several barriers exist that prevent an integrated and systemic response to confronting heat stress and building resilience. These include fragmented sector planning; piecemeal and uncoordinated infrastructure development; people and policy blind spots and lack of awareness; insufficient data, analysis, and monitoring systems; and rising and massive financing gaps. Responses therefore need to integrate policy and projects so that impacts are linked and holistically addressed across different sectors but especially between the highly interlinked themes of air pollution, water management, and heat stress. Financing innovations are especially critical, given the rising fiscal burden of governments, to raise the level of financing for building resilience to extreme heat.

Heat is not just a climate or health challenge—it is also a governance challenge. Managing heat stress requires coordinated action and resources across meteorology, health, labor, urban planning, education, and disaster management, supported by shared data and aligned mandates. Without an integrated governance architecture, responses will remain fragmented and insufficient.

## The Increasing Impacts of Heat in Thailand and Southeast Asia Challenges and Emerging Solutions

Heat waves and extreme heat pose a critical threat—jeopardizing human health, driving up mortality rates, widening social inequities, and straining public health services. Since the 1960s, Southeast Asia’s annual mean temperature has been rising continuously; and, without emission reductions, temperatures in major Asian cities, including Bangkok, could surpass critical thresholds for human survival. Drawing on international good practices, this publication highlights comprehensive technical, institutional, and financial innovations, and proposes a detailed road map for a Heat Action Plan for Thailand. The road map prioritizes protecting vulnerable populations, mobilizing public and private finance, and accelerating coordinated cross-sector solutions to build an inclusive, evidence-based, and sustainable response.
