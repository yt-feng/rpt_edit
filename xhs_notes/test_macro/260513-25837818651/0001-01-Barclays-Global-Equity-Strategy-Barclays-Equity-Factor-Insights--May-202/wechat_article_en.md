# Risk-On Is Back, But the Real Factor Question Is Which Regime Will Survive the Energy Headwind

The most important shift in global equity factors in May 2026 is not simply that risk-on has returned. It is that the rotation is being driven by a narrow set of forces—AI earnings surprises, geopolitical de-escalation, and a Fed that cannot cut—that create a fragile foundation for any single factor regime to persist. The US market has decisively moved away from the defensive, value-oriented posture that dominated during the Iran conflict. Growth and Momentum have surged, Quality has been downgraded, and Value has been cut to Neutral. In Europe, the picture is inverted: Value is Positive, Momentum is crowded and Neutral, and the entire factor landscape depends on whether the AI-led rally broadens beyond its current leadership.

The argument that emerges from the latest factor insights is not about which factor wins this month. It is about whether the current rotation has structural legs or is simply a relief rally within a medium-term energy shock that has not fully played out. The report makes clear that US equities have benefited from a temporary buffer—energy inventories that were large at the start of the Iran conflict—but that buffer is shrinking. The risk-on enthusiasm may be correct for the next few weeks. The question is whether it survives the next few quarters.

Investors who treat the current factor positioning as a stable call rather than a tactical bet are likely to be disappointed. The report's factor changes are coherent with the near-term macro backdrop, but they also contain internal tensions that point to a more complex path ahead.

## US Factor Shifts Reflect a Market Betting on Earnings Momentum Over Defensive Resilience, But the Energy Overhang Remains

The US factor changes are unambiguous in their direction. Growth and Momentum have been upgraded to Positive. Value has been downgraded to Neutral. Quality has been downgraded to Negative. High-over-Low Volatility has been upgraded to Neutral. These are not marginal tweaks. They represent a wholesale reallocation of factor conviction away from the defensive, value-oriented positions that were appropriate during the height of the Iran-related uncertainty.

The rationale is clear and data-supported. First-quarter 2026 earnings season delivered an EPS surprise of 18 percent for the S&P 500, far above the long-term median of 5.2 percent. Big Tech EPS surprises tracked at 40.7 percent versus a median of 7.2 percent. Earnings growth year-over-year reached 18.1 percent for the broad index and 27.7 percent for Big Tech. These are not modest numbers. They are the kind of numbers that justify a risk-on rotation, especially when geopolitical tensions have eased enough for investors to refocus on fundamentals.

But the report also highlights a critical tension. The same earnings strength that supports Growth and Momentum is occurring alongside an energy shock that has not fully dissipated. Energy sector EPS revisions have surged by 70 percent since the start of the year, yet the sector has declined by 9.9 percent in the second quarter. The market is pricing in a geopolitical premium that is unwinding faster than the actual energy supply disruption is resolving. The report explicitly warns that the energy buffer that cushioned the initial shock is shrinking, and that the expectation of only one Fed cut through March 2027 combined with elevated energy prices poses growing medium-term headwinds.

The implication is that the current factor rotation may be front-running a macro resolution that has not yet arrived. Growth and Momentum are riding a wave of earnings momentum that is real, but they are also exposed to a reversal if energy costs begin to compress margins or if the Fed is forced to acknowledge that inflation remains sticky. The downgrade of Quality to Negative makes sense in a risk-on environment, but it also means the portfolio has less protection if the energy headwind intensifies.

## European Factor Divergence Reveals a Market That Is Betting on Value as the Cleanest Beneficiary of Breadth—But Breadth Has Not Arrived Yet

The European factor story is almost the mirror image of the US, and that divergence itself is an insight. In Europe, Value is Positive, Growth is Neutral, Momentum is Neutral but flagged as crowded, and Quality is Neutral. The report frames European Value as the cleanest beneficiary of any broadening beyond Momentum, supported by strong earnings delivery and an EPS growth rate of 7 percent, the highest since the first quarter of 2023.

This is a strategic argument, not a tactical one. The report is not saying that European Value has already outperformed. It is saying that if the AI-led rally in Momentum names begins to exhaust itself, Value is the most natural recipient of rotation capital. The structural negative correlation between Value and Momentum in Europe supports this view. When Momentum becomes crowded and expensive, Value tends to benefit.

But the report also acknowledges that this broadening has not yet happened. Momentum performance in Europe has been strong and AI-led, but the trade is increasingly crowded. The report recommends managing exposure rather than adding. This is a nuanced position. It is not a full-throated endorsement of Value. It is a conditional bet that depends on a regime change that has not yet occurred.

The macro backdrop in Europe adds further complexity. The report notes that renewed rates volatility weighs on long-duration support for Growth, and that cooling macro momentum and restrictive ECB policy may cap near-term rotation upside for Small Caps. European Value may be positioned well structurally, but the near-term catalysts are mixed. The factor view is correct as a strategic allocation, but the timing risk is meaningful.

## The Factor Rotation Is Coherent With the Near-Term Macro, But It Creates a Portfolio That Is Vulnerable to a Reversal in Energy or Inflation

One of the most striking features of the current factor landscape is how internally consistent the US changes are—and how exposed that consistency makes the portfolio. The US portfolio is now long Growth and Momentum, neutral on Value and Volatility, and short Quality. This is a high-conviction bet on continued earnings momentum, low geopolitical risk, and a Fed that remains on hold without becoming restrictive.

If the macro environment cooperates, this portfolio should perform well. The earnings data supports it. The sentiment data supports it. The flow data supports it. But the report itself flags the medium-term headwinds. The energy buffer is shrinking. The Fed is expected to cut only once through early 2027. Inflation remains elevated enough that the bond proxy case for Yield has weakened. The market is pricing a smooth landing, but the data on energy inventories and sticky inflation suggests the path may be bumpier than current prices reflect.

The risk is that the current factor rotation is a relief rally that has correctly priced the de-escalation of the Iran conflict but has not fully priced the lingering effects of higher energy costs on consumer spending, corporate margins, and central bank policy. If energy prices remain elevated through the second half of the year, the earnings momentum that currently supports Growth and Momentum may begin to fade. The sectors that have benefited most from the rotation—semiconductors, hardware, speculative AI exposures—are also the sectors most vulnerable to a margin squeeze if input costs rise or if demand softens.

The downgrade of Quality to Negative is particularly notable in this context. Quality tends to outperform during periods of macro stress and underperform during risk-on rallies. The report is correct to downgrade it given current conditions. But a portfolio that is structurally underweight Quality is also a portfolio that has less buffer if the macro environment deteriorates. The report's own analysis of the medium-term headwinds suggests that this is a real risk, not a theoretical one.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: How to Distinguish a Tactical Rotation From a Structural Regime Change

The report is thorough in its factor analysis and clear in its recommendations. But it leaves one critical question unresolved: Is the current risk-on rotation a tactical shift that will reverse as the energy shock reasserts itself, or is it the beginning of a structural regime change in which AI-driven earnings growth dominates factor performance for an extended period?

The report provides evidence for both interpretations. On the structural side, the earnings data is extraordinary. Big Tech EPS surprises are running at more than five times the long-term median. EPS growth is the fastest since 2024. Operating leverage is improving. These are not one-quarter anomalies. They suggest that the AI investment cycle is generating real earnings power that could sustain Growth and Momentum leadership for quarters or even years.

On the tactical side, the report's own warnings about the energy buffer, the shrinking crude and refined product inventories, and the limited Fed cutting path suggest that the current environment may be more fragile than it appears. The report notes that the resilience in US equities can be partly explained by the energy shock being closer to five weeks old than eight weeks. That is a time-dependent argument. As the buffer shrinks, the resilience may fade.

The report does not resolve this tension, and it should not be expected to. The data does not yet provide a clear answer. But for investors, this is the most important analytical question. The factor recommendations are correct for the current environment, but they are not a set-and-forget allocation. They require ongoing reassessment of whether the macro backdrop is evolving in a way that supports the current factor regime or undermines it.

## A Decision Framework for Factor Positioning in an Uncertain Regime

For readers who want to translate the report's analysis into actionable decisions, the following framework may be useful. It is not a replacement for the full report, but it captures the key trade-offs that the factor changes imply.

First, assess the energy trajectory. The single most important variable for US factor performance over the next two to three quarters is whether energy prices stabilize, decline, or continue to rise. If energy prices stabilize or decline, the current risk-on rotation has room to run, and the long Growth, long Momentum, short Quality posture is appropriate. If energy prices continue to rise, the earnings momentum that supports Growth and Momentum will come under pressure, and the portfolio will need to rotate back toward Value, Quality, or defensive exposures.

Second, monitor the breadth of the European rally. The European Value call is conditional on broadening beyond Momentum. If Momentum continues to dominate and the AI-led rally remains narrow, Value will underperform. If breadth improves, Value should benefit. The report's recommendation to manage Momentum exposure rather than add is consistent with this conditional view. Investors should watch for signs that the rally is spreading beyond the AI leaders.

Third, recognize that the Quality downgrade is a tactical call that carries strategic risk. The report is correct that Quality is unattractive in a risk-on environment. But Quality is also the factor that tends to protect portfolios during drawdowns. Investors who follow the report's recommendations should be aware that they are accepting higher drawdown risk in exchange for higher upside exposure. This is a reasonable trade-off given current conditions, but it should be an explicit decision, not an implicit one.

Fourth, factor in the Fed timeline. The expectation of only one cut through March 2027 means that rate-sensitive factors like Yield and Long Duration Growth will remain under pressure. The report's Neutral stance on Yield and its downgrade of Quality are consistent with this rate outlook. But if the economic data softens and the market begins to price more cuts, these factor views would need to be reassessed.
