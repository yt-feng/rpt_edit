# The AI ASIC Investment Thesis Has Matured. The Next Alpha Lies in CPUs, Supply Chain Bottlenecks, and Underappreciated Pricing Power.

After a year of extraordinary returns, the AI semiconductor trade is entering a new, more discerning phase. The broad-based optimism that lifted nearly every stock touched by the AI narrative is giving way to a more granular, strategic debate. The central question is no longer whether AI demand is real, but rather which companies can sustain outsized earnings growth, command pricing power, and capture the next wave of infrastructure buildout. Based on extensive meetings with over 35 institutional investors in the US, a clear consensus has emerged: AI ASICs remain the preferred thematic exposure, but the conversation has shifted decisively toward the next set of bottlenecks, the overlooked role of CPUs, and the durability of hyperscaler capital spending. The market is now rewarding selectivity, and the companies best positioned to deliver incremental earnings surprises are those whose opportunities remain underappreciated by consensus.

The most important shift in investor dialogue is the move from pure demand validation to a more complex assessment of monetization and cycle sustainability. For the past year, the investment case for AI semiconductors rested on a simple premise: hyperscalers would spend aggressively on training infrastructure. That thesis has been confirmed. Now, the market is asking a harder question: can this spending generate sufficient returns to justify its current trajectory, and will it accelerate further into 2028? This is not a bearish question; it is the natural evolution of a maturing investment cycle. The companies that can answer it most convincingly, through demonstrated pricing power, expanding total addressable markets (TAMs), and growing dollar content per chip, will be the ones that attract incremental capital.

## The AI Monetization Debate Is Shifting from Demand Validation to the Sustainability of Hyperscaler Capital Spending

The most frequently recurring topic in investor meetings was the sustainability of the current AI investment cycle. Investors are no longer content to accept that hyperscalers will continue to spend at accelerating rates. They want evidence of monetization. This shift in focus is a direct consequence of the strong stock performance over the past year, which has made semiconductor exposure one of the largest portfolio positions for many institutional investors. With so much capital already allocated, incremental investment must be justified by a clear path to earnings growth, not just narrative.

A notable development in this context is the evolving investor perception of external capital raising by hyperscalers. Historically, US investors viewed such activities as a potential signal of overinvestment or bubble-like behavior. However, sentiment is shifting. Investors are increasingly interpreting a major US cloud provider's ability to raise capital as a positive indicator of long-term confidence in AI monetization. This is a subtle but important change in mindset. It suggests that the market is beginning to accept the scale of the opportunity, even as it demands more rigorous proof points. The key unresolved question remains whether hyperscaler capital expenditure growth will need to accelerate further into 2028 to support ongoing supply chain expansion, or whether the current pace is sufficient to sustain the earnings trajectories embedded in stock prices.

## CPU Demand Is Emerging as the Most Underappreciated Growth Driver in the Next Phase of AI Infrastructure

One of the most notable and strategically significant shifts in investor discussions was the increasing focus on CPUs. While AI accelerators have rightly dominated the narrative for training workloads, there is a growing recognition that CPUs will play a far more important role in inference and, critically, in agentic AI applications. Agentic AI, which involves autonomous systems that can plan, reason, and execute tasks, places a much higher demand on general-purpose computing resources than traditional AI inference. This is not a replacement for accelerators; it is an additive demand driver that expands the total infrastructure required.

This insight has direct implications for specific stocks. The market broadly understands the AI server exposure of a leading BMC (baseboard management controller) supplier, but it has not yet fully priced in the incremental growth from server CPUs driven by agentic AI workloads. The same is true for a test socket provider, where a significant portion of revenue is already exposed to CPUs, including both x86 and ARM-based architectures. For both companies, the CPU opportunity represents a source of upside that is not yet reflected in consensus estimates. The market lacks a clear view on the magnitude of server CPU growth into 2027 and beyond, and this analytical gap creates a meaningful opportunity for investors who can identify the beneficiaries early.

## TSMC’s Profitability Upside Is Underappreciated as Investors Focus on Pricing Philosophy Rather Than Structural Operating Leverage

For the industry's most dominant player, the debate has moved from demand to pricing and profitability. Investors broadly acknowledge that this foundry leader possesses exceptional pricing power, but many are puzzled by what they perceive as a lack of aggression in price increases. This is a misunderstanding of the company's strategy. The company is likely to maintain its current pricing philosophy, with major revisions occurring on an annual basis, not through frequent adjustments. However, the more important driver of profitability upside is not headline price increases, but rather ongoing capacity debottlenecking, productivity improvements, favorable product mix, and better operating leverage.

Investors are underestimating the cumulative impact of these factors. Consensus margin expectations remain too conservative. The company’s profitability is structurally improving as it scales its most advanced nodes and its CoWoS (chip-on-wafer-on-substrate) advanced packaging technology. The next earnings report could serve as a catalyst, particularly if profitability surprises to the upside and reinforces confidence in the sustainability of its earnings trajectory. The key question for investors is whether the market will continue to focus on the pace of price increases, which is the wrong metric, or whether it will begin to appreciate the structural margin improvement that is already underway.

## MediaTek’s AI ASIC Opportunity Is Under-Owned and Under-Analyzed, with the Full Scope of Content Expansion Not Yet Priced In

Among the most discussed names was a leading IC design house, which is transitioning from a traditional smartphone application processor provider to a key player in AI ASICs. While investors broadly understand the long-term opportunity, many remain underappreciative of the potential pricing and content expansion embedded within its next-generation project. A key point of confusion is the magnitude of future dollar content growth. While investors increasingly recognize that the company's content per chip could rise meaningfully versus previous generations, there is a lack of clarity on the underlying pricing assumptions and the extent of its contribution within the AI ASIC roadmap.

This analytical gap is compounded by investor positioning, which remains relatively light. Several investors indicated that they reduced exposure after the stock's strong rally earlier this year, and many US investors remain underweight. There is also a meaningful degree of skepticism regarding the company's long-term market position, particularly in relation to its US customer's ASIC strategy and the future share allocation between this company and another major ASIC player. However, this debate may be premature. The first AI ASIC program has yet to enter volume production, with commercialization expected by the end of 2026. This first chip is a critical milestone that validates the company's ability to participate in leading-edge programs. Beyond the first project, the company is actively engaging in multiple future opportunities. The market is far from fully reflecting the potential pipeline.

## The Report Leaves Critical Questions Unanswered: The Magnitude of CPU Demand, the Sustainability of ASP Uplifts, and the Potential for In-House Chip Design by Hyperscalers

While the report provides a detailed framework for understanding the current investment landscape, it also leaves several important questions unresolved. First, the magnitude of server CPU demand driven by agentic AI remains highly uncertain. The report identifies this as an underappreciated growth driver, but it does not provide a precise quantification of the incremental demand. How much additional CPU infrastructure will be required, and over what timeframe? Second, the sustainability of average selling price (ASP) uplifts for key components, such as BMCs and test sockets, is a function of both technology complexity and competitive dynamics. Can these companies maintain their pricing power as the supply chain scales, or will competition eventually erode margins? Third, the possibility that major US cloud service providers could gradually bring more chip design capabilities in-house over time represents a significant long-term risk for ASIC design partners. The report acknowledges this debate but does not resolve it. These open questions are not weaknesses in the analysis; they are the natural boundaries of any forward-looking research. For investors seeking to build conviction, these are precisely the areas that warrant deeper due diligence and ongoing monitoring.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: Prioritize Pricing Power, Dollar Content Expansion, and Exposure to Underappreciated Growth Drivers

For investors looking to act on the insights from this report, a structured decision framework is essential. The most important filter is pricing power. Companies that can raise prices or expand their dollar content per chip are better positioned to sustain earnings growth regardless of the overall pace of hyperscaler spending. The second filter is exposure to underappreciated growth drivers, specifically server CPUs. The market has already priced in AI accelerator demand; the incremental alpha will come from identifying the beneficiaries of the next wave of infrastructure buildout. The third filter is valuation relative to the opportunity. The report highlights several names where investor positioning is light and where consensus estimates may be too conservative. The combination of strong pricing power, an underappreciated growth driver, and low investor expectations creates the most attractive risk-reward profile.

The key decision for investors is whether to focus on the established leaders with clear profitability upside, such as the dominant foundry, or on the smaller, more specialized players where the CPU and ASIC opportunities are not yet fully understood. Both approaches have merit, but they require different risk tolerances and time horizons. The established leader offers lower risk and more predictable earnings, while the specialized players offer higher potential returns but with greater uncertainty. The report suggests that both sets of opportunities exist, and the optimal portfolio will likely include exposure to both.
