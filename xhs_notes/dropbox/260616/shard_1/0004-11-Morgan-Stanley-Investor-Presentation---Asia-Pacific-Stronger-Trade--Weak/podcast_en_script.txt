EN_B: Absolutely. That is the central theme of the report. There is a clear divergence. On one side, exports are booming, especially tech-related trade. On the other side, private sector demand, particularly for credit, is softening further.

EN_B: It is actually broadening. The report shows a chart on export breadth. In May, we saw growth not only in integrated circuits and computers, but also in autos, plastic products, and even furniture.
EN_A: So this isn’t a narrow rally. It means the whole manufacturing supply chain is firing?
EN_B: Exactly. And the key driver is the global AI cycle. This means China’s export structure is upgrading. It is moving from being the world’s factory to a critical node in the AI supply chain.
EN_A: That sounds encouraging. But what about the other side? How weak is domestic demand?
EN_B: There is a striking data point. In May, new short-term household loans actually went negative, minus 70 billion yuan. The five-year average was plus 110 billion. Long-term loans also fell sharply.
EN_A: Wait, negative short-term loans? What does that mean?
EN_B: It means households are not just borrowing less to spend. They are actively paying down existing debt. It shows deleveraging is still underway. And the report says the recent housing sales rebound is narrow, not a broad recovery.
EN_A: So with exports strong and domestic demand weak, what does that mean for prices? Are we still talking about deflation risks?
EN_B: Yes. The report looks at PPI and CPI. PPI month-on-month slipped in May, mainly because the oil price impulse faded. And critically, there is very limited transmission to core CPI.
EN_A: What does that mean in plain English?
EN_B: It means the economy is not really reflating. Corporate margins are under pressure, and consumer demand is still soft. The report calls it “limited reflation.”
EN_A: What about policy? Is the government stepping in?
EN_B: Yes. The report notes that fiscal spending was slow in April and May, but it should accelerate from the third quarter. The focus will be on the “Six Networks”: water, logistics, data, 5G, smart grids, and computing.

EN_B: This is the most interesting part. The report questions whether smarter industrial policy alone can close the supply-demand gap. It argues that the baton of investment has just been passed from sectors with massive overcapacity to those with less oversupply, for now.
EN_A: So the real fix is still deeper reforms, like changing local government incentives?
EN_B: Exactly. The report says rebalancing requires reform in cadre evaluation, social welfare, and the fiscal system. But these are complex issues, and the report only points to the direction. It does not give a full answer.
