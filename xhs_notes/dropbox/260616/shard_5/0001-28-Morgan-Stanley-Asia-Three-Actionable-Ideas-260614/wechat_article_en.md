# The AI Infrastructure Build-Out Is Rewiring Asia’s Supply Chain: Three Bets on the New Bottlenecks

The conventional narrative about artificial intelligence investing has focused overwhelmingly on the chip designer and the hyperscaler. The dominant logic has been: identify who builds the GPU, identify who deploys it, and allocate capital accordingly. That framework is now dangerously incomplete. The AI infrastructure build-out has entered a new phase where the critical bottlenecks are shifting from the semiconductor fab to the physical layer of interconnect—the printed circuit boards, the memory subsystems, and the energy that powers it all. Investors who continue to fixate on the front end of the AI stack risk missing the most asymmetric opportunities in the current cycle.

A global investment bank’s latest "Three Actionable Ideas" for Asia Pacific makes this argument explicit through three specific overweight recommendations: Zhen Ding in Taiwan’s PCB sector, Winbond in Taiwan’s memory space, and ONGC in India’s energy complex. These three names are not random picks. They represent a coherent thesis about where value is migrating as AI scales. The pattern is clear: the AI trade is rotating from design to production, from logic to interconnect, and from the data center itself to the energy grid that sustains it. Each of these three ideas captures a distinct layer of that rotation.

The performance data supporting this framework is noteworthy. Across over 1,500 ideas tracked, the cumulative outperformance stands at 9,240 basis points against local benchmarks. The average holding period total return is 4.2%, with a 12-month average total return of 14.3%. A 54% hit ratio across such a large sample size suggests the selection methodology is not random. More importantly, the weekly hit ratio chart shows sustained consistency, not volatility-driven spikes. This is not a momentum-driven strategy. It is a structural thesis being validated over time.

What follows is a strategic unpacking of these three ideas, the logic that connects them, and the second-order implications for portfolio construction in an AI-driven world.

## The PCB Layer Is Becoming the New Constraint in AI Interconnect, and Zhen Ding Is Positioned to Capture the Upside

The first idea centers on Zhen Ding, a Taiwanese PCB manufacturer that the report identifies as the most compelling beneficiary of rising transceiver PCB demand and content expansion. This is not an obvious pick. PCB manufacturing is often viewed as a commoditized, low-margin business. But that framing misses what is happening at the high end of the market.

As AI data centers transition to higher-speed interconnects—moving from 400G to 800G and eventually 1.6T Ethernet—the physical requirements for the PCB become dramatically more stringent. Signal integrity at these speeds demands materials with tighter tolerances, more complex layering, and advanced manufacturing processes. The PCB is no longer a passive substrate. It becomes an active constraint on system performance. The companies that can reliably produce these advanced boards at scale will capture pricing power and share gains that the market has not yet priced in.

The report’s logic suggests that Zhen Ding has the potential to capture meaningful market share as this transition accelerates. The "why now" element is critical: the industry is at an inflection point where the volume of high-speed transceiver shipments is about to inflect upward, and the incumbent PCB supply base is not fully prepared. This creates a window for suppliers with the right technical qualifications and capacity. The question for investors is whether the market is correctly discounting the pace of this transition or whether the shift from standard to advanced PCBs will happen faster than consensus expects.

## Memory Optimization in AI Servers Is a Supply Story, Not a Demand Story, and Winbond Offers a Compelling Entry Point

The second idea, Winbond, requires a different analytical lens. The report states that data points suggest a stronger outlook and that the memory optimization on servers is more of a supply issue. This is a crucial distinction. Many investors approach memory stocks with a demand-driven framework: they ask whether AI will drive higher DRAM or NAND consumption per server. The report suggests this framing is incomplete.

The real dynamic is on the supply side. Memory optimization in AI servers is not primarily about whether hyperscalers want more memory—they clearly do. It is about whether the supply chain can deliver the right types of memory in the right configurations. The bottlenecks are in specialty DRAM, in the interface between memory and logic, and in the ability to qualify new memory solutions for AI workloads. Winbond, as a specialty memory player, is exposed to these dynamics in ways that the broader commodity memory cycle does not capture.

The report explicitly notes that recent share price volatility should provide a good entry point. This is a tactical overlay on a structural thesis. The implication is that the market has overreacted to near-term noise—perhaps inventory adjustments or a single weak data point—while ignoring the underlying supply tightness that is building. For investors willing to look through the volatility, the risk-reward may be asymmetric. The open question is whether the supply constraints are temporary or whether they represent a multi-year structural shift in how memory is allocated within AI servers.

## Energy Security and AI Power Demand Are Extending the Growth Runway for Upstream Energy Producers, and ONGC Stands Out

The third idea, ONGC, is the most counterintuitive of the three. At first glance, an Indian upstream oil and gas company seems disconnected from the AI theme. But the report draws a direct line: energy security and powering AI increase the long-term growth rate for upstream energy producers.

This is a second-order AI trade that most investors have not yet internalized. The scale of energy required to power AI inference at global scale is staggering. Every large language model query consumes an order of magnitude more energy than a traditional search query. As AI moves from training to inference, and as inference becomes embedded in every application, the power demand curve inflects upward. This is not a future scenario. It is already happening, and it is straining grids from Virginia to Singapore.

For upstream energy producers, this means a structural shift in the demand profile for natural gas. Gas is the bridge fuel for AI data centers because it can be dispatched quickly, it is cleaner than coal, and it is abundant in key markets. ONGC, as one of India’s largest natural gas producers, benefits from this directly. The report notes that ONGC now has among the highest natural gas average selling prices globally and has returned capital to shareholders equal to where the market cap sits in 20 years. This is a striking statement: the company has effectively returned its entire current market capitalization to shareholders over two decades, and yet the asset base and pricing power remain intact.

The strategic implication is that ONGC offers a combination of yield and growth that is rare in the energy space. The AI-driven demand for gas provides a long-duration growth tailwind, while the capital return program provides a floor on valuation. The tension is whether the market will re-rate the stock to reflect this dual character or whether it will continue to value it as a mature, declining asset.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Interdependence Risk Across These Three Layers

For all its analytical rigor, the report leaves several meaningful questions unanswered. The most important is the interdependence risk across these three layers. Zhen Ding, Winbond, and ONGC are each exposed to AI in different ways, but they are not independent. A slowdown in data center construction would affect all three simultaneously. A shift in AI architecture away from high-speed interconnects would hit Zhen Ding and Winbond differently. A regulatory crackdown on gas-fired power for data centers would affect ONGC.

The report does not model these scenarios explicitly. It presents each idea on its own merit, which is appropriate for a single-stock selection framework. But for a portfolio manager trying to size positions, the correlation structure matters. Are these three names diversifying or concentrating AI exposure? The answer is not obvious. They are diversifying across the AI value chain—hardware, memory, energy—but they are all long the same macro thesis: that AI infrastructure build-out will continue at an accelerating pace. If that thesis breaks, all three break together.

Investors need to decide for themselves whether this is a feature or a bug. A concentrated bet on a single thesis can generate outsized returns if the thesis is correct. But it requires conviction that the market is not already pricing in the full scope of the AI infrastructure cycle.

## A Decision Framework for Evaluating AI Infrastructure Bets

For readers trying to apply this logic to their own portfolios, a structured decision framework is more useful than a list of recommendations. The report implicitly offers one, and it can be made explicit.

First, identify the bottleneck. The AI value chain has multiple layers, but at any given point in time, the most attractive investments are in the layer where supply is most constrained relative to demand. Right now, that appears to be in advanced PCBs, specialty memory, and baseload energy. Six months ago, it was in GPU packaging. Six months from now, it may be in cooling infrastructure or grid interconnection.

Second, assess the duration of the constraint. Some bottlenecks are temporary—a factory fire, a shipping disruption, a qualification delay. Others are structural—a shortage of engineering talent, a regulatory limit on power availability, a materials science challenge. The report’s three picks all lean structural. Zhen Ding’s advantage is in manufacturing capability that takes years to replicate. Winbond’s opportunity is in supply dynamics that are not easily resolved. ONGC’s tailwind is in energy demand that is secular.

Third, check the pricing. The best opportunities are not in the most obvious names. They are in names where the market has not yet repriced the asset to reflect the new reality. The report signals this explicitly for Winbond, citing share price volatility as an entry point. For Zhen Ding and ONGC, the implication is similar: the market is still applying old frameworks to new realities.

Fourth, stress-test for correlation. If the AI thesis breaks, how much downside is there? If the thesis accelerates, how much upside? The asymmetry matters. The report’s track record suggests that these ideas have historically delivered positive relative returns, but past performance is not a guarantee of future results.
