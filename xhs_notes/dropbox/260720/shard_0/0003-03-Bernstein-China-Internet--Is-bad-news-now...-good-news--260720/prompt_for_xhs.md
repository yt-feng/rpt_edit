## TAKING STOCK OF THE CHINA INTERNET SECTOR

This note serves as a summary of some of our latest thoughts on the China Internet sector. At a high level, a confluence of fundamental and flow headwinds contributed to a difficult first six months of 2026, with the average stock we cover falling $16\%$ year to date (after the recent pop). Some of the concerns we'd had about consumption growth and the state of regulation at the start of 2026 persist. On the other hand, the catch down in sector valuations since the start of the year left most stocks we cover back near trough 2022-2023 level valuations until a couple weeks ago, and should mean risk-reward in the sector is somewhat improved.

Fundamentally, some of the discourse around AI spend (e.g. the idea the platforms will need to sponsor inference cost ad infinitum) had become extreme in our view. The debate around AI capex and ROI will continue, but with Xiaowei (Weixin agent) now rolling out to users, and Alibaba's Apsara cloud conference on the horizon, we're hopeful the debate will at least partly shift to what can go right.

## AI inference costs only scale if transactions and GMV follow

Aside from the ongoing global debate about the magnitude of AI capex, and the ROI that results, one of the core bearish arguments we've heard related to Tencent and Alibaba has centred around earnings revisions, and the risk the AI roll-out for these companies means they'll need to carry significant losses related to inference costs - while consumer AI monetization remains minimal.

Relative to operating cash flow, Tencent's capex (10.9% of LTM revenue) remains modestly lower than Alibaba levels (12.3% on the same basis).
