## Korea Defense

Korean defense drew strong investor interest, driven by: 1) significant 2Q underperformance, 2) low correlation with AI within Industrials, and 3) accelerating order momentum. This combination positions the sector as an attractive option to diversify the portfolio while offering both relative value and upcoming catalysts.

We attribute the 2Q underperformance to three factors: 1) stagnant order development, 2) market skew toward memory, and 3) share price weakness among global defense peers. However, with order momentum expected to build in 2H (link to latest note), an expanding Western European TAM, and compressed multiples that discount these catalysts, we expect Korean defense to regain momentum in 2H26.

We believe key pivotal developments for the Korea Defense sector that occurred in the past month are as follows:

1) Switzerland exploring Korean air defense: Switzerland is considering Korean air defense systems due to delay in Patriot deliveries (link)

2) Spain finalizing K9 order: Spain is likely to finalize its K9 (SpH) order with Hanwha Aerospace (link)

3) LIG D&A and Rheinmetall MOU: LIG D&A and Rheinmetall signed an MOU for air defense missiles (link)

4) Thales and Hanwha Aerospace MOU: Thales and Hanwha Aerospace signed an MOU for Chunmoo guided missiles (link).

5) Peru election concluded: Peru's presidential election is complete (link).

These developments over the past month indicate that key Western European countries are increasingly relying on Korean companies for faster execution. Additionally, previous order bottlenecks for Rotem in Iraq and Peru, which stemmed from political uncertainty, are also easing.

Hence we believe the Korea Defense sector is well positioned to show some signs of revival in 2H, especially with valuations levels now quite attractive respective to earnings profiles (Exhibit 1 - Exhibit 4).

While investors frequently question if it is too early to build exposure to long-duration robotics stocks, we emphasize that robotics has become a top global policy priority, and that it is a race—a core thesis of our recent Korea Robotics initiation piece.

South Korea: The Ministry of Science and ICT are establishing robot data training centers with government funding slated for next year (link) while President Lee is convening major robotics CEOs for a roundtable on June 29th (link).

United States: Commerce Secretary Lutnick recently declared robotics “the next battleground in technological competition” framing China’s state-backed industry as a national security threat (link).

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Hanwha Aerospace

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Hyundai Mobis

We are Buy-rated. Our 12-month target price of W710,000 is based on a target P/E multiple of 15.0x applied to 2035E EPS discounted back to June 2027.

Downside risks: 1) Cost inflation-related margin pressure along with production disruption from the fire in the India plant 2) Slower-than-expected execution in terms of Boston Dynamics or HMG autonomous driving push.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - HL Mando

We are Buy-rated. Our 12-month target price of W109,000 is based on a target P/E multiple of 15.0x applied to 2035E EPS discounted back to June 2027.

We see four downside risks: 1) No humanoid actuator order by the end of 2027, 2) Higher Capex in North America expected in the next few years, hence, end-demand is key for margins, 3) Stagnant ADAS business, 4) Cost inflation.

## Price Target Risks and Methodology - Robotis

We are Buy rated. Our 12-month target price of W630,000 is based on a target P/E multiple of 60.0x applied to 2035E EPS discounted back to June 2027.

We see three downside risks: 1) Execution risks due to slower progress at the Uzbekistan facility, 2) No major orders for humanoid actuators in 2027/2028, and 3) Aggressive pricing on key products leading to dilution in margins and earnings.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

## M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
