The conventional view of Doosan Enerbility as a cyclical capital goods company with lumpy order flows and volatile margins is now outdated. Based on a detailed bottom-up analysis of nuclear projects approaching procurement, approximately 35 gigawatts of new nuclear capacity representing a total addressable market of USD 250 billion are now identifiable and actionable for Doosan. This pipeline translates into an estimated USD 44 billion, or KRW 67 trillion, of potential nuclear equipment contracts alone.

This is not a theoretical opportunity set. These projects have moved beyond feasibility studies and early engineering phases. They are entering the pre-procurement and early procurement stages, where long-lead items such as reactor vessels, steam generators, and other heavy components begin to be ordered. For a company with Doosan's manufacturing depth, proven execution record, and vertically integrated supply chain, this represents a multi-decade earnings transformation that investors have only begun to price in.

The implications extend well beyond nuclear equipment. The same structural forces that support nuclear deployment—rising electricity demand, energy security concerns, and the need for firm, dispatchable clean power—are also driving a sustained upcycle in gas turbines. Doosan's gas turbine business is already benefiting from capacity expansion and a tightening supply-demand balance, with orders expected to grow from KRW 4.7 trillion in 2025 to approximately KRW 7 trillion by 2030. As the installed base scales, gas services revenue could reach KRW 0.4 trillion by 2030 and KRW 1 trillion by 2035, providing a growing, higher-margin recurring revenue stream.

Taken together, these two engines—nuclear equipment and gas turbines—are fundamentally altering Doosan's business mix. By 2027, more than 75 percent of standalone revenue is expected to come from these segments, up from a historically diversified portfolio that included lower-margin coal and desalination businesses. The result is a margin profile that should reach low-teens operating margins by 2028, a significant improvement from recent levels and increasingly aligned with global energy equipment peers.

This article argues that the market has not fully appreciated the quality shift underway at Doosan. The company is transitioning from a project-based, lumpy order business to one with multi-year revenue visibility, structural margin expansion, and scarcity value as one of the few non-Chinese, non-Russian suppliers capable of delivering large-scale nuclear components at scale. The investment case is no longer about the next quarter's orders. It is about the compounding effect of a decade-long energy infrastructure cycle.

## The Nuclear Pipeline Is Not Speculative: It Is Trackable and Approaching Procurement

The most common objection to a nuclear investment thesis is that projects are perpetually delayed, underfinanced, or cancelled. This skepticism is rooted in the painful history of Western nuclear builds in the 2010s, where projects such as Vogtle and Flamanville suffered massive cost overruns and schedule delays. However, the current pipeline is fundamentally different in structure, geography, and maturity.

A global investment bank report identifies approximately 35 gigawatts of nuclear projects where Doosan could realistically participate, with a total project cost of USD 250 billion. Critically, this is not a speculative list of early-stage announcements. The analysis focuses on projects that have progressed sufficiently through key development milestones: regulatory licensing approval, completion of front-end engineering and design, and finalization of project financing and ownership structure. Heavy nuclear equipment contracting typically occurs only after these milestones are met, which means the identified pipeline is actionable over the next three to five years.

Regionally, the near-term pipeline is dominated by Europe. Poland is moving toward equipment and EPC contracting this year for its Lubiatowo-Kopalino project, which will use AP1000 technology. Bulgaria is targeting EPC contracting in 2027 for Kozloduy. The Czech Republic's Dukovany project, awarded to KHNP, is already booked. These European projects benefit from strong government backing, EU policy support for nuclear as a strategic clean energy source, and financing mechanisms that reduce project risk.

Longer term, the key source of upside is the United States. While U.S. nuclear projects have been slower to materialize, policy initiatives such as Department of Energy financing programs, the ADVANCE Act, and growing recognition of nuclear's role in data center power demand are creating a more supportive environment. U.S. projects are expected to emerge toward the end of the decade, and Doosan's established role in AP1000 supply chains positions it to capture a meaningful share.

The distinction between booked and unbooked contracts is important. Of the estimated USD 44 billion in potential nuclear equipment contracts, a portion is already in backlog from projects such as Shin Hanul 3 and 4 in South Korea and Dukovany in the Czech Republic. The remainder represents upside that is trackable and, in many cases, at advanced stages of negotiation. The report's order forecasts incorporate risk-adjusted assumptions based on the probability of project progression, which provides a realistic baseline rather than an optimistic scenario.

## Doosan's Competitive Moat Is Deeper Than Manufacturing Scale

The nuclear equipment supply chain is not a commodity market. It is a high-barrier, regulated, and technically demanding industry where trust, track record, and manufacturing depth are paramount. Doosan's position as the most experienced nuclear power system supplier outside of China and Russia is not easily replicated.

The company's Changwon facility in South Korea is organized as a vertically integrated "one-stop shop" for nuclear components. Doosan handles virtually every stage of production in-house, from raw material steel production to final assembly of complex nuclear components. This vertical integration reduces reliance on outside suppliers, shortens lead times, and provides greater control over quality and cost. For nuclear projects where schedule predictability is critical, this capability is a competitive advantage that commands premium pricing.

Doosan's track record is unparalleled among non-Chinese, non-Russian suppliers. The company has manufactured and supplied 34 reactor vessels and 124 steam generators globally. For AP1000 designs specifically, Doosan has delivered 6 reactor vessels and 12 steam generators. This accumulated experience creates a knowledge base that cannot be replicated quickly. Any new competitor attempting to enter this market would need to dedicate decades of investment, regulatory certification, and project references to reach the same level of capability and trust.

The company is also positioning for the next generation of nuclear technology. Doosan began investing heavily in small modular reactor manufacturing more than five years ago, building new experience ahead of the market. As the SMR market is expected to expand, Doosan is promoting an SMR foundry business as a consignment manufacturer for key SMR companies. The company plans to establish dedicated production lines that can shorten production periods and mass-produce high-quality SMRs. This early positioning provides an option on a technology pathway that could open an even larger addressable market in the 2030s.

The competitive moat extends to cost and execution. Doosan's vertically integrated manufacturing base and accumulated experience allow it to deliver high-quality components with relatively faster lead times and more predictable execution compared to most non-Chinese suppliers. This supports shorter construction timelines and lower overall project costs, which are critical factors for project developers and financiers. In an environment where nuclear project economics are under intense scrutiny, Doosan's execution reliability is a differentiator that strengthens its negotiating position.

## Gas Turbines Provide a Second Structural Growth Engine

While nuclear equipment captures the attention, the gas turbine business is equally important to the investment thesis. The global gas turbine market is experiencing a structural upcycle driven by rising electricity demand, the retirement of coal-fired capacity, and the need for flexible generation to complement intermittent renewables. Supply constraints, particularly for large-frame turbines, are reinforcing pricing power and order visibility.

Doosan's gas turbine capacity expansion is well underway. The company is increasing production capacity to meet growing demand, and we expect gas orders to increase from KRW 4.7 trillion in 2025 to approximately KRW 7 trillion by 2030. This growth is supported by strong end-market demand in Asia, the Middle East, and increasingly in North America.

The real margin story, however, is in gas services. As the installed base of Doosan gas turbines scales, services revenue becomes a growing and higher-margin recurring stream. We estimate gas services revenue could reach approximately KRW 0.4 trillion by 2030 and KRW 1 trillion by 2035. This is a fundamentally different revenue profile from equipment sales, which are lumpy and project-dependent. Services revenue provides visibility, stability, and incremental margin expansion over time.

The combination of nuclear and gas creates a diversified energy equipment portfolio that is less dependent on any single end-market. While nuclear projects are long-cycle and driven by government policy and utility procurement, gas turbines are shorter-cycle and driven by commercial power generation needs. Together, they provide a balanced growth profile that supports sustained order growth beyond current company targets.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: Execution Risk, Financing, and Competition

No investment thesis is without risks, and the nuclear opportunity set carries several that the report acknowledges but does not fully resolve. The most significant is execution risk. Nuclear projects have a history of delays, and the current pipeline is no exception. While the report incorporates risk-adjusted assumptions based on project progression milestones, the reality is that even advanced projects can face permitting challenges, supply chain bottlenecks, or financing gaps that push timelines to the right.

The report identifies a USD 44 billion equipment contract opportunity, but it does not provide a detailed probability-weighted breakdown by project. For investors, the key question is not whether the pipeline exists, but what portion of it will convert into booked orders within a given timeframe. The answer depends on factors that are difficult to predict with precision: regulatory approvals in multiple jurisdictions, the availability of project financing at attractive terms, and the willingness of utilities and governments to commit to large-scale capital projects.

Financing is a particularly important variable. While European projects benefit from government backing and policy support, the scale of capital required is enormous. The total project cost for the identified pipeline is USD 250 billion, and even a small fraction of that represents a significant commitment for project developers. The availability of low-cost financing, whether through government guarantees, development banks, or private capital, will be a critical determinant of project progression.

Competition is another area where the report provides limited detail. While Doosan is the most experienced non-Chinese, non-Russian supplier, it is not the only one. Westinghouse, Framatome, and KHNP (Doosan's domestic partner) are also active in the market. The report assumes Doosan captures a disproportionate share of equipment orders, but the actual allocation will depend on commercial negotiations, technology selection, and local content requirements. In some markets, such as Poland, the technology provider (Westinghouse) may have a stronger position in equipment supply than the report assumes.

Finally, the report does not fully address the SMR opportunity. While Doosan is positioning for SMR manufacturing, the market is still in its infancy. Commercial SMR deployments are not expected until the late 2020s or early 2030s, and the competitive dynamics are uncertain. Doosan's consignment manufacturing model may provide a path to participation, but the revenue contribution from SMRs is likely to be modest in the near term.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: Three Questions to Evaluate the Thesis

For investors considering Doosan Enerbility, the report provides a structured framework for evaluating the investment case. Three questions are central to the decision:

First, how much of the nuclear pipeline is real? The report provides a bottom-up analysis of 35 gigawatts of projects approaching procurement, but investors should distinguish between booked contracts, projects with signed agreements, and those still in negotiation. The progression of projects through licensing, engineering, and financing milestones provides a useful framework for assessing probability. Investors should track the number of projects that move from FEED to EPC contracting each year as a leading indicator of order conversion.

Second, what is the margin trajectory? The report expects Doosan's operating margins to reach low-teens by 2028, driven by mix shift toward nuclear and gas. Investors should monitor quarterly revenue composition and segment margins to verify that the mix shift is occurring as expected. The key variable is the proportion of revenue from high-margin nuclear equipment and gas services versus lower-margin legacy businesses. If the mix shift accelerates, margins could exceed expectations. If nuclear projects delay, margins could disappoint.

Third, what is the appropriate valuation framework? The report uses a sum-of-the-parts valuation with a price target of KRW 100,000, underpinned by a standalone DCF valuation. However, the report argues that valuation should increasingly reflect not just current backlog, but also forward order pipeline and mix shift toward higher-quality businesses. This is a departure from traditional valuation approaches that focus on trailing earnings or order backlog. Investors should consider whether the market is pricing in the pipeline upside or whether it remains discounted due to execution risk.

The report does not provide a definitive answer to these questions, but it provides the analytical framework for investors to make their own assessment. The key insight is that Doosan is no longer a cyclical capital goods company. It is a structural energy infrastructure play with multi-year visibility, margin expansion potential, and scarcity value. The questions for investors are about timing, execution, and valuation, not about the direction of the opportunity.
