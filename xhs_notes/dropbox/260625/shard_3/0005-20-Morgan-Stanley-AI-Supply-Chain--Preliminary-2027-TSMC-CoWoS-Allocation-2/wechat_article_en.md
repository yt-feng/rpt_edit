# Nvidia Still Dominates, but the AI Supply Chain is Becoming a Multi-Polar Battlefield

The conventional narrative that the AI semiconductor supply chain is a one-company show centered on Nvidia is becoming dangerously incomplete. Based on a detailed analysis of TSMC's CoWoS advanced packaging capacity allocation for 2027, a very different picture emerges: one where the overall market is nearly doubling year-over-year, but the growth is increasingly distributed across a wider array of customers and use cases. The key insight for strategists and investors is not merely that demand is strong, but that the composition of that demand is shifting in ways that will reward those who understand the new architecture of the supply chain.

The data from a recent global investment bank report on the subject presents a granular breakdown of CoWoS consumption, which is the critical bottleneck for high-performance AI chips. The headline number is staggering: total global CoWoS demand is projected to reach 2,694k wafers in 2027, up from 1,394k in 2026, representing a 93% year-over-year increase. While Nvidia remains the single largest consumer, its share of the total pie is forecast to decline from 56% in 2026 to 45% in 2027. This is not a sign of Nvidia's weakness, but rather a powerful signal that the AI buildout is broadening beyond just the dominant GPU supplier.

The strategic question is no longer just "how much will Nvidia grow?" but "which of the emerging second-tier players will capture the most value, and where are the structural bottlenecks that will create winners and losers?" The answers lie in the intricate details of wafer allocation, substrate supply, and the unexpected rise of the central processing unit (CPU) as a major consumer of advanced packaging.

## The Rise of the ASIC and the CPU is Reshaping the CoWoS Demand Profile

The most significant structural shift revealed in the 2027 allocation data is the dramatic expansion of demand from custom ASIC designers and, perhaps more surprisingly, from CPU manufacturers. This is not a marginal change; it represents a fundamental broadening of the advanced packaging ecosystem.

Broadcom's total CoWoS consumption is expected to rise 61% year-over-year to 484k wafers, driven largely by Google's TPU v7 (Ironwood) and v8i (SunFish) ASICs. This confirms that the hyperscalers are increasingly committed to building their own silicon, reducing their reliance on merchant silicon for certain workloads. The report suggests that Broadcom's 365k CoWoS-S allocation alone could imply nearly 3.9 million units of the TPU v8i, a product with a larger die size and higher chip value than the previous generation. The implication is clear: the hyperscaler ASIC market is becoming a major force that can absorb significant capacity and drive meaningful revenue for its design partners.

Even more striking is the explosion in demand from AMD. The report projects AMD's CoWoS consumption will surge by 308% year-over-year to 530k wafers in 2027. While a portion of this is for its MI400 series GPU, the real story is the "Venice" CPU. The data shows that CoWoS bookings for the Venice CPU are expected to jump from 50k wafers in 2026 to 270k in 2027, implying production of 6.75 million units. This is a direct response to the growing need for "agentic AI" workloads, where CPUs are required to handle complex orchestration and decision-making tasks that GPUs are less efficient at. Nvidia's own Vera CPU is also a major consumer, with implied production of 5.75 million units. The strategic implication is that the CPU is no longer a peripheral component in the AI data center; it is becoming a core, high-value consumer of the most advanced packaging technology.

## The Substrate Bottleneck is the Next Critical Variable to Watch

While the demand picture is robust, the report also highlights a crucial, unresolved variable that could introduce significant volatility into the supply chain: the availability of ABF (Ajinomoto Build-up Film) substrates for advanced packaging. This is a classic "So What?" moment for supply chain analysts. The entire CoWoS capacity build is predicated on the assumption that the substrate supply will be there to support it.

The report's analysis of MediaTek's role as a design service partner for Google's TPU v8t (ZebraFish) is a case in point. MediaTek's booking of 180k CoWoS-S wafers implies a potential shipment of approximately 3.6 million TPU units. However, the report explicitly notes that this is above the analyst's own 2.5 million unit shipment assumption, which had incorporated a potential shortage of ABF substrates. The key phrase is "if MediaTek can help Google source more T-Glass, there may be upside to our shipment assumption."

This single sentence reveals the fragility of the entire demand forecast. The growth of every player in this ecosystem, from Nvidia to AMD to Google, is ultimately constrained by the capacity to produce these complex, multi-layer substrates. The report does not fully answer the question of how the supply of T-Glass and other high-end substrates will scale to meet the 93% demand growth. This is the single most important open question for investors. If the substrate supply chain fails to scale, the CoWoS capacity expansion will be underutilized, and the revenue expectations for the entire AI semiconductor ecosystem will face a significant headwind. Conversely, any company that can secure or enable new substrate capacity will be a major strategic beneficiary.

## A Decision Framework for Navigating the 2027 AI Supply Chain

For a portfolio manager or corporate strategist, the detailed allocation data provided in this report can be translated into a three-part decision framework to identify the most resilient and high-opportunity areas of the supply chain.

**First, map the bottleneck exposure.** The most critical question for any supplier is: how exposed are you to the substrate and advanced packaging bottlenecks? Companies that are solely reliant on a single substrate source or a single CoWoS variant (e.g., only CoWoS-L or only CoWoS-S) carry higher risk. The winners will be those who either have diversified substrate sourcing (like MediaTek's potential to secure more T-Glass) or who provide the equipment and services needed to expand the bottleneck itself. Companies like Winway, MPI, and Hon Precision, which are involved in chip manufacturing and testing, are well-positioned because their value is tied to the overall volume of chips flowing through the system, not just the success of one specific chip.

**Second, analyze the customer concentration and growth vector.** The data shows that while Nvidia's absolute growth is massive, its relative share is declining. The fastest growth rates are in the "second tier": AMD at +308%, GUC at +329%, and Marvell at +276%. An investor should ask: which suppliers are best positioned to serve these high-growth, emerging customers? For example, ASE/SPIL is a major beneficiary of AMD's Venice CPU ramp, while Amkor is gaining share in Nvidia's CoWoS-R for the Vera CPU. The key is to identify which packaging and testing partners are winning the business of the fastest-growing end customers, not just the largest.

**Third, evaluate the technology inflection point.** The report details a shift from HBM3e to HBM4 and HBM4e across multiple platforms. This is a significant technology upgrade that requires new testing and assembly capabilities. Suppliers that have already invested in the equipment and processes for HBM4 (e.g., Kyec in the Nvidia and Google supply chain) are likely to see a step-change in revenue and margin as these volumes ramp. The decision framework should prioritize companies that are not just riding a volume wave, but are also benefiting from a value-enhancing technology upgrade cycle.

## What the Full Report Reveals That This Analysis Cannot

This article has synthesized the core strategic arguments, but the original report contains far more granular data that is essential for a complete investment thesis. The full report includes a detailed breakdown of HBM consumption by chip type, revealing that total AI HBM demand is projected to reach 51 billion GB in 2027. It also provides specific CoWoS capacity expansion plans by vendor (TSMC, ASE/SPIL, Amkor) through the end of 2027, which is critical for modeling supply versus demand.

Furthermore, the report's stock ratings and price targets for companies like MediaTek (rated Overweight), Aspeed (Overweight), and ASE (Overweight) are based on this proprietary allocation model. The analysis also includes a detailed assessment of the "TriggerFish" TPU, a 2nm inferencing chip that could open up new revenue streams for Google's TPU leasing services. These are the kinds of specific, actionable insights that are impossible to fully capture in a summary.

The most significant unresolved question that the report raises is the exact timeline and magnitude of the substrate supply expansion. The report acknowledges the risk but does not provide a definitive answer on when or if the bottleneck will be fully resolved. This is where the deepest analytical work is required.
