## Takeaways from Soda Music expert call

## Limited copyright budget but potential AI music leadership

On 23 June 2026, the NOM China internet team hosted a conference call with an expert from ByteDance's (unlisted) Soda Music to discuss user growth trends, monetization strategies, and content positioning. Soda Music is a music-streaming platform employing an interest-based recommendation algorithm rather than traditional search functionality. Key takeaways are below:

The NOM China internet team will host a group call at 3:30PM on 26 June 2026 with a manager from ByteDance's Red Fruit to shed some light on the AI animated drama and short drama industry's status quo and growth sustainability. Please approach your NOM sales representative to register for the call if interested.

## Continuing user expansion with growth decelerating to \~50% in FY26E

According to the expert, as of May 2026, Soda Music reached approximately 50mn DAU and 150mn MAU. The expert estimates DAU to reach \~60mn by year-end, representing \~50% y-y growth versus nearly 100% in 2025. The expert believes this deceleration reflects diminishing marginal returns from Douyin's traffic diversion as the convertible user pool almost approaches saturation. According to the expert, supplementary user growth drivers for Soda Music also include cost-effective, sponsor-funded offline events (music festivals, campus activations) targeting lower-tier markets. As per the expert, the average daily user time spent for Soda Music came in at an average of 50 minutes as of May 2026, declining from a peak of 60-70 minutes, owing to user base expansion diluting the average and competition from other online entertainment activities such as reading novels and watching short dramas etc.

## Internal competition among music apps under Bytedance group

In the expert's view, unlike TME's (TME US, Buy) complementary app portfolio serving distinct user cohorts, Soda Music and Tomato Music (also owned by Bytedance) operate as independent business units with separate teams, copyright portfolios, and P&Ls, engaging in internal competition. According to the expert, as both the music platforms have leveraged Douyin's infrastructure for user acquisition, the user overlap between the two platforms is substantial at 60-70%, though demographics differ: Soda focuses on younger and more male audience, while Tomato targets older, female-oriented audiences aligned with web novel and romance drama content (as Tomato Music is also a part of Tomato ecosystem including Tomato Novel, Tomato Audio and Red Fruit short and AI animated dramas). No near-term merger of these two platforms is planned, as per the expert.

## Advertising-first monetization amid copyright budget constraints

According to the expert, Soda Music's revenue will likely grow by 30% in FY26E, mainly driven by growth in ads, which comprises \~70% of total revenue versus \~30% from subscriptions currently — a stark contrast to peers like TME, which derives 60-70% of its online music revenue from subscriptions. As per the expert, rather than pursuing user growth at all costs, Soda Music is mandated by ByteDance to maintain break-even operations, reflecting its positioning as an auxiliary "moat" within the broader Douyin ecosystem to satisfy users' listening needs and absorb excess ad inventory. Consequently, the expert stated that Soda Music maintains strict copyright budget discipline, i.e., limiting acquisition of exclusive premium content that would drive subscription conversion. Soda Music has achieved operational break-even excluding personnel costs and headquarter costs but remains loss-making on a fully-loaded basis, according to the expert.

## Fewer copyright coverage but potential AI music leadership

According to the expert, Soda Music's catalog covers \~75% of nationally consumed songs, below TME's 90-95% and NetEase Cloud Music's (9899 HK, Not rated) 85%, as a result of constrained budgets on copyright music. Within this coverage, 60% comprises licensed copyrighted music while 15% consists of platform-produced and UGC (user generated content) content, as per the expert. The expert expects TME's copyright leadership to persist. However, the expert believes Soda Music may have an advantage in AI music development given ByteDance's substantial AI investments, proximity to internal AI teams, and Douyin's vast data ecosystem enabling superior user preference understanding and trend discovery.

The expert acknowledged proliferation of AI-generated remixes — modified through speed/lyrical alterations and uploaded via UGC channels — that circumvent paywalls, particularly appealing to lower-tier users unwilling to pay for subscriptions. The expert stated that despite such content prompting infringement concerns, Soda Music removes such content only upon receiving specific takedown notices with complete evidence from rights holders, lacking proactive screening obligations. This creates a cat-and-mouse dynamic where rights holders' limited enforcement resources cannot address all infringing content — a challenge difficult to resolve at the current stage, in view of the expert.

## Limited involvement in offline concerts and fan economy ventures

Regarding offline concerts and fan economy businesses, the expert indicated Soda Music is unlikely to pursue resource-intensive initiatives including large-scale idol concerts and K-pop-style fan events, given: (1) stringent regulatory pressure on fan culture; (2) thin profit margins; and (3) lack of specialized operations teams capable of converting Douyin traffic into monetizable fan engagement, resulting in unfavorable ROI. Instead, the expert believes Soda Music will continue executing small-scale, sponsor-funded offline activities (tier-3/4 city music festivals, campus events) as cost-effective user acquisition channels while avoiding deep involvement and investment in fan economy ventures.
