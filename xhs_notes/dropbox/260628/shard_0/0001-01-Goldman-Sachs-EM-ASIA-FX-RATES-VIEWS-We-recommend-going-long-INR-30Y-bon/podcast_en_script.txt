EN_A: So there's a big call on Indian bonds from a major investment bank report. They're recommending buying 30-year Indian government bonds. What's the story behind that?
EN_B: The core idea is that India's macro backdrop is improving, the central bank just announced measures to attract foreign capital, and there's a growing chance India gets included in the Bloomberg Global Aggregate Index. All of that supports lower yields on long-term bonds.
EN_A: Let's start with the economy. The report says Q1 GDP growth came in at 7.8 percent, which was 50 basis points above their forecast. What's driving that?
EN_B: Strong investment and services activity. And Q2 is tracking above expectations too. With oil prices revised lower, they recently raised their calendar year 2026 real GDP forecast by 0.3 percentage points to 6.8 percent, and their fiscal year 2027 forecast by 0.4 points to 6.5 percent.
EN_A: So India is proving more resilient than feared? And lower oil prices are a big help?
EN_B: Exactly. Lower oil reduces the risk of more fuel price hikes and eases fiscal pressure. The report cut core inflation forecasts for 2026 to 4.2 percent and for 2027 to 4.0 percent. Lower inflation and less fiscal strain are a double win for bonds.
EN_A: Now, the RBI announced a bunch of measures on June 5. What were the key ones for bonds?
EN_B: Two stand out. First, they removed interest and capital gains tax for foreign investors in government bonds. That was a big operational hurdle. Second, they expanded the Fully Accessible Route, or FAR, to include all new 15-year, 30-year, and 40-year bond issuances.
EN_A: So foreign investors can now more easily buy Indian bonds across the curve?
EN_B: Right. Especially the ultra-long end. It makes the entire yield curve more investable. It will take time for the market to fully adjust, but the direction is clearly supportive.
EN_A: And the Bloomberg index inclusion? The report says it's a question of timing, not direction. Why so confident?
EN_B: Because India was already fully included in the JPM GBI-EM Global Diversified index in March, reaching the 9 percent cap. That shows market access has improved a lot. Custodians can now post margin on behalf of investors, for example. Combined with the new RBI measures, inclusion in the Bloomberg index seems very likely.
EN_A: How much money would that bring in?
EN_B: The report estimates India's weight would be around 0.7 percent, which could trigger about 15 billion dollars in passive inflows over the phase-in period.
EN_A: So why specifically 30-year bonds? The front end has already rallied on lower oil prices.
EN_B: That's the point. The front end has already priced in a lot of good news. But the ultra-long end still has room. More ultra-long bonds are entering the FAR universe. And there's a structural trend in India called the "savings glut" — household savings are shifting from bank deposits to pension and insurance funds, which naturally need very long-dated bonds.
EN_A: So what's the specific trade recommendation?
EN_B: Buy 30-year Indian bonds at a yield of 7.34 percent, with a target of 6.90 percent and a stop-loss at 7.65 percent. That implies a roughly 44 basis point drop in yields if the trade works.
EN_A: What are the risks? A global rate sell-off?
EN_B: That's the main one. If the Fed turns more hawkish, it could drag EM yields higher across the curve, including India. Also, if domestic inflation or fiscal risks re-emerge. The report actually leaves a couple of questions open, like how exactly the savings glut will play out — the full report goes deeper into that.
