EN_A: So what’s the real question this report is trying to answer?
EN_B: It’s trying to figure out which companies will actually win in this AI-driven world. It looks at a few key players, like Capgemini, Temenos, Wise, and some fintechs like Zopa and Raisin, to see how they are adapting.

EN_B: The first one is about agentic AI. The report says major tech firms launched 1,200 to 1,500 new AI services in the past year, plus two million open-source models. EN_A: What does that mean for a regular company?
EN_B: It means you can’t just buy an AI tool and expect magic. You need deep industry knowledge and customer relationships to make it work. Capgemini is betting on that. Right now, most AI is being used in customer service and back-office functions like fraud detection.
EN_A: Okay, what’s the second insight?
EN_B: The business model is changing. Agentic AI uses tokens to perform tasks, and the cost of those tokens can blow up depending on complexity. This is a real headache for CFOs because IT budgets get eaten up fast.
EN_A: So, software companies need to rethink how they charge?
EN_B: Exactly. The report mentions some companies are looking at smaller, domain-specific models trained on their own data to cut token costs. Also, AI is making people more productive, which will naturally reduce headcount. But the real challenge is how to retrain those people for higher-value roles.
EN_A: And the third insight, around payments and digital assets?
EN_B: In payments, competition is heating up, so everyone is spending more on marketing. But the bigger story is regulation. In the last 12 to 18 months, we’ve seen a huge shift, with laws like the GENIUS Act in the US and MiCA 2 in Europe. This is giving traditional banks more confidence to invest in digital assets.
EN_A: What does that mean for stablecoins and tokenization?
EN_B: The report is very positive on tokenization, saying it improves efficiency and liquidity. But for stablecoins, it’s more mixed. If they don’t pay interest, people might just use them to move money and then cash out. So transaction volume could explode, but the amount of money sitting in stablecoins might not grow as fast.
EN_A: It sounds like the report leaves some questions open.
EN_B: Definitely. For example, it says AI will reduce headcount but doesn’t fully explore which jobs will disappear and which will be created. Also, the long-term value of stablecoins is still a question mark, depending on whether they become yield-bearing. The full report probably goes deeper into these.
