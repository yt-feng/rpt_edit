# The Next Six Months Will Define Which Chinese Internet Companies Have True Earnings Momentum, Not Just AI Hype

The market has spent the first half of 2026 pricing Chinese internet stocks on narrative. AI agents, video generation models, and data center expansion have dominated headlines and driven multiple expansion across the sector. But the second half of the year will force a reckoning. A concentrated wave of product launches, regulatory resolutions, and earnings catalysts between June and December 2026 will separate companies that can convert technology investment into measurable revenue from those that cannot.

This is not a call to rotate out of the sector. The industry view remains attractive, and the structural case for Chinese internet remains intact. But the nature of the opportunity is shifting. The next six months will be dominated by company-specific catalysts rather than macro-driven beta. For investors, this means the differentiation between winners and laggards will be sharper than at any point in the past two years.

The catalyst calendar is unusually dense. From Beijing Kingsoft Office Software's AI agent product release in July to NetEase's major game launch in the third quarter, from Bilibili's new title to VNET's hyperscaler contract wins, the list extends across online entertainment, enterprise software, and data center infrastructure. Each catalyst carries a specific importance rating and surprise potential. But the strategic question is not whether individual events will succeed. It is whether the sum of these catalysts can shift the earnings trajectory for the sector as a whole.

Three themes tie these events together. First, AI monetization is moving from promise to proof, with multiple companies set to report AI-related annual recurring revenue for the first time. Second, the regulatory environment is approaching a resolution point that could unlock valuation for travel and gaming names. Third, the data center cycle is entering a new phase where early capacity investment in specific regions will determine who wins the next round of hyperscaler spending.

Investors who treat the next six months as a passive holding period risk missing the most important signal for portfolio construction in 2027. The companies that deliver on these catalysts will command premium valuations. Those that do not will face a difficult reevaluation.

## Beijing Kingsoft Office's AI Agent Release Will Test Whether Enterprise AI Monetization Has Reached an Inflection Point

The July 2026 release of WPS AI Agent by Beijing Kingsoft Office Software is the single most important catalyst for the enterprise software segment this year. The report rates this event as high importance with in-line surprise potential, meaning the market expects a product launch but has not yet priced in the revenue implications.

The strategic significance goes beyond Kingsoft Office itself. WPS Office has over 500 million monthly active users in China, making it the dominant productivity platform in the country. If WPS can successfully embed AI agents into its document processing, spreadsheet analysis, and presentation workflows, it will establish a template for how enterprise AI monetization works in China. The key metric to watch is not user adoption in the first quarter after launch, but the conversion rate from free AI features to paid subscriptions.

The report does not provide specific revenue forecasts for this catalyst, which leaves an important analytical gap. Investors need to assess what level of AI ARR would constitute success. Based on comparable enterprise software companies globally, a reasonable benchmark would be that AI features contribute 5 to 8 percent of total subscription revenue within six months of launch. If WPS achieves this, it would validate the thesis that Chinese enterprise users are willing to pay for AI productivity tools. If it falls short, the narrative around enterprise AI monetization in China will face skepticism.

## Bilibili and Kuaishou Face Divergent Paths in Gaming and Video AI, and Their Stock Performance Will Diverge Accordingly

Bilibili and Kuaishou are both rated overweight by the report, but their catalyst profiles could not be more different. Bilibili's second-half narrative hinges on two events: Bilibili World and a potential Investor Day in July, followed by the launch of Three Kingdoms: Wangdao Tianxia in the fourth quarter. Kuaishou's catalyst is the iteration of its Kling video generation model and potential corporate action around a spin-off.

The report forecasts RMB 2 billion in annualized grossing for Bilibili's new game. This is a meaningful number. To put it in context, a successful mobile game in China typically generates RMB 3 to 5 billion in annual revenue. Bilibili's forecast implies the company expects this title to be a solid mid-tier hit, not a blockbuster. The question is whether Bilibili can sustain a pipeline of such titles or whether this is a one-off success driven by the intellectual property of Three Kingdoms.

For Kuaishou, the catalyst is more ambiguous. The report mentions model iteration from Kling, SeeDance, Happy Horse, and VEO, along with any information release on ARRs and potential corporate action on Kling. The ambiguity is itself a signal. Video AI monetization in China remains experimental. Kuaishou's Kling model has shown impressive technical capability, but the path from technical demonstration to revenue is unclear. The report does not provide ARR forecasts for Kuaishou's AI initiatives, which suggests the analysts themselves see this as a high-variance outcome.

The divergence between these two names illustrates a broader theme. Companies with identifiable, quantifiable product launches offer investors clearer risk-reward profiles. Companies relying on AI model iteration and potential corporate restructuring offer optionality but also significant uncertainty.

## The Regulatory Resolution for Trip.com Is the Most Underappreciated Catalyst in the Sector

The report identifies two catalysts for Trip.com in the second half of 2026: a regulatory update on the antitrust investigation in the second quarter, and a potential cut of aviation fuel surcharges in the third quarter. Both are rated very high importance, and the report explicitly states that a one-off fine would have minimal impact on value.

The strategic implications are substantial. The report notes that a compulsory change in business model or commission rate would trigger a market revaluation of Trip.com's profitability. This is the key analytical insight. The market has been discounting Trip.com's valuation due to regulatory overhang. If the resolution is a one-off fine, the overhang disappears and Trip.com's valuation should re-rate toward global travel platform peers. If the resolution includes structural changes to the commission model, the earnings impact could be meaningful but the uncertainty would be removed, which itself is a positive.

The fuel surcharge catalyst adds a cyclical tailwind. High oil prices have suppressed travel demand in China. A reduction in surcharges ahead of the summer peak season could release pent-up demand. The combination of regulatory resolution and demand recovery creates a powerful two-part catalyst that the report suggests is not fully priced.

## VNET's Hyperscaler Strategy Depends on a Single Geographic Bet That Is About to Be Tested

VNET's catalyst list is the longest in the report, spanning new order wins, secondary listing progress, strategic cooperation with CATL, overseas expansion, and potential data center rental hikes. But one factor ties all of these together: VNET's early resource buildup in Ulanqab.

The report explicitly states that VNET's position in Ulanqab gives it an advantage in hyperscalers' new round of data center tenders. This is a concentrated geographic bet. Ulanqab, in Inner Mongolia, offers low-cost land, access to renewable energy, and favorable climate conditions for data center cooling. But it also means VNET's near-term performance is tied to the pace of hyperscaler expansion in that specific region.

The potential data center rental hike catalyst is particularly interesting. The report says the possibility is low now, but if it materializes, it should happen in Ulanqab where VNET is strong. This is a second-order implication that investors should monitor. A rental hike in Ulanqab would signal that hyperscaler demand is outstripping supply in that region, which would be a positive for VNET but also attract new competitors to the area.

The strategic cooperation with CATL adds a new dimension. After CATL's affiliate became VNET's biggest shareholder, the potential for cooperation on data center energy storage becomes logical. Energy storage is becoming a critical component of data center economics, especially for AI workloads that require high power density. If VNET can integrate CATL's battery technology into its data center operations, it could achieve a cost advantage over competitors.

## The Report Leaves Unanswered Questions About AI Monetization Timelines and Competitive Dynamics

For all its detail on individual catalysts, the report does not fully address two critical questions. First, what is the realistic timeline for AI to materially impact revenue for Chinese internet companies? The report identifies AI ARR as a metric to watch for Beisen and Kuaishou, but does not provide a sector-wide framework for when AI revenue will become a meaningful percentage of total revenue.

Second, the report does not address competitive dynamics within the AI video generation space. Kuaishou's Kling, ByteDance's Jimeng, and other models are competing in a rapidly evolving market. The report notes model iterations from multiple players but does not assess which company has a sustainable competitive advantage.

These unanswered questions are not weaknesses in the report. They reflect genuine uncertainty in the sector. Investors who can develop their own frameworks for these questions will have an edge.

## A Decision Framework for the Second-Half Catalyst Cycle

For investors navigating the next six months, a structured approach is essential. The following framework translates the report's catalyst analysis into actionable decision points.

First, categorize each catalyst by its revenue visibility. Product launches with specific revenue forecasts, such as Bilibili's game launch at RMB 2 billion annualized grossing, offer high visibility. AI model iterations with no ARR forecasts offer low visibility. Allocate portfolio weight accordingly.

Second, assess regulatory catalysts as binary events. The Trip.com antitrust resolution is the clearest example. Either the outcome is a fine, which removes overhang, or it is a structural change, which resets earnings expectations. In either case, uncertainty is resolved, which is positive for valuation.

Third, monitor data center catalysts for confirmation signals. VNET's order wins from hyperscalers will confirm whether the Ulanqab bet is paying off. Rental hikes in that region will confirm supply-demand tightness. Do not trade on anticipation alone; wait for confirmation.

Fourth, treat corporate action catalysts as optionality, not core positions. The potential spin-off of Kuaishou's Kling or VNET's secondary listing are events that could unlock value, but they are not guaranteed. Do not build a position solely on the hope of corporate action.

Fifth, use the surprise ratings as a calibration tool. The report rates most catalysts as in-line or modest upside surprise. This suggests the analysts believe the market has already partially priced these events. The exceptions are Beisen's annual result and Meitu's vision festival, both rated meaningful upside surprise. These represent the highest-conviction opportunities in the catalyst calendar.
