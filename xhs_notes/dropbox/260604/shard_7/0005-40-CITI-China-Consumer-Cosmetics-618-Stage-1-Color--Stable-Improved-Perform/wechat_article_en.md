# The 618 Data Reveals a Structural Shift: Domestic Brands Are No Longer Just Winning on Volume, They Are Winning on Platform Strategy

The first stage of Tmall's 618 sales event and Douyin's May results are now complete, and the numbers tell a story that goes far beyond a single shopping festival. For the serious observer of China's consumer economy, these rankings are not merely a snapshot of promotional performance. They are a diagnostic of a deeper structural realignment in how beauty brands capture value across two fundamentally different retail ecosystems.

The headline is clear: domestic brands have held their ground on Tmall and improved their position on Douyin. But the more important insight lies beneath the surface. The pattern of who wins, where they win, and how they win reveals that the competitive battleground has shifted. It is no longer simply domestic versus international. It is platform-specific strategy versus a one-size-fits-all approach. The brands that understand the distinct mechanics of Tmall and Douyin are pulling ahead. Those that treat both platforms as interchangeable distribution channels are falling behind.

This matters now because the window of strategic adjustment is closing. The 618 data is not an isolated event. It is the latest data point in a trend that has been building through Double 11 and the Goddess Festival. The brands that fail to internalize the lessons of this cycle will find themselves structurally disadvantaged in the next one.

## Domestic Brands Have Stabilized on Tmall, but the Real Story Is the Divergence in Premium Positioning

On Tmall, the top 20 rankings for stage one of 618 show that domestic brands have maintained their positions from last year. Proya remains number one. Comfy and Winona hold steady at twelve and fourteen respectively. This stability is not a sign of stagnation. It is a sign of consolidation. After years of volatility, the leading domestic brands have established a floor beneath their Tmall performance. They are no longer fighting to break into the top tier. They are fighting to hold it.

But the more telling data point is Maogeping. The brand climbed from seventeenth to fifteenth year-over-year. That two-position gain might seem modest, but its significance lies in the context. Maogeping is the only domestic brand in the premium segment within the Tmall top 20. Every other domestic brand in the ranking competes in the mass market. This is a critical distinction. The premium segment on Tmall is still overwhelmingly dominated by international names: SkinCeuticals, Estee Lauder, SK-II, La Mer, Clarins. These brands command higher price points, higher margins, and stronger brand equity. Maogeping's ascent into this territory signals that a domestic brand can now credibly compete at the premium level on China's most established e-commerce platform.

The question this raises is whether Maogeping's success is replicable. The brand benefits from the personal brand equity of its founder, Mao Geping, a legendary makeup artist with decades of credibility. This is a unique asset that cannot be easily duplicated. Yet the broader implication is that the premium segment on Tmall is not structurally closed to domestic players. It is simply very hard to enter. For investors, the key monitorable is whether other domestic brands attempt to follow Maogeping upmarket, and whether Tmall's algorithms and promotional mechanics reward that move.

Meanwhile, the international brand rankings on Tmall show significant churn. SkinCeuticals jumped from fifth to second. Estee Lauder moved from fourth to third. SK-II rose from seventh to fourth. But Lancome dropped from second to fifth, and L'Oreal Paris fell from third to eighth. This volatility among international brands suggests that even established names are struggling to maintain consistent positioning. Their rankings are increasingly dependent on promotional intensity and specific campaign timing. This is a warning sign. If international brands cannot stabilize their Tmall rankings, they risk losing the platform-level loyalty that has historically been their moat.

## Douyin Is Where the Structural Shift Is Most Pronounced, and It Favors Domestic Brands

If Tmall shows stability, Douyin shows acceleration. The May sales data, which covers nearly half of the 618 event period, reveals a far more dynamic and domestically skewed competitive landscape. Of the top 20 brands on Douyin, twelve are domestic or Chinese-owned, compared to only four on Tmall. This is not a minor difference. It is a structural divergence.

Proya has taken the number one spot on Douyin, up from second during last year's 618 and Double 11. Comfy improved to thirteenth from sixteenth. Winona re-entered the top 20 at sixteenth after being absent during last year's 618. But the most dramatic moves came from emerging domestic brands. Grain Rain jumped from twelfth to fifth. Pechoin rose from fourteenth to ninth. Forest Cabin entered the list at eleventh. These are not household names outside China, and even within China they represent a newer generation of domestic beauty brands that have built their growth strategies around Douyin's content-driven commerce model.

The pattern is unmistakable. Douyin is becoming the primary growth engine for domestic beauty brands, while international brands are losing ground. Most international names on Douyin either maintained or dropped in ranking. Helena Rubinstein stayed flat at third. L'Oreal stayed flat at fourth. La Mer stayed flat at sixth. But Estee Lauder dropped from fifth to eighth. Lancome dropped from seventh to tenth. SK-II dropped from ninth to twelfth. Olay dropped from nineteenth to twentieth. The only international brand to improve was CPB, which moved from twentieth to eighteenth.

This divergence raises a fundamental strategic question. Why are domestic brands winning on Douyin while international brands are not? The answer likely lies in the platform's operational requirements. Douyin rewards content velocity, influencer relationships, and real-time engagement in ways that differ from Tmall's search-and-rank model. Domestic brands have built organizational capabilities around these requirements. International brands, with their global brand guidelines and slower decision-making processes, are struggling to adapt. The data suggests this is not a temporary phenomenon. It is a structural disadvantage that will persist unless international brands fundamentally re-engineer their Douyin operations.

## The Platform Divergence Creates a New Strategic Imperative: Brands Must Choose or Build Dual Capabilities

The 618 data makes one thing painfully clear. A brand cannot succeed on Tmall and Douyin using the same playbook. The two platforms reward different behaviors, different pricing strategies, and different content approaches. Tmall remains a premium-leaning, search-driven marketplace where brand heritage and product efficacy matter most. Douyin is a content-first, algorithm-driven ecosystem where engagement velocity and influencer reach determine outcomes.

For domestic brands, this creates both an opportunity and a risk. The opportunity is that Douyin provides a faster path to scale and a more forgiving environment for newer brands. The risk is that over-investment in Douyin could come at the expense of Tmall positioning, which still drives higher average selling prices and more stable repeat purchase behavior. The data suggests that the most successful domestic brands, like Proya and Maogeping, are managing to win on both platforms. But they are the exception, not the rule.

For international brands, the strategic calculus is even more challenging. They cannot afford to abandon Tmall, where their premium positioning still commands respect. But they also cannot afford to ignore Douyin, which is capturing an increasing share of beauty spending. The data shows that international brands are currently losing ground on both platforms in relative terms. On Tmall, their rankings are volatile. On Douyin, they are declining. This suggests a need for a more radical approach than incremental optimization. International brands may need to create separate brand architectures or product lines specifically designed for Douyin, rather than simply pushing their existing Tmall strategies onto the platform.

The unanswered question is whether any international brand will make this leap. The data from this 618 cycle does not provide an answer. But it does provide a clear warning. The brands that continue to treat Douyin as a secondary channel will find themselves structurally disadvantaged in the next cycle.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Sustainability of Premium Domestic Brand Growth and the Role of Profitability

The 618 data is rich in top-line insights, but it leaves several critical questions unanswered. The most important of these is profitability. The data shows that Maogeping is gaining share in the premium segment on Tmall and that domestic brands are winning on Douyin. But it does not reveal the cost of that growth.

On Douyin, success is often purchased through aggressive discounting, high influencer fees, and significant content production costs. A brand that climbs from twelfth to fifth, as Grain Rain did, may have done so by sacrificing margin. The data does not tell us whether these gains are profitable. Similarly, Maogeping's premium positioning on Tmall may come with high customer acquisition costs that are not visible in the ranking data.

The report also does not address the question of repeat purchase behavior. A brand can win a promotion cycle through aggressive discounting, but if customers do not return at full price, the long-term value is limited. The 618 data is a snapshot of transaction volume, not customer lifetime value. For serious investors, this distinction is critical. The brands that are winning today may not be the brands that are winning three years from now if their growth is built on unsustainable acquisition costs.

Another open question is the role of offline retail. The 618 data covers only Tmall and Douyin. But premium beauty in China still has a significant offline component, particularly in department stores and specialty retail. Maogeping's offline presence may be a material factor in its brand equity that is not captured in the e-commerce rankings. Similarly, international brands like Estee Lauder and Lancome may be maintaining offline market share even as their online rankings slip. The full picture requires integrating online and offline data, which this report does not provide.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: Four Questions to Evaluate Any Beauty Brand After This 618 Cycle

For investors trying to translate the 618 data into actionable decisions, the following framework may be useful. It is designed to cut through the noise of ranking changes and focus on the structural factors that determine long-term value.

First, what is the brand's platform diversification strategy? A brand that wins on only one platform is exposed to platform-specific risk. Tmall's algorithm changes or Douyin's fee structure adjustments could dramatically alter a brand's competitive position. The ideal is a brand that has demonstrated the ability to win on both platforms, even if the strategies differ. Proya and Maogeping are the clearest examples of this from the current data.

Second, what is the brand's premium positioning trajectory? The data shows that premium brands on Tmall are more stable and command higher margins. A domestic brand that can move from mass market to premium, as Maogeping is doing, creates significant value. But this move is difficult and requires brand equity that cannot be built overnight. Investors should look for evidence of premium pricing power, not just volume growth.

Third, what is the brand's cost of growth on Douyin? The ranking improvements on Douyin are impressive, but they may be masking margin compression. Investors need to understand the ratio of influencer costs and promotional discounts to incremental revenue. If a brand is growing revenue but destroying margin, the long-term value proposition is weak.

Fourth, what is the brand's offline presence and brand equity? The most valuable beauty brands in China have historically been those that combine strong online performance with meaningful offline distribution. The 618 data is online-only. Investors should seek to understand whether a brand's online rankings are supported by offline brand strength, or whether they are purely a function of promotional intensity.

These four questions will separate the brands that are building sustainable competitive advantage from those that are simply riding a promotional wave. The 618 data provides the raw material for this analysis, but it does not provide the answers. That is the work of the investor.
