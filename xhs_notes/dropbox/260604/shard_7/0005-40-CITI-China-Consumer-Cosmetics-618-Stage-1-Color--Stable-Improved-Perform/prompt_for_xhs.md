# 618 Stage 1 Color: Stable/Improved Performance for Domestic Brands at Tmall/Douyin; Maogeping Stands Out

# CITI'S TAKE

With the stage 1 of Tmall 618 and Douyin's May sales now concluded, Citi expects the overall cosmetics industry to maintain the moderate improvement trend observed during the latest Double 11 and this year's Goddess Festival. At Tmall, major domestic brands maintained rankings as compared with last year while Maogeping improved; among international brands, rankings shifted more significantly. At Douyin, most domestic brands improved while international brands declined in rankings. Mao Geping remains our top Buy in the China cosmetics sector.

Tmall's 618 sales results in stage 1 — The stage 1 of Tmall's 618 sales event (21-30 May) saw stable performance for domestic brands compared with last year. Among the top domestic names, Proya remained #1, while Comfy and Winona maintained their #12 and #14 rankings vs. last year. The key domestic brand that saw a ranking improvement was Maogeping, which climbed to #15 vs. #17 last year. Overall, domestic brands have held their ground vs. last year. In contrast, the international brand landscape saw more significant shifts, e.g. SkinCeuticals (from #5 to #2); Estee Lauder (from #4 to #3); SK-II (from #7 to #4); Lancome (from #2 to #5); Clarins (from #10 to #7); L'Oreal Paris (from #3 to #8); Helena Rubinstein (from #8 to #10). Competitive landscape: The top 20 is still dominated by international brands. Most brands within the top 20 are in the premium segment. Maogeping stands out as the sole domestic brand in the premium segment. Within the mass market segment, domestic brands are ahead of their international peers in top 20. Among the few international mass brands in the top 20, L'Oreal Paris and Olay both saw their rankings decline, while La Roche-Posay as a derma brand was the only one with improved ranking.

Douyin's May sales results — Douyin's May sales results, which cover almost half of the 618 sales event (May 15 – Jun 18), showed more drastic changes in brands and rankings in the top 20 as compared to Tmall's 618 sales results in stage 1. The list is also dominated by domestic brands or brands owned by Chinese companies at 12 brands as compared to 4 brands at Tmall. Proya gained #1 ranking vs. #2 at last year's 618 and Double 11. Comfy also saw improvement to #13 from #16 during last year's 618, and it did not make top 20 during last year's Double 11 due to reduced KOL livestreaming. Winona made it to the list at #16 vs. last year's 618 (out of top 20), but slightly dropped from #14 during last year's Double 11. As compared to last year's 618, most international brands maintained or dropped in rankings, including Helena Rubinstein (flattish at #3), L'Oreal (flattish at #4), La Mer (flattish at #6), Estee Lauder (dropped from #5 to #8), Lancome (dropped from #7 to #10), SK-II (dropped from #9 to #12) and Olay (dropped from #19 to #20), with the exception of CPB (improved from #20 to #18). In contrast, some emerging domestic brands saw significant improvement, including Grain Rain (up from #12 to #5), Pechoin (up from #14 to #9) and Forest Cabin (entered the list at #11).

# Valuation

Our DCF-based target price for MAO Geping at HK\$97.9 implies 27x 2026E P/E, at the high end of Chinese cosmetics peers and above the average, which we believe is justified for its faster growth potential, superior ROE, and stronger brand equity. We use a DCF valuation methodology to capture the company's strong cash flow generation and long-term growth potential. Our WACC assumption of 10.9% is derived from a risk-free rate of 2.0%, an equity-risk premium of 9.0%, and beta of 1.00. Our 10-year free cash flow growth estimates decelerate from the thirties to mid- to high-single digits and our terminal growth rate is 2%.

# Risks

Our Quantitative model rates the stock High Risk for its short listing history, but we override it considering its qualitative factors such as solid revenue growth in the past few years, a stable management team on the back of the company's long operating history (founded in 2000), and strong execution.

Key downside risks that may prevent the stock from reaching our target price include: 1) heavy reliance on founder IP; 2) challenges while diversifying into other skincare businesses; 3) fierce competition and dynamic market; 4) rising cost of online channel development; 5) consumption trade-down; and 6) a lock-up period of cornerstone investors will expire on Jun 10, 2026, which could put pressure on the stock price.
