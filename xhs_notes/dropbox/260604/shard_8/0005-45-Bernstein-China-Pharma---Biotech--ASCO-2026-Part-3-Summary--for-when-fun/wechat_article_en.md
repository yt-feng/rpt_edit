# China Biotech's Valuation Collapse Has Created a Separation Between Price and Reality That Will Not Last

The post-ASCO selloff in Chinese healthcare stocks has been the most severe in recent memory. Multiples across the sector have fallen to or below the trough levels seen in April 2024, a period when sentiment was arguably far more justified in its pessimism. The Hang Seng Biotech Index has given back nearly all of its gains since early 2025, and individual names that presented compelling clinical data at the American Society of Clinical Oncology meeting have been marked down by 8 to 15 percent in a single week. This is not a rational response to fundamentals.

What makes this rout particularly striking is the absence of any identifiable negative catalyst from the data itself. The ASCO presentations delivered by Chinese biotech companies were, by any objective measure, incrementally positive. Kelun-Biotech's sac-TMT strengthened its case as the most credible immunotherapy partner in lung cancer. Innovent's IBI363 showed survival tails that dwarf existing standards of care. Akeso's AK112 achieved a statistically significant overall survival benefit in squamous non-small cell lung cancer, a first for any PD-1/VEGF bispecific. The clinical narrative improved. The stock prices collapsed.

This divergence between data and price is the defining feature of the current moment. It creates a situation where the most analytically rigorous approach is to ask not whether the fundamentals have changed, but rather what it means when the market systematically ignores them. The answer, for a strategic investor, is that periods of maximum disconnection between price and intrinsic value are precisely when the most asymmetric opportunities emerge. The question is which names have the strongest catalysts to force a re-rating when the tide turns.

## The ASCO Data Strengthened the Core Theses for Three Chinese Biotech Names, Yet All Three Were Punished by the Market

Kelun-Biotech presented updated data from the OptiTROP-Lung05 trial that reinforced sac-TMT as the most effective add-on to existing immunotherapy in first-line non-small cell lung cancer. The combination of sac-TMT with pembrolizumab delivered higher objective response rates, deeper tumor shrinkage, and more durable responses than pembrolizumab alone. The progression-free survival hazard ratio of 0.35, implying a median PFS exceeding 16 months, is among the strongest ever reported for a combination strategy in this setting. The overall survival hazard ratio trending at 0.55 makes it increasingly likely that the OS benefit will hold at maturity. Beyond first-line, sac-TMT remains the only agent with a statistically significant OS hazard ratio in second-line EGFR-mutant NSCLC, a population with high unmet need. The recent global phase three win in late-line endometrial cancer, announced by partner Merck, adds external validation beyond China.

Innovent's IBI363, a PD-1/IL-2 beta bispecific, showed data that challenge the current treatment paradigm in a more fundamental way. In patients previously treated with immunotherapy, the 24-month overall survival rate was 48 percent in squamous and 43 percent in adenocarcinoma. This compares to 8 percent and 21.7 percent for docetaxel, and 29 percent for Datopotamab deruxtecan in adenocarcinoma. In first-line patients, IBI363 achieved an 86 percent objective response rate across all subgroups, the highest among all agents presented at ASCO in this setting. The mechanism of action, combining PD-1 blockade with a engineered IL-2 that preferentially expands tumor-killing T cells while avoiding regulatory T cell stimulation, could ultimately challenge not only current PD-1 monotherapies but also the first wave of PD-1/VEGF bispecifics.

Akeso's AK112 achieved a statistically significant overall survival hazard ratio of 0.66 in the HARMONi-6 trial, comparing ivonescimab plus chemotherapy against tislelizumab plus chemotherapy in first-line squamous NSCLC. This is a first-time achievement for any PD-1/VEGF bispecific or any approach combining immuno-oncology and anti-angiogenic mechanisms in this setting. The data provide positive read-through to the global HARMONi-3-sq trial, which will release its final PFS and interim OS readout in the third quarter of 2026.

Despite these data, Kelun fell 15 percent, Innovent fell 8 percent, and Akeso fell by a similar magnitude. The market's reaction was not a rejection of the science. It was a rejection of the entire sector, driven by macro factors, capital flow dynamics, and a general risk-off posture that has little to do with individual company prospects.

## The Valuation Compression Has Reached Levels That Imply No Future Revenue Growth, Creating a Margin of Safety That Is Difficult to Ignore

The current valuation environment for Chinese biotech is best described as extreme by any historical measure. On a one-year forward price-to-earnings basis, the large-cap pharma names have nearly reached all-time lows. Hengrui trades at 31 times, down from 50 times at the October 2025 peak, and below its April 2024 trough of 47 times. Hansoh at 26 times is near its trough. Sino Biopharm at 18 times and CSPC at 11 times are at or near historical lows. These are companies with revenue growth CAGRs of 6 to 22 percent over the 2026 to 2029 period. The market is pricing in no improvement in margins, no pipeline optionality, and no recovery in the operating environment.

The biotech names are even more compressed. On a one-year forward price-to-sales basis, Kelun trades at 25 times, down from 39 times at the peak, and only modestly above its April 2024 trough of 22 times. Innovent trades at 6 times, below its trough. Akeso at 14 times is near its trough. BeOne at 5 times is at its trough. These multiples are pricing in clinical failure rates that are far higher than what the data support, and commercial outcomes that assume no market access, no pricing power, and no competitive differentiation.

For context, the revenue growth CAGRs for these names over the 2026 to 2029 period are 66 percent for Kelun, 26 percent for Innovent, and 54 percent for Akeso. A 25 times price-to-sales multiple for a company growing revenue at 66 percent is not expensive. It is deeply discounted, particularly when the clinical data supporting that growth trajectory have just been strengthened. The market is effectively saying that it does not trust the revenue projections, or that it does not care about them in the current environment. Both statements are true, but neither is permanent.

The key analytical question is whether the current valuation levels provide a sufficient margin of safety for the risks that remain. The answer depends on one's time horizon. Over a three to six month window, momentum can remain disconnected from fundamentals. Over a twelve to eighteen month window, the connection tends to reassert itself, particularly when there are identifiable catalysts that force a reassessment. The next twelve months are rich with such catalysts.

## The Most Asymmetric Opportunity Lies in Names Where the Data Improvement Is Clear but the Stock Price Has Not Yet Reflected It

Kelun-Biotech represents the clearest example of imbalance between fundamentals and price. The company's sac-TMT asset has the strongest dataset in first-line PD-L1 positive NSCLC, the most credible data in second-line EGFR-mutant NSCLC, and a recent global phase three win in endometrial cancer that validates the platform beyond lung. The pipeline extends beyond sac-TMT, with SKB500, a B7H3 ADC, showing a differentiated safety profile in second-line small cell lung cancer. There is no overhang from upcoming data readouts that could disappoint. The risk-reward is skewed to the upside.

The market's concern appears to be about commercial execution and the competitive landscape in China, where multiple PD-1/VEGF bispecifics are in development. But sac-TMT's mechanism is different. It is a TROP2-directed antibody-drug conjugate, not a bispecific. Its competitive advantage comes from delivering a cytotoxic payload to tumor cells while simultaneously modulating the tumor microenvironment through TROP2-mediated effects. The data suggest it works best in combination with PD-1 blockade, not as a replacement for it. This positions sac-TMT as a partner, not a competitor, to the existing immunotherapy backbone. The commercial hurdle is therefore lower than for agents that must displace the standard of care.

Innovent presents a different but equally compelling case. The company has announced two mega-deals this year with Lilly and Pfizer, providing both financial validation and access to global commercial infrastructure. The IBI363 data at ASCO represent the strongest early-stage data for a novel immunotherapy mechanism in lung cancer. The upcoming American Diabetes Association meeting will feature updates on Innovent's GLP-1 assets, including first-in-human data on an oral GLP-1. This provides a near-term catalyst that is independent of the oncology narrative. The risk on mazdutide sales, which has been a concern for some investors, should be contained based on the company's GLP-1 sales tracker data.

Akeso is the most complex of the three. The HARMONi-6 data were positive, but the ASCO discussant raised legitimate questions about the trial design, including the age and gender mix of the patient population and the exclusion criteria related to bleeding risk. The overall survival benefit was limited to squamous histology, which represents only about 30 percent of NSCLC. The non-squamous population, which is larger, remains an uphill battle based on the totality of evidence from HARMONi-2, HARMONi-6, and the earlier HARMONi trials. The global HARMONi-3-sq readout in the third quarter of 2026 is a meaningful catalyst, but there is uncertainty about whether the data will replicate in a global population. Akeso is a market-perform for a reason. The data are good, but the competitive positioning is more contested.

## The Report Does Not Fully Answer Whether the Current Selloff Is a Buying Opportunity or a Value Trap, and the Answer Depends on Three Unresolved Variables

The report provides an excellent analysis of the clinical data and valuation levels, but it leaves several meaningful questions unanswered. The first is whether the capital outflow from Chinese healthcare is structural or cyclical. If it is structural, driven by a permanent reassessment of China's biotech ecosystem due to geopolitical risk, regulatory uncertainty, or changes in the global pharmaceutical supply chain, then the current multiples may be justified. If it is cyclical, driven by a temporary rotation out of risk assets that will reverse when macro conditions stabilize, then the current levels represent a generational opportunity. The report does not take a definitive stand on this question.

The second unresolved variable is the trajectory of the Chinese healthcare policy environment. The volume-based procurement program, which has compressed drug prices in the generic and me-too segments, continues to evolve. The impact on innovative biotech companies is less direct, but the overhang remains. The report notes that there are no fundamental negatives that could explain the capital outflow, but this may understate the psychological impact of policy uncertainty on investor sentiment. Even if the fundamentals are sound, the perception of risk can dominate price action for extended periods.

The third variable is the competitive dynamics within the lung cancer market. The report provides a detailed comparison of the leading domestic assets, but the global competitive landscape is evolving rapidly. Merck's Keytruda remains the standard of care, and its combination strategies are expanding. Roche's Tiragolumab, an anti-TIGIT antibody, has shown mixed data but remains in development. The entry of global players into the PD-1/VEGF space could compress the commercial opportunity for Chinese assets, even if the clinical data are competitive. The report's peak sales estimates are reasonable, but they assume a degree of market access and pricing that may not materialize.

These uncertainties do not invalidate the investment thesis. They simply define the range of outcomes that a strategic investor must consider. The current valuation levels provide a margin of safety that can absorb a significant degree of negative variance. The question is whether the investor has the conviction and the time horizon to wait for the resolution.

## A Decision Framework for Navigating the Disconnect Between Data and Price in Chinese Biotech

For investors trying to make sense of the current environment, a structured decision framework is more useful than trying to predict the timing of the reversal. The framework has three dimensions: catalyst density, valuation buffer, and data quality.

The first dimension is catalyst density. Names with a high density of near-term catalysts are more likely to force a re-rating, because each catalyst provides an opportunity for the market to reassess. Kelun has the highest catalyst density, with the endometrial cancer approval, the ongoing global phase three program in lung cancer, and the pipeline updates for SKB500. Innovent has the ADA meeting in the near term and the ongoing phase three program for IBI363. Akeso has the HARMONi-3-sq readout in the third quarter of 2026, but that is twelve months away, which is a long time in a momentum-driven market.

The second dimension is valuation buffer. Names that are trading at or below their historical troughs on a price-to-sales basis, with revenue growth rates that are accelerating, have the largest valuation buffer. Kelun at 25 times sales with 66 percent revenue growth has the largest buffer. Innovent at 6 times sales with 26 percent growth has a smaller buffer but is still attractive. Akeso at 14 times sales with 54 percent growth is in the middle.

The third dimension is data quality. Names with the strongest clinical data, as measured by hazard ratios, response rates, and durability of response, have the most defensible investment thesis. Kelun's sac-TMT has the strongest hazard ratios in both first-line and second-line settings. Innovent's IBI363 has the highest response rates and the most compelling survival tail. Akeso's AK112 has a statistically significant OS benefit, but only in squamous histology, and the discussant's commentary raises legitimate questions.

Applying this framework, Kelun scores highest across all three dimensions. Innovent scores high on data quality and catalyst density but lower on valuation buffer. Akeso scores high on data quality but lower on catalyst density and has unresolved questions about the breadth of its addressable market. This does not mean Akeso is a bad investment. It means the risk-reward is less asymmetric.
