# More Clarity on Cross-Border Investment: A Turning Point for Hong Kong Housing Market?

# Digesting the New Outbound Investment Rules

China's State Council has today issued the Regulations on Outbound Investment, effective 1 July 2026, covering mainland enterprises, organisations, and individual residents. Notably, Article 32 explicitly extends regulatory oversight to Hong Kong-related transactions, signalling a clear intent to tighten supervision of cross-border capital flows. Meanwhile, Article 12 introduces stricter procedural requirements, mandating pre-approval or filing, ongoing information reporting, and comprehensive cross-border capital registration for outbound investments. On enforcement, Article 27 sets out stringent penalties. Illegal gains may be confiscated and fines of $0.1\% - 0.5\%$ of the investment amount imposed where investors: i) engage in prohibited outbound investment; ii) fail to complete required approval or filing procedures; or iii) obtain approvals through bribery, fraud, or other improper means. For non-compliance or failure to rectify, fines may increase to $0.5\% - 1.0\%$ , alongside potential restrictions on outbound investment activities for one to three years.

# Implications for the Hong Kong Housing Market

We believe the new regulations may cause more scrutiny on source of funding for mainland homebuyers. Mainland buyers (based on surname spelling) accounted for 49% of primary market transaction value and 32% of total transaction value in 1Q26. However, not all the mainland buyers would be subject to scrutiny. For example, mainland buyers already became HK permanent residents and give up Hukou in mainland. Also, mainland buyers with a legitimate source of funding, eg earnings HK income. During 2017-2019, around 62% of mainland buyer transactions were subject to Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD). This indirectly suggests those purchases were done by non-HK permanent residents. We think the current proportion of mainland buyers who are non-HK permanent residents should be higher than 62% due to removal of BSD.

# Stock recommendations:

We believe the new policy could have a negative read-through for Hong Kong developers, given the potential headwinds to both residential pricing and volume recovery. We expect SHKP and Henderson may underperform the most, while developers with net cash positions (i.e., CKA and Sino) are likely to relatively outperform.
