## June sales: Weak domestic, robust exports; Chinese brands gain share rapidly in Europe

CAAM's June sales release was broadly in line with expectations, with the sector still showing a clear split between strong export momentum and weak domestic demand. We believe the continued softness in domestic PV demand was likely one of the key reasons behind China auto sector's weakness in June, with MSCI China Autos -19% vs. MXCN's -8% during the month. June domestic PV sales fell 26% YoY to 1.5mn units, while PV exports rose 80% YoY to 0.9mn units, reinforcing our view that the sector's near-term positive fundamentals remain export-led rather than domestic market-led. Against this backdrop, we view the current pullback as a selective buy-the-dip opportunity especially for the one(s) with potential earnings beat at the upcoming 2Q26 result season in Aug (e.g. Geely Auto, Nio while BYD likely meet) rather than a broad-based sector call. We would only become more aggressive with clearer evidence of 1) domestic PV stabilization, 2) pricing/profitability discipline, and 3) export durability, while continuing to favor OEMs with resilient export growth, improving overseas competitiveness and positive earnings revision trends. Our preferred picks in 2H26 are Geely Auto, BYD and Nio. Event-wise, we will watch closely: 1) the upcoming interim results season in mid to late Aug; and 2) the next Trump-Xi meeting, tentatively scheduled for late Sept, for any incremental policy shifts from the US side on China's auto industry.

In this report, we continue to monitor what we view as a structural overseas expansion cycle for Chinese OEMs with a focus on three areas: 1) latest export dynamics, including growth momentum and geographic mix; 2) powertrain trends, particularly the rising role of PHEVs, which we believe could surprise overseas investors as adoption follows a similar path to China; and 3) Chinese OEMs' NEV market share in Europe, which has continued to rise and is becoming an increasingly important proof point of global competitiveness.
