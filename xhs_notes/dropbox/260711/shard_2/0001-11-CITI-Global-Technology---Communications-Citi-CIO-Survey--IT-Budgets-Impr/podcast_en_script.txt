EN_A: A new survey from a global investment bank just dropped, and it’s full of signals about where IT money is really going. IT budgets are accelerating, AI is climbing the priority list. But is this a clean win for everyone? B, what’s your big takeaway from this report?
EN_B: The headline number is encouraging. Overall IT budget growth expectations jumped from 2.6% to 3.3%, which is above the historical average. But the real story is the reallocation. AI now accounts for roughly 6.5% of the IT budget, and nearly half of CIOs say AI spending is starting to cannibalize other areas. That number has jumped significantly from 30% in December to almost 50% now. So it’s not a rising tide that lifts all boats. It’s a fierce competition for a finite pool of dollars.
EN_A: Cannibalization. So which areas are getting squeezed?
EN_B: Cybersecurity is the most visible example. It’s still the second-highest priority, but its budget growth has decelerated by about one percentage point. Digital transformation and back-office software are also losing some ground. Meanwhile, customer-facing applications jumped from sixth to fourth place. So companies are shifting money from defensive or support IT projects toward AI applications that can directly impact revenue or customer experience.
EN_A: So for investors, who are the clear winners and who should be worried?
EN_B: The winners are pretty clear. Microsoft remains the top vendor CIOs want to spend more on, followed by Amazon and Google. On the infrastructure side, data center operators and cloud providers are clear beneficiaries because AI workloads are expanding. But the pressure is real. Companies like Cisco, Dell, IBM, and ServiceNow were named as vendors CIOs would cut spending on to fund AI. And here’s a key red flag: over half of CIOs now expect AI to drive headcount reduction in the next 6 to 12 months. That’s a worrying sign for any software company whose business model is based on per-seat pricing.
EN_A: You mentioned headcount reduction. Does the report point to any specific industry where this might happen first?
EN_B: It doesn’t name specific companies, but it flags the business process outsourcing sector as a likely early candidate. AI use cases in customer support and virtual agents are already moving into production. That’s one of the fastest areas of adoption. Also, in cybersecurity, identity security has become the top priority. The logic is that in an agentic world, every AI agent needs to be treated as an identity that must be protected, routed, and authenticated. This trend continues to favor Microsoft and certain identity-focused independent vendors.
EN_A: That’s a fascinating insight. So what are the big questions this report doesn’t fully answer?
EN_B: Two things. First, the “new money” for AI is shrinking. The percentage of AI funding coming from incremental budgets dropped from 73% to 69%. And the share of CIOs saying AI is cannibalizing other budgets is rising fast. If this trend continues, could AI growth actually start to drag down overall IT spending in the next few quarters? The report doesn’t answer that. Second, while demand for agentic AI is clearly rising, the report shows that the share of AI use cases moving into production only ticked up from 22% to 24%. Most are still in proof-of-concept. So the gap between experimentation and large-scale deployment remains huge. Which companies can actually cross that chasm? That’s still an open question.
