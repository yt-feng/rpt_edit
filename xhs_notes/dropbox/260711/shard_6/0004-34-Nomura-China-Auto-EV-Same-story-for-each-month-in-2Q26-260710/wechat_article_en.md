## The Domestic Market Is Shrinking,But EV Penetration Is Still Rising Because ICE Is Collapsing Faster

June 2026 marked the third consecutive month of roughly 20% year-on-year declines in domestic retail sales. Wholesale shipments of 2.4 million units,down 5.3% year-on-year,were propped up only by exports. The underlying domestic demand picture is worse than the headline suggests. Retail sales of internal combustion engine vehicles fell 39% year-on-year in June. That decline is accelerating,not stabilizing.

Electric vehicles,by contrast,posted a 9.3% year-on-year retail decline in June—still negative,but far less severe. The result is that EV penetration reached 62.3% in the month,continuing its upward trajectory. This is not a sign of EV demand strength in absolute terms;first-half EV retail sales of 4.7 million units were down 13.6% year-on-year. The rising penetration rate is a function of the ICE segment's disproportionate weakness. For investors,this means that any company with a heavy reliance on ICE vehicle sales in China faces structural volume erosion that will not reverse with a cyclical upturn.

The policy environment reinforces this dynamic. The government's anti-involution stance has led to tighter subsidy frameworks,removing the artificial demand that previously boosted low-cost EV segments. The A00 micro-EV category,which thrived on subsidies,now represents only 6% of EV sales. The transition is forcing the industry to compete on technology and brand rather than on price alone. Rising battery and raw material costs further constrain the ability to offer aggressive discounts. The domestic market is entering a period where volume growth is subordinated to margin and technology investment.

## The Competitive Landscape Is Being Redrawn:Legacy Leaders Are Losing Share While Technology-Focused Players Gain

The first-half 2026 retail sales rankings reveal a significant reordering. NIO grew 67.1% year-on-year,adding 1.1 percentage points. HIMA and Xiaomi also gained ground. Investors should assess each company's export mix,geographic diversification,and exposure to tariff or non-tariff barriers.

Third,financial resilience. With domestic volumes declining and price competition giving way to technology investment,companies face margin pressure from two directions:lower unit sales and higher R&D spending. Companies with strong balance sheets,low leverage,and diversified revenue streams are better positioned to endure the transition. Those that relied on high-volume,low-margin domestic sales will face the most strain.

Applying this framework,the report's data suggests that BYD's export strength and technology pipeline provide a buffer against its domestic share loss.
