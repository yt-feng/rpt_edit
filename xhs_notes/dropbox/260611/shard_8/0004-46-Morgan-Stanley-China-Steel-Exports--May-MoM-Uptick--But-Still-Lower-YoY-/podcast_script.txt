ZH_A: 最近某外资投行出了一份关于中国钢铁出口的报告，核心判断是欧洲正在变得越来越“绝缘”，这是什么意思？
ZH_B: 简单来说，就是欧洲钢铁市场正在通过政策手段，把自己和中国等外部供应隔离开来。报告的核心结论是，虽然中国五月份的钢材净出口环比增长了约10%，但同比仍然是下降的。
ZH_A: 等一下，环比上升，同比下降，这两个数据放在一起说明什么？
ZH_B: 这说明中国钢铁出口的绝对量依然很大，年化水平达到约1.19亿吨，比分析师对明年的预测还高。但趋势在变弱，今年以来的累计出口已经比去年同期低了大约8%。
ZH_A: 那欧洲市场的“绝缘”具体是怎么体现的？
ZH_B: 报告里提到了两个关键变化。第一是市场在“区域化”，第二是政策在强化价格地板。先说区域化，你看二季度的数据，欧洲从所有地区的钢铁进口都在下降，这说明贸易保护政策开始生效了。
ZH_A: 那这意味着什么？对钢厂和买家有什么实际影响？
ZH_B: 这意味着供应链的安全溢价正在被重新定价。报告举了个例子，中东冲突以来，运到安特卫普的热轧卷价格每吨涨了70美元，而中国离岸价只涨了29美元。欧洲的溢价一下子就拉开了41美元。
ZH_A: 这41美元的溢价，就是买家愿意为“就近采购”和“供应稳定”付出的成本？
ZH_B: 完全正确。在运费高企、保险和中断风险增加的背景下，买家越来越愿意为可靠性和距离买单。这还没算上即将实施的碳边境调节机制和更严格的保障措施带来的额外影响。
ZH_A: 说到政策，报告特别强调了碳边境调节机制和保障措施，它们为什么能强化价格地板？
ZH_B: 因为这两个政策从根本上改变了欧洲市场的供需结构。碳边境调节机制从明年1月1日开始实施，保障措施从7月1日开始收紧，包括提高配额外的关税。
ZH_A: 我注意到报告里提到了一个很有意思的条款，叫“熔炼与浇铸条款”，这是什么？
ZH_B: 这就是一个关键的悬念点。报告指出，虽然中国直接只占欧洲进口的约13%，但这个条款可以通过中间国家，把政策的覆盖范围大大延伸。这意味着，即使欧洲本土需求没有恢复，这个政策框架也能让欧洲市场出现1000万到1500万吨的结构性短缺。
ZH_A: 结构性短缺？这听起来对欧洲本土钢厂是大利好。
ZH_B: 对。报告认为，利润池正在从进口商回流到欧洲本土钢厂。更高的进口平价、更完善的政策框架和不断改善的产能利用率，都在支持钢厂每吨EBITDA的重置。目前欧盟热轧卷的利润已经涨到每吨404美元，高于长期平均的320美元。
ZH_A: 那在这个背景下，哪些公司最受益？
ZH_B: 报告明确看好拥有本地一体化资产、具备产能灵活性的欧洲碳钢企业。比如安赛乐米塔尔，是他们在这个动态下的首选。另外一家是萨尔茨吉特，报告认为它的盈利拐点越来越清晰。
ZH_A: 不过，这份报告有没有留下一些没有完全展开的问题？
ZH_B: 有的，而且很重要。第一个就是那个“熔炼与浇铸条款”到底会怎样执行，具体能拦截多少通过第三国绕道的中国钢材。第二个是，如果中国国内需求持续疲软，这些过剩产能会不会找到其他出口渠道，比如东南亚或中东，从而改变全球的贸易流？

EN_A: So, a new report from a global investment bank about Chinese steel exports caught my eye. The big theme seems to be that Europe is becoming increasingly insulated. What does that actually mean?
EN_B: It means Europe is using policy to effectively wall itself off from external supply, especially from China. The headline is that China’s May finished-steel net exports were up about 10% month-on-month, but they’re still down about 8% year-on-year.
EN_A: Wait, up month-on-month but down year-on-year. That’s a bit confusing. What’s the story there?
EN_B: It shows that the absolute volume is still very high, running at an annualized rate of about 119 million tons, which is actually above what analysts forecast for next year. But the trend is weakening. The year-to-date figure is clearly lower than last year.
EN_A: Okay, so the volume is high but the direction is down. Now, how is this European insulation actually playing out?
EN_B: The report points to two big shifts. First, the market is regionalizing. Second, policy is reinforcing a price floor. On regionalization, look at Q2 data: steel imports into Europe from all destinations are declining. That tells you the protectionist policies are starting to bite.
EN_A: So what does that mean in practice? For a buyer or a steel mill?
EN_B: It means the market is repricing the security of supply chains. The report gives a great example. Since the Middle East conflict started, the price of HRC delivered to Antwerp has risen by 70 dollars per ton, while the FOB China price only went up by 29 dollars. That’s a 41-dollar premium for Europe.
EN_A: So that 41-dollar gap is essentially what buyers are paying for proximity and reliability?
EN_B: Exactly. With higher freight, insurance, and disruption risks, buyers are paying a premium for being close to home and having a reliable source. And this is before you even factor in the impact of CBAM or the tighter safeguards.
EN_A: Speaking of policy, the report really hammers on CBAM and the safeguards. Why are they such a big deal for the price floor?
EN_B: Because they fundamentally change the supply-demand math for Europe. CBAM kicks in on January 1st next year, and the safeguard measures tighten from July 1st, including higher out-of-quota tariffs.
EN_A: I noticed a very interesting term in the report: the “melted-and-poured” clause. What is that?
EN_B: That’s one of the key suspense points. The report says that while China directly accounts for only about 13 percent of Europe’s imports, this clause could significantly extend the policy’s reach by capturing Chinese steel that goes through intermediary countries. The framework could leave Europe structurally short by 10 to 15 million tons, even without a demand recovery.
EN_A: A structural shortage? That sounds like a huge tailwind for European steelmakers.
EN_B: It is. The report argues the profit pool is migrating back to domestic mills. Higher import parity, a stronger policy framework, and improving utilization all support an EBITDA-per-ton re-basing. EU HRC spreads are already at 404 dollars a ton, well above the long-term average of 320.
EN_A: So which companies are best positioned to capture this?
EN_B: The report is constructive but selective. It prefers European carbon steel equities with local integrated assets and shipment flexibility. ArcelorMittal is their top pick. They also highlight Salzgitter, saying its earnings inflection is becoming increasingly visible.
EN_A: That’s a clear call. But the report must have left some questions unanswered, right?
EN_B: Absolutely. Two big ones. First, how exactly will the “melted-and-poured” clause be enforced? How much of the transshipped steel from China will it actually block? Second, if Chinese domestic demand stays weak, where will that excess capacity go? Will it flood into Southeast Asia or the Middle East and change the global trade flows?
