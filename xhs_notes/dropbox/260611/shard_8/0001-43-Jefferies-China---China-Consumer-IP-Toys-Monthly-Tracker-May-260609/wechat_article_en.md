# Pop Mart’s Resilience Masks a Diverging Story: Why the Next 90 Days Will Separate Winners from Watchers

Pop Mart’s share price has held firm since its 1Q26 earnings release on May 13, even as the company flagged potential margin pressure in 2026. The subsequent purchase of approximately 81 million shares by a prominent investor in late May added a layer of confidence that the stock’s defenders were quick to cheer. But beneath the surface of a resilient equity story, the operating data tells a more nuanced tale—one that demands a strategic lens rather than a sentimental one.

The core argument of this analysis is straightforward: Pop Mart’s current valuation is being supported by a combination of narrative momentum and a single large shareholder’s conviction, not by a broad-based acceleration in fundamentals. The real test lies in the coming 90 days, when the launch of Labubu 4.0 and World Cup-related promotions will either validate the bull case or expose a widening gap between market perception and underlying demand trends.

This is not a call to dismiss the company’s achievements. Pop Mart’s ecommerce sales in April were up 70% year-over-year and 25% month-over-month, a remarkable performance by any standard. Its global social media engagement on TikTok showed a strong rebound in May, with the global and Thailand accounts posting 49% and 67% month-over-month gains, respectively. These are real, positive signals.

Yet the data also reveals emerging fault lines that are easy to overlook when the stock price is rising. Instagram follower growth slowed month-over-month across key markets. Global and US app active users declined in May. Google Search Trends for Pop Mart continued to trend downward. And while second-hand Labubu plush prices stabilized, the mix of the most-wanted products on exchange platforms is becoming more diverse, with non-Pop Mart items appearing for the first time among the top 30.

The question for serious investors is not whether Pop Mart is a good company. It is whether the current price already discounts the best-case scenario, and what happens if the next catalyst disappoints.

## The Shareholder Signal Is Not a Fundamental Signal: Duan Yongping’s Stake Does Not Change the Operating Trajectory

The purchase of an approximately 6% stake by a well-known investor has been widely interpreted as a vote of confidence in Pop Mart’s long-term prospects. In late May, the market cheered the news, and the stock’s resilience in the following weeks seemed to confirm that the buying was strategic.

But the distinction between a shareholder signal and a fundamental signal is critical. A large insider or activist purchase can certainly boost sentiment and provide a floor for the stock in the short term. It does not, however, alter the company’s revenue trajectory, cost structure, or competitive positioning. The post-earnings call explicitly highlighted possible margin pressure in 2026, a point that should give pause to anyone relying on earnings expansion to justify the current multiple.

What the shareholder purchase does reveal is that at least one sophisticated investor believes the stock is undervalued relative to its long-term potential. That is a valid data point. But it is not a substitute for evidence that the underlying business is accelerating. If anything, the timing of the purchase—after the stock had already corrected from its highs and after management had guided for margin headwinds—suggests a value-oriented thesis rather than a momentum-driven one.

Investors should ask themselves: Would the same enthusiasm exist if the purchase had not been disclosed? The answer is probably not. The stock’s resilience is partly a function of the narrative that a famous investor is backing the company. Narratives can be powerful, but they are also fragile. The moment the operating data fails to support the story, the narrative can reverse faster than the fundamentals.

## The Labubu Ecosystem Shows Signs of Maturing: Second-Hand Price Stability Masks a Shift in Collector Behavior

One of the most closely watched indicators for Pop Mart’s health is the second-hand market for its Labubu plush toys. In May, the prices of Labubu 1.0 series stabilized month-over-month, which on the surface appears to be a positive sign. After months of volatility, collectors and flippers seem to have found an equilibrium.

But the stability is not the full story. What is more revealing is the composition of the top 30 most-wanted products on second-hand exchange platforms. For the first time, three of these products are not Pop Mart items. The Pop Mart x aespa collaboration remains the most sought-after, but the presence of non-Pop Mart products suggests that collector attention is becoming more fragmented.

This is a natural evolution for any successful IP-driven company. As the brand matures, the initial wave of hyper-concentrated demand gives way to a more diversified collector base that rotates between different IPs and collaborations. For Pop Mart, this means that future product launches may need to work harder to generate the same level of secondary-market excitement. The days when every Labubu drop automatically commanded a significant premium may be behind us.

The practical implication is that revenue growth may become more dependent on volume and distribution rather than on price appreciation in the secondary market. That is not necessarily a negative—it simply changes the nature of the growth algorithm. But it does mean that the margin profile could look different in the future, especially if the company needs to invest more in marketing and promotion to sustain demand.

## Global Social Media Engagement Is Recovering, But the Follower Growth Rate Is Decelerating—A Leading Indicator Worth Watching

The TikTok engagement numbers for May are genuinely encouraging. A 49% month-over-month increase in global interactions and a 67% increase for the Thailand account suggest that Pop Mart’s content strategy is resonating with audiences in key markets. The ongoing normalization trend that had been weighing on engagement metrics appears to be reversing, at least for now.

However, the Instagram data tells a different story. In May, Pop Mart added only 13,000 new followers across its Global, Thailand, US, and UK accounts combined, compared to 19,000 in April. That is a 32% month-over-month decline in follower acquisition. While the absolute numbers are still positive, the deceleration is noteworthy.

Follower growth is often a leading indicator for future engagement and, ultimately, for sales. If the pace of new follower acquisition continues to slow, it could signal that the brand is reaching a saturation point in its most developed markets. The US market, in particular, has been a focus of investor attention as a potential growth driver. If US follower growth is stalling, the path to meaningful revenue contribution from that market may be longer than expected.

The decline in app active users globally and in the US adds another layer of concern. Active users are a more direct measure of engagement than followers, and a decline suggests that even existing fans may be spending less time with the brand. This could be a seasonal effect, or it could reflect competition from other entertainment and collectible platforms. The data does not yet provide a definitive answer, but it is a trend that warrants close monitoring in the coming months.

## The Upcoming Catalyst Window Is Narrow: Labubu 4.0 and World Cup Promotions Will Either Confirm or Contradict the Bull Case

The next major catalysts for Pop Mart are the launch of Labubu 4.0 and the World Cup-related promotions that have already begun to appear. The company has stepped up its global marketing initiatives, including an appearance in the official FIFA World Cup 2026 song music video and a collaboration with Coca-Cola to launch Labubu-branded cans in China. These are high-visibility moves that could drive a new wave of demand.

But the window for these catalysts to deliver is narrow. If Labubu 4.0 fails to generate the same level of secondary-market excitement as previous iterations, or if the World Cup promotions do not translate into sustained revenue growth, the market may begin to reassess the growth trajectory. The risk is that the current stock price already discounts a successful launch, leaving little room for disappointment.

For investors, the key question is not whether these initiatives will be successful in an absolute sense. It is whether they will be successful enough to offset the deceleration in follower growth, the decline in app active users, and the margin pressure that management has flagged. That is a high bar, and the data available today does not provide a clear answer.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer Yet: The Missing Link Between Marketing Spend and Revenue Conversion

The data in this analysis provides a comprehensive view of Pop Mart’s current operating metrics, but it leaves one critical question unanswered: What is the relationship between marketing spend and revenue conversion?

The report tracks social media engagement, follower growth, and ecommerce sales, but it does not provide a direct link between the two. It is possible that Pop Mart is spending more on marketing to generate the same level of engagement, which would imply deteriorating unit economics. Alternatively, the company may be achieving higher conversion rates from a smaller but more engaged audience, which would be a positive sign.

Without this data, it is difficult to assess whether the recent rebound in TikTok engagement is a sustainable improvement or a temporary spike driven by promotional activity. Similarly, the decline in app active users could be a natural consequence of the marketing mix shifting toward TikTok and away from the app, or it could indicate that the app is losing relevance as a primary engagement channel.

Investors who want to make an informed decision about Pop Mart’s trajectory will need to dig deeper into the cost side of the equation. The margin guidance from the earnings call suggests that management is already seeing pressure. The question is whether the revenue growth from the upcoming product launches will be enough to offset that pressure.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: How to Evaluate Pop Mart Over the Next Three Months

Given the complexity of the signals, investors need a clear framework for evaluating Pop Mart over the next 90 days. The following three-factor model can help separate signal from noise.

First, monitor the conversion of marketing engagement into ecommerce sales. The May ecommerce data will be available after June 15, and it will be the most important data point of the quarter. If the TikTok engagement rebound translates into a meaningful acceleration in ecommerce GMV, the bull case gains credibility. If sales growth decelerates despite higher engagement, the narrative shifts.

Second, track the second-hand price trajectory of Labubu 4.0 after launch. The initial days after a product launch are the most informative. If the new series commands a premium that is comparable to or higher than previous iterations, it suggests that collector enthusiasm remains strong. If the premium is lower or takes longer to materialize, it confirms the maturing of the Labubu ecosystem.

Third, watch the follower growth rates across key markets, particularly the US and Thailand. A sustained deceleration in follower acquisition would be a leading indicator of market saturation. A rebound, on the other hand, would support the thesis that the brand still has room to grow in its most important international markets.

This framework is not a substitute for a full financial model, but it provides a structured way to interpret the high-frequency data that is available on a monthly basis. The next 90 days will either confirm that Pop Mart’s current valuation is justified by accelerating fundamentals, or they will reveal that the stock’s resilience was more about narrative than about substance.
