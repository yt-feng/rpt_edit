ZH_A: 最近数据中心项目管道增长了10%，达到324吉瓦，这数字大得惊人。但我想问的是，这背后真正意味着什么？是AI基建还在狂飙，还是已经出现了一些值得警惕的信号？

ZH_B: 好问题。这份报告的核心判断是，数据中心建设确实在加速，但结构正在发生关键变化。最大的变化是，开发商和加密货币矿工成了新增主力，而超大规模云服务商的管道容量反而略有下降。这意味着，市场不再只是由那几个巨头驱动了。

ZH_A: 等等，超大规模云服务商在下降？这不是和“AI军备竞赛”的叙事矛盾吗？

ZH_B: 不完全矛盾。他们的在建容量其实还在增加，比如Meta和亚马逊贡献了超大规模新增的90%。但管道下降说明，他们可能更聚焦于推进已有项目，而不是大量新签。这背后可能和电力供应瓶颈、审批周期有关。真正的故事，在“表后电力”这个趋势里。

ZH_A: 表后电力？这听起来像是个技术术语。对普通投资者来说，这意味着什么？

ZH_B: 简单说，就是数据中心不再完全依赖电网，而是自己建发电站。报告指出，表后电力管道一个月内增长了12%，达到了129吉瓦。更关键的是，目前只有4%的已装机数据中心用这种方式，但在建项目中有25%会用，管道项目里更是高达40%。这意味着，像卡特彼勒和康明斯这样的发动机制造商，以及伊顿这样的电气设备商，将迎来巨大的增量需求。

ZH_A: 所以，这不仅仅是建房子，更是建发电站。那另一个值得关注的点是什么？

ZH_B: 是“搁浅容量”问题。因为当地社区反对，也就是NIMBY现象，搁浅的容量增长了1.9吉瓦，达到34吉瓦，占整个管道的11%。虽然这个比例比去年夏天的7-9%有所上升，但报告认为它开始企稳了。不过，这里有一个悬念。

ZH_A: 什么悬念？

ZH_B: 报告提到，所有新增的搁浅容量都来自“未说明的项目”。也就是说，我们不知道这些项目是谁的，在哪里，为什么被卡住。这背后可能隐藏着一些非公开的协议或更复杂的审批障碍。完整报告里会继续追踪这些“幽灵项目”的去向。

ZH_A: 这确实让人好奇。那如果把这些都加总起来，对相关公司的市场空间有多大影响？

ZH_B: 报告给出了一个惊人的TAM估算。比如，仅数据中心电气设备市场，伊顿对应的机会是7580亿美元，维谛技术是1万亿美元，施耐德是8650亿美元。但注意，这只是基于当前管道和公司披露的每兆瓦机会算出来的。这里面有一个很大的假设。

ZH_A: 什么假设？

ZH_B: 假设所有这些管道项目最终都能建成。但现实中，从管道到真正开工，再到投产，中间有大量的不确定性。比如搁浅容量、电力接入、设备交付周期等等。所以，这些数字更像是一个“天花板”而非“地板”。完整报告里，对每个环节的转化率有更细致的拆解。

ZH_A: 明白了。那对于想深入研究的人，报告里还有哪些没有完全展开的关键问题？

ZH_B: 有两个。第一，加密货币矿工转型做AI基础设施的可持续性。他们确实有30吉瓦的电力组合，但报告指出，他们只签出了6吉瓦给超大规模和AI公司。剩下的24吉瓦，能不能真的转化为AI算力租赁收入，这需要继续验证。第二，表后电力虽然加速，但它的成本结构和电网供电相比，到底有没有经济性？报告没有给出具体的成本比较，这可能是下一个需要关注的焦点。

ZH_A: 所以，AI基建的故事远没有结束，但驱动者和风险点都在变化。如果你想了解那些“幽灵项目”的细节，或者表后电力的真实成本，唯一的方法就是去读完整报告。

ZH_B: 没错。这份报告就像一张藏宝图，我们只是指出了宝藏可能在哪，但真正的路径，还需要你自己去探索。如果你想加入我们的社群，获取更多这类深度分析，欢迎在评论区留言。

EN_A: So the data center project pipeline just jumped 10% to 324 gigawatts. That sounds massive. But what does it actually mean for investors?

EN_B: Great question. The key takeaway here is that construction is still accelerating, but the mix is shifting. The big change this month is that developers and crypto miners drove most of the growth. Hyperscalers actually saw their pipeline shrink a bit. So it is not just the big tech names driving everything anymore.

EN_A: Wait, hyperscalers are shrinking? Does that mean the AI buildout is slowing down?

EN_B: Not exactly. Their capacity under construction is still going up. Meta and Amazon alone accounted for 90% of the hyperscaler increase there. But the pipeline drop suggests they are focusing on executing existing projects rather than signing a ton of new ones. That could be due to power constraints or permitting delays. The real story is something else though.

EN_A: What is that?

EN_B: Behind-the-meter power. That is where data centers generate their own electricity instead of relying solely on the grid. The pipeline for that jumped 12% in one month to 129 gigawatts. And here is the kicker. Only 4% of existing data centers use this approach, but 25% of those under construction will, and 40% of planned projects will. That is a massive structural shift.

EN_A: So it is not just about building more server rooms. It is about building more power plants. Who benefits from that?

EN_B: Companies like Caterpillar and Cummins, which make large engines. Also electrical equipment players like Eaton and Hubbell. The report says behind-the-meter projects spend about 30% more on electrical distribution gear than grid-only projects. So that is a direct tailwind.

EN_A: That makes sense. What is the other big theme?

EN_B: Stranded capacity. This is projects stuck due to local opposition, or NIMBYism. It grew to 34 gigawatts, which is 11% of the total pipeline. That is up from 7 to 9% last summer, but the report says it is starting to stabilize. However, there is a catch.

EN_A: What kind of catch?

EN_B: All of the increase in stranded capacity came from projects that are unaccounted for. We do not know who owns them or where they are. That is a bit of a mystery. The full report tracks these ghost projects in more detail, and it is worth reading to understand the hidden risks.

EN_A: So there is a lot of potential, but also a lot of uncertainty. If we add it all up, what is the total addressable market for the suppliers?

EN_B: The numbers are huge. For example, Eaton's data center TAM is estimated at 758 billion dollars. Vertiv's is over a trillion. Schneider's is 865 billion. But remember, that is based on the current pipeline and assumes everything gets built. The report does not fully answer how much of that pipeline will actually convert into real revenue.

EN_A: So the headline numbers are exciting, but the details matter. What are the questions the report leaves open?

EN_B: Two big ones. First, can crypto miners really pivot to become AI infrastructure providers? They have a 30 gigawatt power portfolio, but only 6 gigawatts are contracted so far. The rest is speculative. Second, behind-the-meter power is clearly growing, but is it actually cost competitive? The report does not give a direct cost comparison. That is something investors will need to watch.

EN_A: So the AI infrastructure story is far from over, but the drivers are shifting. The big opportunity might not be where everyone is looking.

EN_B: Exactly. The full report dives much deeper into these conversion rates and hidden projects. If you want to understand where the real bottlenecks are, that is where you need to go.
