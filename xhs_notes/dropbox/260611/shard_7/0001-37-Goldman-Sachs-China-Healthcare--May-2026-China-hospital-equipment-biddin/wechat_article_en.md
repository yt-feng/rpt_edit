# The Chinese Hospital Equipment Market Has Reached an Inflection Point That Most Investors Are Still Underestimating

After five consecutive months of year-over-year declines, the total bidding value for nine major medical equipment categories in China registered a marginal increase of 0.1% in May 2026. This is not a statistical blip. It is a signal that the long-awaited recovery in China's hospital equipment procurement cycle has begun, albeit in a muted and uneven form.

The data is unambiguous. Since December 2025, the market has been in a phase of mild year-over-year contraction. The transition to mild growth in May confirms a pattern that a global investment bank report had anticipated: the sector would move from a trough of negative bidding momentum into a stabilization phase, with a more visible recovery expected in the second half of 2026. The question is no longer whether recovery will come, but which segments and which companies are positioned to capture the disproportionate share of the rebound.

This matters because the Chinese medical equipment market is not a monolithic block. The recovery is highly selective. Only three categories—CT, MRI, and LINAC—recorded positive year-over-year growth in May. Patient monitors, which had been a rare bright spot in prior months, faltered. Ultrasound and endoscope bidding values continued to decline. The pattern suggests that the recovery is being driven by large-ticket, high-technology diagnostic and therapeutic equipment, not by general monitoring or imaging systems.

For investors, the implication is straightforward: the recovery will favor companies with deep product portfolios in the premium segments, not those with broad but undifferentiated exposure. Two companies—United Imaging and Mindray—illustrate the divergent trajectories that are now emerging.

## United Imaging Has Already Broken Away from the Pack, While Mindray Is Still Navigating a Destocking Cycle

United Imaging's bidding value increased by 9% year-over-year in the first five months of 2026. Among its major competitors—including GE HealthCare, Siemens Healthineers, Philips, and Neusoft—United Imaging was the only one to deliver positive growth. This is not a coincidence. The company has maintained stable market share in CT and MRI, two of the three categories that drove the May uptick. More importantly, it has gained share in DSA (digital subtraction angiography) and LINAC, categories where it previously had less penetration.

The company's recently announced equity incentive plan adds another layer of conviction. The plan stipulates that annual revenue growth must exceed 20% in each of 2026 through 2028 for the full incentive to be triggered. This is a bold target, given that domestic tendering demand is still in the early stages of recovery. A global investment bank report forecasts 19.3% revenue growth for United Imaging in 2026, slightly below the company's implied guidance. The gap is small enough to be closed by a modest acceleration in second-half bidding activity.

United Imaging's overseas growth trajectory reinforces the argument. The company's overseas revenue contribution is expected to exceed 25% in 2026, with overseas growth rates consistently higher than domestic rates. This dual-engine model—domestic recovery plus overseas expansion—provides a buffer against any single-market slowdown.

Mindray presents a more complex picture. In the first quarter of 2026, the company's China revenue decreased by 11% year-over-year, while overseas revenue grew by 16% (or 20% in USD terms). The divergence between domestic and international performance is stark. Mindray's PMLS (patient monitoring and life support) and medical imaging businesses in China are still in a destocking process, which the company now expects to end in the second quarter of 2026. The IVD (in vitro diagnostics) business showed some improvement, with coagulation and immunology growing approximately 10% year-over-year.

The critical metric for Mindray is market share. The company stated that the average market share of its three core IVD businesses—immunology, biochemistry, and coagulation—increased from 10% in the first half of 2025 to 12% at year-end 2025, and further to 13% in the first quarter of 2026. This steady share gain suggests that Mindray is winning even in a contracting market. When the destocking cycle ends, the revenue impact could be significant.

The contrast between United Imaging and Mindray is instructive. United Imaging is riding a recovery in high-end imaging and therapeutic equipment, where demand is driven by hospital capital expenditure cycles and technology upgrades. Mindray is fighting through a destocking overhang in its core domestic segments, but gaining share in IVD, which has a more recurring revenue profile. Both stories are credible, but they imply different risk profiles and time horizons.

## The Recovery Is Real but Narrowly Concentrated, and the Second-Order Implications Are Just Beginning

The May data reveals a recovery that is both real and narrow. Only three of nine categories posted positive year-over-year growth. The remaining six—patient monitors, ultrasound, endoscope, PET-CT, DR, and DSA—all declined. This is not a broad-based recovery. It is a selective rebound concentrated in the highest-value diagnostic and therapeutic modalities.

The second-order implications are worth examining. If the recovery remains concentrated in CT, MRI, and LINAC, the primary beneficiaries will be companies with strong positions in those segments. United Imaging is the clearest example, but Siemens Healthineers and GE HealthCare also have significant exposure. Conversely, companies that rely on patient monitors, ultrasound, or endoscopes for a large portion of their revenue may see a delayed recovery.

There is also a geographic dimension. The bidding data is national, but hospital procurement decisions in China are influenced by provincial budgets, local government debt levels, and the pace of central government stimulus. If the recovery is driven by large tertiary hospitals upgrading their equipment, the benefit will flow to premium segments. If it broadens to include county-level hospitals, the demand for mid-range and entry-level equipment could accelerate.

The patient monitor category is a case study in complexity. After sustaining positive growth for several months, monitor bidding declined 23% year-over-year in May 2026. Yet the replacement cycle argument remains valid: many monitors procured during the COVID period are now entering their replacement window. The question is whether the May decline is a temporary pause or the beginning of a renewed downtrend. The data does not yet provide a clear answer, but the replacement cycle thesis suggests that monitor demand should improve in the coming quarters.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer Yet, and Why That Matters

The global investment bank report provides a detailed snapshot of bidding data and company-level revenue trends. But it leaves several critical questions unresolved.

First, the report does not fully disaggregate the impact of price versus volume. Bidding value can increase because hospitals are buying more units, or because they are buying higher-priced models. The difference matters for revenue models. If the recovery is volume-driven, it benefits companies with broad product portfolios. If it is price-driven, it benefits companies with premium product mixes. The report does not provide a clear decomposition.

Second, the report does not model the potential impact of policy changes. China's medical equipment market is heavily influenced by central government procurement policies, local government debt constraints, and the national volume-based procurement (VBP) program for medical devices. Any acceleration or deceleration in policy implementation could significantly alter the recovery trajectory. The report acknowledges the destocking cycle but does not provide a scenario analysis for policy-driven shocks.

Third, the report does not address the competitive dynamics in the overseas markets that are becoming increasingly important for both United Imaging and Mindray. Overseas growth rates are high, but the base is small. The sustainability of overseas expansion depends on regulatory approvals, distribution partnerships, and pricing strategies that vary by country. The report provides revenue breakdowns by geography but does not analyze the competitive landscape in key overseas markets such as Europe, Southeast Asia, or Latin America.

These gaps are not criticisms of the report. They are natural limitations of a data-driven analysis focused on bidding trends. But they point to the need for a more holistic framework that integrates bidding data, company fundamentals, policy analysis, and competitive dynamics.

## A Decision Framework for Investors in the Chinese Medical Equipment Market

For investors trying to position themselves in this recovery, the following framework may be useful.

Step one: determine your exposure to the recovery's shape. The recovery is currently narrow. If you believe it will broaden to include patient monitors, ultrasound, and endoscopes, then Mindray and other diversified players become more attractive. If you believe it will remain concentrated in CT, MRI, and LINAC, then United Imaging and its peers are the primary beneficiaries.

Step two: assess the destocking risk. Mindray's domestic destocking cycle is expected to end in the second quarter of 2026. If the company's guidance is accurate, the second half of 2026 should see a meaningful improvement in China revenue. But destocking cycles can be unpredictable. Investors should monitor quarterly revenue trends and inventory data closely.

Step three: evaluate overseas growth as a hedge. Both United Imaging and Mindray have strong overseas growth trajectories. For United Imaging, overseas revenue is expected to exceed 25% of total revenue in 2026. For Mindray, overseas revenue grew 16% year-over-year in the first quarter of 2026. This overseas exposure provides a hedge against domestic policy risk and demand fluctuations.

Step four: watch the bidding data monthly. The May 2026 data is a positive signal, but it is just one data point. The transition from mild decline to mild growth is fragile. A single month of negative data could reverse the narrative. Investors should track the monthly bidding data for the nine categories, with particular attention to CT, MRI, and patient monitors.

Step five: consider the equity incentive plan as a signal. United Imaging's incentive plan targets 20% annual revenue growth. Whether the company achieves this target or falls slightly short, the plan signals management's confidence in the recovery. Companies with aggressive but achievable incentive plans often outperform in recovery cycles.

## The Full Picture Requires Data That Goes Beyond Bidding Trends

The May 2026 bidding data is a critical piece of the puzzle, but it is not the entire puzzle. Bidding data captures hospital procurement intent, not actual orders, deliveries, or revenue recognition. There can be a lag of several months between bidding and revenue. Moreover, bidding data does not capture service revenue, consumables revenue, or recurring revenue from installed bases.

For companies like Mindray, where IVD consumables and service revenue are becoming increasingly important, bidding data for capital equipment may understate the true revenue trajectory. For United Imaging, the growing overseas revenue stream is only partially reflected in domestic bidding data.

The global investment bank report provides a rigorous foundation for understanding the recovery. But the most valuable insights come from integrating this data with company-level financial analysis, policy monitoring, and competitive intelligence.
