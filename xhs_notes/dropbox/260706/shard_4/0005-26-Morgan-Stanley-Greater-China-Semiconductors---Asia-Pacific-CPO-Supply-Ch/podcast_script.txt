ZH_A: 那这份报告真正要回答的问题是什么？是帮我们厘清GlassBridge和传统FAU到底谁更强吗？

ZH_A: 所以，报告的第一个关键洞察就是，GlassBridge和传统FAU根本不是同一类东西？

ZH_B: 对。传统FAU，比如TFC、FOCI做的，是把光纤直接固定在V型槽里，是一种非常成熟、精度极高的直接物理连接。而GlassBridge本质上是一个玻璃波导连接器，它插入在光纤和光子芯片之间，更像是一个“光学重布线层”。这意味着它能更灵活地实现间距转换和可拆卸连接，但同时也引入了新的光学损耗。

ZH_A: 那这意味着什么？对投资者来说，这个区别重要在哪里？

ZH_B: 这意味着，至少在接下来几年，台积电主流的COUPE平台和它的核心客户，比如英伟达、AMD，依然会选择传统的“光栅耦合”方案。因为光栅耦合更容易实现大规模量产，报告预计这个量产将在2026年下半年看到。而GlassBridge目前主要支持“边缘耦合”，并且只能支持一维光纤布局，这与当前主流设计不兼容。

ZH_A: 所以短期内，传统FAU供应商的护城河还是很深的。那第二个关键洞察呢？报告提到了台积电的光子芯片产能规划，这背后有什么信号？

ZH_B: 这是一个非常大的正面信号。报告指出，台积电计划将光子芯片产能从2026年第二季度的每月1万片，提升到第四季度的每月1.5万片，再到2028年的至少每月2.5万片。这意味着产能正在快速爬坡，而且2026到2027年的主要量产客户会是英伟达、博通和AMD。到了2028年，联发科、美满电子这些厂商也能加入。

ZH_A: 所以这意味着CPO的供应链正在从“概念验证”走向“真实放量”？那报告里有没有提到一个关键瓶颈，就是那个CPO的“插入测试”？

ZH_B: 这正是第三个关键洞察。CPO的“插入测试2”曾经是最大的瓶颈之一，因为测试一片晶圆需要整整一天。但现在，测试时间已经缩短到了6小时，而未来6到12个月的目标是3到4小时。这个进步非常快，说明整个生态系统的良率和效率都在显著改善。

ZH_B: 报告认为不太可能。因为“插入测试2”是第一个能够同时对光电信号进行测试的环节，它对于确保最终产品的可靠性至关重要，无法轻易跳过。

EN_A: So today we are digging into a fresh report on the CPO supply chain from a global investment bank. And the big word everyone is talking about is GlassBridge. What exactly is it, and why should investors care?

EN_B: Great question. The report's main takeaway is that GlassBridge is a promising solution, but it is still far from mass production. It is more of a system-level connector architecture for next-gen CPO optical engines, not a direct replacement for the traditional fiber array units, or FAUs, that are widely used today.

EN_A: So the core question the report is trying to answer is: should investors be worried that GlassBridge will disrupt the existing FAU market?

EN_B: Exactly. There is a fear in the market that GlassBridge could eat into the total addressable market for FAU suppliers. The report argues that this fear is overblown, at least for the next couple of years. The key reason is that the two technologies are fundamentally different.

EN_A: How so? Walk me through the difference.

EN_B: Traditional FAU is a direct fiber attach. You take fibers, put them into precision V-grooves, polish them, and align them directly to the photonic chip. It is mature, proven, and very low loss. GlassBridge, on the other hand, is a glass waveguide interposer. It sits between the fiber and the chip, acting like an optical redistribution layer. This gives it more design flexibility for pitch conversion and detachability, but it also adds complexity and insertion loss.

EN_A: So what does that mean for the current CPO roadmap?

EN_B: It means that for the next few years, the mainstream platform, which is TSMC's COUPE, and its key customers like NVIDIA and AMD, will stick with grating coupling. Grating coupling is easier to mass produce, and the report expects that to happen in the second half of 2026. GlassBridge mainly supports edge coupling and currently only handles one-dimensional fiber layouts, so it does not fit the current design.

EN_A: So traditional FAU suppliers are safe for now. What is the second big insight from the report?

EN_B: The massive ramp in TSMC's photonic integrated circuit, or PIC, capacity. The report shows TSMC plans to go from 10,000 wafers per month in the second quarter of 2026 to 25,000 wafers per month by 2028. That is a huge jump. And the initial customers in 2026 and 2027 will be NVIDIA, Broadcom, and AMD. By 2028, other players like MediaTek and Marvell can also join.

EN_A: So the supply chain is really moving from prototype to volume production. What about the bottleneck everyone was worried about, the CPO insertion test?

EN_B: That is the third key insight. The Insertion 2 test was a major bottleneck, taking a full day per wafer. Now, the test time has improved to six hours, and the target is to get it down to three to four hours in the next six to twelve months. That is a dramatic improvement.

EN_A: Could they just skip that test step to save time?

EN_B: The report says that is unlikely. Insertion 2 is the first point where you can simultaneously test both the electrical and optical signals. It is crucial for ensuring the final product's reliability, so skipping it is not a viable option.

EN_A: So the CPO story is looking very positive. But what are the open questions? What does the report leave for the reader to find in the full document?

EN_B: A big one is the timing for GlassBridge. The report notes that currently, no project on TSMC's COUPE platform has adopted this technology. So when will it actually enter the mainstream? And will it only replace low-end FAU, leaving the high-end market untouched? The report does not fully answer that.
