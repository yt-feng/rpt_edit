EN_A: So there's this report from a global investment bank on semiconductor equipment that just rolled its price targets all the way out to 2028. What's the big idea here?
EN_B: The main message is that the market has already fully priced in 200 billion dollars of WFE in 2027. So the report argues that the valuation anchor has to shift forward, otherwise you simply can't make sense of why these equipment stocks are trading where they are.
EN_A: Wait, fully priced in? What does that actually mean?
EN_B: It means the large cap US semiconductor equipment companies are now trading at 37 times their 2027 earnings estimates. That's a 75 percent premium to the through-cycle average of 21 times since 2020, and more than 20 percent above the previous peak of 30 times in July 2024. So the report says, looking at 2027 as a base year is no longer useful.
EN_A: So what did they do instead?
EN_B: They shifted the valuation base year to 2028. They assume 227 billion dollars of WFE in 2028, which brings the large cap PE down to 33 times. But they also admit that this implies 250 billion dollars in 2028 might already be close to being priced in.
EN_A: So the real question becomes, can we actually get to 250 billion?
EN_B: Exactly, and this is the most interesting part of the report. They say 250 billion is possible, but they are not ready to underwrite it yet. They raise three key questions. First, can the memory market digest over 80 billion in DRAM and 30 billion in NAND WFE? That would mean bit supply growth of more than 40 percent. Second, can ASML supply the roughly 130 EUV tools needed? Third, can emerging logic players like TeraFab and Rapidus contribute over 10 billion dollars?
EN_A: Those are all really hard questions to answer right now. So how does the report view individual companies?
EN_B: Their preference is very clear. They are looking for companies whose earnings power on 200 billion dollars of WFE in 2027 is still underappreciated. Their top picks are MKS and ONTO, which still trade at undemanding valuations on 2027 earnings. They raised target multiples for KLA and AMAT, because KLA benefits from a broadening of leading-edge logic customers, and AMAT benefits from strong DRAM WFE. But they left LAM and MKS unchanged, because NAND WFE revisions haven't come through yet.
EN_A: So they are less enthusiastic about the large caps?
EN_B: Correct. They say their enthusiasm for large caps is slightly more muted because expectations for 250 billion plus are already partially priced in. They even cut target multiples for NVMI and CAMT, questioning whether NVMI can outgrow WFE in 2027, and worrying about market share loss for CAMT in HBM.
EN_A: So what is the one big takeaway from this report?
EN_B: One sentence. The market has fully priced in 2027, so now you have to bet on 2028. But the report itself is not fully convinced that 2028 can reach 250 billion. And those three questions around memory digestion, EUV supply, and emerging logic players are explored in much more detail in the full report, with sensitivity analysis.
