## Seagate

## F4Q26 Review: The Demand-Supply Waltz Continues; Raise Estimates & PT Higher

Seagate once again delivered a clear beat-and-raise, one that looks more like a continuation of the durable setup we saw in F3Q26, with results coming in ahead of expectations driven in large part by upside to pricing. On a per-exabyte basis, pricing grew +11% y/y (vs. +4% y/y in F3Q26), with an implied acceleration to at least +20% y/y in F1Q27. Beneath the aggregate trends, management pointed to data center pricing tailwinds from a widening demand-supply gap, while the robust tailwinds in the Edge IoT business reflect opportunistic pricing against a backdrop of very strong price increases for competitors' NAND-based storage products. These increases supported incremental gross margins of 83%+ y/y in F4Q26, along with implied guidance for 88%+ incremental gross margins in F1Q27. Stepping away from pricing, management characterized demand as structurally strengthening, led by sustained cloud buildouts and AI-driven incremental data creation and retention on top of already data-intensive workloads (including video) with no evidence of a near-term slowdown and customer planning horizons extending multiple years, supporting guidance for accelerating revenue growth into FY27. Supply, meanwhile, continues to lag this robust demand, as evident in the vast majority of Nearline exabyte supply already being allocated into CY28 under long-term agreements, with customers actively extending their planning horizons into CY29 and beyond. On new technology platforms, Mozaic 3+ is qualified and in production across all major cloud customers, while Mozaic 4+ is ramping with the two largest CSPs and expanding to additional qualifications. Overall, we are raising our forecasts to bring our pricing assumptions in line with the latest trends and implied guidance, lifting our out-year earnings estimates more than +30% above prior. We reiterate our Overweight rating, driven by unprecedented demand and a favorable pricing environment, and raise our Dec-26 price target to \$1,240 (from \$1,095 prior), driven by a higher earnings outlook.

\- F4Q26 (Jun-end) Results: Robust beat driven by revenue and margins tracking ahead of JPMe/consensus. Revenues tracked to \$3,629 mn (vs. JPMe of \$3,553 mn and consensus of \$3,491 mn), driven by robust data center demand driving data center revenue of \$2,933 mn (+57% y/y). Gross margins tracked to 52.7% (vs. JPMe of 50.9% and consensus of 50.4%), or +570 bps q/q, reflecting the favorable pricing environment driven by a significant gap relative to demand and supply. Opex discipline (8.1% of sales vs JPMe of 8.3%) helped further in driving record operating margins of 44.6% (vs. JPMe of 42.6% and consensus of 41.9%), translating into an EPS beat, with EPS tracking at \$5.71 (vs. JPMe of \$5.25 and consensus of \$5.08).

\- F1Q27 (Sep-end) Guide: Better-than-expected guide across revenue, margins and EPS. Revenue guidance tracked to \$4,000-\$4,200 mn (vs. JPMe of \$3,799 mn and consensus of \$3,792 mn heading into the print), with EPS guidance of \$7.10-\$7.50 (vs. JPMe of \$5.77 and consensus of \$5.81). Operating margin is guided to \~50% (vs. JPMe of 43.6% and consensus of 44.4%), including opex of \~\$300 mn (in-line with JPMe); and an implied gross margin of \~57% (vs. JPMe of 51.5% and consensus of 51.9%) with pricing growth acceleration to at least 20% y/y (vs. JPMe of +12% y/y heading into the print).

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

Downside risks to our rating and price target include a slowdown in Cloud capex spending on AI infrastructure, which could present risk to demand growth forecasts for the HDD industry despite the current robust pipeline of investment announcements. Additionally, technology disruptions – including faster-than-expected migration to flash-based storage driven by narrowing cost differentials between HDDs and NAND, and greater progress on

NAND density could present headwinds to our expectations for Seagate shares.
