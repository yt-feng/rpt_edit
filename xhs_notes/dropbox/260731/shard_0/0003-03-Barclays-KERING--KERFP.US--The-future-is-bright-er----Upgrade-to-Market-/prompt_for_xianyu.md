# The future is bright(er) — Upgrade to Market Weight

We upgrade our rating on KERFP to MW from UW reflecting our greater confidence in the stabilisation of the model following H1 26 results as well as progress towards reducing leverage. In this note we also downgrade the European High Grade Retailers sector from UW from MW.

## Good news comes in threes

Hitting the ground running. Kering's CEO, Luca De Meo, officially began his tenure on 15 September 2025 after having stated on 9 September 2025 (at Kering's AGM) that he was determined to make tough decisions to slash debt and cut costs at Kering without delay. De Meo has proven a man of his word, delivering on two key objectives within days:

1. Reducing issuance risks... The next day (10 September 2025), Kering and Mayhoola jointly postponed the latter's put option on its 70% stake in Valentino from 2026-27 to 2028-29, and Kering's call option was delayed from 2028 to 2029. We argue that the additional time to acquire Valentino reduces the financial pressure on Kering, thus allowing management to focus on debt reduction and the Gucci turnaround.

## S&P in focus: BBB+/Stable

Kering is rated BBB+/Stable by S&P with its latest update on 7 November 2025, when the outlook was upgraded from Negative back to Stable. The change back to a Stable outlook in November 2025 followed the Beauté sale, predicated on deleveraging prospects and that the company's operating performance would gradually improve during 2026, driven by the execution of the turnaround strategy, the easier comparison base, and a better operating environment for the whole industry. Thanks to expected cash proceeds from asset disposal, S&P estimates that the company's S&P Global Ratings-adjusted debt to EBITDA will materially improve in FY26 from a forecast peak level of about 4.0-4.5x at year-end 2025 to about 3.0x in 2026. This compares with our previous expectation of leverage remaining above 3.5x until 2027. S&P said it could raise the rating on Kering if it shows a clear organic deleveraging trend with adjusted debt to EBITDA below 2.5x on a sustainable basis. This scenario could derive from a track record of positive organic growth, with some regaining of market share lost in its key markets, coupled with a sustainable improvement in EBITDA and cash flow generation. An upgrade would also depend on the company successfully executing its business plan and turnaround strategy of the Gucci brand.

## Operational performance

Q2 26

\- Kering reported 2Q26 revenue of €3.7bn with organic growth at 2% y/y, beating the company-compiled consensus of +1.4% y/y. Fashion and Leather Goods (FLG accounting for c.81% of sales) organic growth was flat in the quarter (versus consensus of -0.6% y/y) while Gucci's organic revenue declined 2% y/y, a solid beat vs consensus $^{1}$ of -4.7% y/y.

\- We view Gucci's beat versus consensus very positively and note that by region, the beat was driven by North America (+6% y/y), up 6%, while other regions improved.

\- As expected, Kering Jewelry grew strongly, with organic growth up 18%, beating consensus of +17.2% y/y, driven by strong double-digit growth in North America, Asia Pacific and Japan.

\- Kering Eyewear reported organic growth of 8.0% y/y versus consensus of 6.1%, accelerating slightly from Q1, driven by expansion of brand portfolio.

\- Focusing on regions, North America increased its contribution to 24% of group revenue in Q2, while Western Europe was stable at 30%. When asked about the Middle East, management said the rest of the world remained challenging, down 8% in Q2, mainly reflecting the instability in the Middle East, even as retail in the region improved sequentially, month after month, through the quarter. The Middle East typically accounts for around 5% of group retail revenue. Its negative effect on group revenue growth was -1% in Q2 26, in line with Q1, when the disruption affected only one month of the quarter.
