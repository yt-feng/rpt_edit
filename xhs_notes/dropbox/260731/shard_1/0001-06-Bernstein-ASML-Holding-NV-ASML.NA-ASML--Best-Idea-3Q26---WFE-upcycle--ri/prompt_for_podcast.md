## LITHO INTENSITY CONTINUES TO INCREASE, DEFYING INVESTOR CONCERNS

We forecast litho intensity to continue its upward trend, increasing from 24% to 28% by 2028, driven by the faster growth of DRAM and advanced logic relative to the overall semiconductor market. Both segments have considerably higher litho intensity than mature logic and NAND, supporting a structurally higher blended litho intensity over time.

\- In DRAM, we estimate litho intensity increased from slightly below 20% at the 1Y and 1Z nodes to 26% at 1c. This significant increase was driven by the rising number of EUV exposures, starting with 3–4 exposures at 1a, increasing to 4–6 at 1b, and then to 6–7 at 1c. With 1c penetration expected to increase substantially over the next few years, we expect overall DRAM litho intensity to rise considerably. In addition, with the introduction of double-patterning EUV or HNA at the 1d node, we expect litho intensity to increase further to around 30%.

\- Similarly, logic litho intensity increased significantly from around 20% at 10nm, driven by a sharp rise in EUV exposures, peaking at 5nm and 3nm at above 35% intensity. While logic litho intensity declined from 3nm to 2nm due to the adoption of GAA, which requires more non-litho process steps, it remains elevated at around 32%. We expect litho intensity to remain at similar levels, or potentially increase, with HNA at 1.4nm, and to rise further at the 1nm node.

## DRAM GROWS FASTER THAN LOGIC TO BECOME THE BIGGER GROWTH DRIVER OF ASML

DRAM has now overtaken logic as the most important driver of ASML's litho demand. ASML guided 2026 memory system sales growing more than 75% YoY, compared with only 25% growth for advanced logic/foundry. This has already become visible in ASML's order mix, with memory's share of system sales increasing from roughly 30% historically to

\~50% in recent quarters (Exhibit 4).

\- The main reason is that, as we mentioned, DRAM litho intensity is rising faster than in logic. As DRAM manufacturers migrate to increasingly advanced nodes such as 1c and then 1d, the number of critical litho layers continues to increase. In particular, DRAM is seeing a rapid increase in both EUV and immersion layer counts, with EUV increasingly replacing multi-patterning flows that previously used multiple DUV exposures. Every time a customer replaces multi-patterning with single-exposure EUV, a larger share of process spending shifts toward lithography. ASML specifically highlighted that both EUV and immersion demand are rising in DRAM because of this node migration trend.

\- At the same time, DRAM capacity expansion is accelerating faster than logic. Tight supply conditions and elevated pricing in both DDR and HBM have triggered significant investment in new fabs and megafabs. ASML noted that customers are adding meaningful capacity today while also planning multiple future fab expansions. The HBM buildout adds a volume component on top of the technology migration, creating what management described as a "perfect storm" for DRAM litho demand.

\- This is reflected in our EUV exposure growth forecasts with total DRAM EUV exposures will increase 4.5x over the next 5 years, rising from roughly 3.4 MWPM in 2025 to 26.3 MWPM by 2030, compared with logic EUV exposures increasing by just over 3x during the same period. While logic continues to benefit from AI-related demand at 2nm, 3nm and future 1.4nm nodes, the combination of higher litho intensity and faster capacity additions makes DRAM the more powerful growth engine for ASML over the next several years (Exhibit 5).

## EUV ADOPTION CONTINUES TO DRIVE LITHO SHARE GAIN, ESPECIALLY IN DRAM

DRAM scaling continues to support rising lithography intensity as memory manufacturers push to ever-smaller feature sizes. Feature dimensions are expected to shrink from approximately 14nm at 1a to around 10nm at 1d and ultimately toward sub-10nm at 1d node. While the industry may transition from a $6F^{2}$ to a $4F^{2}$ cell architecture starting around 0a, this should be viewed primarily as a temporary easing of scaling requirements rather than a departure from the long-term trend of shrinking feature sizes. ## HNA ADOPTION: HIGHER LITHO INTENSITY THROUGH PROCESS SIMPLIFICATION

HNA should drive a step-up in litho intensity, similar to the increase seen when EUV LNA replaced DUV multi-patterning. The key reason is that HNA shifts process value from non-litho steps into lithography. Just as EUV LNA increased litho intensity by replacing complex DUV multi-patterning schemes with fewer but more valuable lithography exposures, HNA can replace multiple-patterning LNA flows with single-exposure solutions on critical layers. HNA's higher resolution and contrast allow many critical layers that would otherwise require LNA multi-patterning flows, such as SALELE, to be printed in a single exposure. By eliminating multiple masks, exposures, litho-etch loops, and cut steps, HNA simplifies process flows, improves pattern fidelity and yield, and reduces overall process complexity. Although an HNA exposure is more expensive than an LNA exposure, it replaces a significant amount of non-litho processing and can reduce total process cost. As a result, a larger share of WFE spend moves into lithography, increasing litho intensity. We therefore expect a step-up in litho intensity as customers transition from LNA multi-patterning to HNA single-exposure patterning (Exhibit 8, Exhibit 9).

Intel's adoption of HNA in 18A this year (Intel Foundry is using HNA on the Intel 18A) confirms that the technology is ready for high-volume manufacturing and removes a key uncertainty around adoption. However, we believe the strongest economic case for HNA is in DRAM, as DRAM dies are smaller than logic dies and therefore do not require stitching. This translates into higher throughput and a lower cost per exposure, making HNA adoption easier to justify economically. SK hynix has the clearest early signal: the company announced in September 2025 that it had assembled ASML's TWINSCAN EXE:5200B, the first production-oriented HNA system, at its M16 fab for next-generation DRAM development. Samsung is also moving quickly, with industry reports indicating that it ordered two ASML EXE:5200B tools, with the first expected by late 2025 and the second in H1 2026. Samsung logic should follow shortly after, while TSMC appears likely to delay broad HNA insertion until around 10A, after prioritizing R&D first and extending LNA for earlier nodes (Exhibit 10).

## DUV IS ANOTHER GROWTH DRIVER INSTEAD OF DRAG

While investor attention is understandably focused on EUV, we continue to believe the outlook for DUV remains underappreciated. As we highlighted in our (ASML: Don't forget DUV. Outperform) report, the blended DUV:EUV capex ratio remains approximately 1:1, implying that every incremental dollar spent on EUV drives a corresponding dollar of DUV demand. In DRAM, DUV still accounts for roughly 50% of litho spend even at the 1c node, meaning the rapid expansion of EUV-intensive DRAM capacity also drives substantial DUV demand. In advanced logic, the DUV:EUV ratio is lower at roughly 1:4, but absolute DUV spending remains resilient. Although EUV has replaced some DUV multi-patterning, higher litho intensity, increasing wafer capacity, and a richer mix of ArFi tools have kept DUV capex broadly stable (Exhibit 11).

A second driver is China, where we expect WFE to grow at a CAGR of more than 15% over the next three years, reaching approximately US\$77Bn by 2028. With no access to EUV and limited domestic lithography capabilities, China remains heavily dependent on DUV, often requiring additional multi-patterning steps that further increase DUV intensity. We estimate China's advanced logic capacity will increase massively over the next few years, providing a structural tailwind for DUV demand (Exhibit 12).

Despite consensus DUV estimates revision following ASML's Q2 results, our DUV revenue forecasts remain above street throughout the next five years (Exhibit 13).
