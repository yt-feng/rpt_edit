## The Increasing Importance of Demand Technicals in Credit

The acceleration in new issue activity across the corporate credit market has been closely watched by market participants so far this year. Both the USD and EUR Investment Grade (IG) markets are on pace for new annual gross issuance records, with USD IG issuance approaching \$1.5 trillion year to date (up 31% year over year) and EUR IG volumes at €535 billion (up 4% YoY). Leveraged finance supply has also remained steady, albeit shy of record territory. USD HY gross supply at \$195 billion is up nearly 10% over last year's pace and EUR HY supply at €70 billion is down 13%.

As a result of the heavy pace of primary market activity, investors have been acutely focused on the demand side of the equation. In this Global Credit Trader we take stock of several measures of credit market demand. At a high level, we expect the aggregate demand technical will be an important offset to the heavy pace of supply, which we anticipate will continue across most markets. That said, the concentrated nature of debt issuance within the AI-related ecosystem is likely to push spreads modestly wider per our existing forecasts, with the risks skewed toward more meaningful widening.

## 'Dealer lift' volumes reveal an investor preference for shorter maturities

Another robust metric of corporate credit demand is trade-level data, specifically the “net dealer lift” amount, which measures dealer-to-customer trades reported in TRACE, netting customer buys against customer sells. Aggregate data for both the USD IG and HY bond markets reveals a healthy appetite for corporate bonds, with the IG market on track for record net buying and the HY market pacing for its second-highest year on record after 2020 (Exhibit 5).

Zooming in on the net dealer lift data across the curve corroborates the fund flow narrative: net demand for long-end bonds has underperformed relative to the front end and belly. For instance, bonds with maturities greater than 12 years have accounted for 26% of YTD total trading volumes but account for just 22% of YTD net demand. Conversely, IG bonds with a maturity between 3 and 7 years have represented 27% of aggregate trading volumes this year but have accounted for an outsized 49% of net customer demand.

## Other measures of demand also point to a solid backdrop

To complete our review of corporate bond demand, we look at three additional metrics: foreign net purchases of US bonds, new issue concessions, and passive demand from recycled coupon payments. All three point to a supportive backdrop.

Exhibit 6 shows a 3-month average of net purchases of US corporate bonds using the Treasury TIC data. (Note: this data references the residence of the issuer, not the currency of the bond.) The key takeaway is that foreign demand (with a caveat that the data is lagged by six weeks) has currently reached new post-financial crisis highs. In fact, the top five highest monthly totals for net foreign demand since the end of the financial crisis have all come in the last 18-months. As of May 2026, net long-term purchases by foreign investors totaled \$439 billion over the trailing 12-month period, pushing total foreign holdings to a record \$5.3 trillion. Looking below the surface, about half of the foreign demand so far this year has originated from Europe, with the rest spread across other regions.
