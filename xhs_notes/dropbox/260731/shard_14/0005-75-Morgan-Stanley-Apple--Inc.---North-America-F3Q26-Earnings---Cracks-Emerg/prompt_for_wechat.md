## What we learned from Apple's F3Q26 (June quarter) earnings results:

(-) Demand remains robust, but supply chain challenges persist. While Apple's September quarter outlook introduces a new source of uncertainty, we came away from the print believing demand remains the less controversial part of the story. June quarter results were exceptionally strong, with iPhone revenue growing 22% Y/Y to a record \$54.3B, Mac revenue up 29% Y/Y despite supply constraints, and mgmt highlighting June quarter records across every geographic segment. Importantly, Apple set records for both active installed base and upgraders, suggesting contracting replacement cycles after multiple years of elongation. The challenge, however, is increasingly becoming supply rather than demand. Mgmt acknowledged that the advanced-node (i.e. 3nm) supply constraints will impact iPhone, Mac, and iPad in September. Notably, Apple characterized this as less of a supplier execution issue and more a consequence of demand running ahead of forecasts, while repeatedly emphasizing that the supply chain has "less flexibility" than normal to respond. Mgmt pointed to very lean channel inventory exiting the June quarter that likely remains lean exiting September as well. As a result, although Apple guided iPhone growth in the mid-teens Y/Y range in September, mgmt implied growth could have been stronger absent supply limitations. The key debate for investors now shifts to whether this demand can sustain into December and beyond, or whether higher prices create an elasticity effect (see our thoughts here). Our latest checks continue to point to unchanged iPhone build forecasts in C2H26, which means that Apple believes consumers will still upgrade after the iPhone 18 Pro/Pro Max/Foldable are released.

(-) Gross margins now seeing more significant memory headwinds – where do margins go from here? If supply constraints are limiting revenue upside, memory inflation is increasingly weighing on profitability. Beneath the headline September quarter gross margin guide of 47-48%, roughly 1 point comes from tariff refunds, implying a \~46.5% underlying margin at the midpoint, a 150bps Q/Q contraction from June ex-refunds vs. historical seasonality of flat to up 50bps. More importantly, mgmt noted that memory costs will account for >100% of the gross margin

compression in the September quarter outlook – despite tailwinds from mix and non-memory component cost savings – as memory costs rise further. The question now becomes – where do gross margins go from here, as memory costs are unlikely to show any relief? Apple still has two incremental tools in its toolbox to wield in the December quarter – iPhone price hikes and foldable iPhone shipments – and thus we believe Apple should be able to expand Product gross margins Q/Q in the December quarter. However, the bigger/broader question that will remain is – does memory inflation delay a more sustained margin recovery deeper into FY27?

(-) Acknowledgement of App Store headwinds puts Services price hikes / Siri AI launch in limelight. June quarter Services revenue growth of 12% Y/Y missed Street estimates of +14.6% Y/Y, while September quarter guide also implies growth decelerates to \~9.5% Y/Y after incorporating an incremental 2.5-point Y/Y FX headwind. FX remains the largest driver of this decel, but what stood out to us was Apple's unusually direct acknowledgement of App Store softness – mgmt pointed to headwinds in mobile gaming, variability in game-release schedules, changes to App Store business models in certain countries, ongoing litigation-related impacts, and even broader shifts in consumer engagement patterns. While Apple stopped short of attributing weakness to any single factor, the commentary reinforces that App Store growth is no longer an outperformer within Services (confirmed by the Q/Q contraction in Services margins), consistent with what we've observed from Sensor Tower data. That said, mgmt was enthusiastic about Siri AI, describing internal and developer feedback as overwhelmingly positive, while also suggesting increased usage could eventually drive opportunities for higher-tier iCloud+ subscriptions.
