EN_A: So, you just went through that global EV and battery report. What is the one big takeaway?
EN_B: The big picture is that EV and plug-in hybrid penetration is still rising in Europe and China, but the US is really struggling. And the clear bright spot is energy storage, or ESS.
EN_A: So what is the core question this report is trying to answer? Is it just about whether EV growth is still happening?
EN_B: Not exactly. It is more about where the real opportunities are, given this regional divergence. The report looks beyond just sales numbers. It digs into supply chains, policy changes, and the booming ESS market to find which companies and sectors are actually benefiting.
EN_A: Let us start with Europe. It sounds like things are going well there.
EN_B: Yes, very well. In May, sales in the five largest EU markets grew 39 percent year over year, and penetration hit 32 percent. This is driven by subsidies, more affordable mass-market EVs, and a new EU battery support package worth 1.5 billion euros. The key point is that Europe is moving from policy-driven growth to more market-driven adoption.
EN_A: And China? I saw the penetration rate is over 60 percent.
EN_B: That is right, but you have to be careful. Overall passenger vehicle sales in China are falling. NEVs are just holding up relatively better. In fact, the report's China auto team has cut their 2026 retail sales forecast from a 4 percent decline to a 15 percent decline. So the rising penetration might be more about a shrinking total market than surging absolute demand. This means overseas expansion and product mix are becoming more important profit drivers than just domestic growth.
EN_A: What about the US? Why is it so weak?
EN_B: US EV sales fell 21 percent in May, and penetration is stuck at just 7 percent. The report points to fewer consumer choices, because automakers are canceling new EV launches and pulling back on marketing. So, unless there is a major policy shift or a new wave of compelling products, the US market looks like it will stay soft for a while.
EN_A: You mentioned overseas expansion. What is happening with Chinese automakers in Europe?
EN_B: This is a really interesting shift. Chinese OEMs now have an 18 percent share of the European NEV market, which has tripled year over year. But the big insight is that their strategy has evolved. They are no longer just pushing cheap EVs. They are offering a balanced mix of powertrains, building brand ladders, and telling technology stories. This means their global expansion is not just a price war. It is a systematic push on multiple fronts.
EN_A: And what about the energy storage market? You said it is the bright spot.
EN_B: Absolutely. Global ESS shipments more than doubled year over year in the first four months of 2026. And a huge new driver is emerging: AI data centers. The report notes that some hyperscalers have signed master supply agreements for ESS, with first orders expected in the third quarter. This is a big deal because it means ESS is moving beyond just grid-scale applications into a whole new high-growth vertical.
EN_A: That sounds very promising. But are there any questions the report does not fully answer?
EN_B: Yes, a couple. For example, how much upside is left for lithium prices? The report says the market will be in deficit, but near-term headwinds like rising Chinese inventories and pushback from battery makers could limit gains. Also, the shift of US-bound ESS supply from China to Korean and Japanese makers is mentioned, but how that plays out in the long run is still an open question.
EN_A: So, for an investor, what is the core message of this report?
EN_B: It is about picking your spots. In Korea, the report favors automakers and battery cell makers over material suppliers. In China, it likes companies with strong global execution. But the full report has much more detail on specific stock picks and the catalysts to watch.
EN_A: So the real value is not a simple "EVs are good or bad" call, but a map of where the opportunities and risks are hiding.
EN_B: Exactly. It helps you see that in this fragmented market, the real action is in energy storage, overseas expansion, and supply chain shifts. To get the full picture on specific names, you really need to read the whole report.
