EN_A: So, there’s been a lot of buzz about China planning to spend two trillion yuan on data centers. Is this really as big a deal as it sounds?
EN_B: Actually, it’s not new news. That plan was already part of the “Six Networks” strategy laid out in the 15th Five-Year Plan back in March.
EN_A: “Six Networks”? That sounds like a big framework. Can you break it down for us?
EN_B: Sure. The six networks include water, new power grids, computing power, next-gen communication, urban underground pipelines, and logistics. Data centers fall under the computing power network.
EN_A: So why is it getting so much attention now? Is there something new happening?
EN_B: Recent policy signals suggest implementation is speeding up. For example, state media re-emphasized the “Six Networks” in June, and local governments are accelerating the use of new policy bank financing tools to support these projects.
EN_A: So it’s not just about data centers, but a broader shift in investment priorities?
EN_B: Exactly. This reflects a shift in government-led investment toward high-tech manufacturing, AI infrastructure, strategic supply chains, and people’s livelihoods. It’s a consistent theme from the 15th Five-Year Plan.
EN_A: How significant is that two trillion figure in the grand scheme of things?
EN_B: Our estimates show it’s only about 0.8% of fixed asset investment from 2026 to 2030. More importantly, total investment in the “Six Networks” this year could exceed seven trillion yuan, which is about 14% of total FAI.
EN_A: So data centers are just a small piece. Where’s the money coming from? And are we going to see a repeat of the local government debt issues?
EN_B: The funding mix includes ultra-long-term central government bonds, local government special bonds, policy bank tools, and commercial bank loans. This structure is designed to attract private capital and avoid the risky off-balance-sheet borrowing of the past.
EN_A: That sounds healthier. So why the push to accelerate now? Is it because the economy is slowing down?
EN_B: That’s part of it. After a strong Q1, fiscal spending slowed in Q2, and our fiscal deficit measure narrowed. But policymakers are now getting more cautious about the growth slowdown and are preparing investment pipelines for faster deployment.
EN_A: So what’s the key date to watch?
EN_B: We see July as a critical window. If Q2 GDP disappoints, the Politburo meeting in late July could signal more easing and faster use of remaining fiscal buffers.
EN_A: The report also mentions that AI-related industries could reach ten trillion yuan by 2030. Is that definition clear?
EN_B: That’s one of the open questions. The report doesn’t fully define what’s included in that figure. Also, data center electricity consumption is projected to hit 800 TWh by 2030, but the impact on the power grid isn’t fully explored.
