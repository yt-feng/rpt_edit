EN_B: It points to two big factors. One is the localization of India’s steel industry. The other is China’s medium-term anti-involution theme. Together, they should keep steel spreads supported beyond the usual seasonal ups and downs.
EN_A: So the report is basically asking: is this rally sustainable or is it just a flash in the pan?
EN_B: Exactly. And the conclusion leans towards sustainable. They think the strong performance cycle isn’t over yet.
EN_A: Give me some of the key insights. What’s happening with domestic steel prices in India?
EN_B: Domestic hot-rolled coil prices have rallied about 26% from their mid-December lows. That was helped by the extension of safeguard duties. They’ve pulled back a bit recently, but that’s mostly due to seasonally weaker demand.
EN_A: So what does that mean for investors?
EN_B: It means spreads have come down about 11% from their April peak and could stay under pressure through the monsoon season. But once demand picks up after the rains, the report expects spreads to expand again. The key is they think this time it’s backed by structural factors, not just seasonal recovery.
EN_A: What about the Middle East conflict? That’s been a big theme for commodities.
EN_B: The report says the direct impact on Indian steel is limited. Most of their raw material sourcing is independent of the Middle East. But they do flag a risk: if the conflict drags on, it could hit downstream industries and indirectly weigh on domestic demand. That’s something to watch.
EN_A: And you mentioned China’s anti-involution theme. How does that play into this?
EN_B: That’s the second big insight. Chinese steel exports have softened recently due to the Middle East situation and weak domestic demand. That could pressure near-term spreads. But the medium-term anti-involution policy is designed to support domestic margins for Chinese steel mills. And that should be supportive for global steel prices.
EN_A: So the report is bullish. Is it making specific calls?
EN_B: Yes. They’ve raised their price assumptions for Indian HRC and increased earnings estimates for some stocks. They’re overweight on JSW Steel, Jindal Steel, and Tata Steel, but underweight on SAIL.

EN_B: Two things come to mind. First, they mention that valuations already price in a lot of the re-rating potential. But they don’t fully unpack what drives the stock from here if valuations don’t expand. Is it just earnings growth? The full report probably dives deeper into that.
EN_A: And the second?
EN_B: They briefly touch on the cement sector facing demand slowdown and cost pressures. But they don’t fully stress-test the steel thesis under a more severe demand slowdown scenario. How resilient is that structural support if demand really falls off a cliff? That’s a question the full report might explore.
