EUROPE WEEKLY KICKSTART

Deal Momentum Builds, Europe Stays Cautious

Global M&A momentum remains robust. Our Global Financials Team forecasts c.18% growth in global M&A volumes over the next 12 months. So far the recovery has been skewed towards mega-deals, as evidenced by a stronger rebound in aggregate deal value than in transaction count (Exhibit 1).

The H2 2026 pipeline is underpinned by structural supply from corporates. Portfolio simplification is accelerating, with divestitures supporting earnings visibility and capital reallocation towards strategic priorities, including AI. Survey data shows a record share of UK CFOs citing asset disposals as a top priority (Exhibit 3). In parallel, private equity faces increasing pressure to return capital, reinforcing reliance on M&A exits amid a still-fragile IPO backdrop (outside the US). With European IPO activity near historical lows, well-capitalised strategies are well positioned to capture assets at attractive valuations. Key risks include geopolitical uncertainty keeping rates elevated and concerns around private credit, which we view as overstated. European balance sheets remain resilient, with net debt to EBITDA below historical averages across corporates, financials and households.

European M&A remains subdued (Exhibit 2). The recovery is lagging global trends, with Europe's share of global M&A declining to less than one-third of total activity, whether as acquirer, target or both.

Activity is increasingly driven by cross-border flows. In Europe, when deals occur, they are predominantly led by foreign buyers (Exhibit 4), reflecting valuation support and differentiated exposure. International acquirers are targeting European assets to capture discounts and access specific factor profiles, including Value and HALO segments, relative to the US.

Domestic buyers remain sidelined, reflecting weak local risk appetite. European corporates show limited acquisition appetite, consistent with equity flows where international inflows only offset persistent domestic outflows. This divergence extends to primary markets, with IPO activity still depressed in Europe. Survey data indicates that the share of CFOs prioritising expansion via acquisitions is near cycle lows, as focus shifts to cost discipline. Beneath the surface, take-private activity has moderated and sponsor activity, while recovering, is largely driven by exits rather than new deployment, as reflected in the Equity Research Sponsor Activity Barometer.
