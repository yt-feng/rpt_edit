## Grid constraints and SOFC policy tailwinds drive new era for AIDC onsite power

We hosted a well attended expert call on AIDC onsite power solutions to discuss the next phase of global data center infrastructure buildout. The timing of this call is especially relevant, as both government policy and private sector investment in AI and data centers is accelerating in China, the US, and globally, with no signs of slowing down. This is driving a new wave of demand for high-reliability backup and onsite power, and investors are increasingly focused on which companies can deliver the scale, technology, and channel execution needed to capture this expanding TAM. Our expert speaker provided a comprehensive update on the latest trends in data center power demand, technology choices, and competitive dynamics, with particularly bullish read-throughs for the AIDC power supply chain. We maintain OW on Weichai Power (2338 HK/000338 CH) and Yingliu Electromechanical (603308 CH), as both are well positioned to benefit from the global AIDC cycle, with strong order momentum, technology leadership, and clear paths to scale in gas engines, SOFC, gas turbines and related critical components, in addition to back-up power engines.

\- Grid constraints and power shortages are now a primary driver of demand for onsite power solutions in the US and globally, accelerating the shift toward AIDC engines, gas turbines, and SOFC. The expert emphasized that US data center power demand continues to surge, but grid interconnection delays and energy shortages are directly impacting project timelines and investment decisions. As a result, data center operators are increasingly turning to onsite solutions—such as gas turbines, gas engines, and SOFC—to bridge the gap and ensure reliable, scalable power. While about $60\%$ of US data centers still rely on the grid, according to the expert, over $20\%$ now use gas turbines for primary power, and the share of gas engines and SOFC is rising quickly. The industry is focused on scaling up supply, and China-based players are well positioned to deliver at scale as the market tightens, in our view.

\- SOFC is emerging as a highly competitive solution, with an expanded TAM and rising policy support in China, positioning it as a key technology for the next phase of data center power. The expert noted that global SOFC demand is poised to reach 20–30GW by 2030, with China’s policy support accelerating both capacity buildout and cost-down. SOFC’s lifecycle cost is currently \$0.08–0.15/kWh (assuming 8-10 years life cycle), with further cost-down expected as capacity scales and localization increases. SOFC’s flexibility, lower temperature, and rapid start-stop capability are increasingly valued by data center customers, and the technology is now seen as a core part of integrated onsite power solutions. The expert highlighted that China’s government is actively supporting SOFC, similar to the early days of the lithium battery and solar industries, which is expected to drive further TAM expansion and margin opportunity for leading players.
