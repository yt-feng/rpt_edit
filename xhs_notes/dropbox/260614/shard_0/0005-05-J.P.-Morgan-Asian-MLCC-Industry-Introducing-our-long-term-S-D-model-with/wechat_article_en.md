# The Next Great Component Shortage: Why MLCC Supply Will Tighten Through 2028 and Reshape the Entire Electronics Value Chain

The passive component industry is about to enter a structural supply deficit that will rival, and potentially exceed, the famous 2016-2018 cycle. According to a detailed supply-demand model from a leading global investment bank, the multi-layer ceramic capacitor (MLCC) market will shift from a 10 percent oversupply in 2025 to a 6 percent shortage by 2028. This is not a cyclical blip. It is a secular transformation driven by two forces that most investors have not yet fully priced: the exponential MLCC content growth in AI servers and a hidden capacity penalty that is silently eroding the industry's ability to deliver.

The numbers are striking. Industry revenue is projected to grow from approximately USD 17.5 billion in 2025 to over USD 31 billion by 2028, a compound annual growth rate of 24 percent. That is nearly 50 percent faster than the 16 percent CAGR recorded during the 2016-2019 period, which many still remember as the most profitable cycle in MLCC history. Yet the volume growth over the same period is only 9 percent CAGR. The gap between revenue and volume tells the real story: this is a pricing and mix-driven expansion, not a simple unit volume story.

What makes this cycle different is the structural nature of the supply constraint. The industry has historically added nameplate capacity at roughly 10 percent per year. But effective capacity, after accounting for production penalties from server and automotive mix shifts and the yield challenges of advanced specifications, is growing far more slowly. This is the underappreciated point that will define the next three years. Investors who focus only on headline capacity numbers will miss the tightening that is already underway.

The implications extend far beyond MLCC manufacturers. Every company that designs, builds, or procures electronics hardware will be affected. Procurement teams that remember the 2017-2018 allocation crisis should be preparing now. The window for strategic action is closing.

## AI Server Demand Is Creating an MLCC Content Explosion That Outpaces Anything in the Industry's History

The single most important driver in the MLCC demand model is the server segment, and within that, the AI server category. The numbers are breathtaking. A standard general-purpose server today uses approximately 8,000 MLCC units. An AI server uses roughly 50,000 units. By 2030, the model projects that a single AI server will require 148,000 MLCC units. That is a 3x increase from today's already elevated levels.

To put this in perspective, the entire server segment represented just 1.6 percent of total MLCC demand in 2023. By 2028, the model projects it will account for 11.1 percent. The absolute volume of MLCCs consumed by servers will grow from 63 billion units in 2023 to 629 billion units in 2028, a tenfold increase in five years. This is not incremental growth. This is a discontinuity.

The implications for the supply-demand balance are direct. When a single AI server consumes as many MLCCs as 18 smartphones, the demand shock is concentrated in the highest-value, most technically demanding segments of the market. These are precisely the segments where capacity is hardest to add. The model shows that procurement will outpace supply starting in 2027, with the gap widening to negative 6 percent by 2028. Every percentage point of shortage in a USD 30 billion market represents significant pricing power for suppliers.

What this means for procurement organizations is that AI server build plans cannot be treated as independent variables. The availability of MLCCs will become a binding constraint on server production, just as semiconductor availability was during the pandemic. Companies that do not secure long-term supply agreements now will face allocation decisions later.

## The Hidden Capacity Penalty Is the Most Underappreciated Structural Constraint in the Industry

Most investors and industry observers track nameplate capacity announcements and assume that new capacity translates directly into additional output. This assumption is dangerously wrong for the current cycle. The model introduces a concept that should be central to every investor's framework: the capacity trade-loss ratio.

When MLCC manufacturers shift production from standard IT applications to server and automotive grades, they face a significant production penalty. Server and automotive MLCCs use larger form factors, require advanced specifications like side-gap construction, and demand ultra-high capacitance values such as 1005-47 microfarad. These specifications are not just harder to make; they fundamentally change the manufacturing economics.

The assembly yield for AI server-grade MLCCs is estimated to be 3 to 6 times lower than for standard MLCCs. This means that a production line that could produce 100 million standard units per month might only produce 15 to 30 million AI server-grade units. The effective capacity is far lower than the nameplate capacity suggests.

What is accelerating this trend is the rapid specification escalation driven by both GPU and ASIC camp customers. As next-generation server systems demand higher power handling and reliability, the technical requirements for MLCCs are rising faster than the industry's ability to scale production. The model indicates that the negative impact of capacity penalties on headline capacity growth will be in the double-digit percentage range over the next three years, compared to low-to-mid single-digit impacts in the past five years.

This creates a powerful dynamic. Even as manufacturers announce new factories and capacity expansions, the effective output growth will lag. The gap between announced capacity and deliverable product will widen. This is not a temporary phenomenon. It is a structural feature of the industry's transition to higher-value applications.

## The Profitability Cycle Will Mirror the 2016-2018 Boom, But the Earnings Trajectory Is Different This Time

The model projects industry operating profit margins reaching 38 percent by 2028, compared to 36 percent at the peak of the 2016-2018 cycle. But the path to that peak is meaningfully different. In the previous cycle, margins spiked rapidly as procurement demand surged during the first cloud capex and first-generation EV demand take-off. The current cycle is building more gradually but with greater sustainability.

The top five MLCC players collectively generated operating margins of approximately 15 percent in both 2023 and 2024. The model projects these margins will recover to 21 percent in 2026, then surge to 30 percent in 2027 and 38 percent in 2028. The inflection point is 2026, when the supply-demand balance tightens from 10 percent oversupply to 5 percent oversupply. As the market moves toward balance, pricing power shifts decisively to suppliers.

The revenue trajectory reinforces this view. Industry revenue is projected to grow from USD 17.5 billion in 2025 to USD 31 billion in 2028, with the largest single-year jump coming in 2028 at 27 percent year-over-year growth. This is not a linear ramp. The acceleration in the later years reflects the compounding effect of volume growth, mix improvement, and pricing power as the shortage deepens.

For investors, the key insight is that consensus earnings estimates are likely to lag the reality. The market is still treating MLCC as a cyclical commodity business, but the structural shortage fundamentally changes the earnings power of the industry. The model suggests that the current valuation multiples, which appear elevated at 61 times 2026 consensus earnings, may prove justified as earnings revisions accelerate.

## The Report's Framework Leaves Critical Questions Unanswered That Investors Must Resolve Independently

While the supply-demand model is comprehensive, it deliberately leaves several important questions open. The most significant is the pace and magnitude of secondary market pricing dynamics. The report notes that secondary market pricing has traditionally been a leading indicator of contract pricing sentiment, but it does not quantify the relationship or provide a framework for predicting when secondary prices will move.

This is not an oversight. It reflects genuine uncertainty. The AI server MLCC segment is so new that there is no historical precedent for how pricing will behave. The report suggests that end customers' procurement appetite could be substantially higher regardless of price point, which implies that pricing could overshoot to the upside. But the magnitude of that overshoot is unknown.

Another open question is the spillover effect from AI MLCC to IT and commodity segments. If suppliers allocate more capacity to high-value AI server products, what happens to the supply of standard MLCCs used in smartphones, PCs, and consumer electronics? The model suggests a tightening across all segments, but the degree of spillover depends on supplier behavior and customer relationships.

Finally, the report does not fully address the risk of demand destruction. At what price point do customers begin to redesign systems to use fewer MLCCs or substitute with alternative technologies? The model assumes demand is relatively inelastic, but that assumption has not been tested at the price levels projected for 2027-2028.

## A Decision Framework for Investors and Procurement Leaders Navigating the Coming Shortage

For investors, the framework should focus on three variables: capacity trade-loss ratio trends, customer specification escalation rates, and competitor capacity announcements. The most important leading indicator is not headline capacity but effective capacity. Investors should track yield commentary and production penalty disclosures from manufacturers, not just capital expenditure plans.

For procurement leaders, the framework is different. The first priority is to secure long-term supply agreements with multiple suppliers. The model suggests that allocation will become the norm by 2027, and companies without established relationships will face severe constraints. The second priority is to design for supply flexibility. Systems that can accommodate MLCCs from multiple suppliers and multiple specification tiers will have a strategic advantage.

The third priority is to monitor secondary market pricing as an early warning system. The report identifies secondary pricing as a leading indicator of contract pricing sentiment. When secondary prices begin to move, the window for negotiating favorable long-term contracts will close.

For both groups, the most important decision is timing. The model indicates that 2026 is the inflection point. Companies that act in 2025 will have negotiating leverage. Companies that wait until 2027 will be reacting to a shortage that is already embedded in the supply-demand structure.
