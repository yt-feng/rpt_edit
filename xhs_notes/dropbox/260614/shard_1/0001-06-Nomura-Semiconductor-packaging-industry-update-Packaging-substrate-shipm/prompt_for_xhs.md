## Semiconductor packaging industry update

Global Markets Research

13 June 2026

EQUITY: JAPAN ELECTRONIC PARTS

# Packaging substrate shipments up 20% y-y

Start of competition for supremacy in 3.5D packaging

## Packaging-related monthly statistics: April shipments down from March record high

According to the Current Survey of Production for April, released on 12 June, shipments of packaging substrates (rigid module substrates) rose 20% y-y to ¥23.3bn, down from ¥27.3bn in March. Shipments in terms of m $^{2}$ rose 1% y-y and the average price per m $^{2}$ rose 19% y-y, and only slightly m-m, to a record high of ¥1.226mn. Based on conditions in other supply chains, we think shipments of packaging for Rubin processors may have been temporarily sluggish because of the pushing back of server production. Shipments in April 2025, when Blackwell packages got into full swing, were high in m $^{2}$ terms. We think that shipments of Rubin packages will move into full swing in summer 2026 (June at the earliest and around August at the latest).

## Adoption of 3.5D packaging likely to pick up in 2028: Moves at TSMC, Intel, Google

In the packaging industry, there has been an increase in news related to 3.5D packaging, mass production of which is due to start in 2028. 3.5D packaging is a back-end process technology that involves hybrid bonding of accelerators, HBM, and small inference processors, and bonding them together on the package. At its AGM, TSMC said that: (1) it plans to launch a pilot production line for panel-level packaging (Chip-on-Panel-on-Substrate (CoPoS)) in June 2026 and start mass production in 2028 following verification work; and (2) it plans to prepare for mass production of glass core substrates with partners (Ibiden, etc) over the next two to three years, despite issues in development. Based on these comments, we think that TSMC's 3.5D packaging consists of chip stacking using System on Integrated Chips (SoIC), CoPoS, and Flip Chip Ball Grid Array (FC-BGA) using glass core substrates (however, adoption of glass core substrates is likely to come after that of CoPoS). It appears that Intel's Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge Through-Silicon Via (EMIB-T) technology is being adopted in 3.5D packaging and that companies are increasingly making moves to place more orders for it, perhaps because of uncertainty about when TSMC's CoPoS will go into mass production. For example, The Information reported on 8 June that Google/Alphabet had placed orders for Intel to package at least 3mn TPUs with EMIB-T in 2028, and that Nvidia was considering adopting the technology (we have not confirmed the accuracy of the report). As Ibiden does not have the capacity to supply substrates for the packaging of 3mn TPUs per year with EMIB-T technology, we do not expect the news to be immediately reflected in Ibiden's earnings forecasts, but we will be interested to see if Ibiden announces any plans to increase production capacity.

One point to watch with regard to the adoption of 3.5D packaging is that it won't be easy for companies developing accelerators on TSMC's back-end process design platform (TSMC 3DFabric Alliance) to transition to EMIB-T, as 3.5D packaging includes key functions such as power supply and heat management. EMIB-T design will require companies to either rely on Intel's back-end process design platform or to build their own back-end semiconductor process supply chains. In power supply, we think vertical power supply or integrated voltage regulators (iVRs) would have to be developed jointly with partners, while in heat management, new cold plates with microchannel mechanisms optimized for cooling each chip would require the same. We expect collaboration among companies to pick up.
