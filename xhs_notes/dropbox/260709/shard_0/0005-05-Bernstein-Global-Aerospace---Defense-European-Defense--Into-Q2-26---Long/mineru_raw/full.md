European Defense stocks are decoupling after a period of broad performance. Ahead of Q2'26, we favor self-help stories (Leonardo, Thales) and superior growth (Rheinmetall).

Leonardo (30 July). We expect another beat-and-raise. We are +5% above consensus on EBITA and expect FCF to improve too. Growth should be broad-based. We would not be surprised to see the 2026 guidance upgraded across all metrics (except FCF). In a more challenging environment for European defense stocks, Leonardo stands out as a self-help story with room for earnings upgrades and re-rating, if execution continues. The stock trades on 11x EBITDA (vs. 14x for the sector). The correction that followed the surprise CEO change is an opportunity, in our view, as the strategy should be unchanged.

Thales (23 July). We expect a solid Q2'26 print, driven by momentum in the core A&D businesses. We are +6% above consensus on A&D, but -6% below on Cyber & Digital. We expect our tactical upgrade to materialize in Q2'26, as we believe Cyber will return to growth and is no longer material enough to drive downgrades (8% of sales, easy comps and lower expectations).

Rheinmetall (6 August). Q2'26 is the key ST catalyst for Rheinmetall. We expect the company to meet its implied +60% topline target. If it does, we see scope for the stock to regain some losses. The next catalyst will be the Arminius Boxer order. We expect the first tranche to come in full by the end of 2026. This should help rebuild credibility. We are less confident about the following ones (end of the decade). And we see risk to LT consensus estimates, even if they have already come down. We are -18% below consensus on 2030 EBIT. Yet, at these levels, we believe the risk-reward is attractive for a company that has an unmatched growth profile.

Dassault Aviation (22 July). We do not expect particularly strong H1'26 results, due to the soft Falcon deliveries. Last year's results pushed the stock down in a similar scenario, but we believe expectations have been managed more effectively. The key catalyst remains the mega Indian Rafale order, which we expect to be finalized by the end of 2026. Longer term, Dassault benefits from unmatched visibility thanks to its Rafale backlog, but we see limited near-term catalysts post-India order.

BAE Systems (30 July). We expect a relatively soft set of H1'26 results, with growth and margins in line with the annual guidance. The key focus for investors is likely to be the 2027 US defense budget, where BAE is well positioned (45% of sales). It will come later in the year. While we see BAE as a high-quality compounder, we do not expect earnings upgrades before the US budget is approved.

TKMS (12 August). We saw a tactical opportunity, driven by major order wins (here). The thesis has played out, and we do not expect large campaigns to be finalized before the end of the year, at the earliest (India, F127). Hence TKMS should revert to a fundamental “show-me” story, which we view as less attractive. We will update our expectations ahead of the quarter after speaking with the company.

## LEONARDO (30 JULY)

Another beat-and-raise. We expect Q2'26 results to be strong, and stand +5% consensus on EBITA. We also expect FCF generation to be stronger as Leonardo reduces the phasing impact (strong Q4, softer quarters before). We expect the strength to be driven across all businesses. The acquisition of Iveco implies upside to forecasts on DES, in our view. We expect the 2026 guidance to be upgraded across all metrics (except FCF).

Combination of earnings upgrades and re-rating. As we perceive the current environment as challenging for the sector, Leonardo stands out for being more of a self-help story. We believe the stock can keep getting estimates upgrades and should re-rate if it keeps delivering improving fundamentals. Leonardo is also screening well to resist the “new warfare” narrative, as its portfolio is skewed to electronics, missiles and air defense.

CEO change. In early April, media sources reported that the Italian government intended to replace Leonardo's CEO Roberto Cingolani as part of the regular renewal of boards at state-controlled companies. Leonardo was among the three companies mentioned (Leonardo, Eni, Enel). The news came as a surprise and the timing was poor, given Cingolani has been running the turn-around story since 2023. And had just hosted a positive Industrial Plan in March, where he dominated all the presentations. Despite the surprise effect and the lack of communication at the time, we do see the rationale for such a change. Cingolani has been a very successful driver of the performance for the company. But, he is a scientist and an Italian politician by background. The new CEO, Lorenzo Mariani, has been at Leonardo and MBDA since 2002. He brings experience in the key structural segments, namely MBDA and Electronics, and should be well positioned to run the business in the second leg of the story, as Leonardo is shifting from a turn-around story to a growth story within European defense.

## THALES (23 JULY)

Tactical call materializing in H1'26. We upgraded Thales earlier in the year as we saw the set-up as improving. Thales has been a funding short for many hedgefunds in 2025 because it was one of the few European Defense stocks with lower exposure to military activities (just over 50% of sales) and a clear problem: the Cyber business. We believe the drivers behind this negative thesis are weakening. We expect Q2'26 to deliver strong growth in the core A&D business (we are +5% above consensus on each division) and despite our negative stance on Cyber, we think the business does not matter anymore (8% of sales) and will return to growth, thanks to easy comps (detailed notes from the group pre-close below).

Acquisition of Exail. Thales' acquisition of Exail is strategically compelling but unlikely to materially impact the group in the near term given Exail's relatively small size (approximately €479 million in 2025 sales, or around 2% of Thales' revenue). The transaction strengthens Thales' naval franchise by adding leading positions in maritime robotics, navigation systems, mine countermeasures, and naval drones, while complementing its existing defense portfolio and stake in Naval Group. We view the deal positively from a strategic standpoint, as it reinforces Thales' core defense activities at a time when investor attention has been focused on the weaker performance of its Cyber business and the integration of Imperva. However, the acquisition comes at a rich valuation of roughly 28x forward EV/EBIT, well above European defense peers, reflecting Exail's superior growth and margin profile. While management expects meaningful synergies over time and Exail offers attractive growth prospects, we see limited near-term financial upside until those synergies begin to materialize. It supports the view that Thales is focusing on the core A&D business, which we view as a strong positive.

F126 cancellation impact. Following Germany's decision to terminate the F126 frigate program, Thales announced it expects to record an exceptional, largely non-cash charge of approximately €450m in H1 2026. It will reduce net income by around €350. The company stressed that the charge will have no impact on adjusted EBIT, adjusted net income, or free operating cash flow. After the exceptional charge. The impact should be limited (0.5% of 2026 sales and less than 1% of annual sales thereafter). Thales was surprised by the decision from the German DoD and is seeking compensation.

## RHEINMETALL (6 AUGUST)

H1'26 results on August 6 $^{th}$ . Our base case is that Rheinmetall meets the implied guidance of +60% topline growth in Q2'26. After speaking to the company, modeling the easy comps and the reverse one-offs from Q1'26, we believe it is achievable. We also checked the company's hit-rate on “informal” quarter guides from the past, which is close to 100%. We view Q2'26 as the most important near-term catalyst for Rheinmetall shares. A successful Q2 print should help break the earnings downgrade cycle and support re-rating.

Arminius Boxer order. The large Boxer order should come later in the year. Rheinmetall's hopeful guidance of the order coming in 2026 has been consistently delayed. Other suppliers have mentioned Q3 and Q4 as a more reasonable expectation, once the German government returns from the summer break. We believe this order will come in full (\~1800 vehicles, between €12bn and €25bn), as Germany needs to send a supportive message and encourage capacity increases. We see the next tranches are riskier. The recording of the Arminius order should be the most important catalyst for the stock in 2026.

Sentiment finally improving. The stock was hammered by the surprise F126 frigate order cancellation. We interpret the cancellation as a rationale decision from the German government for a problematic order (Damen has been late for years) rather than a message sent to Rheinmetall. We also got confirmation from TKMS and Thales that the shift was unexpected and unannounced. We would not extrapolate this to other orders, but flag that Rheinmetall's framework agreement backlog (including non-contracted orders) puts the company at risk on a credibility aspect. The stock collapsed, because of the credibility hit (stock down -19% while the order has a LSD% impact on 2030 earnings). We expect it will take time to recover credibility. But we have finally seen investors more interested in the stock as it remains one of the fastest growing and most profitable companies in the defense, with a clear growth cycle, a great positioning, and a very cheap valuation. We are still substantially below consensus on 2030 earnings (-18% below) but believe that the risk-reward is attractive, at these levels.
