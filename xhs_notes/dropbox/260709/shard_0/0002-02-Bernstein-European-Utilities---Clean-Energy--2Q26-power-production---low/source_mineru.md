In this report, we analyse the 2Q26 power generation figures for France, Germany, Austria, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Great Britain, Finland and Sweden, with a particular focus on renewable energy output and gas demand. Overall, solar output increased yoy, driven almost entirely by new capacity additions. Hydropower generation was down in all geographies but Sweden. Wind production was a mixed bag, up in France offshore, Great Britain, Italy, Finland, Sweden and German offshore - but declining in German onshore, France onshore, Portugal and Spain. French nuclear production saw a 9% increase.

Hydropower generation down in 2Q26 in all geographies but Sweden. Hydropower decreased in Portugal (production -42.7%/storage level -8.5%), Spain (-27.2%/-1.9%), Italy (-25.1%/11.8%), Finland (-14.8%/+3.4%), Austria (including pumped storage, -10.9%/-1.2%), France (-2.4%/-7.2%) and Germany (including pumped storage, production was down -0.4%). On the other hand Sweden saw a production improvement (+2.6%/-21.3%). Note, however, there is a tough comparative effect versus high 2025 hydro levels in Spain and Portugal.

Wind production - mixed performance. In 2Q26 wind power output varies across Europe once again. A decline in load factor led to reduced output in Germany onshore wind (load factor -2.8pp/generation -12.0%), France onshore wind (-1.5pp/-5.9%), Portugal (-1.6pp/-5.4%) and Spain (-0.5pp/-0.6%). France offshore wind production increased 22.1% despite a lower implied load factor (-5.7pp) thanks to new capacity. A similar trend was observed in German offshore wind (+1.3% power/-2.7pp load). Wind production rose in Great Britain (+7.3%), Italy (+4.8%), Finland (+2.4%) and Sweden (+1.9%).

Solar output up in all geographies. In 2Q26, Austria, Spain, France, Portugal, Germany, Great Britain and Italy saw solar production rise by 27.4%, 22.3%, 19.0%, 16.8%, 6.9%, 6.2% and 4.5% respectively, thanks to new capacity.

Power demand slightly up. Based on Platts data, the 1H26 combined demand of Germany, France, Italy, Great Britain and Spain amounted to 855TWh, up 0.6% yoy, but 6.7% below 1H21 level. At the EU level demand was also up 0.6% yoy, using Eurelectric data. Based on TSOs data, we estimate that power consumption in 2Q26 was up +1.6% yoy in France, +2.1% in Spain and +1.5% in Germany - which is equivalent at 1H26 level at -0.5% yoy in France, +1.2% in Spain, and +1.9% in Germany. Gas demand rose in 2Q25. We estimate that European gas demand increased 3.1% yoy in 2Q26, with industrial demand up 3.0%, LDZ (residential and commercial) demand 2.0% higher yoy, and gas-to-power up 5.8% yoy. Alongside this, German industrial gas consumption was up 3.1% yoy in 1H26, still 7.4% below 2018-21 average levels. Gas storage levels stand at 50% of total capacity, at the bottom of the historical range.

Company impact. In our view, Enel should be negatively impacted by lower hydro production in Italy. In Spain and Portugal, Iberdrola, EDP and Engie should be the main companies impacted by hydropower's decline, as should Endesa to a lesser extent. Similarly, Verbund should be negatively impacted by lower hydro generation in Austria.

## DETAILS

In this report, we analyse the 2Q26 power generation figures for France, Germany, Austria, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Great Britain, Finland and Sweden, with a particular focus on renewable energy output, and gas demand. Overall, solar output increased yoy, driven almost entirely by new capacity additions. Hydropower generation was down in all geographies but Sweden. Wind production was a mixed bag, up in France offshore, Great Britain, Italy, Finland, Sweden and German offshore - but declining in German onshore, France onshore, Portugal and Spain. In the nuclear sector, French production saw an increase of 9.0%.

## FRANCE

Based on TSO data, we estimate French solar output rose 19.0% yoy in 2Q26, thanks to new capacity additions (+23% yoy) offsetting a lower load factor (-3.2pp). Similarly, offshore wind production was up 22.1% yoy, thanks new capacity addition (+49.6%) - with a negative load factor effect (-5.7pp). France's onshore wind production was down 5.9%, due to a negative load factor effect (-1.5pp), while capacity was up (+3.1%). Hydropower output down 2.4% yoy.

Nuclear power production was up 9.0% yoy in 2Q26. As a reminder, EDF estimates French nuclear output, including Flamanville 3, between 350-370TWh in 2026 and 2027.

Based on Bundesnetzagentur data, we estimate that German solar production was up 6.9% yoy in 2Q26 as capacity increase (+16.6% to 124.0GW) more than offset a lower load factor (-1.1pp). Similarly, offshore wind production was slightly up (+1.3%), as capacity addition (+15.0% at 10.6GW) offset a lower load factor (-2.7pp). ## VALUATION METHODOLOGY

## Verbund

Our SOTP based PT of €54.6 uses:

\- For the hydropower assets a DCF with a post-tax WACC of 6.5% and a terminal growth rate of 0%. We use a mark to market for the baseload price on the unhedged exposure from 26-27-28-29. For 2030 we assume a baseload price of €60/MWh and peakload of €75/MWh.

\- We value Verbund's regulated activities APG (power) with a 9-year DCF with a post-tax WACC of 4.3% and a terminal value set at 15% premium on FY34e RAB — implying a 22% premium to FY26e RAB; and GCA (gas) at FY26e RAB.

\- We value the New Renewable segment 3-year DCF with a post-tax WACC of 5.5% and a terminal value set at 12.0x EV/EBITDA on our FY29e estimate.

• We value the Sales segment using a 6.0x EV/EBITDA multiple, based on our estimate of 2026.

• We value 'All other segments' using an 8.0x EV/EBITDA multiple, based on FY25e EBITDA.

## Enel SpA

We value Enel on an SOTP basis to arrive at our target price of €9.3. We value Enel's renewables assets in Italy with a FY26e-FY28e DCF, WACC of 6% and FY29e EV/EBITDA multiple pf 10x. Assets in North America are valued with a FY26e-FY28e DCF, WACC of 7% and FY29e EV/EBITDA multiple pf 11x. Enel's Spanish renewable assets the FY26e EV/EBITDA multiple of 10x. For Italy and Iberia, we use 2026e EV/EBITDA multiples of 5-6x for Generation & Trading, 8.0-8.8x for Infrastructure and Networks and 8x-8.4xx for Retail. LatAm activities we valued at 3.9-7.0x FY26e EV/EBITDA, in line with implied market valuations. We subtract net financial debt, hybrid debt, provisions and minorities (7x FY26e P/E) from the estimated EV.

## Engie SA

We value Engie on a 1-year forward SOTP basis, to arrive at our €29.4 price target. We value gas networks as 15% premium to RAB, while Brazilian and UK electricity grids are valued at a 20% and 50% premium, respectively. We value Supply and Energy Management activities at 6x FY26e EV/EBITDA, while contracted Local Energy Infrastructure is at 10x. Renewables valuation is a combination of a DCF (6.7% WACC) and a target multiple of 11x given the young age of the fleet and a high exposure to hydro generation, while Gas generation is valued at 6x EV/ EBITDA for merchant and 9x EV/EBITDA for contracted capacities including the potential disposal proceeds. We value EV adjustments (e.g. minorities, provisions, hybrids, net financial debt, nuclear liabilities, pension liabilities and financial assets) by taking into account the book value.

## Endesa SA

Our €33.25 price target is based on a sum-of-the-parts valuation. We employ a DCF with a 5.9% WACC for unregulated assets, comprising conventional Generation and non-mainland generation assets. We use a multiples approach for electricity Supply (EV/EBITDA of 6.5x on average EBITDA throughout the next three years, a discount to peers due to higher than average volatility). For

Renewables, we value the assets in operation using a DCF with a WACC of 4.8% while we value the pipeline using a spread on the WACC. We apply a 4.8% WACC for regulated assets (Distribution).

## EDP - Energias de Portugal SA

Our €5.3 price target is derived from a sum-of-the-parts, based on a DCF analysis in which we apply a WACC of 4.8% for the regulated Iberian assets, 6.2% for the liberalized Iberian assets and 9.0% for the Brazilian assets. For the supply unit in Iberia we apply an EV/EBITDA multiple of 6.5x in line with peers. For the renewable assets we value the assets in operation through a DCF while the future growth valuation is based on an IRR spread on the WACC.

## Iberdrola SA

To arrive at our price target of €19.8 we use a sum-of-the-parts approach to value Iberdrola, based on a separate DCF model for each asset class. In our EV valuation, the liberalized assets account for 11%, networks for 53% and renewables for 37%. We apply a WACC of 6.2% for the liberalised assets, 5.9% for the regulated networks and 4.6% for the renewable assets.

## RISKS

## Verbund

Upside risks:

\- Demand for regulated earnings can vary significantly depending on macro economic developments. A more risk averse environment could lead to a higher rating in terms of EV/EBITDA and premium to RAB.

\- Changing the RAB premium for GCA by +10pp changes our PT by +€0.20.

\- Changing our long-term baseload power price assumption for 2030 and beyond from €60/MWh by +€5/MWh impacts our PT by +€2.8.

## Enel SpA

Downside: 1) LatAm accounts for about 25% of Enel's EBITDA. A 10% depreciation of LatAm currencies would reduce FY27e EBITDA by 2.5%; 2) Failure to deliver on Enel's renewables expansion strategy adding 11.8GW of consolidated gross renewable capacities in FY26e-FY28e on our estimates; 3) Lack of a significant supply margin recovery.

Upside: 1) Attractively valued inorganic growth; 2) More investments in networks; 3) Positive regularity surprises

## Engie SA

Performance of any of the company's large business segments would pose risks to our price target. Downside risks for the Conventional generation business include lower Clean Spreads and lower volatility on the power market. Downside risks for renewable assets include lower volumes or lower returns due to higher installation, capital or financing costs. Downside risks for networks include lower volumes for the French gas distribution activity.

Upside risks: Power demand impacting B2B and B2C, Gas demand impacting networks, Power prices, spread and volatility impacting Engie's trading division, gas power generation and merchant renewables

## Endesa SA

Downside: Lower-than-expected power prices and supply margins; lower than expected regulated returns in networks; delay in renewable capacity deployment; unexpected political events such as new taxes and clawbacks and higher sovereign risk. Upside: higher returns and capex in regulated networks then expected, the life extension of nuclear plants and higher power prices.

## EDP - Energias de Portugal SA

Downside risks: Lower generation and supply margins than anticipated and delays in the commissioning of the new renewable capacity. Lower electricity prices would have a negative impact in our valuation. A USD and/or Brazilian Real depreciation would have a negative impact. Cost overruns in networks and/or renewable capacity assets as well as lower capital gains in the planned

asset rotation would have a negative impact.

## Iberdrola SA

Upside risks: Higher power prices in Iberia, the UK and the US than anticipated, more profitable remuneration frameworks for regulated assets than expected, higher load factors on the renewable assets than anticipated.

Downside risks: Unexpected regulatory and/or political measures limiting the profitability of assets, lower than expected power prices, delays in renewable capacity deployment, higher interest rates and bond yields than anticipated, lower achieved returns on the US grids than expected.
