EN_B: Because the cost structure is shifting. Right now, building a one gigawatt AI data center, almost half the money goes into infrastructure, not the compute itself. EN_A: So what does an orbital compute setup actually look like?
EN_B: The report describes it very clearly. It is racks in a sun-synchronous orbit. They are connected by lasers traveling through vacuum. That laser link technology is already widely used in modern satellites.

EN_B: First insight: in the near term, orbital compute is not going to replace ground-based hyperscale data centers. The more realistic use case is orbital edge AI. Satellites process imagery, sensor data and inference workloads in orbit, and only send useful results back to Earth.

EN_B: Exactly. Second insight: orbital compute does not solve infrastructure constraints. It replaces terrestrial bottlenecks with new engineering challenges. Instead of water cooling, you have radiative cooling in a vacuum. Instead of swapping out a fan, you have hardware that is almost impossible to repair.
