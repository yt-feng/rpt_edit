# AI Supply Chain: 2027 CoWoS Allocation and ASIC Dynamics Update

## AMD's 2027 CoWoS Allocation and Product Roadmap

AMD's 2027 CoWoS allocation remains at 240k for now, with combined MI455/MI450 shipments at 1.5mn units. TPU mass production timing shifts toward Q4. The Blackwell chip "inventory" observed in the supply chain is actually a buffer and is expected to be fully consumed by 2026. We project approximately 7mn Rubin chips and 90k NVL72 racks in 2027.

**AMD CoWoS Allocation Details:** Following our preliminary 2027 CoWoS allocation published last week, we received numerous inquiries regarding AMD's CoWoS numbers. Some MI300 series and upcoming MI500 production will continue into 2027. The MI400 series will be available in two versions: (1) MI455 - the standard version with 2 compute dies and 12 HBM4 12hi, paired with the Helios rack (18 CPUs and 72 GPUs), with Microsoft, AWS, and Oracle as key customers; (2) MI450 - a Meta-customized half-size chip with 1 compute die and 6 HBM4 12hi (9 CPUs and 36 GPUs). We maintain our expectation of AMD's 2027 CoWoS at 240k, while acknowledging execution risk given the company's history of adjusting CoWoS bookings in 2026. 2027 chip shipments: MI455 at 1mn units and MI450 at 500k units. For CPU, Venice represents AMD's first CPU adopting CoWoS, with production concentrated at OSATs including ASE/SPIL, Amkor, and Powertech. Total CPU chip shipments could reach 5.7-6mn units in 2027, compared to 1mn in 2026.

## Google TPU Progress Update

Industry checks suggest KYEC's Q3 2026 revenue could grow close to 10% QoQ, below our initial 15% QoQ expectation, primarily due to slight delays in Rubin and Sunfish, as well as MediaTek's smartphone SoC order adjustments. Sunfish shipments are expected largely in Q4, with full-year volume at 960k units. With Sunfish and Rubin pushed out, we believe more revenue will concentrate in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 without order reductions. Regarding Zebrafish delays, we maintain our view that Q4 2026 volume ramp remains unchanged.

## Blackwell and Rubin Chip/Rack Comparison

We previously provided a chip vs. rack shipment comparison for Blackwell in last June's AI tracker. We now include HGX (8-GPU per server) in our chip consumption model, treating 9 HGX servers as equivalent to one NVL72 rack. We expect 5.4mn Blackwell units in 2026, with chip volume potentially meeting Grace Blackwell NVL72 demand by H2 2026. Our latest checks suggest approximately 7mn Rubin and Rubin Ultra units in 2027, while total Rubin NVL72 server racks could reach 90k in 2027. All Blackwell chip "inventory" appears to be a supply-chain buffer that will be fully consumed by 2026, so inventory concerns appear unwarranted. We expect Rubin to follow a similar pattern.

## Meta ASIC Progress Update

Meta cancelled its original 2nm ASIC Olympus earlier this year; a new 2nm chip, code-named Apollo, has succeeded it. Broadcom will continue design services, with mass production expected in Q1 2028. For GUC, we believe it is likely to win one of the Meta ASIC projects led by the Rivos team, possibly a side project from the usual MTIA product line. The AI ASIC targets tape-out in H1 2027, with potential CoWoS output by year-end 2027 or H1 2028.

## 2027 Global CoWoS Capacity Update

Our preliminary 2027 CoWoS allocation analysis indicates continued growth across major AI chip vendors. Total CoWoS demand is projected to reach 2,694k wafers in 2027, representing approximately 93% year-over-year growth. NVIDIA remains the largest consumer at 1,222k wafers (45% share), followed by AMD at 530k wafers (20% share) and Broadcom at 484k wafers (18% share). MediaTek enters the CoWoS ecosystem with 180k wafers allocated for TPU-related production.

## AI HBM and Wafer Consumption

AI HBM consumption is projected to reach up to 50bn Gb in 2027, driven primarily by NVIDIA's Rubin R200 and Google's TPU v8i (Sunfish). AI wafer consumption is estimated at approximately US$47bn in 2027, with NVIDIA accounting for the largest share at US$21.7bn, followed by Google at US$16.7bn.

## Key Observations

- CoWoS capacity expansion continues across multiple vendors, with TSMC maintaining the largest share
- ASIC designs from Google, AWS, and Meta are gaining meaningful CoWoS allocation
- The transition to HBM4 and HBM4e memory technologies is accelerating
- Supply chain buffers rather than genuine inventory buildup characterize current chip availability
