ZH_A: 最近国际油价又冲到80美元附近了，这背后到底发生了什么？

ZH_B: 简单说，霍尔木兹海峡的局势再次升温，直接冲击了全球石油供应。

ZH_A: 我们刚看到一份某外资投行的最新研报，专门追踪石油市场的动态。这份报告真正要回答的问题是什么？

ZH_B: 核心问题就一个：在伊朗和美国冲突升级的背景下，波斯湾的石油流动还能不能恢复正常？报告给出了一个很关键的判断。

ZH_A: 什么判断？

ZH_B: 报告认为，现在制约石油流动恢复的主要因素，不是运输能力不足，而是伊朗的意愿。你看，霍尔木兹海峡附近其实有大量空油轮，但就是不敢过。

ZH_A: 所以这意味着，即使有船，如果政治风险不解决，供应照样上不去？

ZH_B: 完全正确。报告里有个数据很能说明问题：波斯湾的石油流动在重新开放后的头10天，一度恢复到战前水平的80%以上，但最近因为油轮遇袭，又跌到了70%出头的水平。

ZH_A: 那除了霍尔木兹海峡，还有没有其他供应侧的风险？

ZH_B: 有。俄罗斯那边的情况也很紧张。无人机对俄罗斯炼油厂的袭击明显加速了。

ZH_A: 这对市场意味着什么？

ZH_B: 意味着成品油的利润会持续高企。你看，俄罗斯的炼油厂停工量已经达到每天380万桶，超过一半的产能都离线了。这导致俄罗斯国内柴油和汽油零售价涨了超过10%。

ZH_A: 那俄罗斯的原油出口呢？

ZH_B: 很有意思。因为炼不了油，俄罗斯反而把更多原油直接出口了。原油出口同比增加了每天110万桶，但成品油出口却减少了每天90万桶。

ZH_A: 所以全球的柴油和汽油供应会更紧张？

ZH_B: 对，报告也强调了这一点。欧洲柴油利润已经涨到每桶60美元，美国柴油利润更是到了75美元。这背后就是俄罗斯供应减少和全球炼厂开工率偏低共同作用的结果。

ZH_A: 那这份报告有没有提到什么不确定的地方？或者哪些问题还没有完全展开？

ZH_B: 有一个很关键的悬念。报告提到，如果美国对伊朗的制裁豁免取消，伊朗的石油出口可能会再次受挫。但报告并没有完全展开分析，如果伊朗真的被全面封锁，霍尔木兹海峡的流动会跌到什么程度。这需要等待更多谈判结果。

ZH_A: 还有一个悬念，就是全球的库存情况。

ZH_B: 没错。报告说全球可见库存曾在6月中旬触底，然后有所回升，但最近一周又因为运输中的产品下降而大幅减少。这个趋势会不会持续？报告里没有给出确定的答案。

ZH_A: 看来这份报告还有很多值得深挖的内容。如果想了解完整的分析和数据，去哪里看？

ZH_B: 完整报告里有更多的图表和详细推演，包括每个产油国的具体产量损失和库存变化。建议直接去阅读那份研报原文。

EN_A: So oil prices are back near 80 dollars a barrel. What is driving this move?

EN_B: It really comes down to the Strait of Hormuz. The situation there has escalated again, and that is putting direct pressure on global oil supply.

EN_A: We just got a new oil tracker report from a global investment bank. What is the one big question this report is trying to answer?

EN_B: The main question is: can Persian Gulf oil flows actually recover to normal levels given the renewed conflict between the US and Iran? And the report gives a pretty clear answer.

EN_A: What is that answer?

EN_B: The report argues that the main bottleneck right now is not a lack of tankers. It is Iran’s willingness to let the flows resume. There is actually a huge amount of empty tanker capacity sitting inside the Gulf, but nobody wants to risk crossing.

EN_A: So even if the ships are there, if the political risk is not resolved, supply cannot really come back?

EN_B: Exactly. The data in the report shows this clearly. In the first ten days after the Strait reopened, flows recovered to over 80 percent of pre-war levels. But then, after the recent attacks on tankers, they dropped back to the low 70s.

EN_A: And what about other supply risks? Is it just the Strait of Hormuz?

EN_B: No. Russia is another big factor. Drone attacks on Russian refineries have really accelerated.

EN_A: What does that mean for the market?

EN_B: It means refined product margins are going to stay high for a long time. Russian refinery outages have jumped to 3.8 million barrels per day. That is more than half of their capacity offline. And as a result, domestic gasoline and diesel prices in Russia are up over 10 percent.

EN_A: So Russia is exporting less fuel?

EN_B: Actually, it is more nuanced. Russia is exporting more crude oil because it cannot refine it. Crude exports are up 1.1 million barrels per day from a year ago. But refined product exports are down 0.9 million barrels per day, mostly diesel.

EN_A: So that means global diesel and gasoline supply is getting tighter?

EN_B: That is exactly the report’s view. European diesel margins are at 60 dollars a barrel. US diesel margins are at 75 dollars. This is driven by lower Russian supply and still low refinery runs elsewhere.

EN_A: Are there any big unknowns the report does not fully answer?

EN_B: Yes, there is one key question. The report mentions that if the US sanctions waiver on Iran is canceled, Iranian oil exports could take another hit. But it does not fully explore what happens if Iran is completely blockaded. That scenario is still a big unknown.

EN_A: And there is another one about global oil inventories.

EN_B: Right. The report shows that global visible stocks bottomed in mid-June and then started to build. But in the last week, they fell again because of a drop in products in transit. Whether that trend continues is still an open question.

EN_B: The full report has all the detailed charts and the full breakdown by country. It is definitely worth reading the original research note.
