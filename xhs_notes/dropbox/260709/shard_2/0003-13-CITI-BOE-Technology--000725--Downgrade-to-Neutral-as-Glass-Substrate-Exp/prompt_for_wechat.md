## Glass-based packaging substrates

High-end packaging substrates need to support large area with low warpage, high-density routing and low dielectric loss. Traditional PCB materials face growing limitations in AI and HPC applications. Glass substrates have three key advantages versus traditional materials: (1) Coefficient of thermal expansion, or CTE, close to silicon, resulting in low warpage. (2) Ability to achieve very small via diameters, supporting high-density packaging for AI chips. (3) Strong thermal performance, supporting heat dissipation in high-computing-power scenarios.

Packaging-related glass technologies can be divided into three categories (1) Panel-level packaging, or PLP. Square glass is used as a carrier and then peeled off in wafer-level packaging. (2) Glass interposer. Used for high-end, high-density direct die-to-die connection. (3) Glass substrate. Serves as the connection layer between the die and the PCB. Among these, glass substrate is currently the direction with the fastest industrialization progress.

The major technical bottlenecks include: (1) TGV drilling. The process must solve 20:1 aspect-ratio hole drilling, simultaneous processing of dense/sparse and large/small holes, roundness qualification, no open circuits and no hidden cracks. (2) Metal filling. Electroplating uniformity must be ensured. Surface copper and via copper thickness need to be consistent. Filling must be void-free, while stress between metal and glass must be managed. (3) Build-up and cutting. Multi-layer build-up can create stress accumulation and line breaks. Cutting brittle glass after build-up requires chip-free cutting technology. Automated inspection solutions must also be developed for transparent glass.

BOE aims to continue to work with domestic and overseas research institutions and industry partners to advance technology implementation. BOE's roadmap is as follows (1) In 2027, to launch a two-dimensional multi-channel micro-LED optical interconnect solution demo. (2)

In 2028, to launch mature multi-channel product demos aligned with 1.6T, 3.2T and 6.4T industry requirements. (3) To expand applications into data centers, automotive interconnect and other scenarios.

## Third-generation semiconductor and GaN

BOE Huacan has completed construction of a gallium nitride (GaN) pilot line and now has preliminary mass-production conditions. Its 650V high-voltage product has passed customer validation. The company expects to launch a 40V low-voltage product by end-2026. It plans to later expand into automotive-grade and AI-scenario applications. In GaN power electronics, BOE Huacan has delivered the second high-voltage GaN device product to customers through its Yiwu GaN pilot line. GaN products remain at an optimization stage. Related products are likely to launch in 2027, with future applications targeting AI, industrial and automotive-grade power chips. Large-scale investment may be considered at the appropriate time, according to the company.

## Display

D&A and capex. Core production lines are generating attractive profitability, according to the company, with the Chongqing and Fujian Gen-8.5 lines both recording net margins above 20%. The two Gen-10.5 lines have continued to increase shipment volume through quality and efficiency improvements without capacity expansion. Depreciation likely peaked in 2025; although there may be a modest increase in 2027 due to depreciation from B16, BOE said the overall depreciation trend is downward. It also expects capex to decline after completion of the OLED Gen-8.6 line.

LCD TV industry consolidation is likely close to completion. The concentration ratio of the five largest LCD TV panel makers has remained flat in 2026 versus 2025, and BOE expects leading display companies to capture a larger share as industry concentration rises. It believes rising memory prices have a greater impact on smaller-size TV products, pushing the market towards larger-size TVs. The shipment share of 70–75-inch TVs increased from 8% in 2025 to 22% in 2026. BOE expects shipments of 80-inch and above TVs to increase by more than 20% in 2026 versus 2025. This should drive a continued increase in average TV size. BOE expects the average TV shipment size to reach 55 inches in 2027. Management views 55 inches as a key threshold for the TV panel industry. Once the average TV shipment size reaches 55 inches, it believes the TV segment could reach supply-demand balance. BOE expects this to happen in 2027, which would be favorable for BOE as a leader in large-size panels.

Competition in the OLED industry could persist over the next two years. BOE expects global OLED smartphone demand in 2026 to decline by 10-17%, mainly due to memory price increases pushing up terminal prices and suppressing consumer demand. Domestic smartphone brands are under pressure. Despite this, BOE's OLED business maintained stable growth in 1H26. The business, however, remains loss-making at the operating level. Management expects competition in the OLED industry to persist over the next two years. Supply volume to key customers continued to grow in 1H26.

Memory price impact. Downstream terminal brands are responding to high memory prices in two main ways, according to BOE. First, they are reducing specifications to alleviate cost pressure. This is being seen across TV, IT and smartphone categories. Second, they are launching higher-specification products to increase perceived consumer value and offset price increases. Several OEMs have already raised operating expectations for high-spec products, according to BOE.
