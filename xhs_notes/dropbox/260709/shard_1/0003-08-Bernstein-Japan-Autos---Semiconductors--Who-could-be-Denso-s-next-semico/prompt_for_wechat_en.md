## INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS

## Japan Autos and Auto Parts:

As the industry shifts toward SDVs, automakers are increasingly seeking control over hardware-linked upstream software layers, where suppliers such as Denso have historically captured value through integrated hardware and embedded software offerings. This shift could reduce the scope for supplier-led system design over time, making Denso's expansion into upstream hardware such as semiconductors, as well as non-auto businesses, strategically important. For Toyota, cost reduction of power semiconductors, particularly SiC, is important for improving its xEV competitiveness, which would make a potential acquisition by Denso's of a power semiconductor company strategically reasonable. However, if Denso were to use proceeds from a potential sale of its Toyota stake to fund semiconductor M&A, this could disappoint Denso's investors expecting shareholder returns. Conversely, if Toyota were to buy back those shares, it would be positive for Toyota. We remain Market-Perform on Denso (PT = 2,050.00) and Outperform on Toyota (PT=4,200.00).

## Japan and European Semiconductors:

We are constructive on power semi given the rise of AI power (Infineon/Renesas: CPU renaissance emerges as a second engine for power semi demand, set to outstrip supply). We believe a successful Fuji Electric transaction would likely limit direct impact on Renesas, as the strategic benefit for Denso would be concentrated in power semis rather than in Renesas's core MCU franchise. A Rohm/Toshiba-related structure could be more relevant for Renesas, as it would give Denso a broader domestic power semiconductor platform and potentially reduce external sourcing needs in selected auto discrete and analog areas. However, Renesas remains much stronger in automotive MCUs and has a broader customer base across OEMs and Tier-1s, limiting the risk of meaningful share loss in its core business. For Infineon, the impact would potentially be limited given only 9% sales exposure to Japanese OEMs, although it remains the second-largest automotive semiconductor supplier in Japan by market share. We rate Renesas Outperform with PT = ¥6,300.00 and rate Infineon Outperform with PT = €102.00.

## OVERVIEW OF DENSO'S SEMICONDUCTOR BUSINESS

## Current state of Denso's semiconductor business

Denso's semiconductor business currently spans three categories: MCU & SoC, Power & Analog, and Sensors (Exhibit 1). At present, all semiconductor products are developed primarily for internal use, supporting Denso's auto parts business. In 'Microcomputer and SoC' subsegment, Denso focuses on architecture design and specification development rather than manufacturing. The company works closely with microcomputer and SoC vendors to develop custom chips tailored to its automotive applications, while wafer fabrication is outsourced to foundries such as TSMC and UMC.

Within ‘Sensor’ subsegment, Denso develops semiconductor devices that support its ADAS/AD businesses. While the company continues to procure a significant portion of semiconductor content from external suppliers, it develops and manufactures application devices, including image sensors, millimeter-wave radar chips, and LiDAR-related semiconductors.

'Power & Analog' subsegment is the most important subsegment of Denso's semiconductor business, representing the area where the company has internalized the entire value chain from device design to manufacturing. Denso develops and produces key semiconductors for electrification, including Si and SiC power semiconductors used in xEV inverters as well as battery management ASICs. This vertically integrated model enables the company to maintain control over critical technologies while supporting the growing semiconductor content required for automotive electrification.

Denso's history in power and analog semiconductors dates back several decades. The company began IC research in the 1950s, established its first wafer fabrication facility in 1975, and further expanded its semiconductor capabilities through the acquisition of Toyota's electronic component business in 2020.

Particularly in power semiconductors, Denso possesses a broad range of proprietary technologies (Exhibit 2, Exhibit 3). In Si power semiconductors, the company jointly developed RC-IGBTs with Fuji Electric, integrating IGBTs and diodes into a single device to achieve smaller size and lower power loss, and has deployed the technology in HEVs. In SiC, Denso has proprietary RAF and gas growth technologies for SiC wafer production, enabling the manufacture of high quality, low cost SiC wafers.

## Denso's semiconductor business strategy

Denso identified semiconductors as one of its ‘Societal Value Expansion Domains’ in the mid-term plan announced in Mar-2026 (Exhibit 6). The company aims to expand the application of its semiconductor technologies beyond automotive markets and ‘contribute to a wide range of fields, including industrial equipment and consumer electronics’. Given that Denso currently generates no external semiconductor revenue and derives nearly all of its semiconductor sales from automotive applications, we believe that power semiconductor companies with meaningful exposure to industrial and consumer end markets could be attractive strategic targets for the company.

## MARKET ATTRACTIVENESS OF DENSO'S TARGET SEMICONDUCTOR SEGMENTS

According to Gartner, the global semiconductor market is expected to grow from USD 809 bn in 2026 to USD 2,153 bn in 2030, implying a CAGR of 21.6% (Exhibit 8). Growth is expected to be driven primarily by memory and ASIC/ASSP markets, which benefit from rising AI-related demand, including discrete GPUs and AI accelerators (Exhibit 9). Combined, these categories are expected to account for more than 80% of the semiconductor market throughout the period from 2026 onward.

By comparison, the discrete and analog markets, which represent Denso's primary areas of focus, are expected to account for only around 5% of the overall semiconductor market. Nevertheless, the combined market size is projected to increase steadily from USD 79 bn in 2026 to USD 111 bn in 2030, making them attractive markets characterized by stable growth rather than rapid expansion.
