EN_A: So the report is really trying to answer: who is Denso’s next semiconductor acquisition target?

EN_B: Exactly. The report breaks down Denso’s semiconductor business. Right now, almost all of it is for internal use. 99.7% of revenue comes from automotive. But their mid-term plan explicitly says they want to expand into industrial and consumer electronics.

EN_A: That’s a big shift. Why would a company that makes chips for cars suddenly want to go after industrial and consumer markets?

EN_B: Because the auto industry is changing. With software-defined vehicles, automakers want more control over hardware and software. Suppliers like Denso, who used to capture value through integrated hardware and embedded software, are seeing that model shrink. So they have to move upstream into semiconductors and non-auto businesses.

EN_A: Makes sense. So what’s the size of the market they’re targeting?

EN_B: The report focuses on discrete and analog chips across automotive, industrial, and consumer applications. The combined market is expected to grow from 50 billion dollars in 2025 to 80 billion by 2030. That’s a 10% CAGR. Not as explosive as AI-driven chips, but stable, sizable, and ripe for consolidation.

EN_A: 10% growth is solid. So out of all the candidates, who does the report think is the best fit?

EN_B: Fuji Electric. It has the lowest market cap among the major power semi players, around 2 trillion yen. Denso and Fuji Electric already have a long history working together on IGBT and SiC development. And Fuji Electric has meaningful industrial exposure, which is exactly what Denso needs.

EN_B: For the consortium that includes Rohm, Toshiba, and Mitsubishi Electric, the report thinks Denso could only take a partial stake. A full acquisition would be operationally difficult. And a hostile bid for Rohm would carry too much reputational risk.

EN_B: The report says a full acquisition of any of those would be financially impractical. So Fuji Electric really stands out as the most feasible option.

EN_A: If Denso does buy Fuji Electric, what does that mean for Toyota?

EN_B: For Toyota, cutting the cost of power semiconductors, especially SiC, is critical for improving the profitability of their xEVs. So an acquisition by Denso makes strategic sense. But the funding question is key. If Denso sells its Toyota stake to fund the deal, that could disappoint investors expecting shareholder returns.
