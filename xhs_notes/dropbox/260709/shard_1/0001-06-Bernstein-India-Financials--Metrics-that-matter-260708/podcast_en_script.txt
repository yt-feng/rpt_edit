EN_A: There has been a lot of research coming out on Indian financials lately, but most of it focuses on macro risks. What makes this report different?
EN_B: This report asks a more important question. Instead of just listing risks, it looks at whether bank fundamentals can actually hold up under all that uncertainty. And the answer is yes. They are holding up better than many people feared.
EN_A: So what is the main call here?
EN_B: The main call is that Indian banks are in a phase where resilient fundamentals are clashing with noisy macro sentiment. Credit growth is still strong. Asset quality is stable. Margin pressure is easing. But the market is still focused on geopolitics and foreign outflows.
EN_A: Let us break down the key metrics the report highlights. What is the first one?
EN_B: The first is credit growth. System-wide credit is growing at around 18 percent year on year. And it is broad-based. Industrial lending, services, and secured retail are all contributing. What this means is that domestic financing demand has not collapsed despite external shocks.
EN_A: But everyone is worried about margins, not growth. What does the report say about net interest margins?
EN_B: It says the downside risk to margins is limited. Two reasons. First, the spread between loan yields and deposit rates is widening. Second, the loan-to-deposit ratio is rising. And yes, CD rates spiked during the West Asia conflict, but after the ceasefire, they dropped by more than 100 basis points. That has significantly eased funding costs for banks.
EN_A: What is the second key metric?
EN_B: The second is the performance gap between public sector banks and private sector banks. This is one of the most debated topics right now. PSBs have outperformed in recent quarters, driven by surplus liquidity and very low credit costs. But the report argues that both of those drivers are fading. Private banks are regaining earnings momentum.
EN_A: So does that mean private banks will start outperforming again?
EN_B: The report says convergence, not a sudden reversal. It expects the gap in growth and profitability to narrow gradually over the coming quarters. But it does not give a clear timeline. That is something worth digging into in the full report.

EN_B: For Axis Bank, the big question is about margin trajectory and credit cost sustainability after that sharp rerating. And for Kotak, it is about defending margins while dealing with slower growth and a management transition.
