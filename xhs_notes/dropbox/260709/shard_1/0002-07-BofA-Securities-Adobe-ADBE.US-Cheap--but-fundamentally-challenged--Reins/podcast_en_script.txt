EN_A: Adobe stock has dropped about 70% from its 2024 peak. That is a massive decline for a software giant. What is going on?
EN_B: That is the exact question a new report from a global investment bank tries to answer. They just reinstated coverage on Adobe with an Underperform rating and a 190 dollar price target.
EN_A: 190 dollars? That is below the current price of 218. So they see more downside. What is their main argument?
EN_B: Their core argument is that generative AI is narrowing Adobe’s competitive moat. AI is lowering the barrier for content creation, which invites cheaper, AI-native competitors. They see Adobe’s own AI strategy as largely defensive.
EN_A: Defensive? What does that mean exactly?
EN_B: It means the AI features are mostly used to keep existing users engaged and prevent them from leaving. They are not driving significant new revenue. The report highlights a key number: AI-first annual recurring revenue is still less than 2% of total ARR.
EN_A: Only 2%? That is tiny. But the stock already looks cheap, right? Isn’t that risk priced in?
EN_B: That is exactly the central question of the report. The valuation looks tempting, around 8 times estimated 2027 enterprise value to free cash flow. That is way below its historical range of 25 to 30 times. But the problem is there is no catalyst.
EN_A: No catalyst. So cheap does not mean it will go up.
EN_B: Exactly. The report argues that low valuation alone is not enough to drive outperformance. They need clear evidence of AI monetization and growth reacceleration. And right now, they do not see either.
EN_A: So how slow do they expect growth to get? Are there specific numbers?
EN_B: Yes. They model total revenue growth to slow from 10.5% in 2025 to 8.8% by 2027. So they see a steady deceleration, not a rebound.
EN_A: Does that mean all users are leaving? What about professional designers who rely on Photoshop?
EN_B: Good question. The report makes a critical distinction. They see two groups. One is the professional and enterprise users. These are more resilient because they need pixel-level control and have high switching costs.
EN_A: And the other group?
EN_B: The other group is the much larger base of non-professional and occasional users. For these users, “good enough” AI-generated content can replace their paid workflow. The risk of losing these users is the highest.
EN_A: So it is a pincer movement. The core is stable, but the biggest volume base is at risk.
EN_B: Yes. And it gets worse. AI is also cannibalizing Adobe’s own high-margin legacy businesses. The report specifically calls out Adobe Stock, which is seeing continued revenue decline.
EN_A: Adobe Stock? The asset library? AI-generated images are definitely a threat to that.
EN_B: Exactly. That is the cannibalization risk. The more powerful AI becomes, the less users need to buy stock assets.
EN_A: Besides the business challenges, are there any internal issues at Adobe?
EN_B: The report also flags leadership turnover. The CEO and CFO both left around the same time. In a critical AI transition period, that adds execution risk.
EN_A: A double whammy. Pressure on the business, the finances, and the management team. So what would make this report change its mind? What is the bullish trigger?
EN_B: They are very clear. They need to see concrete evidence that AI is generating incremental revenue and driving a reacceleration in growth. Until then, they see limited upside, even if downside is also limited.
