EN_B: It really comes down to one question: will banks update their full year guidance to reflect the Bank of Japan’s rate hike in June?
EN_A: So you’re saying a lot of banks didn’t have that hike baked into their numbers?
EN_B: Exactly. The data in the report shows that many banks built their forecasts assuming no rate hike at all, or perhaps just one. If a bank moves quickly to raise its guidance with the first quarter results, that would be seen as a strong signal of confidence in their business momentum.
EN_A: That’s interesting. What if a bank doesn’t change its guidance? Is that a bad sign?
EN_B: Not necessarily. If a bank already assumed a mid-year hike in its original guidance, it might not feel the need to adjust. But the key is for those who didn’t assume a hike and still choose not to revise. The market will start asking why.
EN_A: Sounds like a bit of a stress test. So besides guidance changes, what else should we be watching this earnings season?
EN_B: Another big one is share buybacks. Many regional banks didn’t announce any buybacks in May, while the megabanks and Sumitomo Mitsui Trust already did. So the first quarter results could be the moment when regional banks start announcing their buyback plans.
EN_A: Any specific names to watch?
EN_B: The report expects Shizuoka Financial Group and Yokohama Financial Group to announce buybacks worth around 40 billion yen each, which is about a full year’s worth. Meanwhile, Mebuki Financial Group and Chiba Bank might announce buybacks equal to about half their annual forecast.
EN_A: What about profit growth? Is everyone benefiting from higher rates?
EN_B: It’s very mixed. Take MUFG for example. Its guidance already assumed a rate hike, so its first quarter results might be pretty uneventful. But Japan Post Bank could see a nice profit rebound because its international net interest income was weak last year due to a time lag.
EN_A: So that’s a low base effect story. Are there any banks that might actually see profits drop?
EN_B: Yes. Banks like SBI Shinsei, Aozora, and Fukuoka Financial Group had one-off gains or tax benefits in the same quarter last year. Without those, their year-on-year comparisons could look quite weak. Also, Seven Bank’s subsidiary might book an extraordinary loss.
EN_A: Beyond profits and buybacks, what’s the loan growth picture like? Are we seeing accelerating demand in a rising rate environment?
EN_B: That’s a really important point. According to BOJ data, average domestic loan balances grew 5.7% year on year in May 2026. What’s even more striking is that at the major city banks, loan growth to large corporations hit 10% in March and accelerated further to 14% in May. That’s a direct tailwind for net interest income.
EN_A: And what about the bond market? How are changes in yields impacting banks?
EN_B: This is actually a positive story for now. From end-March to end-June, JGB yields rose modestly at the short end and more significantly in the 7 to 10 year zone. At the same time, the TOPIX index surged 14% quarter on quarter. For many banks, the gains on their equity holdings more than offset any losses on their bond portfolios. So overall, valuation gains and losses actually improved.
EN_A: So overall, the tone for this earnings season seems fairly positive?
EN_B: The tone is constructive, but the details will matter a lot. Who revises guidance first, who announces buybacks, and who can actually translate higher rates into real profit growth—that’s what will separate the winners from the losers.
