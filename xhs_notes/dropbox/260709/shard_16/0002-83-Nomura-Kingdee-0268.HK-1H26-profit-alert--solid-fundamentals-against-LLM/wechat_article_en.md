## The Subscription Flywheel Is Finally Generating Operating Leverage, Confirming the Core Thesis

The most important number in the profit alert is not the revenue growth rate. It is the operating cash flow swing from a net outflow of CNY18 million to an inflow of CNY130-150 million. This is the metric that separates a turnaround story from a genuine business transformation. Subscription-based enterprise software is a front-loaded cost business: heavy R&D and sales spending in early years, followed by expanding margins as the customer base renews and upsells. Kingdee has been in the investment phase for years. The 1H26 numbers suggest it is now entering the harvesting phase.

The mechanics are straightforward. Subscription revenue carries high incremental margins because the marginal cost of serving an additional cloud customer is low once the platform is built. As the installed base grows, the fixed cost of the platform is spread over a larger revenue pool. Kingdee’s management explicitly cites “economies of scale of the subscription business” as a driver of profitability. This is not a new insight—it is the standard SaaS model—but it is the first time Kingdee has demonstrated it at scale in a first-half period, which is seasonally weaker than the second half.

The implication for observers is clear. If the subscription flywheel is now generating operating leverage, the profit trajectory should improve with each successive period, barring a macroeconomic shock or a competitive disruption. The 1H26 profit of CNY40-60 million is modest relative to revenue, but it represents an inflection point. The market’s deepest concern about enterprise software companies is that generative AI will render their products obsolete. This concern has weighed on Kingdee’s valuation even as its fundamentals improve. The 1H26 profit alert provides a counterargument.

It suggests that Kingdee is embedding AI into its products, not waiting to be disrupted by them. The company is positioning itself as an “AI native” enterprise software provider, meaning that AI capabilities are being built into the core architecture rather than added as a bolt-on feature.

The strategic logic is compelling. Enterprise resource planning is not just about automation; it is about data integrity, compliance, audit trails, and integration across business functions. A general-purpose large language model cannot replace a system that manages procurement, inventory, payroll, and financial reporting in a single, auditable framework. What AI can do is make those systems more intelligent—reducing manual data entry, flagging anomalies, and generating insights from transaction data. Kingdee appears to be pursuing this path.

The second-order implication is that AI may actually accelerate Kingdee’s revenue growth rather than cannibalize it. If AI-native products command higher average selling prices or reduce customer churn, the top-line compounding effect could be significant. The risk is not that AI destroys the ERP market, but that Kingdee fails to execute on its AI strategy as well as competitors do. That is a company-specific risk, not an existential one.

## The Profit Alert Does Not Fully Address the Competitive Threat from Large Language Model Companies

For all the positive signals in the 1H26 results, one critical question remains unanswered: what happens when major AI platform companies decide to build their own enterprise software suites? The market’s fear is not that a startup will disrupt Kingdee. It is that a company like OpenAI, or a Chinese equivalent, will launch a competing product that integrates seamlessly with its own AI models and undercuts traditional ERP vendors on price.

Kingdee’s profit alert does not address this scenario directly. It highlights AI-native product growth and AI-driven efficiency, but it does not quantify the competitive moat or explain why a large language model company cannot replicate its functionality. This is a legitimate analytical gap. The report acknowledges that “LLM headwinds might linger in the near term,” which is a diplomatic way of saying that the market remains unconvinced.

The unresolved question is whether Kingdee’s competitive advantage is structural or temporary. Structural advantages include switching costs, data network effects, and regulatory compliance requirements. Temporary advantages include first-mover status in cloud migration or a lead in AI integration that competitors can close. The profit alert provides evidence that Kingdee is executing well, but it does not prove that its position is defensible against a well-funded entrant with a superior AI platform.

Observers need to form their own view on this question. The bull case is that enterprise software is sticky and complex, and that AI companies will struggle to replicate the decades of workflow optimization embedded in Kingdee’s products. The bear case is that AI models will eventually make workflow configuration trivial, reducing switching costs to near zero. The truth probably lies somewhere in between, and the outcome will depend on how quickly Kingdee can embed AI into its products in a way that raises switching costs rather than lowering them.

## A Decision Framework for Evaluating Kingdee’s Prospects in the AI Era

Given the uncertainty around AI disruption, observers need a structured approach to evaluating Kingdee’s prospects. The following framework focuses on the key variables that will determine whether the company can sustain its profit inflection and justify a higher valuation.

If growth is driven primarily by existing customers migrating from on-premise to cloud and increasing their subscription spend, the trajectory is sustainable. If growth is coming from new customer acquisition in the small and medium business segment, the unit economics need to be scrutinized. Small customers tend to have higher churn rates and lower lifetime value. The profit alert does not break down revenue by customer segment, but this is a critical detail for observers to monitor.

Second, measure the pace of AI monetization. Kingdee’s AI-native products are growing strongly, but from a small base. Observers should look for evidence that AI features are driving higher average revenue per user or reducing customer acquisition costs. If AI capabilities are being given away for free to drive adoption, the near-term revenue impact will be limited. If they are being sold as premium add-ons, the margin expansion story gains credibility.

Third, assess the competitive response. The most important competitive data point will not come from Kingdee’s own earnings calls. It will come from the product roadmaps of large language model companies and global ERP competitors. If major AI platforms launch enterprise application suites within the next 12-18 months, the market’s fear will be validated. If they do not, or if they partner with ERP vendors rather than compete with them, the current discount in Kingdee’s valuation will prove unwarranted.

Fourth, watch the operating cash flow trajectory. The 1H26 cash flow improvement is the strongest signal in the profit alert. If Kingdee can sustain positive operating cash flow through the seasonally weaker first half of the year, the business model is fundamentally sound. A deterioration in cash flow in the second half would raise questions about the quality of the profit improvement.
