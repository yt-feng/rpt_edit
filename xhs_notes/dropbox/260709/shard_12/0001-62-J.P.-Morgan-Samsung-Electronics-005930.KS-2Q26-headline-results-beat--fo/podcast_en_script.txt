EN_B: The report says no, not yet. It makes a bold call: memory supply is only meeting 50 to 60 percent of procurement demand. That gap is likely to widen because hyperscalers are ramping up enterprise SSD orders for AI workloads.
EN_A: So what does that mean for investors? Why should they care?
EN_B: It means earnings estimates still have room to go up. Samsung is trading at just 5.2 times forward earnings, the cheapest among global memory stocks. The market is pricing in very little of the structural shift, like multi-year shortages and growing long-term agreements. The report argues investors are underestimating how long this upcycle can last.
EN_A: What about the other parts of Samsung, like foundry? Any good news there?

Second, the report mentions that customer pushback due to rising costs could emerge in the second half, but server demand may offset that. The full report dives much deeper into these two issues.
