ZH_A: 今天我们要聊一份来自某外资投行的全球消费策略报告，标题叫“消费者：要精挑细选”。这份报告到底想告诉我们什么？

ZH_B: 核心就一句话：全球消费板块不是一刀切的悲观，而是分化严重。欧洲消费者面临多重逆风，但中国消费和奢侈品有亮点，而低端美国消费者和汽车板块我们比较谨慎。

ZH_A: 听起来像是一份“选股指南”。那我们先从欧洲消费者说起吧，报告里列了不少逆风因素。

ZH_B: 对，报告提到几个关键点。首先是实际工资增长可能转负，因为通胀预期会升到3.4%左右，而工资增长只有2.3%。这意味着消费者购买力在缩水。

ZH_A: 但这并不是唯一的问题吧？

ZH_B: 没错。还有商品价格的压力。即使中东冲突明天结束，天然气价格预计到2026年第四季度仍比冲突前高120%，油价也高40%。这些都会直接冲击消费。

ZH_A: 那有什么抵消因素吗？比如政府补贴或者储蓄？

ZH_B: 报告提到欧洲家庭有大约相当于GDP 10%的超额储蓄，但和2022、2023年相比，现在的财政补贴非常小。而且就业增长也在放缓，欧元区第一季度就业环比只增0.1%，英国甚至是负增长。

ZH_A: 所以整体来看，欧洲消费可能比冲突前的预期低1%以上？

ZH_B: 是的，报告认为这个数字是相当可行的。不过，他们特别指出西班牙是个例外，因为西班牙就业增长强劲，房地产也在回暖，而且能源结构更依赖核能和可再生能源，受油价冲击较小。

ZH_A: 那我们转向亚洲。报告对中国消费似乎比较乐观？

ZH_B: 是的。报告认为中国消费有三大支撑。第一，中国房地产占家庭财富约60%，而某投行对房地产已经转向更积极。第二，工资增长可能从2%加速到4%，通缩也在结束。第三，长期看，中国消费占GDP的比重必须上升。

ZH_A: 所以这意味着什么？哪些板块值得关注？

ZH_B: 报告建议关注与中国消费相关的奢侈品和本土品牌，比如某国际美妆巨头有17%的销售来自中国。另外，奢侈品板块整体也被看好，因为中国消费者贡献了26%的奢侈品支出，而且估值已经比历史均值便宜了。

ZH_A: 说到估值，报告里提到很多板块已经跌得很便宜了。比如家庭用品板块，全球范围内已经比正常市盈率低了2.5个标准差。

ZH_B: 对，报告认为市场可能过度反应了。家庭用品板块的估值已经异常便宜，但报告表示“我们很难理解为什么它们被认为比其他板块更容易被颠覆”。这里提到的“颠覆”包括新一代消费者、减肥药，以及最重要的——AI代理对非知名品牌的去品牌化影响。

ZH_A: 那有没有什么板块是明确建议回避的？

ZH_B: 有，汽车板块是明确的低配建议。报告指出，汽车板块的市盈率并不便宜，而且面临巨大的结构性压力，比如来自中国的竞争、需要在燃油车和电动车双重投资，以及电动车新进入者的成本优势。

ZH_A: 听起来汽车板块的前景确实不太乐观。那低端美国消费者呢？

ZH_B: 报告也比较谨慎。实际工资增长可能转负，超额储蓄基本用完，而且零售板块的盈利势头正在放缓。不过，高端消费者受益于股市财富效应，因为最富有的20%人群拥有83%的股票。

ZH_A: 最后，报告有没有留下一些悬念，让读者想去看完整版？

ZH_B: 有两个问题值得继续深挖。第一，报告提到家庭用品板块被严重低估，但“为什么市场认为它们比其他板块更易被颠覆”这个谜题并没有完全解开。第二，关于奢侈品板块，报告提到盈利预期非常保守，但“这种保守是否会在未来几个季度被打破”仍需要验证。

EN_A: So today we are digging into a global equity strategy report from a major investment bank, titled “The Consumer: Be Selective.” What is the big takeaway here?

EN_B: The main idea is that consumer stocks are not all in the same boat. European consumers face real headwinds, but there are bright spots in China and luxury goods. On the flip side, we are cautious on low-end US consumers and the auto sector.

EN_A: Let’s start with Europe. The report lists several headwinds. What are the biggest ones?

EN_B: First, real wage growth could turn negative. The bank forecasts inflation rising to 3.4 percent, but wages are only growing at around 2.3 percent. That directly hurts purchasing power.

EN_A: And there is more than just wages, right?

EN_B: Absolutely. Even if the Middle East conflict ends tomorrow, gas prices are expected to stay 120 percent above pre-conflict levels by late 2026. Oil is also 40 percent higher. Each 10 percent rise in oil knocks about 0.2 percent off consumption.

EN_A: Are there any offsets? Like government support or savings?

EN_B: Yes, European households still have excess savings worth about 10 percent of GDP. But fiscal gifts to households are tiny compared to 2022 and 2023. Also, employment growth is fading. In the Eurozone, it was just 0.1 percent quarter-on-quarter in Q1, and the UK is actually negative.

EN_B: So overall, consumption could be 1 percent or more lower than pre-conflict estimates. But there is one exception: Spain. Employment there is up 2.3 percent year-on-year, house prices are rising 13 percent, and its energy mix is more resilient to higher oil and gas.

EN_A: Let’s move to China. The report seems more optimistic there.

EN_B: Yes. The bank has turned more positive on Chinese property, which accounts for about 60 percent of household wealth. Also, wage growth might accelerate from 2 percent to 4 percent, and deflation is ending. In the long run, China’s consumer share of GDP has to rise.

EN_A: So what does that mean for specific sectors?

EN_B: They recommend looking at luxury goods and Chinese domestic brands. For example, one major cosmetics company gets 17 percent of its sales from China. Luxury as a whole is also interesting because 26 percent of global luxury spending comes from China, and valuations are cheap relative to history.

EN_A: Speaking of cheap valuations, the report points out that household products are extremely oversold globally.

EN_B: That is right. The sector is 2.5 standard deviations cheap on a price-to-earnings basis. The report says they struggle to understand why the market thinks these companies are more disrupted than others. The disruptions include Gen Z trends, GLP-1 drugs, and AI agents de-branding non-conspicuous brands.

EN_A: So there is a potential opportunity there. But what about sectors to avoid?

EN_B: Autos are a clear underweight. The P/E is not cheap, and the sector faces huge structural pressures. Chinese competition, the need to invest in both internal combustion and electric vehicles, and new entrants with a 60 percent cost advantage in EVs.

EN_A: And what about US consumers?

EN_B: The low-end US consumer faces substantial headwinds. Real wage growth could turn negative, excess savings are largely used up, and retail earnings momentum is rolling over. But the high-end consumer benefits from a wealth effect, since the richest 20 percent own 83 percent of equities.

EN_B: Two things. First, why does the market think household products are more disrupted than other sectors? The report does not fully answer that. Second, for luxury, the earnings estimates are very conservative. Whether those estimates will be revised up is something the full report explores further.
