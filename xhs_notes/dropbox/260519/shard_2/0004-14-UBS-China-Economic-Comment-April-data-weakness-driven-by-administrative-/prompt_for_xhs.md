# China Economic Comment

# April data weakness driven by administrative policy

April data surprised significantly on the downside. Administrative policy tightening was likely the key driver. Administrative loosening to reverse the slowdown is expected.

April activity data surprised significantly on the downside.

This year is the start of the 5 Year plan, having a strong start is a key policy goal. There were likely some front-loading activities in 1Q, especially in terms of state-led investments. After the strong start of the year, policy makers became more confident about the economic outlook. Administrative policy stance turned effective less supportive with greater emphasis on (1) not setting over ambitious economic goals and reaching them by making wasteful mega investment projects which tend to be debt intensive, and (2) over report data when these overly ambitious goals could not be achieved. At the same time, officials were told not to “sit idle” either. Therefore, the incentive facing officials on the ground is to “do something but not too much”. But the actual penalties imposed on officials recently have been tilted towards those who had wrongdoings, instead of no actions. This can potentially explain why despite the PBOC’s significant loosening of interbank liquidity, credit growth has been weak.

This mechanism is the main reason why in recent years we often have an intra-year mini cycle, with strong 1Q, weaker 2Q, then policy turns sometime in 3Q, then 4Q rebounds. Market forecasts on annual growth tends to go up after 1Q data releases, go down after 2Q releases and then go back up towards the end of the year. Whether we see a repeat of this remains a question. An immediate adjustment to the tightening overshooting is needed. One good thing compared to earlier years is the PBOC has been keeping liquidity conditions very loose since April, in light of weak credit growth. The magnitude and breath of April slowdown is so significant that we expect administrative policy to respond without the need to wait for the next senior level regular policy meeting. The difficulty with this kind of policy change is it is hard to gauge and impossible to quantify an ante. Even with a reversal in policy stance, weakness in April suggests Q2 GDP growth is likely under pressure.

Data anomalies will likely pose additional difficulties as it was the case with FAI data in 2H2025. In general, data under GDP accounting (e.g. Gross Capital Formation under GDP instead of Fixed Asset Investment Completed; Consumption under GDP instead of Retail Sales), foreign trade, and money and credit are of relatively higher quality. Micro data such as the units of specific products are of particular importance to gauge the underlying momentum.

We don't expect higher inflation to be a hurdle for policy adjustment for two reasons: (1) CPI is way more important than PPI and it remains low despite the rise in energy price; (2) the level of weakness in April is such that if no actions were taken the lower bound of the annual GDP target can be missed. GDP is by far the most bidding economic target, especially at the start of a 5-Year Plan period.

Higher oil price and the scaling back of consumption subsidy program also made a negative contribution but they come only secondary to the impacts of administrative changes. But they are mostly relevant for retail data and cannot explain the broad data weakness.
