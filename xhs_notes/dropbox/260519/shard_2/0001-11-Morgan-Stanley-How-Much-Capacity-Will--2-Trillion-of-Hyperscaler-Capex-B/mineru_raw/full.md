# Executive Summary (Continued)

We detail 4 main debates and takeaways:

Debate 1: How much capacity will the hyperscalers (AMZN, GOOGL, META, MSFT) bring online in '26/'27?

Our View: We see AMZN, GOOGL, META, and MSFT adding an incremental 14 GW/20 GW in '26/'27...for context, we believe AWS's total capacity added over its first \~18 years of existence (through YE24), was only \~5 GW...and 20 GW is enough to power 15 million+ US homes for a year...so capacity is coming

We see GOOGL (\~7 GW) again bringing on the most capacity in '27, followed by AMZN/MSFT (\~5 GW each). META's capex is only adding 3.5 GW, but when grossing up its hyperscale spend, we estimate META is really adding \~4 GW in '27. META doesn't have a hyperscale business, which means this level of investment speaks to the importance for META to productize its investment and drive incremental durable revenue.

Debate 2: Where is the cost and compute efficiency gap between NVDA and Custom ASICs now and what are we monitoring to close that gap?

Our View: We estimate hyperscaler capex to build a 1 GW datacenter with current-gen NVDA GPUs (Blackwell) is up to \~2x the cost of current-gen custom ASICs (TPU, Trainium)...with the server/rack cost driving the biggest delta...but compute power efficiency matters, and this is where NVDA shines...with compute performance/watt 2x-8x ahead of custom ASICs. For the custom ASICs, we look for system-level perf/watt improvements via networking, high-bandwidth memory capacity, and software optimizations. In effect, cost/token/watt advances will matter from here.

# But Compute Power Efficiency Matters, and This is Where NVDA Shines...with Compute Performance/Watt 2x-8x Ahead of Custom ASICs

- Compute power efficiency matters, and NVDA's FLOPs per Chip Divided by Watt Consumption is anywhere from 2x-8x ahead of current-gen, custom ASICs   
- GPU performance is measured in FLOPs (floating point operations per second). For example, a GPU capable at 1 teraFLOPs can execute one trillion operations per second, determining how quickly it can process the matrix math that underlies AI training and inference   
- We look for system-level performance/watt to improve by networking, memory bandwidth and software optimizations, but raw silicon remains key in increasing performance/watt

# What are We Focused on for Custom ASICs to Bridge the Performance Gap?

- Beyond the chips, we see HBM capacity playing an increasing role in AI inference economics given the need to move data efficiently across chips   
- Mixture of expert models dictate higher bandwidth given the need to rapidly route between multiple expert models while also storing increasingly large KV caches for inference workloads   
- NVDA historically the leader here with 1) more HBM capacity on-chip (current-gen GB300 matches next-gen TPUv8 in HBM3e capacity) and 2) Better pricing and allocation from the HBM ecosystem relative to custom ASICs   
- TPU + Trainium: For TPU + Trainium, GCP/AWS control the whole stack, and therefore we are looking for system-level perf/watt improvements via networking, high-bandwidth memory capacity, and software optimizations to close the gap with NVDA
