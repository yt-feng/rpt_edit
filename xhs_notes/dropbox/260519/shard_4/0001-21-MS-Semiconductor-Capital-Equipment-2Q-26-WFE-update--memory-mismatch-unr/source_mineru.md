# 2Q'26 WFE update, memory mismatch unresolved, LAM up to OW, AMAT down to EW

We revise up our 2026/27 WFE market forecast to +27%/+28% and introduce 2028 (+13%). We're positive about NAND and Intel into 2027. MKSI to Top Pick.

What's Changed? We revise up our 2026 forecast from \$143bn (+23% y/y) to \$149bn (+27%) and our 2027 forecast comes up from \$182bn (+27%) to \$191bn (+28%). For 2026, we revise up DRAM/foundry logic by \$3bn/\$2bn, while for 2027 we revise up DRAM/NAND by \$5bn/\$4bn. We introduce our 2028 WFE forecast of \$215bn (+13% y/y). We model 18% growth in foundry logic and 6% growth in memory. Our foundry logic growth forecast is led by a broadening of spenders, while in memory we would look to adjust our estimates as we gain greater visibility into project scale. Overall, we believe the WFE cycle has longevity, with memory clean room availability constraining some near-term upside while extending the medium-term opportunity, alongside a broadening of leading-edge logic spending.

We have 4 key takeaways this quarter: 1) DRAM: Our 2027 DRAM WFE forecast of \$57bn supports 31% bit growth, but with unconstrained bit demand at 40%+, we do not think the supply/demand imbalance is resolved. 2) NAND: We expect NAND WFE to be the fastest-growing end-market in 2027, up 52% y/y, as greenfield activity accelerates across key producers. Similar to DRAM, we see unconstrained bit demand at 40%+, vs our 2027 bit supply growth forecast of 32%. 3) Intel: We see capex increases as inevitable, even in a scenario where Intel remains an IDM. We estimate that 5mn incremental Xeon 6s would require \$5.3bn+ of WFE, or a minimum of \$24bn if Intel aims to capture two-thirds of the incremental 33mn-unit CPU TAM by 2030. 4) Packaging: We forecast packaging to grow ahead of WFE this year, at 41% vs 27%. However, we see the strongest value proposition at the front end, benefiting AMAT, LAM, KLA, and TEL, as front-end packaging grows 50% y/y and increases to 50% of the packaging TAM. Our broader 2027 WFE pecking order is NAND > non-TSMC foundry > TSMC > DRAM.

LAM up to OW, AMAT down to EW. We've preferred DRAM WFE exposure over NAND WFE given the relative revisions, and hence have preferred AMAT over LAM. The magnitude of our DRAM WFE revisions have narrowed, we're more positive on NAND WFE revisions from here. We revise up our CY27 forecast for LAM from \$34.6bn/\$9.46 to \$35.4bn/\$9.71 as we now model 59% NAND systems growth, surpassing prior peak in 2021. We had valued LAM at 31x vs AMAT at 28x, approx. a 10% premium to reflect our confidence on AMAT's 2026 share gains. Given our confidence on LAM's 2027 share gains, we raise that premium to 20%, a touch-above the 3-year average of 16%, which brings up our target multiple to 34x and raises our price target from \$293 to \$331 implying 16% upside.

# Logic: Broadening of spenders

We fine tune our 2026-27 WFE forecasts from \$85.4bn/\$109.4bn to \$87.6bn/\$109.0bn. As we think about further revisions from here, we think upside is skewed towards 2 areas:

- Intel: We think there is an inevitability towards Intel raising capex on the back of CPU demand, and we see further revisions down the line if the company captures a material foundry customer or sees some sort of development with TeraFab.   
- Analog/MCU: Analog/MCU companies spoke of increasing utilization rates across the board at their MarQ reports. NXP spoke of front-end utilization coming up from the low 80s in the 1H to mid 80s in the 2H, ON spoke about manufacturing utilization increased sequentially to $77\%$ and expecting Q2 flat to up slightly, and Infineon mentioned 300mm fabs really have a high capacity utilization. However, we do believe we can start modelling positive y/y growth across non-China ICAPS.

We aren't quite ready to put that upside fully into numbers, but we think risk is skewed to the upside.

# Thought #1: NAND WFE

Our View: We think NAND WFE surpasses historic highs in 2027 on the back of increased clean room allocation and lithography constraints for DRAM.

The Numbers: We raise our 2027 NAND WFE forecast from \$19.7bn to \$24.0bn, above prior high of \$19.1bn in 2021.

NAND WFE has continued to stay low. NAND is a cyclical industry, and past cycles have always seen spending resume at some point. Our view is predicated on history and bit growth, and see spending reaccelerating, with the debate being “When” not “If”. However, NAND WFE shipments have tracked at historic lows during Q3 25 to Q1 26, and our thesis has been slow to play out for 3 reasons:

- Clean room availability: DRAM WFE has accelerated from strength to strength as Samsung and Hynix have allocated all incremental clean room towards DRAM as well as shifting clean room initially allocated from NAND to DRAM.   
- Capital Discipline: Samsung, Hynix and Micron have shifted their capex budgets away from DRAM and towards NAND, while Kioxia has been more capital disciplined, leaving just YMTC to add NAND wafers.   
- Bit Demand: We estimate node upgrades have been able to satisfy high-teens bit growth, which is what the demand profile of the industry has been during 2023-2025.
