# China’s High-End Spirits Market Is Sending a Clear Signal: Pricing Power Is Shifting From Brand to Execution

The conventional wisdom in China’s baijiu market has long held that the strongest brand wins the pricing game. That assumption is being quietly overturned. Recent channel data from East China reveals that Feitian Moutai has maintained wholesale price resilience at RMB 1,640–1,680 per bottle, with channel inventory dropping to roughly 15 days in one province—down from 20–25 days in April. Meanwhile, Wuliangye and Guojiao 1573 prices have remained flat. This divergence is not random. It reflects a structural shift in how pricing power is being earned and sustained in the high-end spirits sector.

The argument is not that Moutai’s brand has weakened. It is that brand alone is no longer sufficient. The ability to manage distribution, control digital supply, and calibrate channel incentives has become the decisive variable. The report from a global investment bank shows that Moutai has completed 48% of its full-year target by May, with May and June quotas accounting for a mid-to-high single-digit percentage of the annual plan. Prepayments are on track. Sell-through demand has increased year-on-year, supported by a favorable comparison base. But beneath these headline numbers lies a more nuanced story about how the industry’s leaders are navigating a demand environment where business banquets have stalled and government-related consumption has declined significantly.

The timing matters. We are entering the Dragon Boat Festival window, traditionally a period of restocking and price validation. The data from the past two weeks shows that original case Feitian Moutai wholesale prices rose by RMB 15 per bottle, while unpacked bottles increased by RMB 10. Non-standard SKUs such as Jingpin Moutai, Moutai 15 years, and 1-litre Feitian Moutai saw even larger gains of RMB 40, 50, and 80 per bottle respectively. These are not random fluctuations. They are the result of deliberate operational choices—specifically, a 40% reduction in i-Moutai supply during May and a shift from case sales to unpacked bottle allocations. The platform’s monthly active users dropped to 10 million in April, down from 12 million in March, but daily active users held at around 1.1 million. The implication is clear: Moutai is using its digital platform not as a volume channel, but as a price-smoothing mechanism.

This article interprets the most recent channel checks, distills the strategic logic behind the numbers, and identifies what the data does not yet tell us. The goal is to help serious investors and industry observers move beyond the weekly price ticker and toward a decision framework that separates signal from noise.

## Moutai’s Digital Supply Management Is Now the Primary Pricing Lever, Not Brand Premium Alone

The most underappreciated development in the high-end baijiu market is the transformation of i-Moutai from a sales platform into a pricing tool. The report indicates that i-Moutai supply was cut by approximately 40% in May compared to the first quarter. At the same time, the platform shifted its allocation from original cases to unpacked bottles, typically two to four bottles per transaction. This has tightened supply for original cases, which are the preferred format for gift-giving and speculative storage, while maintaining availability for end consumers.

The result is a bifurcated price dynamic. Original case Feitian Moutai has firmed up, while unpacked bottles have followed a slightly different trajectory. The wholesale price gap between the two formats has narrowed, but the direction is upward for both. This is not accidental. It reflects a deliberate strategy to prevent the kind of price volatility that plagued the market in previous years, when large case allocations led to aggressive discounting by distributors needing to move volume.

The strategic logic is twofold. First, by controlling the mix of case versus bottle sales, Moutai can influence which demand segments it serves. Cases are more sensitive to speculative and investment demand; bottles are more tied to actual consumption. Second, by reducing overall digital supply during a seasonally weaker month, Moutai signals to the market that it will not chase volume at the expense of price. This is a marked departure from the approach of some competitors, who have relied on higher rebates and lower nominal prices to maintain distributor engagement.

The implication for investors is that Moutai’s pricing resilience is no longer a given of brand heritage. It is being actively engineered through operational decisions that require precision and discipline. The question is whether this approach is scalable across different demand cycles, or whether it works only in a market where inventory is already lean.

## The Demand Landscape Is Splitting: Residential Banquets Are Growing, But Business and Government Segments Are Contracting

The report provides a granular breakdown of sell-through demand by consumption scenario, and the picture is more complex than a simple recovery narrative. Residential banquet demand grew by a teens percentage year-on-year, supported by a longer holiday period this year. This is the bright spot. But business banquet demand has almost stalled, and government-related consumption has decreased significantly.

This divergence matters because the high-end spirits market has historically been driven by two engines: gifting and business entertainment. Gifting remains dominated by Feitian Moutai, with non-standard SKUs tied more heavily to business banquets—over 70% of non-standard Feitian consumption is linked to that segment. If business banquets remain weak, the growth in non-standard SKU prices may be more fragile than the recent data suggests.

The report notes that non-standard SKU prices have risen, with Jingpin Moutai, Moutai 15 years, and 1-litre Feitian all posting gains. But the consumption base for these products is narrower. A recovery in business banquets would provide a stronger foundation for these price increases to persist. Without it, the current price gains may reflect temporary supply constraints rather than sustainable demand improvement.

For Wuliangye and Luzhou Laojiao, the situation is more challenging. Common Wuliangye wholesale prices have remained flat at RMB 840 per bottle, with no upward momentum. Guojiao 1573 has also held steady at RMB 840. These brands lack the digital distribution platform that Moutai uses to fine-tune supply, and they face a demand environment where their core consumption scenarios—business and government—are under pressure.

The strategic takeaway is that the high-end market is not recovering uniformly. The segment that is growing—residential banquets—tends to be more price-sensitive and less brand-loyal. The segments that are shrinking—business and government—are where premium pricing is most easily sustained. This creates a structural headwind for any brand that cannot offset the decline in those channels with either digital control or international expansion.

## Wuliangye’s Capital Allocation Strategy Is a Counterpoint to Moutai’s Operational Focus

The Wuliangye AGM provided several important signals that should not be overlooked. Management reiterated its target of delivering double-digit growth for 2026, with a long-term strategy focused on core brands. Specifically, 1618, 39-degree Wuliangye, and Wuliangchun are each aiming for RMB 10 billion in annual sales. More notably, management reinforced its commitment to RMB 8–10 billion in share buybacks within 12 months, with full cancellation, alongside RMB 20 billion-plus in annual dividends for 2025 to be completed within two months. The company also announced plans to formulate a new three-year shareholder return plan for 2027–2029.

This is a fundamentally different approach to value creation than what Moutai is pursuing. Moutai is focusing on operational precision—managing digital supply, adjusting channel incentives, and using price increases on non-standard SKUs to improve channel profitability. Wuliangye is focusing on capital allocation—buybacks, dividends, and a clear shareholder return framework.

Neither approach is inherently superior, but they imply different risk profiles. Moutai’s strategy depends on continued execution in a fragmented distribution system. Wuliangye’s strategy depends on generating enough free cash flow to sustain buybacks and dividends while also investing in brand building. The risk for Wuliangye is that if demand weakens further, the cash flow required to maintain shareholder returns could come under pressure. The risk for Moutai is that operational precision works in a stable environment but may not be enough to counteract a more severe demand downturn.

The report also notes that Wuliangye’s nominal prepayment price for Common Wuliangye was lowered to RMB 900 for 2026, with distributor costs below RMB 850. This is a pragmatic adjustment, but it also signals that the brand’s pricing power is not what it once was. The flat wholesale price trend for Wuliangye and Guojiao 1573 suggests that the market is not yet convinced that demand will rebound strongly enough to support price increases.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Sustainability of Non-Standard SKU Pricing and the Role of Inventory Speculation

The report provides extensive data on wholesale prices, inventory levels, and channel dynamics. But it leaves several important questions unresolved. First, the recent price increases in non-standard SKUs such as Jingpin Moutai, Moutai 15 years, and 1-litre Feitian may be driven in part by supply constraints rather than genuine demand growth. The report notes that i-Moutai supply was cut by 40% in May. If this reduction is temporary, and supply normalizes in June, the price gains could reverse.

Second, the report does not fully address the role of inventory speculation. Distributor inventory in East China is low, with products selling within 10 days of shipment in some regions. But low inventory can also be a sign of caution, not strength. Distributors may be keeping inventory lean because they are uncertain about future demand, not because they cannot get enough product. The report indicates that 48% of the full-year target has been completed, but the remaining 52% will need to be sold in a demand environment where business banquets are stalled and government consumption is declining.

Third, the report highlights that Moutai 1935 (Premium Edition) has become the first sauce-flavor product to adopt a consignment model, priced at RMB 998 per bottle with tiered incentives for distributors. This is a significant development. Consignment models shift inventory risk from distributors back to the manufacturer. If this model is expanded, it could fundamentally change the economics of distribution for Moutai and its peers. But the report does not provide enough data to assess whether the consignment model is working or whether it is a sign of weakness in the mid-tier segment.

Finally, the report notes that Moutai adjusted the opening hours of offline stores and shifted the daily product release on i-Moutai to 8 p.m. from 9 a.m. This may seem like a minor operational change, but it could have significant implications for pricing dynamics. An evening release may attract a different user demographic and could reduce the speculative arbitrage that occurs when prices are set during trading hours. The full impact of this change will take several weeks to assess.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: What to Watch in the Next 60 Days

Rather than attempting to predict the next price move, investors should focus on a set of observable indicators that will determine whether the current pricing resilience is sustainable.

First, watch the i-Moutai supply trajectory. If the platform restores supply to first-quarter levels in June, and wholesale prices hold, that would be a strong signal that demand is genuine. If prices decline on increased supply, the recent gains were likely supply-driven.

Second, monitor the Dragon Boat Festival sell-through. This is the first major consumption event after the Labor Day holiday. The report indicates that May and June quotas account for a mid-to-high single-digit percentage of the full-year target. Strong sell-through during this period would support the case that residential banquet demand is sufficient to offset weakness in business and government channels.

Third, track the adoption of the consignment model for non-standard SKUs and Moutai 1935. If more distributors opt into consignment, it would indicate that they are unwilling to take inventory risk. That would be a bearish signal. If consignment remains limited, it would suggest that distributors are confident in their ability to sell through allocated product.

Fourth, observe Wuliangye’s share buyback execution. The company has committed to RMB 8–10 billion in buybacks within 12 months. If Wuliangye follows through aggressively, it could provide a floor for the stock even if pricing remains soft. If the buyback is delayed or scaled back, it would raise questions about management’s confidence in the outlook.

Fifth, pay attention to the government-related demand trend. The report notes that government consumption decreased significantly. This is unlikely to reverse quickly. If business banquets also remain weak, the high-end market will become increasingly dependent on residential demand, which has lower pricing power. Investors should track any policy signals that could stimulate business activity or government spending.
