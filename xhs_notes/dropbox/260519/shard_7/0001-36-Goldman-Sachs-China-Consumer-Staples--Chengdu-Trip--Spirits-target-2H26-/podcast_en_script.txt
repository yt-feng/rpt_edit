EN_B: It’s mostly about the base effect. Many baijiu companies deliberately stopped shipments in the third quarter of 2025 to clear channel inventory. EN_A: What does weak banquet demand actually mean for these companies?
EN_B: It means consumers are trading down. In Sichuan, the report found that residential banquet spending is subdued, and people are choosing cheaper options. To keep volumes up, companies are increasing subsidies for banquet events. That’s essentially buying market share with lower prices, which puts pressure on margins.
EN_A: So what are the companies doing to cope?
EN_B: Two things stand out. First, they are being much more disciplined on the supply side, controlling base liquor production and capacity utilization. Second, they are focusing on operational efficiency, cutting staff and controlling expenses. EN_A: It sounds like they are focusing on internal strength. Now, switching to snack discount stores. The report describes a fast-growing triopoly. Who are the three players?
EN_B: In the southwest region, the leader is a local chain called Yummy Snacks. It has extremely high store density in Chengdu. In one county we visited, stores were only 300 to 400 meters apart. The other two are national players, Wanchen and Busy Ming.
EN_A: With such dense competition, how are their economics?
EN_B: Surprisingly good. For the national brand in Sichuan, the report shows a gross margin of around 20% and an operating margin in the high single digits. And interestingly, stores in lower-tier counties and towns actually have higher margins because rents are much cheaper.
EN_A: That goes against the common wisdom that lower-tier markets have thinner margins. So how much room for growth is left?
EN_B: The report sees significant white space. Currently, these brands have only penetrated one-third to one-half of towns with a population of around 30,000. They are taking market share from fragmented traditional trade, the small mom-and-pop stores. And the discount stores are also creating new demand, for example, by opening near university campuses.
EN_A: Let’s move to beverages. The report focuses on Uni-President’s POS optimization. What does that mean in practice?
EN_B: It’s about managing the productivity of every single retail outlet. In the southwest, Uni-President directly covers over 300,000 points of sale. But the report found that newly developed high-potential outlets generate 2 to 3 times the average sales per store.
EN_A: How do they identify these high-potential outlets?
EN_B: The report highlights two strategies. One is scenario expansion, like penetrating restaurants, billiard halls, internet cafes, and even pharmacies and optical stores. The other is using smart vending machines to create new sales points. In one urban-rural commercial hub they visited, optimizing the consumption scenario boosted monthly sales by over 30%.
