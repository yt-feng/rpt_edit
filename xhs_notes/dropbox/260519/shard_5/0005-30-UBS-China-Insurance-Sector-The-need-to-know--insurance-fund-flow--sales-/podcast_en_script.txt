EN_A: So, what was the biggest takeaway for you from this insurance sector report?
EN_B: The most striking thing was that insurers actually increased their equity exposure in the first quarter, even though the stock market was pretty weak.
EN_A: That sounds counterintuitive. When the market is down, don't most investors try to cut risk?
EN_B: Exactly. And that is the most interesting puzzle the report tries to solve. It suggests institutions were actively bottom-fishing.
EN_A: So what is the core question this report is trying to answer?
EN_B: It is really about a central contradiction. Premium income is still growing steadily, but the stock market volatility is causing fair value losses on the investment side. How are insurers actually rebalancing their portfolios?
EN_A: What were the actual numbers?
EN_B: Total insurance fund assets under management reached 39.4 trillion RMB in the first quarter of 2026, up 2.5 percent quarter on quarter.
EN_A: What was driving that growth?
EN_B: Solid premium growth provided the cash inflow, up 6.2 percent year on year. But on the other side, mark-to-market losses on both equities and bonds ate into that growth.
EN_A: What was the biggest shift in asset allocation?
EN_B: The allocation to stocks and securities investment funds rose to 16 percent, up 0.1 percentage points quarter on quarter. And long-term equity investments also ticked up.
EN_A: What does that mean in practice?
EN_B: Insurers were using the market dip to add to their equity holdings. They are also shifting their product mix toward participating products, which are more equity-sensitive.
EN_A: What about bonds?
EN_B: Bond allocation also edged up to 51 percent. Insurers took advantage of the rise in long-dated bond yields to lock in higher rates.
EN_A: And what got cut?
EN_B: Bank deposits and other non-standard debt assets continued to decline. As higher-yielding assets mature, there just are not enough good alternatives to replace them.
EN_A: Let us talk about the sales side. What is happening with new premiums?
EN_B: Year-on-year growth for first-year regular premiums has moderated in the second quarter so far. But that was widely expected.
EN_A: Why was it expected?
EN_B: The first quarter saw a big wave of bank deposit maturities flowing into insurance products. That tailwind naturally faded in Q2. Plus, a new bancassurance regulation takes effect in July, so insurers are adjusting their strategies ahead of time.
EN_A: How big is the impact of that new regulation?
EN_B: The new rule requires much more granular reporting of expenses and cost allocation. In the short term, it could reduce sales incentives and suppress bancassurance volumes. But over the long run, it is seen as healthy for the industry.
EN_A: Which companies are outperforming on sales?
EN_B: CPIC has been a standout in Q2-to-date. It changed its bancassurance product strategy and is trying to catch up with its peers. China Life is also doing better than most.
EN_A: What about Ping An?
EN_B: Ping An is facing more growth pressure in Q2. After some regulatory scrutiny, it has been much more disciplined about keeping commission structures consistent.
EN_A: The report also mentioned some interesting investor feedback on recent stock performance.
EN_B: Right. Even though the equity market rebounded in Q2, insurance stocks actually fell over the past week. And A-share insurers have underperformed their H-share counterparts by a huge margin this year.
EN_A: What is causing that underperformance?
EN_B: A few things. The national team may have reduced their A-share insurance holdings. There is also a big sector rotation into tech stocks in the A-share market. And interestingly, insurance funds themselves have been increasing their holdings of H-share insurers.
EN_A: So which stocks does the report like the most?
EN_B: It highlights China Life H-shares and Ping An H-shares.
EN_A: Why China Life?
EN_B: Because its earnings have the highest sensitivity to the stock market. 72 percent of its equity holdings are classified as fair value through profit or loss, compared to the peer average of 57 percent. So when the market rallies, its profit gets the biggest boost.
EN_A: And Ping An?
EN_B: Ping An offers sustained double-digit growth in new business value and group operating profit. Plus, it has an attractive dividend yield of 5.3 percent and trades at only 6.1 times operating profit. That is a pretty compelling valuation.
EN_A: Did the report leave any questions unanswered?
EN_B: Yes, a couple of big ones. For example, how strictly will the new bancassurance rules be enforced after July? That depends on the new regulatory leadership, and the report explicitly says the level of enforcement is uncertain.
EN_A: So that is a key variable to watch.
EN_B: Exactly. Also, starting in June, the comparable base for new business value gets much tougher because of the buying frenzy before the rate cut last August. The report does not fully resolve how big that headwind will be.
