# APAC Focus: Will AI Deliver What Technology Never Could for Asian Banks?

![](images/8b7de37118520623dfb656dea0b14559f8127f277fdf4a016d17886f5b072c63.jpg)

A $35\%$ relative ROE premium already separates Asia's most AI-ready banking markets from the least. Does this reflect genuine AI foresight? We don't think so. We see this as a reflection of macro resilience and the "safety trade" that has driven Asian banks performance since last year through a period of uncertainty. AI, however, could introduce real optionality. If macro conditions were to shift and preference for safety were to fade, but AI were to deliver on the efficiency gains promise, we believe this can continue to support the outperformers.

# Six structural factors to assess AI readiness

Not all Asian banks are equal in their ability to convert AI investments into efficiency gains. Studying two decades of cost efficiency journeys across 13 markets in close collaboration with the UBS Asia banks team reveals 6 structural factors that matter: Tech Culture, IT Infrastructure, Efficiency Room, Labour Flexibility, Competitive Urgency and Scale. On a scorecard based on these factors, banks in Korea, Singapore, Australia & Thailand emerge as better positioned. Banks in the Philippines, India (PSUs in particular), Taiwan and Japan face more structural headwinds.

# AI premium - Prescient Call or a Beautiful Coincidence?

A 35% relative ROE premium separating Asia's top five AI ready banking markets from bottom five might sound like a bold and prescient market call on AI efficiency. Using a narrower lens makes this even more intriguing. For the most AI-ready banks in the top 5 markets versus the least AI-ready banks in the bottom 5, the valuation premium widens to 40%. Is the market making a prescient AI call at the market level as well as at the stock level? We don't think so. This is not a prescient AI call. This is more of a beautiful coincidence. The same markets sit atop both AI readiness and macro resilience framework ("safety trade" playbook) i.e. banking markets with high dividend yields, FX stability and defensive characteristics that have attracted flows since last year and outperformed given the preference for safety among investors in a period of heightened uncertainty. AI however could introduce a real optionality. Even if the macro shifts and preference for safety were to fade, AI optionality can provide a reason to hold on to the winners.

# Efficiency gains only partially offset revenue pressure

Asian banks have faced structural revenue headwinds driven by demographics-led pressure on net interest income (NII) and persistent challenges in scaling fee income over the last two decades.

While operating cost discipline over the last two decades has been meaningful, it has not been sufficient to fully offset this revenue erosion. Evidence of higher technology investments by the banks resulting in clear efficiency gains is also at best mixed. This raises the question whether the ongoing and future AI investments will yield any different results? Can AI help unlock the next leg of sustainable efficiency gains for Asian Banks?

Structural pressures like demographics have led to a 45bps decline in overall net interest income to assets for Asian Banks. Efforts to pivot to fee income have only delivered limited relief. Regulatory constraints, structural market features and competitive intensity made it challenging to scale fee income, resulting in an overall decline of 15bps in fee income to assets for Asian Banks in aggregate. Overall revenue to asset fell by close to 60bps for the sector in the last 20 years. We discussed these in greater detail and the varying journeys of Asian banking markets in two of earlier reports -

- Q-Series "Silver wave: How an ageing population is reshaping Asian..."   
- APAC Focus: Asian Banks "The Pivot to Non-interest Income – how...".

Against this backdrop, banks in the region leaned heavily on cost discipline to protect profitability. Operating efficiency improved steadily over the past two decades, with total operating expenses to assets declining \~40bps over the last 20y on average for Asian Banks, but only partially offsetting the revenue erosion. Please see our companion report for a more detailed discussion on these trends (After two decades of cost discipline, what's next for Asian banks?).
