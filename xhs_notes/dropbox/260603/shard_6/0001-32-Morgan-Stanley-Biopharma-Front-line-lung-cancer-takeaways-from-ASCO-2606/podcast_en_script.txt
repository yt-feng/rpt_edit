EN_A: So the ASCO conference just wrapped up, and there was a ton of data on first-line lung cancer. What stood out to you?

EN_A: And what are the main categories the report focuses on?
EN_B: Two main areas. First, TROP2 ADCs, like Merck and Kelun's Sac-TMT. Second, PD-1 and VEGF bispecific antibodies, like Akeso and Summit's ivonescimab.
EN_A: Let's start with Sac-TMT. What data did they present at ASCO?
EN_B: They showed results from a Phase 3 trial in China, comparing Sac-TMT plus Keytruda against Keytruda alone. The combination arm didn't reach a median progression-free survival, while the Keytruda arm was only 5.7 months. That's roughly in line with investor expectations of 15 to 17 months.
EN_A: So does that mean Sac-TMT is a winner?
EN_B: Not so fast. The data is from a Chinese-only population, and the global Phase 3 results are still pending. Also, the safety profile is a concern. Grade 3 or higher adverse events were 55.3% in the combo arm versus 31.4% for Keytruda alone. So we need to see if the benefit-risk profile holds up in a global setting.
EN_A: What about the PD-1 and VEGF bispecifics?
EN_B: That class had some really interesting data. Akeso and Summit's ivonescimab showed the first statistically significant overall survival benefit for this drug class in the HARMONi-6 trial. That's a big deal because it's the first time we've seen progression-free survival translate into an overall survival advantage.
EN_A: That sounds like a breakthrough.
EN_B: It's an important milestone, but the report has several caveats. The trial compared ivonescimab against a different PD-1 drug, not Keytruda. And it's a China-only study. The big question is whether these results can be replicated in a global trial against Keytruda plus chemo.
EN_A: So what's the key event investors should watch?
EN_B: The HARMONi-3 global Phase 3 trial, which is expected to report final PFS and interim OS data in the second half of 2026. That's the real test. If ivonescimab can beat Keytruda plus chemo on overall survival, that would be transformative.
EN_A: And where does Merck fit into all this?
EN_B: Merck is uniquely positioned. They have Sac-TMT in the ADC camp, and they also have LM-299, a PD-1 and VEGF bispecific from LaNova. So they have a horse in both races.
EN_A: So what's the one thing investors should keep an eye on right now?
EN_B: The global data for Sac-TMT and the HARMONi-3 results for ivonescimab. But the report also leaves a big open question: how these drugs perform in patients with low PD-L1 expression. The data there is still incomplete, and that could change the competitive landscape significantly.
