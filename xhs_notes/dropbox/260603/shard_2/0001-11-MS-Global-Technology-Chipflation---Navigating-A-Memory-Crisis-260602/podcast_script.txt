EN_A:A permanent reset? That's a strong claim. We just went through a chip shortage during COVID. How is this different?

EN_B:The pandemic was a supply chain disruption. Factories shut down, then they reopened. This is different because the demand driver is structural. AI's need for memory, especially HBM and advanced DRAM, is growing exponentially. And building new fabrication plants takes years. Hyperscalers like Google and Microsoft are at the front of the line. They use long-term agreements and pre-payments to lock up almost all the advanced HBM and server DRAM capacity.

EN_B:Exactly. Behind them are the markets for smartphones, PCs, cars, and industrial equipment. The report estimates that by 2027, the supply of memory for smartphones and PCs will fall short of demand by 12 to 15 percent. This means either higher prices, lower specs, or delayed product launches.

EN_B:This is where the report's insight gets really interesting. It divides the hardware industry into the haves and the have-nots.
