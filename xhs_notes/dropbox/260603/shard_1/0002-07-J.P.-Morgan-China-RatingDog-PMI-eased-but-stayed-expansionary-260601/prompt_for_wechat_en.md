Exports may face headwinds

- RatingDog manufacturing PMI eased to 51.8, with key components falling but production maintaining a healthy pace of expansion.

In keeping with NBS PMIs, RatingDog manufacturing PMI readings also declined but remained broadly in healthy expansion: both sets of PMIs point to a more modest production expansion after solid 1Q growth. Demand has weakened further, with external demand now an emerging concern. Although Operation Epic Fury officially ended, price components in both sets of PMIs moderated but continued to rise at rather high rates, reflecting difficult negotiations and still elevated energy prices. Future output PMI remained in solid expansion, moderating 0.7 pts to 55.0 (NBS: 53.9), in part reflecting concerns over slowing external demand and rising costs.

RatingDog manufacturing PMI came in above expectations, falling by 0.4 pts to 51.8 (JPM: 51.0; consensus: 51.3), still a solid reading that has recorded expansion since December 2025. The slowdown was seen across major supply and demand components, with new export orders falling into contractionary territory. Output PMI edged down to 53.4. The still solid reading supports the view of a continued production expansion, which possibly moderated due to the Labor Day holiday and weaker export growth. New orders PMI declined to 52.5 (NBS: 49.9), still above trend and among the highest readings over the past five years. According to the survey, this reflected firmer market demand, product improvements, new customers and promotional drives. Notably, export orders weakened by 1.5 pts to 49.6 (NBS: 48.6, down 1.7 pts), suggesting a contraction in external demand. Export growth may run into headwinds and drag growth after the strong 1Q momentum.

Inflationary pressures eased. As seen in the NBS PMIs, RatingDog input prices edged down to a still high 55.8 (NBS: 60.5), while output prices fell to 52.0 (NBS: 51.9). Increased costs of raw materials and energy, and continued supply chain disruptions resulting from the ongoing Middle East conflict remained the key drivers of the price increases. The rise in input and output prices continued to be asymmetric, with producers absorbing a greater share of the upstream price shocks, providing a buffer and preventing a larger slide in demand.
