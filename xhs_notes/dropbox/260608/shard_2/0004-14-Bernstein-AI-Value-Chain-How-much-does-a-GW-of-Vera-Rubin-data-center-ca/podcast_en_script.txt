EN_A: So we’ve got this report on AI data center costs, and it says a Vera Rubin rack could be over 9 million dollars. Is that for real?
EN_B: Yes, we estimate about 9.1 million per rack, which is notably higher than the 8 million figure that’s been floating around in the media.
EN_A: What’s driving that gap? Is it the GPU price?
EN_B: Actually, the GPU is still the biggest single component at around 4 million. But the main surprise is memory. We expect HBM4 prices to jump to 53 dollars per gigabyte by 2027, when Vera Rubin ships in volume.
EN_A: So memory costs are inflating the whole rack?
EN_B: Exactly. Memory and storage together come to about 3.2 million, or 35 percent of the rack cost. And we think Nvidia will pass that increase on to customers.
EN_A: What about other components? Networking, cooling?
EN_B: Networking adds about 1.2 million, with NVLink switches and SpectrumX. Cooling and power delivery are each around 150,000. They’re smaller, but still significant.
EN_A: And if you scale that up to a gigawatt of data center capacity?
EN_B: We estimate total capex at about 47 billion per gigawatt. That includes 32 billion for racks and 15 billion for physical infrastructure. It’s a big jump from the Blackwell generation.
EN_A: So the bar for building AI data centers is getting higher?
EN_B: Yes, and it’s likely to keep rising. One thing the report highlights is that the real economic cost is dominated by depreciation, not electricity.
EN_A: Depreciation? Can you explain that?
EN_B: Sure. Servers and networking gear depreciate faster than buildings. So the annual depreciation is about 7.2 billion, while electricity is only 1.3 billion, even at a high rate of 15 cents per kilowatt-hour.
EN_A: Does that mean AI adoption could slow down?
EN_B: Not necessarily. The report points out that compute performance is accelerating fast. Vera Rubin delivers 2.5 times the FP8 performance of Blackwell. So performance per dollar is still improving.
EN_A: That sounds promising. But are there any uncertainties?
EN_B: Definitely. Memory prices are very volatile. NAND prices have gone up 11 times from the 2023 trough. And the report doesn’t fully address whether LPDDR shortages could limit Vera Rubin shipments.
EN_A: That’s a key question. I guess we’d need to read the full report for more?
EN_B: Yes, and also for details on cooling choices and network architecture variations. These can all affect the final cost. So if you’re serious about this space, the full report is worth a look.
