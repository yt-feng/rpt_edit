It is a recognition that the underlying demand trajectory has steepened. When a company of TSMC’s scale raises its capex by nearly USD 10 billion in a single revision, the implication is that the cost of advanced lithography, deposition, and etch tools has risen faster than anticipated, and TSMC is absorbing that cost rather than delaying its capacity roadmap.

The strategic logic is clear. TSMC is not building fabs for today’s demand. The capex increase also sends a signal to the ecosystem: equipment suppliers, materials companies, and even competitors should expect that TSMC will maintain its technology leadership through aggressive investment, not through pricing power alone. Notably, TSMC did not provide the three-year capex guidance of USD 200 billion that some had anticipated, but the implication of a single-year target already exceeding USD 60 billion is that the cumulative total over the next three years will likely be far higher. The capex intensity of the business is increasing, and that is a feature, not a bug, of the AI super-cycle.

## The Margin Compression from 2nm Ramp Is a Temporary Cost of Entry into a Higher Revenue Plateau

The 3Q26 gross margin guidance of 66% at the midpoint, down from 67.7% in 2Q26, has drawn attention from the buyside, where unrealistic expectations of 70% had been circulating. But this margin compression is not a sign of operational weakness. It is a direct consequence of the 2nm node ramp. Every new node transition in TSMC’s history has involved a temporary margin trough as yields improve and utilization rates climb. The 2nm node is no exception, and the magnitude of the dilution is consistent with historical patterns.

The more important question is what comes after the trough. If 2026 revenue grows above 40% year-over-year, the absolute gross profit dollars will be substantially higher than in 2025, even if the margin percentage dips by 100-200 basis points. The buyside fixation on a 70% margin target ignores the arithmetic: a company generating USD 100 billion in revenue at 66% gross margin produces more profit than a company generating USD 70 billion in revenue at 70% gross margin. The margin compression is a bridge to a higher revenue plateau, and the bridge is worth crossing. Those who focus on the margin miss are likely to miss the revenue acceleration that follows.

The information from this earnings release can be organized into a three-variable decision framework that helps observers assess whether TSMC’s current valuation is justified. The three variables are capacity deployment, margin trajectory, and demand duration.

The risk is that if AI demand decelerates before 2028, the company could be left with underutilized fabs. However, the report indicates that AI demand is tracking above prior forecasts and is expected to remain strong until at least 2028, which is the timeframe during which the new capacity will come online. The capacity deployment variable, therefore, is supportive of the bull case.

On margin trajectory, the near-term compression from the 2nm ramp is temporary, but the medium-term margin outlook depends on pricing discipline. TSMC has historically raised prices for large customers to maintain gross margins above 56% in the long term, as noted in the report’s risk disclosures. If the company can successfully pass through cost increases to hyperscaler customers, the margin trough will be shallow and the recovery rapid. If pricing power erodes, the margin recovery could be delayed. The margin variable is slightly positive, contingent on pricing discipline.

On demand duration, the report’s scenario analysis provides a useful range. In the base case, AI demand is strong until 2028. In the bullish scenario, it lasts until the end of the decade. In the bearish scenario, it could be affected by memory costs. The demand duration variable is the most uncertain, but the guidance raise to above 40% year-over-year growth for 2026 suggests that the base case is already being validated. Observers should monitor hyperscaler capex trends and memory pricing as leading indicators, not as lagging confirmations.

The framework suggests that TSMC is currently priced for a scenario between the base case and the bullish case, with the margin compression creating a point of consideration for those who believe the demand duration will persist. The key risk is not the margin miss. It is a sudden deceleration in hyperscaler capex, for which there is currently no evidence.
