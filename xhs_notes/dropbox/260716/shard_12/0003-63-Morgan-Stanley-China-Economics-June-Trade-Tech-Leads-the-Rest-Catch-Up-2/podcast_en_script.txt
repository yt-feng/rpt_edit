EN_A: China’s June trade numbers came in hot again. Did you see that latest research report?
EN_B: Yeah, it’s a strong beat. Exports grew 27% year-on-year, imports jumped 36%. Both well above expectations.
EN_A: What’s driving that kind of surge?
EN_B: Two main forces. First, tech—semiconductors and computers alone contributed 9.4 percentage points to export growth and 17.4 points to imports. Second, the non-tech sector is catching up. Export growth there accelerated from 11.2% in May to 19.8% in June.
EN_A: So tech is still the big story, but other industries are starting to pull their weight?
EN_B: Exactly. That’s the key message of the report. It’s not just about chips. Auto exports rose 12% month-on-month, pushing annual growth from 39% to 70%. Labor-intensive consumer goods also picked up.
EN_A: 70% for autos? That’s huge. What about imports?
EN_B: Import improvement was more commodity-driven, but uneven. Coal imports rose 17% month-on-month, iron ore up 7%, mostly driven by volume. But crude oil imports fell 16% in value terms, signaling a continued slump in volume.
EN_A: Crude is interesting—prices stable but volumes down. Is that a demand issue?
EN_B: The report doesn’t fully answer that. It notes domestic safety inspections may have boosted coal imports, while crude weakness could be linked to refinery margins. That’s something the full report explores in more detail.
EN_A: So is this trade growth sustainable?
EN_B: Structural tech demand should keep headline numbers strong for a few more months. But there’s a risk: renewed tensions in the Strait of Hormuz. With thin inventory buffers, even a moderate shock could hit shipping and global trade hard.
EN_A: So the boom comes with a geopolitical shadow?
EN_B: Right. And the report leaves one big question open: is the non-tech recovery real demand or just temporary catch-up? That needs more data to confirm.
EN_A: Sounds like there’s a lot more to dig into. For those who want the full picture, the complete report goes deeper into industry and destination breakdowns.
