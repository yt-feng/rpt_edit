Foreign Exchange - Asia ex-Japan

## Many AeJ central banks sold USD in June following the FOMC's perceived hawkishness

BOK, BI and BSP may have bucked the trend on local factors.

\- Our estimates suggest that the CBC, BOT, RBI, MAS and PBoC have sold FX/USD in June to lean against USD strength following the FOMC's perceived hawkishness at its June meeting.

\- The BOK accumulated spot FXR in June despite USD/KRW being \~2.9% higher, but this could have been offset by forward selling (June data are not yet available), as was observed in October and November 2025. It might have also been due to stronger exporter repatriation in June.

\- BI's back-to-back 25bp hikes in June and measures to attract bond inflows may have given BI scope to accumulate FX/USD in June.

\- We estimate the RBI sold USD2.6bn of spot FX reserves (FXR) in June, despite introducing measures to attract inflows at its June MPC meeting.

\- We estimate the BOT net sold USD2.7bn of FXR (spot + forward; \~1.1% of FXR) in June to lean against THB depreciation pressures.

## Key observations

\- In India, we estimate the RBI continued to net sell spot FXR in June, though at a slower pace of USD2.6bn, less than half of the USD5.7bn of spot FXR selling in May 2026. Several factors contributed to this slower pace, including: a) the US-Iran interim deal with the associated lower oil prices and the RBI's announced measures for attracting capital inflows - FCNR(B) and ECB.

The RBI's net short forward book expanded by USD11.4bn in May 2026 to a record level of USD106.7bn. This included the USD5.0bn FX swap conducted by the RBI on 26 May. In the coming months, the RBI's net short forward book is likely to expand further as it enters FX swaps at concessional rates with banks because of FCNR(B) and ECB inflows.

\- In Indonesia, we estimate that the BI net accumulated USD2.6bn (spot + forwards) in June (\~2.1% of May FXR), even after adjusting for FX valuations, coupon receipts and FX bond issuance and maturities, and FX term deposits. Arguably, BI's back-to-back 25bp hikes in June and measures to attract bond inflows might have given BI scope to augment its weak FX reserves position – we saw IndoGBs inflows of USD1.3bn in June and SRBI inflows of USD1.2bn from 1 to 15 June (last available).

In its statement, BI also noted that the slight increase in its FXR was “primarily attributable to tax and services receipts…” (BI, 7 July).

\- In Korea, we estimate the BOK net bought USD3.0bn of spot FXR in June (0.7% of May FXR) after adjustments. The BOK's press release (BOK, 3 July) highlighted that despite market stabilization measures (such as the FX swaps with the NPS), headline FXR rose only slightly, driven by "factors including an increase in foreign currency deposits at financial institutions" (i.e., +USD0.9bn). We note that local media (Pulse, 12 June) highlighted that the Korea government had requested major corporates to convert their FX holdings into KRW in June.

This estimated net accumulation in spot FXR emerged despite KRW being the worst performing AeJ currency in June (i.e., USD/KRW was \~2.9% higher) with onshore spot USD/KRW trading as high as 1,562.2 on 5 June.
