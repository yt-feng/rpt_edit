# Johnson & Johnson 2Q26: Solid beat and raise, reassuring commentary on underlying medtech trends

Solid beat and raise. Sales grew 5.7% organic (Pharma 6.9% / MedTech 3.7%), beating by 1.1%. Adj EPS grew 4.7% to $2.90 and beat by 1.2%. After a Q2 beat of $275mn and 3c on revenue and EPS, JNJ raised FY26 revenue guidance by $300mn and EPS by 12.5c (Exhibit 1). JNJ is on track for $100bn revenue in 2026, and the company is confident about line of sight to double-digit revenue growth by the end of the decade.

Pharma was strong again, with 6.9% organic growth despite a 760bps Stelara headwind. TREMFYA remains a standout with 71% growth supported by the IBD indication, while INLEXZO and ICOTYDE are ramping quickly. Management reiterated confidence in becoming the #1 oncology company by 2030.

Small MedTech miss (by 0.7%) on Abiomed weakness. Small beats in Ortho (1.4% beat), Surgery (1.3% beat), and Vision (1.5% beat) were offset by Cardiovascular weakness (5.4% miss). The shortfall was driven by Abiomed, where sales declined 2% (14% miss) as U.S. procedure volumes fell following the March readout of the CHIP-BCIS3 trial.

Confident on MedTech procedure trend. After HCA highlighted year-over-year declines in inpatient and outpatient procedure volumes in yesterday's Q2 pre-release (see our take), investors were eager to hear JNJ's view on market trends. JNJ sees no sign of a slowdown in underlying procedure markets and no impact from declining enrollment in ACA or Medicaid. In fact, JNJ forecasts acceleration from 1H26 to 2H26 in all four of its MedTech businesses.

## DETAILS

## Solid quarter with 1% revenue beat driven by Innovative Medicine

Sales of $25.3bn grew 5.7% organic despite a 760-bps headwind from declining STELARA sales. Growth in the U.S. was 7.4% organic and 3.5% organic outside the U.S. Acquisitions and divestitures had a net negative impact on WW growth of 10bps. Innovative Medicine sales of $16.4bn beat consensus by ~2% on 6.9% organic growth. MedTech sales of $8.9bn missed consensus slightly (-0.7%) on 3.7% organic growth. JNJ affirmed that they are on track to meet their 2026 target of more than $100bn annual revenue for the first time, and management remains confident that progress will continue into 2027 with line of sight to double-digit revenue growth by the end of the decade.

Adj EPS rose 4.7% to $2.90, slightly above consensus ($2.87). COGS leveraged by 30bps, driven by favorable operational drivers in currency, partially offset by unfavorable product mix in the Innovative Medicine business, as well as the impact of tariffs in the MedTech business. SG&A deleveraged by 60bps, driven by increased investments in new product launches. R&D leveraged by 40bps, primarily driven by expense phasing in Innovative Medicine, partially offset by increased investment in MedTech.

Looking at adj income before tax by segment for the quarter, Innovative Medicine margin declined from 42.7% to 42.5%, primarily driven by unfavorable mix and heavier investments in new product launches, partially offset by expense phasing in R&D. MedTech margin declined from 22.2% to 22.0%, primarily driven by commercial investments, increased R&D, and the increased impact of tariffs, partially offset by favorable operational drivers in currency and cost of products sold. Overall, adj income before tax for the enterprise as a percentage of sales decreased from 34.5% to 34.2%.

## Confident on MedTech procedure trend

JNJ sees no sign of procedure growth slowdown, despite fears after HCA pre-released 2Q26 revenue yesterday and highlighted a 2.3% decline in same-facility inpatient surgery volumes and a 3.4% decline in same-facility outpatient surgery volumes (see our takeaways on HCA's pre-release). Hospital commentary has stoked fears that utilization growth could slow in 2H26, driven in part by declining enrollment in ACA plans and cuts to Medicaid. JNJ was very clear they were not seeing any impact in their business.

"To your question about procedure volumes, from our perspective, when we look across the breadth of our portfolio, we see that volumes are generally in line with market trends, and we're not currently seeing any evidence of a broad-based slowdown in activity. Demand remains consistent with what we would expect, given the fundamentals of these markets [...] We are acutely aware of the recent data that has been provided by a number of large US hospital operators, highlighting pressure on certain procedure volumes, particularly in more elective areas of care. And while we're monitoring these trends very closely, they are not reflected in what we're seeing across our portfolio today. As you know, our business is diversified across a broad range of categories, many of which are less dependent on discretionary elective procedures. And from our perspective, overall procedure trends remain consistent in the quarter with our expectations. [...] there has been a bunch of chatter about the potential impact of the ACA subsidy removals. Based on what we're seeing,... we haven't observed any meaningful impact on procedure volumes across our portfolio related to the ACA. And while we expect that the expiration of the Affordable Care Act subsidies may create some affordability pressures for a small cohort of patients, we do not expect this to translate into a material impact on demand for MedTech and procedures. And so to be clear, we would not attribute our MedTech performance in Q2 to a systemic slowdown in procedures."

In a follow-up, management confirmed that consensus miss in U.S. Knees (-3%) and in U.S. Spine (-1%) were the result of market dynamics (softening trend in JNJ's higher-value revision knees business; competitive pressures in U.S. Spine) rather than representative of utilization pressure on elective procedures.

## FY 26 sales, EPS guidance raised

Per Exhibit 1, management raised 2026 (1) adjusted operational sales growth guidance by 40bps at the midpoint to 6.2%-6.8% (vs prior range of 5.6%-6.6%) and (2) adj EPS guidance by ~13 cents at the midpoint to $11.60-$11.75 (vs prior $11.45-$11.65).

P&L considerations. In 2026, JNJ benefits from a 53rd week in the financial calendar worth ~100 bps to full year sales growth (53rd week impacts Q426). The Intra-Cellular benefit lapped in Q2. Heavier investment is expected in 1H26 vs 2H26 while higher EPS growth is expected in 2H26 vs 1H26.

Phasing considerations. For Innovative Medicine, JNJ set expectations for: (1) more pronounced impact from newly launched products as the year progresses, (2) generic competition for OPSUMIT (second half in U.S.) and SIMPONI (first half in EU; potentially second half in U.S.), and (3) impact of voluntary agreement with the U.S. government to be evenly distributed throughout the year. For MedTech, JNJ expects (1) continued acceleration of newly launched products as the year progresses with normalized seasonality, (2) surgery transformation sales headwinds to increase through the year, and (3) additional rounds of VBP in China throughout the year (heavier in 2H26).

2H acceleration expected in both Innovative Medicine and MedTech. Management noted that Innovative Medicine 2H growth would be driven by emerging growth in ICOTYDE, INLEXZO, and TREMFYA with the launch of its IBD indication. MedTech's 2H growth is expected to be driven by continued strength in Orthopaedics, Surgery, and Vision, along with a recovery in Cardiovascular as Abiomed rebounds toward the end of 2026 and China inventory-related headwinds in EP, which created a one-time 400bps impact, subside. JNJ also emphasized the company's slate of Cardiovascular launches in the pipeline expected to accelerate segment growth in 2H.

"...we always had a little bit better revenue growth projected in the second half of the year, irrespective of the extra week of sales that will occur in the fourth quarter. When you think about products that are really in their infancy with respect to ICOTYDE, INLEXZO and just the emergence of TREMFYA continuing to do well with the IBD indications that were received over the last 18 months, there was always that projection. [...] we do expect MedTech to grow better in the second half than the first half as well. We have tempered expectations around the Abiomed business with respect to that. So we see that recovery happening, but probably as we leave this year into next year. So not declines, but more modest growth until our PROTECT IV data comes out."

Within MedTech, management confirmed in a follow-up that all MedTech segments - Orthopedics, Surgery, Vision, and Cardiovascular - are expected to grow faster in 2H26.

## Innovative Medicine beats by 2%; plenty of growth drivers

Innovative Medicine grew 6.9% to $16.4bn and beat consensus by ~2% despite the ~760-bps headwind from STELARA.

Becoming the "#1 oncology company by 2030." Oncology sales grew 16.1% WW to $7.41bn but missed consensus ($7.48bn) by 1.1%, reflecting broad based momentum across multiple myeloma and solid tumor launches, though results were modestly below consensus in RYBREVANT, ERLEADA, and IMBRUVICA. That said, management remains confident in its ambition to become the "#1 oncology company by 2030," with oncology sales projected to exceed $50bn (2030 consensus currently $48.2bn). Management specifically highlighted DARZALEX's performance in Q2, delivering sales of $4.2bn and growth of 17.6% YoY, while emphasizing the depth of the portfolio — including multiple myeloma assets CARVYKTI, TECVAYLI, and TALVEY, which continued to deliver high DD growth. Despite coming in below consensus, ERLEADA (4.1% miss) and RYBREVANT (6.8% miss) both saw robust underlying growth, increasing 7.6% and 62% YoY, respectively. Management also remains encouraged by the early momentum of the recently approved INLEXZO, which expands the franchise into bladder-sparing therapy and underscores the company's push into earlier-stage, procedure-adjacent oncology care.

"In the US, INLEXZO is outperforming all of the recent competitive launches. And as you know, noted one in three eligible patients are now starting on in INLEXZO regimen. And this is an uptick versus first quarter where it was one in four last quarter. It's now one in three, and it's leading all of the novel agents in terms of new patient share. In terms of new patients and new patient insertions, those grew 75% quarter-over-quarter. And this was post the permanent J-code, so giving a lot of confidence to providers in terms of that reimbursement. Importantly, INLEXZO is included in the NCCN guidelines with a Category 2A designation, and they've been updated to expand the opportunity to include the papillary population, which represents about 80% of the BCG unresponsive patients. While we don't report sales on this yet, please know that we confidently beat consensus for the quarter, and we more than doubled sales in second quarter versus first quarter. So we believe that we're really on a very, very good track with this."

ICOTYDE continues to show strong early launch momentum, with management highlighting rapid uptake and broad prescriber adoption in the first few months post-FDA approval (March 2026). JNJ sees ICOTYDE as a key driver of their immunology growth and as a pillar of its strategy to expand beyond injectable biologics.

"To date, over 18,000 prescriptions have been written for a total of now 11,000 patients. So that's an update from the 10,000 just shortly. There are 6,000 unique prescribers, and if you take a look at those, over 50% are actually advanced practice providers. About 40% are dermatologists, and the rest are our primary care physicians and others. And so that tells us that there's really good confidence in the product in both the efficacy and safety profile as well as the simplicity of the simple once-daily pill. We're already seeing a number of writers who are repeat. We're seeing patients getting repeat prescriptions. As we take a look at the payer coverage, within 90 days we already have over 50% of commercial coverage. And so we think that's great. That's actually a little bit ahead of our projections."

Management explicitly noted that strong ICOTYDE growth has not slowed TREMFYA at all in psoriatic disease, with both products performing well in the quarter and being co-positioned: ICOTYDE as first line systemic, TREMFYA as first line biologic.

"And then in terms of really co-positioning, ICOTYDE is the first choice systemic treatment. All of those patients who've been cycling on topicals, who really need to have advanced therapy, we think ICOTYDE fits right in that sweet spot as that first choice systemic. For TREMFYA, we think it's the first choice biologic, particularly for patients that have potential for or have any psoriatic arthritis involvement. And many, many patients, a significant percentage with psoriasis actually progress to psoriatic arthritis as well. So we think really distinct and unique positions for both and well reflected in the results that we saw in the quarter with both products doing really well."

TREMFYA growth bolstered by IBD indication. TREMFYA saw yet another strong quarter, with sales growing 71% YoY to $2.04bn and a consensus ($1.83bn) beat of 11.7%. While part of the robust growth was driven by share gains across all indications and continued market growth, management explicitly pointed out their performance in ulcerative colitis (UC) and Crohn's disease as the major driver. TREMFYA now spans plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), with U.S. approvals in both moderately to severely active UC and Crohn's disease.

Neuroscience growth robust at 13.4% (~3% beat), as strength continued in SPRAVATO and CAPLYTA. SPRAVATO growth was strong at 40%, driven by continued increased physician and patient demand. CAPLYTA also saw very strong growth at 70.9%, with new patient starts up 122% YoY, significantly outpacing the market leader. Growth was driven by strong continued launch momentum in adjunctive major depressive disorder.

Firefly Bio acquisition. JNJ announced a $1bn cash agreement on June 8 to acquire Firefly Bio (private), a biotechnology company advancing its proprietary Firelink degrader antibody conjugate (DAC) platform, in order to strengthen JNJ's oncology pipeline. Management expects the acquisition to close in 3Q26.

## Small miss for MedTech (3.7% growth; 0.7% miss), dragged down by Cardiovascular (especially Abiomed)

Sales grew 3.7% to $8.9bn with divestitures negatively impacting growth by 0.1%. Growth was driven by consensus beats in Orthopaedics (1.4%), Surgery (1.3%), and Vision Care (1.5%), but offset by weakness in Cardiovascular (5.4% miss). Of the ~$80mn miss in U.S. MedTech, most of the weakness came from lower-than-expected Abiomed U.S. sales (~$60mn miss) and U.S. Electrophysiology (~$16mn miss). Outside the U.S., JNJ MedTech sales grew 3.2% organic to $4.4bn, slightly ahead of consensus.

Abiomed sales decline on CHIP-BCIS3 readout. At ACC.26 in late March, the trial readout for CHIP-BCIS3 (see recap) raised questions for JNJ's Abiomed business. The UK-based, 300-patient trial was designed to test whether JNJ's Impella left ventricular assist (pLVAD) device reduces major adverse events during high-risk PCI procedures in patients with severe left ventricular dysfunction and extensive coronary artery disease. The results showed that Impella CP did not reduce major adverse events when used in the setting of high-risk PCI. Physician caution following the trial readout led to a slowdown in Impella usage, as global Abiomed sales of $440mn declined 2% organic (13.7% miss) on lower U.S. procedures, partially offset by continued OUS growth including sustained adoption of Impella 5.5.

Management is optimistic that the Abiomed business will recover towards the end of 2026, and emphasized that (1) they are actively engaging with physicians and their teams to reinforce appropriate patient selection and that (2) results from PROTECT IV are expected to read out in 2027 and should bolster the Impella case. PROTECT IV (see trial) is a multicenter, ~1,300-patient trial that assesses whether using the Impella CP or Impella 2.5 device during high-risk PCI in patients with reduced left-sided heart function will result in an improvement in symptoms, heart function and health after a heart procedure compared to the current standard of care.

"...we believe that the slowdown is in relation to specifically a neutral study published out of the UK focused on the area of high risk PCI. And I think it's important, firstly, to really reinforce that the impact is behavioral. It's really driven by physician caution as they really interpret this new data rather than anything structural. And there's no change in the underlying need, access or reimbursement. These are the sickest patients, many of whom have very few choices. And so we clearly see this as a near-term dynamic. We've seen this before and we are actively engaging with physicians and their teams to really reinforce appropriate patient selection, leveraging both our capabilities, but most importantly, the depth of our clinical evidence base. And I think it's important to put this in context and recognize that the area of heart pumps is a category that Abiomed pioneered. And we are the only player today, and it remains a relatively evolving area of medicine. And with any emerging field, you know that scientific perspectives will continue to evolve. And that's exactly why we remain committed to really investing in robust clinical research, including... PROTECT IV, which is a highly powered study. Almost 1,300 patients focused specifically on high risk PCI, which we expect to read out in 2027. [...] So we remain very confident the long-term potential for Impella, supported by the significant runway for growth, no current commercialized competitors, which is a key point, opportunities for further geographic expansion, and the large and growing global burden of heart disease. [...] we do expect this to take some time, but we absolutely expect that our performance in the back half will be stronger than the first. And we see gradual improvements in performance back to double-digit growth over the coming quarters."

EP sales grew 3.1%, (3.4% miss). Performance was driven by procedure growth, commercial execution, and new product performance (VARIPULSE, TRUPULSE, NUVISION, and CRYSTAL), but partially offset by competitive pressures in PFA and a one-time, 400bps impact from China inventory dynamics. JNJ highlighted several 2026 growth catalysts in the EP portfolio, including CARTOSOUND SONATA (launched Q2 at HRS 2026), Dual Energy STSF Catheter (received FDA approval and expected to launch this year), and VARIPULSE PRO (expected to build momentum in EMEA and anticipated FDA approval later this year).

Shockwave sales grew 14.7% (3.8% miss). Double-digit growth was driven by continued strong adoption of coronary and peripheral portfolios and new product launches (C2 Aero IVL catheter).

Ortho sales grew 4.2% (1.4% beat), with growth across all platforms driven by new product launches and strong commercial execution. In our follow-up, management clarified that hospital utilization pressures were not the driver of the weakness in U.S. knees or spine, alleviating concerns around elective procedure demand. U.S. Knees missed consensus by ~3% driven by a softening trend in revision knees, where JNJ is over-indexed. U.S. Spine came in ~1% below consensus on competitive pressures. Management affirmed that the ortho separation is still on track for mid-2027.

Consensus beats in Surgical (2.3% growth, 1.3% beat) and Vision Care (5.6% growth, 1.5% beat). Management is anticipating FDA approval for Ottawa in 2H26 after being submitted on 7-Jan-2026, with a second IDE trial underway for inguinal hernia repair. Assuming an approval comes through, JNJ is committed to launching both Ottawa and Monarch for urology (kidney stones) this year. Management continues to expect robotics to be "a meaningful contributor to MedTech growth by the end of the decade."
