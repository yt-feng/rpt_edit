# Johnson & Johnson 2Q26: Solid beat and raise, reassuring commentary on underlying medtech trends

Solid beat and raise. Sales grew 5.7% organic (Pharma 6.9% / MedTech 3.7%), beating by 1.1%. Adj EPS grew 4.7% to \$2.90 and beat by 1.2%. After a Q2 beat of \$275mn and 3c on revenue and EPS, JNJ raised FY26 revenue guidance by \$300mn and EPS by 12.5c (Exhibit 1). JNJ is on track for \$100bn revenue in 2026, and the company is confident about line of sight to double-digit revenue growth by the end of the decade.

Pharma was strong again, with 6.9% organic growth despite a 760bps Stelara headwind. TREMFYA remains a standout with 71% growth supported by the IBD indication, while INLEXZO and ICOTYDE are ramping quickly. Management reiterated confidence in becoming the #1 oncology company by 2030.

Small MedTech miss (by 0.7%) on Abiomed weakness. Small beats in Ortho (1.4% beat), Surgery (1.3% beat), and Vision (1.5% beat) were offset by Cardiovascular weakness (5.4% miss). The shortfall was driven by Abiomed, where sales declined 2% (14% miss) as U.S. procedure volumes fell following the March readout of the CHIP-BCIS3 trial.

Confident on MedTech procedure trend. After HCA highlighted year-over-year declines in inpatient and outpatient procedure volumes in yesterday's Q2 pre-release (see our take), investors were eager to hear JNJ's view on market trends. JNJ sees no sign of a slowdown in underlying procedure markets and no impact from declining enrollment in ACA or Medicaid. In fact, JNJ forecasts acceleration from 1H26 to 2H26 in all four of its MedTech businesses.

## DETAILS

## Solid quarter with 1% revenue beat driven by Innovative Medicine

Sales of \$25.3bn grew 5.7% organic despite a 760-bps headwind from declining STELARA sales. Growth in the U.S. was 7.4% organic and 3.5% organic outside the U.S. Acquisitions and divestitures had a net negative impact on WW growth of 10bps. Innovative Medicine sales of \$16.4bn beat consensus by \~2% on 6.9% organic growth. MedTech sales of \$8.9bn missed consensus slightly (-0.7%) on 3.7% organic growth. JNJ affirmed that they are on track to meet their 2026 target of more than \$100bn annual revenue for the first time, and management remains confident that progress will continue into 2027 with line of sight to double-digit revenue growth by the end of the decade.

Adj EPS rose 4.7% to \$2.90, slightly above consensus (\$2.87). COGS leveraged by 30bps, driven by favorable operational drivers in currency, partially offset by unfavorable product mix in the Innovative Medicine business, as well as the impact of tariffs in the MedTech business. SG&A deleveraged by 60bps, driven by increased investments in new product launches. R&D leveraged by 40bps, primarily driven by expense phasing in Innovative Medicine, partially offset by increased investment in MedTech.

Looking at adj income before tax by segment for the quarter, Innovative Medicine margin declined from 42.7% to 42.5%, primarily driven by unfavorable mix and heavier investments in new product launches, partially offset by expense phasing in R&D. MedTech margin declined from 22.2% to 22.0%, primarily driven by commercial investments, increased R&D, and the increased impact of tariffs, partially offset by favorable operational drivers in currency and cost of products sold. Overall, adj income before tax for the enterprise as a percentage of sales decreased from 34.5% to 34.2%.

## Confident on MedTech procedure trend

JNJ sees no sign of procedure growth slowdown, despite fears after HCA pre-released 2Q26 revenue yesterday and highlighted a $2.3\%$ decline in same-facility inpatient surgery volumes and a $3.4\%$ decline in same-facility outpatient surgery volumes (see our takeaways on HCA's pre-release). Hospital commentary has stoked fears that utilization growth could slow in 2H26, driven in part by declining enrollment in ACA plans and cuts to Medicaid. JNJ was very clear they were not seeing any impact in their business.

"To your question about procedure volumes, from our perspective, when we look across the breadth of our portfolio, we see that volumes are generally in line with market trends, and we're not currently seeing any evidence of a broad-based slowdown in activity. Demand remains consistent with what we would expect, given the fundamentals of these markets [...] We are acutely aware of the recent data that has been provided by a number of large US hospital operators, highlighting pressure on certain procedure volumes, particularly in more elective areas of care. And while we're monitoring these trends very closely, they are not reflected in what we're seeing across our portfolio today. As you know, our business is diversified across a broad range of categories, many of which are less dependent on discretionary elective procedures. And from our perspective, overall procedure trends remain consistent in the quarter with our expectations. [...] there has been a bunch of chatter about the potential impact of the ACA subsidy removals. Based on what we're seeing,... we haven't observed any meaningful impact on procedure volumes across our portfolio related to the ACA. And while we expect that the expiration of the Affordable Care Act subsidies may create some affordability pressures for a small cohort of patients, we do not expect this to translate into a material impact on demand for MedTech and procedures. And so to be clear, we would not attribute our MedTech performance in Q2 to a systemic slowdown in procedures."

In a follow-up, management confirmed that consensus miss in U.S. Knees (-3%) and in U.S. Spine (-1%) were the result of market dynamics (softening trend in JNJ's higher-value revision knees business; competitive pressures in U.S. Spine) rather than representative of utilization pressure on elective procedures.

## FY 26 sales, EPS guidance raised

Per Exhibit 1, management raised 2026 (1) adjusted operational sales growth guidance by 40bps at the midpoint to 6.2%-6.8% (vs prior range of 5.6%-6.6%) and (2) adj EPS guidance by \~13 cents at the midpoint to \$11.60-\$11.75 (vs prior \$11.45-\$11.65).

P&L considerations. In 2026, JNJ benefits from a $53^{\text{rd}}$ week in the financial calendar worth $\sim 100$ bps to full year sales growth (53 $^{\text{rd}}$ week impacts Q426). The Intra-Cellular benefit lapped in Q2. Heavier investment is expected in 1H26 vs 2H26 while higher EPS growth is expected in 2H26 vs 1H26.

Phasing considerations. For Innovative Medicine, JNJ set expectations for: (1) more pronounced impact from newly launched products as the year progresses, (2) generic competition for OPSUMIT (second half in U.S.) and SIMPONI (first half in EU; potentially second half in U.S.), and (3) impact of voluntary agreement with the U.S. government to be evenly distributed throughout the year. For MedTech, JNJ expects (1) continued acceleration of newly launched products as the year progresses with normalized seasonality, (2) surgery transformation sales headwinds to increase through the year, and (3) additional rounds of VBP in China throughout the year (heavier in 2H26).

2H acceleration expected in both Innovative Medicine and MedTech. Management noted that Innovative Medicine 2H growth would be driven by emerging growth in ICOTYDE, INLEXZO, and TREMFYA with the launch of its IBD indication. MedTech's 2H growth is expected to be driven by continued strength in Orthopaedics, Surgery, and Vision, along with a recovery in Cardiovascular as Abiomed rebounds toward the end of 2026 and China inventory-related headwinds in EP, which created a one-time 400bps impact, subside. JNJ also emphasized the company's slate of Cardiovascular launches in the pipeline expected to accelerate segment growth in 2H.

"...we always had a little bit better revenue growth projected in the second half of the year, irrespective of the extra week of sales that will occur in the fourth quarter. When you think about products that are really in their infancy with respect to ICOTYDE, INLEXZO and just the emergence of TREMFYA continuing to do well with the IBD indications that were received over the last 18 months, there was always that projection. [...] we do expect MedTech to grow better in the second half than the first half as well. We have tempered expectations around the Abiomed business with respect to that. So we see that recovery happening, but probably as we leave this year into next year. So not declines, but more modest growth until our PROTECT IV data comes out."

Within MedTech, management confirmed in a follow-up that all MedTech segments - Orthopedics, Surgery, Vision, and Cardiovascular - are expected to grow faster in 2H26.
