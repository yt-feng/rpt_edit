## June imports posted another strong gain

Imports continued to outperform expectations amid the global AI investment boom. Headline imports rose 36%y/y in June, well above consensus forecast of 26% and accelerating from 27% in May. The strength was driven primarily by mechanical and electrical products, imports of which surged 47.5%y/y, the fastest pace in more than 16 years. Agricultural imports also recovered, with soybean imports returning to growth, while commodity imports slowed noticeably, expanding by around 5%y/y after averaging 15% growth in April-May.

On a volume basis, energy-related imports showed mixed trends. Crude oil imports fell sharply by $41\%$ y/y to 29.3mn tons, the lowest level since October 2016, despite more favorable oil prices during the month. The decline came against continued inventory drawdowns, with China's crude stockpiles falling to 1.2bn barrels by end-June from 1.25bn barrels at end-April, according to Kpler. Looking ahead, there are signs of a recovery in crude demand. Bloomberg reported that some Chinese refiners returned to the market for discounted Middle Eastern crude in early July as producers sought to clear cargo backlogs, with purchases from Saudi Arabia, Iraq, and

the UAE $^{1}$ . In addition, authorities have released fuel export curbs and reportedly instructed major refiners to maintain high fuel output amid recent Middle East tensions $^{2}$ , factors that could support crude imports in the coming months.

In contrast, coal imports surged 29.5% y/y to 42.8mn tons, a five-month high, following tighter domestic coal supply following recent mine-safety inspections. Natural gas imports also improved, rising 3.7% y/y in June from flat growth in May.

Imports of industrial materials were mixed. Iron ore import volume growth rebounded, while copper import growth moderated to 3% y/y from 4.3% previously. Soybean imports increased 10.5% y/y in June after declining 15% in May, with monthly arrivals reaching 13.5mn metric tons, the second-highest level on record after May 2025. Demand for US soybeans appears to be improving. Media reports indicate that China made its largest single-day purchase of US soybeans since November on 8 July $^{3}$ . This follows agreements to expand agricultural trade reached after the mid-May Trump-Xi summit, with the US stating that China would purchase at least USD17bn of US agricultural products annually during 2026-28, in addition to previously announced soybean commitments.
