# What Are US Companies Doing with Cash?

Cash continues its ascent, while the cash/EV ratio and FCF yield moved in the opposite direction, hitting their 20-year lows of 3.3% and 2.6%, respectively. Companies continue to pour money earned and raised into growth capex and consensus believes that margins will continue expanding.

The Russell 1000 cash balance grew to \$2.3 trillion, as its cash/EV ratio continued its path lower to 3.3%. Most of the cash is held in the Information Technology, Consumer Discretionary, and Communication Services sectors. >\$50 billion market cap companies such as ABNB, NKE, NEM, INTU, MDT, EW, VRTX, HPE, ROST, and DDOG have robust levels of cash. Exhibit 5 contains the full list of companies with ample cash balances.

Companies with ample FCF are self-financing and may better withstand any deeper or prolonged corrections in the market. >\$50B market cap companies with expected strong FCF growth include F, HPE, DASH, NKE, NEM, AMGN, DDOG, EW, MDT, and ABNB. ## Screen #1. Fortress Balance Sheets

Here we've identified companies with strong balance sheets and sufficient liquidity, that also generate excess returns over their cost of capital. With liquidity and solvency to run operations and service debt, these stocks could have better downside protection than the average.

## Screening Criteria:

• Russell 1000 companies, excluding Financials, Real Estate, and Utilities sectors

\- Cash / EV >5.0%

\- Positive FCF growth CAGR expected over the next two years, with positive growth in each year

\- $>10.0\%$ return on invested capital expected in each of the next two years

\- Current ratio (current assets / current liabilities) >1.0x

\- Low leverage (debt / equity) <2.5x

• Investment grade credit rating

\- Excludes companies with negative equity

Note: This is a quantitative screen and qualitative assessment is warranted. Additionally, Apple [AAPL] was quantitatively excluded from this screen, despite having one of the strongest balance sheets around. Apple holds \$45.6 billion of cash, \$22.9 billion of short-term securities, and \$78.1 billion of long-term investments, with the majority of its long-term investments held in corporate debt securities (46.3%), mortgage- and asset-backed securities (30.4%), and U.S. Treasuries (14.5%).

## Screen #3. Total Shareholder Return Ideas

Dividends provide current income to investors, but are typically more restrictive than a free standing buyback authorization. Buybacks may indicate that management believes its stock is undervalued and could potentially reduce the total number of shares outstanding. A strong shareholder yield suggests that a company is of higher quality than lower returning alternatives, all else equal.

## Screening Criteria:

• Russell 1000 companies

\- Total shareholder return (dividend yield + share repurchase yield) >7.5%

• LTM dividend coverage ratio >2.0x

\- Positive net income CAGR expected over the next two years

\- Positive FCF CAGR expected over the next two years (non-Financials sector companies)

• Investment grade credit rating

## Cash Efficiency Is Key

The Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) is a heuristic measure that estimates how long it takes a company to convert an inventory investment into cash. Assessing trends in these components can yield insight into working capital and free cash flow trends. Furthermore, CCC may be an underutilized tool by the investment community as academic research has shown CCC's effectiveness in gauging short-term funding risks and as a robust source of alpha (e.g., Wang (2018)).

The Russell 1000's CCC increased to 84 days, up +1 day q/q (+1.1%) and +10 days y/y (+13.3%), with the slowing coming from a less favorable days inventory outstanding.

CCC = days inventory outstanding + days sales outstanding - days payables outstanding where:

\- Days inventory outstanding [DIO] = average inventory / (COGS/365)

\- Days sale outstanding [DSO] = average accounts receivable / (net sales/365)

\- Days payables outstanding [DPO] = average accounts payable / (COGS/365)

## Screen #4. Cash Conversion Ideas

A positive (and/or growing) CCC means that operating cash is needed to finance further growth of the business. On the flip side, a negative CCC means that a company sells and collects cash faster than it needs to pay its suppliers, implying that the company's suppliers are financing the company's operations.

## Cash and Interest

Higher interest rates allow companies with ample reserves to generate extra interest income, but on the other hand, they can also create a more difficult and costly environment for companies that need to issue new debt or refinance existing loans. While interest rates have been trending lower, companies should still be able to earn at least mid-single digits on any cash or marketable securities. Often immaterial and overlooked when forecasting earnings, growing cash balances and higher interest rates could boost earnings and surprise to the upside.
