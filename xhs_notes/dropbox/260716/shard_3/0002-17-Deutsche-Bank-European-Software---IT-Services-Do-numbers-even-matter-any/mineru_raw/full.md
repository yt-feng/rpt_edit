# Do numbers even matter anymore?

Expect a solid reporting season but share prices to be largely driven by factors other than fundamentals

Going into Q2 reporting season, our checks and industry conversations point to a mixed picture with some companies such as SAP likely to see a fairly stable demand environment (despite the challenging geopolitical backdrop) while the picture is very diverse across Engineering Software and IT Services. In Engineering Software, we prefer higher-quality names with stronger recurring revenue profiles and lower cyclical exposure, while in IT Services outcomes are likely to diverge more meaningfully, with limited Middle East exposure leaving players such as Capgemini better shielded from recent geopolitical disruption despite ongoing weakness in discretionary spending. While headline risk from new AI model launches seems to have somewhat subsided (i.e. Software stocks react less) due to already very bearish investor positioning across hedge funds but also long onlies, we do not expect Q2 to be a turning point yet even for names with decent/better-than-feared quarters. The main driver of Software share prices remains positioning and the relative strength of AI Semis/Hardware, in our view, combined with medium-term uncertainty around the financial model for SITS players in an AI world. The latter is likely to change as we go into 2027 where we expect major players such as SAP and Dassault to provide more of a framework around how to model its AI business and financial opportunity towards the end of this decade (and beyond). Yet, for now it remains wait and see with new customer wins and AI contract proof points being what the market should focus on, in our view, in order to gauge success of the AI strategies of the companies within our coverage.

## All eyes on more AI proof points from SAP

Following new AI solutions announcements at Sapphire 2026, we see additional customer proof points and details on commercial success as key from here (and more important than results). Our checks indicate good customer feedback on the new AI offering post Sapphire with the availability of large parts of SAP's AI roadmap to on-prem customers (if they sign a RISE Cloud migration deal) a catalyst for many Cloud migration latecomers to make the move. This should help Cloud growth towards next year and FY28, in our view. SAP will have to provide some incremental KPIs and a more detailed financial framework around the AI opportunity, which is expected to account for \~1/3 of Cloud revenues by 2030, at one point. We would expect this with Q4 results or Sapphire 2027 after the company has been able to sign AI deals based on their new offering for 6-12 months.

## Company previews

## Enterprise Software – SAP remains our top pick

Amadeus (Hold) - weaker volumes, resilient pricing, focus on guidance We expect Amadeus to report Q2 revenue growth of 3.1% cc y/y to E1,665m vs cons at E1,656m, reflecting a materially weaker travel backdrop, particularly in Air Distribution, partly offset by resilient unit revenues and continued cost control. The key issue for the quarter is not only the Q2 volume weakness, but whether trends in June and early July are sufficient to support the FY outlook.

External traffic data point to a clear deterioration through Q2. IATA reported global RPK growth of -3.4% y/y in April and -2.2% y/y in May, following +2.1% y/y in March. The decline was mainly driven by Middle East disruption, with Middle Eastern carrier traffic down 46.6% and 28.4% y/y in April and May, respectively. While June passenger data are not yet published, flight tracking suggests Gulf carrier operations recovered to around 90% of normal levels by mid-June, implying a sequential improvement from a very depressed base. In early June, IATA cut its 2026 passenger traffic growth forecast to 2.1% from 4.9% previously, now substantially below the \~3% level Amadeus had previously indicated could still support the lower end of guidance. While pricing, mix, disruption-related revenues and cost actions provide important offsets, the further escalation in the region has reduced our confidence in the company's ability to deliver within the lower end of its guidance range. We therefore model FY26 revenue growth of 6.5% cc y/y, reflecting a more conservative recovery trajectory than implied by the current outlook. Based on this, we see an increased likelihood that management adopts a more cautious stance on FY26 expectations.

In Air Distribution, we expect bookings to remain under pressure. Q1 benefited from two strong months before a sharp March deterioration, whereas Q2 likely reflects weak activity throughout. While cancellations improved versus March, forward bookings remained subdued and May showed no meaningful rebound. We therefore expect bookings of 111.9m vs cons at 113.9m, down 6% y/y, with revenue per booking of E6.93, up 4.5% cc y/y, resulting in Air Distribution revenue of E773m (vs cons at E772m), down 1.4% cc y/y. In Air IT, we expect PB growth to slow materially versus Q1. PB growth weakened further in April and May showed only modest improvement. June is the key swing factor for whether PB growth is positive or negative for the quarter. We expect PB decline of 0.9% y/y, with revenue per PB of E1.08, up 7.3% cc y/y, resulting in Air IT revenue of E616.5m vs cons at E609.8m, up 6.3% cc y/y. Pricing should remain supportive, although the disruption-related uplift seen in Q1 should moderate. In HOS, we expect revenue of E276.1m vs cons at E276.2m, up 9.8% cc y/y. Growth should slow versus Q1 despite continued inorganic support. While less exposed than Air Distribution and Air IT, the segment is not immune, particularly in transactional areas such as hotel distribution, payments and media. M&A should provide some support, but not enough to offset weaker organic trends. On costs, we expect fixed-cost growth to remain well contained at \~3.6% y/y, supporting Q2 EBITDA of E644m vs cons at E654m and a margin of 38.7%.

Overall, we see Q2 as a trough for volumes, with Air Distribution bookings clearly negative and Air IT PB growth materially below Q1. Resilient unit revenues should cushion the impact, but the focus is on guidance. While some recovery has emerged into June, we do not think this is sufficient to fully de-risk the outlook. As a result, we see an increased likelihood of a more conservative stance on guidance, with the balance of risk skewed to the downside.

ATOSS Software (Buy) – Q2 likely solid, but little to change the narrative ATOSS is due to report its Q2/26 results on 24 July. With the evolving agentic AI paradigm weighing on sentiment and limited near-term evidence of its long-term impact, shares remain stuck in the “disadvantaged by AI” bucket, alongside many other Application Software/SaaS names experiencing a narrative-driven de-rating. This is despite the overall solid Q1/26 print, with fundamentals continuing to contradict the “AI disruption” narrative, as NRR (112%) and GRR remained stable, while Cloud ARR growth of 27% was still robust. The only negative in the Q1/26 print that raised investor questions (based on our recent discussions) was the flat sequential increase in Cloud & Subscription order backlog in absolute terms, despite the completed GTM transformation and S&M hiring in FY25. The company attributed this to a weaker macro environment, though some investors remain cautious as to whether this could represent an early indication of AI-related headwinds, based on our discussions. We therefore expect the market to focus on these KPIs and leading indicators to assess any potential AI impact, with some nervousness likely should ATOSS disappoint here, even if primarily macro-driven.

That said, we anticipate a decent Q2, helped by comps looking more favorable following a weaker Q2 last year. We model revenues growing \~13% to \~E52m, implying a slight acceleration versus Q1 as sales hires from 2025 continue to ramp up, partly offset by a somewhat weaker macro and the industry-wide trend of budget reprioritization. Growth should be driven primarily by Software (Q2e +15%), particularly Cloud (Q2e +26%), supported by Q1 Cloud ARR of \~E110m and Cloud order backlog of \~E114m. We expect Q2 EBIT margins of \~35%, broadly flat sequentially (with Q1 benefiting from a smaller one-off) but remaining above ATOSS's updated FY26 guidance (≥34%).

All considered, we believe shares trading at \~17x FY27E uFCF look attractive for a company with ATOSS' growth profile, continuously expanding margins, and healthy cash conversion. We acknowledge multiples reflect long-term AI uncertainty, likely limiting near-term outperformance absent meaningful estimate revisions, with a decent Q2 likely providing little to shift the near-term AI narrative. That said, we see a significant portion of downside risk as already priced in, and view concerns around the pace of potential AI disruption as overstated, with the market potentially underappreciating the ability of incumbent software vendors to adapt. We maintain our Buy but lower our target price to E100 based on updated peer multiples.

## AutoStore (Hold) – 2026 had a good start to the year...

During its Q1 results which built on the positive momentum of Q425, AutoStore talked about a positive start to 2026 with improving activity levels and increased confidence in long-term automation. In the current geopolitical landscape, this increasingly reflects a growing emphasis on resilient supply chains. While market volatility persists, momentum remained strong across several segments, including retail, 3PL, and industrial with increased demand for high throughput solutions. Growth in North America was particularly strong combined with a deeper level of customer engagement vs. in the past. ## Dassault Systemes (Hold) – Q2 likely broadly in line, but H2-weighted FY26 still carries execution risk

Dassault reports Q2/26 on 23 July, where we expect an in-line quarter, with revenue likely to land around the midpoint of guidance for 2-5% cc growth (DB \~4%). Based on our checks, while the macro backdrop remains fluid and there is still deal-timing sensitivity towards quarter-end, there has so far been no meaningful impact on deal conversions that would lead Dassault to miss on the quarter. That said, if current macro conditions persisted, we would not rule out some macro headwinds in the second half. We expect Medidata to remain in decline in Q2, while Centric growth should normalize from an exceptional Q1, stepping down sequentially but still supporting the expected mid-teens H1 trajectory. Mainstream Innovation should therefore maintain robust growth, underpinned by SolidWorks, while Industrial Innovation should remain stable at mid-single-digit growth, helped by normalizing comps rather than any material underlying softening in demand. While Medidata bookings continue to trend positively, supporting the better H2-vs-H1 thesis, we still expect negative growth in Life Sciences in Q2, with large contracts signed in prior quarters still ramping and not yet moving the needle in terms of revenue growth. On the backdrop of macro uncertainties, we expect Dassault to guide relatively cautiously for Q3 while confirming full-year guidance, putting greater weight on stronger Q4 seasonality and execution to reach at least the upper end of the full year guidance range. The bottom line is we expect Dassault to deliver against relatively modest expectations for Q2, but Q2 itself is unlikely to be a major catalyst for the stock, with investor focus remaining on H2 execution and the CMD in November. While the valuation has taken a substantial beating this year, we believe further proof points around the Medidata H2 acceleration (and sustained growth beyond) are needed to drive a sustained re-rating. Until then and with some macro risks looming we remain on the sidelines and lower our target price to E20, valuing Dassault on a 50/50 blend of FY26 P/E and EV/uFCF of \~15x and \~16x, respectively, at a discount to peers to reflect lower revenue and earnings growth. Hold.

## Hexagon (Hold) – Robotics remains a missed opportunity

Hexagon is set to publish its Q2/26 results on 29 July following completion of the Octave spin-off and recent CMD (see analysis here). For Q2/26, we forecast total revenue of E955m, with 6.1% organic growth in-line with cons at 6.1%. We expect Infrastructure & Geospatial (previously Geosystems) to post 2.0% y/y organic growth with weakness in China likely to persist which could be partially offset by strength in North American while the Middle East situation might continue to weigh on the EMEA region. We expect the Autonomous Solutions division to see 14.0% y/y organic growth as we see strong activity in the Aerospace & Defense sector to continue but with likely some growth moderation against tough comps into H2. For Manufacturing Intelligence, we expect 7.0% y/y organic growth in Q2/26 where we expect the order wins in FY25 to reflect in FY26 revenue while China Auto softness could become more of a headwind into H2, in our view. We do not yet model a contribution from the Robotics divisions in group financials as we await a strategic decision on the direction of this business and more progress on customer wins following an underwhelming update at the CMD that left too many outcomes open in a fast developing market where investors would have appreciated a clear "doubling down" on robotics. In our view, Hexagon should commit to becoming a leading European robotics OEM with investments on the continent and globally in capacity over being a component supplier or partner of other OEMs robots. We view the product (Aeon) as good enough to be successful in this market and believe a strategic commitment by management on robotics would be a strong positive for the share price. Hexagon has given a TAM estimate of \$34b by 2035 based on an external report during the CMD. This includes majority TAM of \$31b for industrial which we see as Aeon's main target market and a remaining \$3b for Home which we believe can be substantially larger but also more competitive and less of a focus for Aeon.

Based on latest DB research (link here), we expect the potential installed base for industrial/manufacturing humanoid robots to be 7.6m units by 2050. The company has earlier announced a successful pilot with Schaeffler followed by agreement to deploy 1,000 Aeon robots. It would be interesting to note updates around other projects with BMW, Pilatus, and Fill as well as further pilots or commercial deals.

On profitability, we forecast EBITAC margin (ex robotics) of \~23% and EBIT margin of 26.5% which is \~270 bps lower y/y due to significant FX headwinds and weaker volumes. For FY26, we expect organic revenue growth of \~5.7% y/y with Autonomous Solutions growth somewhat normalizing against tougher comps. However, we expect significant FX headwinds to result in a y/y decline in reported revenue. In terms of profitability, we expect EBITAC margins to reach \~24.0% and EBIT margins of \~27% which is flat y/y as we expect FX headwinds to offset the benefits from the ongoing cost rationalization program. We keep our estimates mostly unchanged after we updated the model for the Octave spin-off. However, we update the model for FX changes resulting in a 1%/2% change in FY26/FY27 EPS. We also include the Waygate acquisition in our SOTP valuation.

With underlying growth likely moderating somewhat after a strong Q1 and robotics still a side show, we remain on the sidelines for now and rate Hexagon Hold. We value the company using a SOTP and apply a FY27e EV/EBIT multiple of \~18x to Hexagon and -18x for the Waygate acquisition which broadly in-line with European Industrial peers. Hexagon's stake in Cadence is valued at market prices but we understand that the company is reducing this stake currently in the market. Proforma FY27 net debt is adjusted for the Waygate acquisition.

## Mensch und Maschine (Buy) – lower 3-year renewal cohort continues to weigh on growth

Mensch und Maschine is due to report Q2 results on 21 July, which we expect to be another quarter of muted growth. This is despite still favorable comps in Digitization, as Q2 last year was impacted by headwinds related to the Autodesk partner model transition. However, the Autodesk reselling business remains impacted by a smaller cohort of 3-year contract renewals, weighing on growth. On reseller margins, discussions with partners suggest some volatility due to Autodesk's revised partner incentives structure, which increasingly prioritizes new business and selected products over renewals. Given that new business represents a smaller share of MuM's Autodesk reselling, we believe the company is not yet able to fully offset this dynamic, which also represents a drag on growth. That said, we expect a slight sequential acceleration in Digitization, supported by a stronger services contribution. Growth in the Software segment should continue to outpace Digitization, albeit not sufficiently to reach the mid-point of the guidance range of 6–10%. We hence continue to expect FY26 gross profit growth to land below the mid-term target range, at \~5-6%. On profitability, we expect EBIT and EPS to continue to grow disproportionately strong in Q2 on the back of last year's cost-saving measures. However, we note that the exceptionally strong growth in Q1 was supported by a one-off gain related to the disposal of a stake in a SOFiSTiK subsidiary. We hence continue to see FY26 EBIT/EPS growth trending towards the lower end of the 11-19% mid-term ambition, broadly in line with recent management commentary.

## Nemetschek (Buy) - Expecting a guidance update with Q2 to include HCSS

Nemetschek is due to report Q2 results on 30 July. Focus will be on the expected guidance update to include HCSS, as well as further commentary around the acquisition, including implications of Thoma Bravo's minority stake in the B&C segment. On guidance, we expect Nemetschek to maintain its current organic growth outlook of 14–15% cc, with HCSS adding a high-single-digit percentage contribution to FY26 growth (DBe \~9%, although we note some uncertainty around the size of potential revenue haircuts at HCSS). We also expect an update to EBITDA guidance (32-33% pre-HCSS), where we now see Nemetschek coming in at the lower end (DBe \~32%) or potentially even below, due to one-off costs related to the acquisition.

Apart from the guidance update, we expect a decent Q2, again driven primarily by the Build segment, where we model \~24% y/y growth in Q2, driven especially by Bluebeam. In Design, we expect \~8% growth, despite tougher comps from the strong Q2 last year, helped by ongoing demand for multi-year subscription contracts (which allow upfront revenue recognition). In the smaller Manage segment, we model \~4% growth, broadly flat growth sequentially, though this remains dependent on the timing of closing of larger deal in the pipeline. We remain cautious on Media, for which we model only \~2% growth, despite softer comps. On margins, we note that Q2 is usually the softest quarter in terms of profitability and we hence model a Q2 EBITDA margin of $\sim 31\%$ (equating to EBITDA of $\sim$ E103m), also considering some one-off costs related to Nemetschek's recent M&A activity.

While Q2 and Q3 will likely be slightly messy and impacted by one-offs related to the HCSS acquisition (incl. revenue haircuts at HCSS, one-off costs at the EBITDA level, and the impact of Thoma Bravo's minority stake in B&C on minorities at Nemetschek Group level), we see long-term value in the HCSS acquisition. In addition, we remain positive on the growth opportunity in Bluebeam, both from user growth, with Bluebeam's internationalization still in the early innings, and from ARPU growth through upselling to Bluebeam Max (see our recent in-depth notes on these topics here and here). Buy.

## Ocado (Buy) – Expected to be on track, investor focus also on recently announced leadership transition

## DB forecasts in line with guidance

As a reminder, for FY26, the group is guiding for c. £500m of Tech Solutions revenue and c. 30% adj. EBITDA margin. Logistics is expected to deliver c. £30-35m of adj. EBITDA on the back of mid-to high-single-digit revenue growth. Note the Tech Solutions guidance reflects c. 10 new modules expected in FY26, offsetting the 12 modules from the Kroger and Sobeys closures in early 2026. The group then expects a further 10-15 modules in FY27. Overall, 6 CFCs are expected to go live over the next 2-3 years. Focus on the cost base remains strong with total cash costs across Technology and Support in FY27 to reduce by around £150m relative to FY25. The group has reiterated that it expects to turn cash flow positive during H2'26 - but that for underlying cash outflow to still be c.£(200)m for the year as a whole. It expects to be full-year cash flow positive in FY27. Our forecasts are essentially in line with these forecasts. Note that FY26 revenue and adj. EBITDA figures will be impacted by the accounting treatment of the Kroger Letter of Credit and Kroger and Sobeys CFC closure fees. The impact on FY26 revenue and adj. EBITDA is c £350m.
