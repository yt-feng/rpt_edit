# Sac-TMT Has Just Answered the Most Important Question About Merck’s Post-Keytruda Strategy

On the back of a single interim analysis from a China-based Phase 3 trial, the strategic calculus for Merck & Co. has shifted in a material way. Kelun-Biotech’s OptiTROP-Lung06 study has demonstrated that the combination of sac-TMT (a TROP2-directed antibody-drug conjugate) and Keytruda yields significantly prolonged progression-free survival compared to the current standard of care—Keytruda plus chemotherapy—in first-line non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer patients with PD-L1 TPS below 1%. This is not merely another positive data point. It is the first direct, randomized evidence that a sac-TMT-based regimen can outperform the Keytruda/chemo doublet, which is the backbone of first-line lung cancer treatment globally. For a company facing the inevitability of Keytruda’s loss of exclusivity later this decade, this result provides the clearest signal yet that its pipeline successor may not be a placeholder—it may be an upgrade.

The significance of this trial lies in its control arm. Earlier successes, such as OptiTROP-Lung05 in the PD-L1 TPS ≥1% population, compared sac-TMT plus Keytruda against Keytruda monotherapy. That was a lower bar. Lung06, by contrast, pits the experimental combination directly against the most widely used chemotherapy-containing regimen in the field. By clearing this hurdle at a pre-specified interim analysis, sac-TMT has moved from a promising asset to a pipeline-defining one. The overall survival trend is already positive, though not yet mature. The quantitative details will emerge at ESMO in October, but the directional outcome is now established.

This development arrives at a moment when the research community has been examining a specific question: how will Merck defend the PD-L1 TPS <50% segment of the first-line NSCLC market after Keytruda loses exclusivity? The company’s own commentary at a recent investor conference flagged the Lung06 readout as a key de-risking event. That event has now occurred. The implications extend beyond a single trial into the architecture of Merck’s entire oncology franchise.

## The PD-L1 TPS <1% Population Was the Highest Hurdle, and Sac-TMT Cleared It

The first-line NSCLC market is segmented by PD-L1 expression levels, and the competitive dynamics differ sharply across those segments. In the TPS ≥50% population, Keytruda monotherapy already sets a high bar, and biosimilar erosion will be gradual because the regimen is simple. In the TPS 1-49% population, the standard is Keytruda plus chemotherapy, and the advantage of adding a third agent must be clinically meaningful to justify incremental toxicity. But the TPS <1% population is the hardest to crack. These patients derive the least benefit from immunotherapy alone, and the chemotherapy backbone is essential. Any regimen that improves upon Keytruda/chemo in this subgroup is, by definition, a first-line standard-of-care challenger.

The Lung06 result validates sac-TMT’s mechanism in precisely the patient population where incremental innovation is hardest to prove. This is not a marginal improvement in a favorable subgroup; it is a direct win in the toughest comparator setting. For Merck, this means the sac-TMT/Keytruda combination now has a clinical argument that can be extended into the TPS 1-49% segment as well, where the Lung05 data already showed superiority over Keytruda alone. The combined evidence creates a unified narrative: sac-TMT plus Keytruda is superior to Keytruda with or without chemotherapy, depending on the expression level. That is a commercially potent claim.

## The Trial Design Itself Sends a Strategic Signal About Merck’s Regulatory Intent

Merck chose to run OptiTROP-Lung06 in China through its partner Kelun, not as a global study. That decision is instructive. China-based Phase 3 trials with positive results can support registration in China and, depending on the data package and ethnic sensitivity analyses, may also support filings in other regions. But the primary strategic value of this trial is not jurisdictional. It is about generating proof-of-concept data that can be used to design a definitive global Phase 3 program with a high probability of success.

By demonstrating superiority over Keytruda/chemo in a randomized setting, Merck now has the confidence to run a global trial with the same design, knowing the effect size is likely to be replicated. The alternative—running a global trial without this signal—would have carried substantial binary risk. The Kelun partnership, which some observers viewed as a second-tier arrangement, has now delivered the highest-value derisking data in Merck’s pipeline. This should reframe how the market values both the asset and the partnership structure.

## The Competitive Landscape Just Became More Difficult for Challengers

Several other developers are pursuing TROP2-directed ADCs and combination regimens in first-line NSCLC. The most closely watched external readouts in the near term include Summit Therapeutics’ Harmoni-3 trial and AstraZeneca’s AVANZAR trial, both of which are testing their own approaches in similar populations. Before Lung06, the field was open. Each contender could argue that its molecule had a differentiated safety profile or a novel mechanism of action that might yield a better therapeutic index.

Now, Merck has set a benchmark. Any future positive readout in the PD-L1 TPS <1% population will be compared not to Keytruda/chemo alone, but to the emerging sac-TMT/Keytruda standard. This raises the bar for what constitutes a clinically meaningful improvement. Late entrants will need to show superiority over a regimen that has already beaten the prior standard of care. The hurdle for differentiation just got higher, and the timeline for catching up just got longer.

For Merck, this creates a window of competitive insulation that extends beyond the patent life of Keytruda. Even if biosimilar PD-1 inhibitors enter the market in the late 2020s, the sac-TMT/Keytruda combination is a proprietary fixed-dose or co-administered regimen that cannot be easily replicated by biosimilar manufacturers. The ADC component is the barrier to entry, not the checkpoint inhibitor.

Observers evaluating Merck’s post-Keytruda thesis should now apply a structured framework that weights pipeline assets by their ability to generate revenue independent of Keytruda’s patent status. The framework has three tiers.

If the ESMO data confirm a statistically significant and clinically meaningful benefit, sac-TMT could become the backbone of Merck’s lung cancer franchise in the post-LOE era. The key question is whether the effect size is large enough to support a label expansion into TPS 1-49% and ≥50% populations, which would maximize the addressable patient pool.

The sac-TMT data do not directly affect these, but they reduce the pressure on management to pursue large-scale M&A to fill the revenue gap. ## The Key Risk Is No Longer Clinical—It Is Commercial Execution and Competitive Timing

With the clinical risk substantially reduced, the remaining uncertainties shift to execution and competition. On the execution side, Merck must now demonstrate that it can manufacture sac-TMT at scale, file for regulatory approval in a timely manner, and secure favorable pricing and access. The partnership with Kelun introduces a layer of complexity, but the Chinese biotech has already demonstrated clinical development capability. The question is whether it can also deliver commercial-scale supply.

On the competitive side, the most immediate threat is not from another TROP2 ADC but from bispecific antibodies and next-generation checkpoint inhibitors that may render the PD-1/ADC combination obsolete. AstraZeneca’s AVANZAR trial and Summit’s Harmoni-3 trial are the two most important watchpoints. If either of those programs generates data that match or exceed the Lung06 results, the competitive window for sac-TMT narrows. However, the Lung06 data give Merck a first-mover advantage in generating direct comparative evidence against the Keytruda/chemo standard. That advantage is not easily erased.

The regulatory environment also warrants attention. The FDA’s evolving stance on accelerated approvals, confirmatory trials, and ethnic diversity requirements could affect the speed of global registration. Merck will need to decide whether to file in the US based on the Chinese data alone or to wait for a global confirmatory study. The Lung06 result makes the latter option lower-risk, but the former option could accelerate time-to-market by two to three years.
