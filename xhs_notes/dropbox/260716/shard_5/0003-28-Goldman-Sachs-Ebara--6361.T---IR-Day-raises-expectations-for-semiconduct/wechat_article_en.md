## Precision Machinery Demand Has Already Broken Out Above the Medium-Term Plan, Creating a Clear Guidance Upgrade Catalyst

The most immediate and quantifiable takeaway from the IR Day is that Ebara's precision machinery business is operating in a demand environment that has surpassed the assumptions embedded in its own medium-term plan. The company's February plan assumed an 8% CAGR for the overall WFE market. At the IR Day, management revised this view upward, noting that demand looks strong into 2026-2027 and that the CMP systems market specifically could grow at a rate of 9% or higher. This is not a subtle distinction. A one-percentage-point difference in market growth, when compounded over several years and applied to a business with high operating leverage, produces a material earnings impact.

The evidence is already visible in the order book. Precision machinery orders in the first quarter of fiscal 2026 totaled JPY 151.2 billion, compared to the segment's full-year order guidance of JPY 488.1 billion. To put that in perspective, the quarterly order run rate would need to be roughly JPY 122 billion to hit the full-year target. The first quarter delivered 24% above that run rate. Even allowing for normal quarterly volatility in large-ticket semiconductor equipment orders, this level of early momentum strongly suggests that the company will need to raise its full-year guidance for precision machinery orders, sales, and profits when it reports second-quarter results. The company has already expanded production capacity at its Kumamoto plant with the completion of the K3 building, and management indicated that further capacity investments are under consideration. When a company is already building capacity ahead of demand and then sees demand accelerate, the natural outcome is upward revision.

The pricing strategy further reinforces the margin story. Ebara acknowledged the difficulty of raising prices on identical products in an inflationary environment, but it is pursuing a more sophisticated approach: increasing the value-add of its equipment to lift average selling prices. This is not a cost-pass-through mechanism. It is a product strategy shift that, if successful, allows the company to improve margins without fighting customers on price. In a segment where operating margins are already projected to rise from 16.9% in fiscal 2025 to 26.0% by fiscal 2028, even modest ASP improvements compound into significant profit expansion.

## The Energy Segment Is Recovering from a Cyclical Trough, but Half of Its Margin Improvement Will Come from Internal Actions Independent of Revenue Growth

The energy segment presents a different but equally compelling margin story. On the surface, the numbers look underwhelming. Fiscal 2026 guidance calls for lower sales and profits compared to fiscal 2025, reflecting a sluggish period for product orders. Operating profit margins in the segment are expected to dip from 11.9% to 8.8% before recovering to 11.3% by fiscal 2028. A superficial reading would suggest this is a cyclical business waiting for a market recovery. The IR Day revealed a more nuanced picture.

The company has already secured orders for relatively high-margin upstream and midstream projects in the Middle East for fiscal 2026, and multiple additional projects are anticipated. This means the fiscal 2026 trough is already backfilled with profitable work, and the recovery trajectory beyond that is visible. More importantly, management explicitly stated that roughly half of the targeted margin improvement from 11.9% in fiscal 2025 to 14.5% by fiscal 2028 will come from internal efforts, not from sales expansion. These internal measures include transitioning to electric motors for powering compressors in the LNG field, automation investments and delivery time reductions at US manufacturing sites, and optimizing service and support locations to be closer to customer operations.

This distinction matters for observers. A margin recovery driven solely by cyclical demand pickup is difficult to forecast and subject to commodity price risk—in this case, crude oil and LNG prices. A margin improvement driven by structural cost reductions and operational efficiency is more durable and more predictable. The company is not waiting for the market to save it. It is reshaping its cost base so that when the cycle does turn, margins will expand from a lower breakeven point. The downside risk, which the company itself acknowledges, is that a sustained decline in crude oil or LNG prices could delay project awards. But the internal margin drivers are designed to work regardless of where energy prices settle.

## Building Service and Industrial Margins Are Set to Rise through a Dual Strategy of Data Center Exposure and Service Revenue Expansion

The building service and industrial segment has historically been the laggard in Ebara's portfolio, with operating margins of just 6.3% in fiscal 2025. The medium-term plan targets a gradual improvement to 7.9% by fiscal 2028, which seems modest in absolute terms but represents a 25% increase in margin rate. The IR Day clarified that this improvement will come from two distinct and mutually reinforcing strategies.

First, the company is repositioning its product mix toward higher-growth end markets, specifically data centers and semiconductors. Chillers and cooling pumps for data centers carry higher margins than general building service products, and demand in this segment is structurally growing as AI and cloud computing drive data center construction globally. This is not a speculative bet. The company is already executing on this strategy, and the benefits are expected to emerge gradually from fiscal 2026 onward. Second, Ebara is aggressively expanding its service and support revenue as a percentage of segment sales. The S&S ratio was approximately 23% in fiscal 2025, and the company aims to reach 30% in the medium term. The margin arithmetic is compelling: profit margins on services are roughly 1.5 to 2 times higher than margins on products. Every percentage point increase in the service mix directly lifts segment margins.

The company is already demonstrating progress. The S&S ratio has risen by a few percentage points through wider adoption of the EBARA Maintenance Cloud and other digital tools. This is not a theoretical plan. It is a measurable shift that is already underway. The combination of a higher-value product mix and a rising service component creates a margin trajectory that is less dependent on overall construction activity and more dependent on the installed base of Ebara equipment, which is large and growing. The segment also benefits from the company's ongoing efficiency improvements in existing businesses, including structural optimization in response to the slowdown in the Chinese construction market. Management is not simply cutting costs. It is reallocating resources toward higher-return activities.

This framework leads to a specific conclusion: the margin expansion is not a single-point forecast but a multi-year process with identifiable milestones. When all three segments are moving in the same direction, the probability of the company achieving its fiscal 2028 target of 17.5% group operating margins is materially higher than the market currently prices.
