EN_A: So, TSMC just reported another strong quarter, but the market reaction seems a bit mixed. What’s the real story here?
EN_B: The headline numbers were great. Revenue and gross margins hit the top end of guidance, and operating margins crossed 60% for the first time. But the big surprise was the 2026 revenue guidance upgrade to over 40% year-on-year growth, and a capex hike to 60 to 64 billion dollars. AI demand commentary was also very strong, with management signaling growth could stay robust into 2029 to 2030.
EN_A: That all sounds positive. So why might the stock feel some pressure?
EN_B: It comes down to gross margins. The actual number was 67.7%, but some of the more bullish expectations, including our own, were around 69.5%. The gap is mainly because N2 depreciation kicked in earlier than expected, plus some dilution from overseas fabs. So in the very short term, the lack of a big margin surprise could weigh on the stock.
EN_A: So does that mean margins have peaked?
EN_B: The report argues it’s a one-off reset. As N3 profitability continues to improve in the second half of the year, and with very high utilization rates and better operational efficiency, gross margins should settle back in the high 60% range over the next few quarters. So the long-term trend is still upward.
EN_A: What about AI demand? Any new insights there?
EN_B: Management was quite confident. They raised their AI accelerator compound annual growth rate forecast to the mid-to-high 50% range. And they specifically noted strong signals from customers around agentic AI, covering accelerators, CPUs, networking chips, and memory base dies. This suggests AI demand could keep growing very strongly well into 2029 and 2030.
EN_A: The capex increase is huge. Does the report address concerns about over-investment?
EN_B: Yes, it does. TSMC laid out its long-term capacity planning methodology in detail. They explained how they account for overly bullish customer demand forecasts, and also watch for other bottlenecks like data center rollout and power supply. That should ease some investor worries about a potential inventory correction or overbuild in the medium term.
EN_A: And what about competition? How confident is TSMC?
EN_B: Very confident. They reiterated they will maintain their leading-edge market share, and announced another 100 billion dollars in US investment. Interestingly, they welcome new players in advanced packaging, like Intel EMIB, because there’s a severe shortage. But they pushed back hard on the idea that OSATs could use packaging as a gateway into foundry services.
EN_A: Are there any key questions the report leaves unanswered?
EN_B: Two big ones. First, while long-term margins look good, the N2 ramp in the second half of 2026 will drag gross margins by 3 to 4 percentage points. Whether N3 improvement can fully offset that is still to be seen. Second, non-AI mature node demand remains weak, and consumer and smartphone seasonality might be less pronounced. The report doesn’t fully quantify that impact.
