# A Capex Reprioritization - 5 Key Takeaways

IBM saw companies re-prioritize capex budgets towards spending bottlenecks in 2Q - in this case towards fast-inflating enterprise server/storage products, at the cost of Mainframe HW, TP, Data and Automation SW. The persistence of this trend is the go-forward risk for IBM.

## Key Takeaways

2Q revs came in 5% short of MSe, with SW and Infra both missing and Consulting in-line. Negative mix drove GM miss while cost initiatives protected PTI margin.

\- Challenged in the Q: IBM Z, Transaction Processing, Data, and Automation SW. Solid in the Q: RHT up 11% Y/Y, HashiCorp, and Confluent.

IBM views 2Q dynamics as a short-term optimization of capex decisions vs. a prevailing trend. 2Q slippage amounted to a few big deals.

■ Directional confirmation of enterprise server/storage and HDD spending strength- CDW, DELL, HPE, INGM, NTAP, P, SNX, STX (Top Pick), WDC.

■ Floor in stock? Using intra-day multiples, our regression-based FCF approach points to \$207; SOTP points to \$164-219. Question is what is right EPS/FCF base.

An example of how the memory 'supercycle' is de-prioritizing broad categories of spending. In speaking to management - the high-level cause of today's negative pre-announcement is clear. Customers making large capex purchases viewed memory-laden storage and servers as a greater spending priority this quarter - and thus downsized their ELA purchases across mainframe hardware and transaction processing, data and automation software - by delaying deals which are typically back-end-loaded in the quarter. If this issue - memory inflation causing a reprioritization of enterprise compute/storage spend - were isolated to 2Q, we could look at today's stock move and theorize the negative risk event has passed. But as we argued in IT Hardware: The Enterprise Compute Shortage - Pull-Forward or Paradigm Shift?, memory prices are being viewed as a structural/multi-year headwind, and companies are persistently pulling forward compute/storage spend as a result, which means this same type of capex re-prioritization could theoretically happen in 2H26, and into CY27, as well. Furthermore, we'd highlight that despite shares falling 25% intra-day, at \$215 IBM is trading back to where it was in mid-May 2026, though on fundamentals that presumably just took a step lower. So it's hard to look at today as the floor in the stock right now. We have more to learn next week at earnings, but we wouldn't be surprised to see shares remain weak near-term given (1) the persistence of the memory headwind, and (2) the broad-based impact this spending shift could have on IBM spend if longer-lasting. We share 5 key thoughts and implications below.

\- What this means for some of the 2Q (or full year) segment KPIs. Based on our conversation with IR, we estimate IBM Z revenue fell 40-50% Y/Y in 2Q. RHT growth of 11% Y/Y was a positive surprise, but Data, Automation and Transaction Processing - which are purchased as part of ELAs - were weaker than expected. We suspect TP was likely down closer to 10% Y/Y in 2Q (and given a 70% annuity / 30% transactional split, the transactional part of TP was likely down closer to 50% Y/Y). Given mgmt's positive commentary on HashiCorp and Confluent performance in the quarter, is appears the Software weakness in 2Q was entirely organic, and thus organic software growth in the quarter was likely close to 0% Y/Y. Organically, this means total IBM revenue growth in 2Q was negative Y/Y for the first time in several quarters. Finally, FCF through 1H26 is \$4.8B, flat Y/Y. In 2023 (same point for z-cycle), 1H FCF represented 31% of full year FCF. Assuming similar seasonality this year would imply \$15.5B of FCF in 2026, below guidance for \~\$15.7B.

\- Directionally correct on 2Q preview for Power/Storage outperformance - a positive read-through/confirmation of enterprise compute and storage strength. Part of our 2Q Preview thesis played out this quarter, in that Distributed Infrastructure growth was robust at +37% Y/Y. Without Mainframe weakness, this would have driven Infra upside in the quarter. Nevertheless, we believe the strength in 2Q Infrastructure spend is a positive read-through to enterprise server and storage spending, where demand is currently inelastic and taking share of IT budgets. Direct beneficiaries: DELL, HPE, P, NTAP, SNX, CDW and INGM. Indirect beneficiaries: STX (our Top Pick) and WDC.

\- Qualitatively, what we are hearing from IBM. IBM's perspective is that there was a short-term optimization of capex towards the end of 2Q ("a few days in the quarter") that reprioritized spend to Power and Storage. This primarily impacted ELAs/3 year capex purchases. IR was clear - they do not believe the 2Q miss in Mainframe was related to any pull-in of demand in 2025, nor did they see any Mainframe migrations this quarter. They pointed to other areas of spending strength - RedHat, Confluent, HashiCorp - as evidence that this issue was isolated to products purchased disproportionately through ELA's. But they also noted that the weakness in TP purchased via ELAs more than offset an lagged capacity growth from prior z17 placements that could have benefitted TP. The IBM team is still working through the pipeline (quality/size), and looking at deals that slipped, etc. to determine any broader impact of the capex reprioritization.

\- Where valuation stands today and where a floor in the stock could lie.
