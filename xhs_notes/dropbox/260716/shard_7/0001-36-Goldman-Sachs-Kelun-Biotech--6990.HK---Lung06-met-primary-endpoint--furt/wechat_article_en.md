# One Positive Phase 3 Readout Has Reshaped the Debate Over sac-TMT's Potential to Redefine First-Line NSCLC Treatment

On July 15, Kelun Biotech announced that OptiTROP-Lung06, a China phase 3 trial evaluating sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab in first-line non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer patients with PD-L1 TPS below 1%, met its primary endpoint of progression-free survival. In a head-to-head comparison against the current standard of care—pembrolizumab plus pemetrexed and platinum chemotherapy—the sac-TMT combination demonstrated significantly prolonged PFS and a positive overall survival trend with a manageable safety profile. This is not merely another positive data point. It is the first direct evidence that a TROP2 antibody-drug conjugate combined with an immune checkpoint inhibitor can outperform the KEYNOTE-189 regimen in the most difficult-to-treat segment of the first-line NSCLC population. The implications extend far beyond this single trial, altering the strategic calculus for global development plans that have long been the subject of intense debate.

The significance of Lung06 becomes clear only when placed alongside its predecessor, OptiTROP-Lung05, which reported a PFS hazard ratio of 0.35 in patients with PD-L1 TPS of 1% or higher at ASCO 2026. Lung05 compared sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab against pembrolizumab monotherapy. Lung06 now compares the same sac-TMT combination against pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy—the backbone of first-line treatment for decades. Together, these two trials create a contiguous evidence base spanning the full PD-L1 expression spectrum. The question that has divided oncology strategists is no longer whether sac-TMT has activity in first-line NSCLC, but whether its magnitude of benefit is sufficient to justify global phase 3 trials targeting the entire KEYNOTE-189 population, including the roughly 40% of patients with PD-L1 TPS below 1%. Lung06 provides the strongest affirmative signal to date.

The mechanism behind this result deserves careful attention. Sac-TMT is a TROP2-directed ADC with a novel design that includes a belotecan-derivative payload and a stable, pH-sensitive linker. In prior early-stage data, the combination of sac-TMT with a PD-1 inhibitor produced PFS of 12.4 months in the TPS-below-1% subgroup. That figure, while encouraging, came from a phase 2 trial using a different PD-1 partner. The Lung06 result now confirms the superiority in a randomized phase 3 setting against the active comparator that defines the current standard. The key question for observers and strategists is what this means for the probability of success in a global phase 3 trial, and how the emerging data should inform portfolio decisions across the ADC and immuno-oncology landscape.

## The Lung06 Result Closes a Critical Evidence Gap, Shifting the Debate from Feasibility to Magnitude of Superiority

Before Lung06, the strategic conversation around sac-TMT in first-line NSCLC revolved around two uncertainties. The first was whether the combination could beat the pembrolizumab-chemotherapy doublet, which has been the global standard since KEYNOTE-189 established its superiority over chemotherapy alone. The second was whether any observed benefit would be large enough to justify the expense and risk of a global registration program. Lung05 had already shown a compelling PFS hazard ratio of 0.35 against pembrolizumab monotherapy in the TPS-1%-and-above population, but that left open the question of incremental benefit over chemo-containing regimens. Lung06 now supplies the missing comparator.

The reported PFS superiority in Lung06 is statistically significant, and the positive OS trend provides additional confidence. The control arm in Lung06 is exactly the regimen used in KEYNOTE-189: pembrolizumab plus pemetrexed and platinum. This is not a straw-man comparator. It is the regimen that has generated billions in revenue and remains the backbone of first-line treatment for non-squamous NSCLC regardless of PD-L1 expression. Beating this regimen in the TPS-below-1% subgroup—the segment with the lowest expected benefit from immunotherapy—raises the bar for what sac-TMT can achieve across the entire PD-L1 spectrum.

The magnitude question remains open until detailed data are presented at an upcoming medical meeting, likely ESMO. Based on the phase 2 data showing 12.4 months of PFS in the TPS-below-1% subgroup with sac-TMT plus a PD-1 inhibitor, and the phase 3 Lung05 data suggesting 13 to 15 months of PFS in the TPS-1-to-49% subgroup, a reasonable expectation for Lung06 median PFS is in the range of 9 to 12 months. The lower end of that range reflects the inherent challenge of treating TPS-below-1% patients, who derive less benefit from checkpoint inhibition alone. The key comparison is against the historical PFS of pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy in this subgroup, which typically ranges from 7 to 9 months. Even at the lower end of the expected range, the hazard ratio would represent a clinically meaningful improvement.

## The Strength of the Lung05 Subgroup Data in PD-L1 1-49% Patients Provides a Second Anchor for the Global Development Thesis

While Lung06 addresses the TPS-below-1% population, Lung05 contains a signal that may prove equally important for the global strategy. In the Lung05 subgroup analysis, the PFS hazard ratio for sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab monotherapy in patients with PD-L1 TPS of 1 to 49% was 0.28. This is an unusually strong effect size. The estimated median PFS of 13 to 15 months in this subgroup compares favorably with historical data for pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy doublets, which have shown median PFS in the range of 7 to 9 months.

The PD-L1 1-to-49% population is the most commercially important segment in first-line NSCLC. It represents a large portion of the addressable patient population, and it is the group for which the optimal treatment backbone remains debated. Current guidelines allow for either pembrolizumab monotherapy or pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, with no clear consensus on which patients should receive the doublet. A therapy that could deliver PFS in the 13-to-15-month range with a single combination partner—eliminating the need for chemotherapy—would represent a meaningful advance. The Lung05 data suggest that sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab may achieve exactly that.

The combination of Lung05 and Lung06 creates a coherent narrative across the PD-L1 spectrum. In the TPS-1-to-49% group, the data suggest that sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab can outperform pembrolizumab monotherapy by a wide margin and may approach or exceed the efficacy of chemo-containing regimens. In the TPS-below-1% group, Lung06 now shows direct superiority over the chemo-containing standard. The only remaining question is whether the hazard ratio in the TPS-below-1% group is large enough to support a global phase 3 trial powered for overall survival, which is the endpoint regulators prefer for first-line NSCLC registration.

## Management's Decision on the Global Phase 3 Design Will Reveal the Strategic Bet on Either PD-1 or PD-1/VEGF as the Combination Partner

Kelun Biotech management has stated that the global phase 3 trials for the PD-L1 1-to-49% population hinge on a single decision: whether to combine sac-TMT with a PD-1 inhibitor alone or with a PD-1/VEGF bispecific. This choice reflects a deeper strategic question about how to position the asset in a competitive landscape that includes both checkpoint inhibitors and anti-angiogenic agents.

A PD-1-only combination would be simpler, cheaper, and easier to execute. It would align with the Lung05 data, which used pembrolizumab as the partner. The risk is that a PD-1-only combination may not achieve the overall survival benefit needed to displace the current standard in a global registration trial, particularly if the comparator arm includes chemotherapy. A PD-1/VEGF combination could potentially drive deeper and more durable responses by targeting both the immune microenvironment and tumor vasculature. The trade-off is increased complexity, potential for additive toxicity, and a longer development timeline.

The Lung06 result may tip the balance toward the PD-1-only approach. If the sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab combination can beat pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy in the TPS-below-1% population, the argument for adding a VEGF inhibitor becomes weaker. The data already show that the ADC plus checkpoint inhibitor is sufficient to outperform the current standard. Adding another mechanism may improve efficacy further, but it also introduces regulatory and manufacturing complexity that could delay the program. The more conservative path—repeating the Lung06 design in a global population—now appears more viable than it did before the July 15 announcement.

## A Decision Framework for Assessing sac-TMT's Probability of Success in Global First-Line NSCLC Trials

For strategists evaluating Kelun Biotech or the broader ADC landscape, the Lung05 and Lung06 data can be translated into a structured decision framework with three key inputs.

The first input is the magnitude of the PFS benefit in the TPS-below-1% population. The detailed Lung06 data at ESMO will provide the exact hazard ratio and median PFS. If the hazard ratio is below 0.70 and the median PFS exceeds 10 months, the case for a global phase 3 trial targeting the full KEYNOTE-189 population becomes strong. If the hazard ratio is between 0.70 and 0.80, the trial may still be positive but the powering for overall survival becomes more challenging, potentially requiring a larger sample size or longer follow-up.

The second input is the consistency of the benefit across subgroups. The Lung05 subgroup data showed a particularly strong effect in the TPS-1-to-49% group, with a hazard ratio of 0.28. If the Lung06 data show a similar pattern—stronger benefit in higher PD-L1 expression within the TPS-below-1% group—that would support the hypothesis that sac-TMT enhances checkpoint inhibitor activity across the expression spectrum. If the benefit is concentrated in a narrow subgroup, the global development case weakens.

The third input is the safety profile. Lung06 reported no new safety findings, which is encouraging. But the combination of a TROP2 ADC with a checkpoint inhibitor carries risks of interstitial lung disease, stomatitis, and hematologic toxicity. A global phase 3 trial will require rigorous safety monitoring and may need to exclude patients with certain comorbidities. The tolerability data from Lung06, when fully disclosed, will inform whether the combination is suitable for the broader, less selected patient population typical of global registrational trials.

These three inputs—magnitude, consistency, and tolerability—form the basis for updating the probability of success for the global development program. The current probability of success for the ex-China PD-L1 TPS-below-50% indication has been raised from 54% to 63% based on the Lung06 result. Further adjustments will depend on the detailed data.

## The Broader Implications for the ADC Landscape Extend Beyond Kelun Biotech into Competitive Positioning and Standard-of-Care Evolution

The Lung06 result has implications that reach beyond Kelun Biotech's valuation and into the competitive dynamics of the ADC and immuno-oncology fields. TROP2 is one of the most intensively targeted antigens in oncology, with multiple ADCs in development from different sponsors. The sac-TMT data now establish that a TROP2 ADC can be combined with a checkpoint inhibitor to improve outcomes over the current standard in first-line NSCLC. This creates a benchmark that other TROP2 ADCs will need to meet or exceed.

For the field as a whole, the Lung06 result validates the hypothesis that ADCs can serve as chemotherapy replacements rather than mere additions. The sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab combination replaces platinum-based chemotherapy entirely. If this approach is confirmed in a global phase 3 trial, it could redefine the first-line NSCLC treatment paradigm, shifting from a chemotherapy-plus-immunotherapy backbone to an ADC-plus-immunotherapy backbone. This would have downstream effects on the use of platinum agents, the role of pemetrexed maintenance therapy, and the sequencing of subsequent treatments.

The result also reinforces the strategic value of partnerships between ADC developers and checkpoint inhibitor holders. The sac-TMT program has benefited from Merck's involvement, both as a collaborator and as a top shareholder in Kelun Biotech. The ability to combine sac-TMT with pembrolizumab—the most widely used checkpoint inhibitor globally—provides a commercial advantage that would be difficult to replicate with a less established PD-1 inhibitor. For other ADC developers, the message is clear: access to a leading checkpoint inhibitor is not optional; it is a prerequisite for competing in the first-line NSCLC setting.
