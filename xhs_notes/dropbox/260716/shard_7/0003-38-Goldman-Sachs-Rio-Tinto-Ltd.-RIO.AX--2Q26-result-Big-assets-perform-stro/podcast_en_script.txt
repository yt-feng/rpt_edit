EN_A: Rio Tinto just released its second quarter operations report. The market seems to have mixed feelings. What is this report really telling us?
EN_B: It is a quarter of good news and bad news. The good news is iron ore and copper production remain strong. The bad news is copper output fell 7 percent quarter on quarter, and operating cash flow was eaten up by a 1.2 billion dollar working capital build.
EN_A: Wait, copper production dropped, but the company cut its copper cost guidance. That sounds contradictory.
EN_B: That is exactly the core question this report tries to answer. The drop in copper output was mainly due to two issues at Kennecott. One was geotechnical problems that forced a mine resequencing. The other was a flash converting furnace breach in June that needs about 75 days to rebuild. That will hurt second half production. But the cost guidance cut was driven by higher gold prices and productivity gains. So production and costs are two different stories.
EN_A: Got it. What about iron ore? Rio has always been an iron ore giant. How did it perform this time?
EN_B: Iron ore was the real highlight. Second quarter Pilbara sales hit 85.3 million tonnes, the highest since 2020. First half production was the best since 2018. This means Rio’s iron ore business is recovering from its previous slump. And realized pricing was slightly above expectations. However, the company noted that due to several major capital projects, port outload capacity will drop below 360 million tonnes per year in some quarters of the next two years.
EN_A: Does that affect capacity? Sounds like a potential bottleneck.
EN_B: It is a point to watch. But Rio’s medium term guidance remains 345 to 360 million tonnes per year. These projects are meant to improve system flexibility and prepare for the future Rhodes Ridge mine. Also, the Simandou project is progressing. Second quarter production rose 16 percent quarter on quarter, but it is still constrained by temporary crushing facilities. First customer shipments happened in April, but full ramp up to 60 million tonnes per year is not expected until the second half of 2028.
EN_A: Let us talk about copper. Besides Kennecott, how did other copper mines perform?
EN_B: Oyu Tolgoi was the star. Its copper concentrate output grew 12 percent year on year in the second quarter. First half growth was 31 percent. The mine is ramping up steadily toward its target of 500,000 tonnes per year by 2028 to 2036. Escondida also improved, thanks to better leaching performance. But overall, Rio did not change its full year copper production guidance, because some unsold matte will be converted into cathode and contribute to 2027 cash flow.
EN_A: So the market should still have confidence in the long term copper story?
EN_B: Yes, but there are short term disruptions. Also, Resolution copper has started surface drilling. Underground drilling is scheduled to begin in the third quarter. That is a very long term project, but Rio is making progress step by step.
EN_A: Besides iron ore and copper, how did aluminum and lithium do?
EN_B: Aluminum production was flat, but bauxite output recovered 14 percent quarter on quarter. Rio has one of the highest margin low emission aluminum businesses in the world. Over 2.2 million tonnes of capacity is powered by hydro. A 10 percent move in aluminum price would add about 1.1 billion dollars to EBITDA. On lithium, lithium carbonate equivalent production rose 15 percent quarter on quarter to 14.6 thousand tonnes, driven by the Rincon starter plant ramp up. Rio also disclosed its realized lithium price for the first time, at 18,960 dollars per tonne for the first half.
EN_A: What about cash flow? The report mentioned a 1.2 billion dollar working capital build. That sounds significant.
EN_B: It is a key concern for the market. The working capital increase was mainly due to higher iron ore inventory after cyclones and stock builds at Simandou. Also, Rio paid about 443 million dollars in disputed Mongolian tax assessments. These led to net debt increasing by about 500 million dollars in the first half, to an estimated 14.8 billion dollars by the end of June.
EN_A: So Rio’s balance sheet is under some pressure. What is the key takeaway for investors from this report?
EN_B: Overall, Rio has very high quality assets. The long term growth story for iron ore and copper is very clear. But in the short term, copper production disruptions and cash outflows are headwinds. The investment bank report gives a neutral rating. It says Rio’s valuation is fair, but it prefers BHP because BHP has higher copper exposure and a stronger balance sheet.
EN_A: Are there any questions the report did not fully answer, that would make us want to read the full document?
EN_B: Two things. First, at last year’s capital markets day, Rio announced a plan to divest 5 to 10 billion dollars of non-core assets. But this report did not provide a specific update. The progress of that plan will directly impact capital returns and debt levels. Second, the Kennecott furnace rebuild will clearly hurt second half production and costs, but the report did not give a complete quantitative analysis of the impact. We will have to wait for the half year results on July 29 for those answers.
