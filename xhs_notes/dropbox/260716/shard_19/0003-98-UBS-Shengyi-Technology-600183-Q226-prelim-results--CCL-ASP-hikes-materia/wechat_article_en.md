## The CCL Upcycle Has a Second-Order Effect on Capacity Allocation That Favours Vertically Integrated Players

SYT's corporate structure is a critical advantage that is not fully captured in a sum-of-the-parts valuation. The company is the world's leading CCL manufacturer and also controls SYE, a high-growth AI PCB fabricator. This vertical integration allows SYT to capture value at two points in the supply chain, but the more subtle advantage is in capacity allocation.

During a CCL upcycle, the temptation for a standalone CCL maker is to maximise volume and push prices as high as the market will bear. For a vertically integrated player like SYT, the calculus is different. The company can prioritise CCL supply to its own PCB subsidiary, ensuring that SYE has guaranteed access to high-quality material even when the broader market is tight. This not only supports SYE's revenue growth but also allows SYT to optimise the overall margin profile of the consolidated group. The preliminary results suggest that this internal coordination is working: SYE's revenue growth has accelerated sequentially after two quarters of decline, and its margin is improving.

This is a structural advantage that is difficult for competitors to replicate. A pure-play PCB maker must negotiate CCL supply on the open market, often at spot prices that rise during upcycles. A pure-play CCL maker has no captive downstream demand to smooth out utilisation rates. The integrated model provides a natural hedge and a source of competitive advantage that becomes most valuable precisely when the supply chain is under stress.
