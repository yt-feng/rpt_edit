China's exports and imports both strengthened markedly in June. Export growth exceeded expectations at 27.0% YoY (Citi/market: 18.0%/19.0%), accelerating for a third consecutive month. Imports also outpaced expectations, surging 36.0% YoY (Citi/market: 25.0%/26.1%). As a result, the trade surplus widened to US\$125.6bn, the largest since January 2025. Outbound shipments expanded 20.2% YoY in 2Q26, marking the fastest growth in 18 quarters, while import growth reached 29.5% YoY, the strongest since 3Q21. In 26H1, exports registered robust growth of 17.6% YoY, compared with 5.5% in 2025, while imports advanced even more rapidly by 26.6% YoY after remaining broadly flat last year. With imports outperforming exports, the trade surplus narrowed modestly to US\$576bn in 1H26, from US\$583bn in 25H1.

The export outperformance was mainly driven by robust demand for AI hardware and surging semiconductor prices, while strong energy-transition demand also bolstered China's green-tech exports.

By partner, the improvement was broad-based, with ASEAN providing the largest boost. Shipments to ASEAN accelerated to their fastest pace since March 2023 (Jun: 34.5% vs. May: 24.3%) and remained the biggest contributor to headline growth. Likely supported by surging air-conditioner shipments amid Europe's heatwave, exports to the EU quickened to a four-month high (18.5% vs. 7.6%), with those to Germany jumping notably (27.2% vs. 16.7%). Boosted by higher AI-hardware and semiconductor prices, import growth surged to a five-year high.

By partner, import growth was broad-based, with Indonesia the main exception. Imports from Africa climbed to their fastest pace since March 2022 (40.2% vs. 21.1%), likely partly reflecting China's tariff free policy for 53 African countries, effective May 1.
