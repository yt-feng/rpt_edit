EN_A: So we are looking at a report today about a major policy shift in China's medical equipment procurement. It could be a big deal for European medtech companies. B, what is the one big takeaway here?
EN_B: The big takeaway is that China is expanding its volume-based procurement, or VBP, from drugs and consumables into medical devices, especially big-ticket capital equipment. For Western companies, this means more pricing pressure and potential market share losses. The report flags Elekta and GE HealthCare as the most exposed.
EN_A: So the real question this report is trying to answer is how damaging this new proposal actually is, right?
EN_B: Exactly. It is asking whether this new proposal is the moment when China stops being a safe haven for Western medical device companies. Until now, capital equipment was largely excluded from these price cuts. That might be changing.
EN_A: What does the new proposal actually say? Can you break it down for us?
EN_B: It creates a three-tier framework. National VBP covers the most advanced stuff, like certain radiotherapy machines and any device with a first-time price tag over 50 million RMB. Provincial VBP covers things like PET-CT scanners and surgical robots. And municipal VBP covers everything else. Every province has to complete at least one round by the end of 2026.
EN_A: So what does that mean for a company like Elekta? The report seems pretty negative on them.
EN_B: It is the most direct hit. China is 13 percent of Elekta's group revenue, and they have made expanding in China a key strategic goal. Now, their high-end MR-linac Unity is in the national VBP. Even without strong local competition, the price cuts could crush its high profitability. And their mainstream product, the Evo, faces strong competition from local player United Imaging. Elekta has about 40 percent market share in conventional radiotherapy in China, so they have the most to lose.
EN_A: And what about the imaging companies like GE and Siemens?
EN_B: For imaging, the risk is that VBP expands to cover most of the market. GE HealthCare has the highest revenue exposure in China, and its product portfolio overlaps a lot with local giants like United Imaging and Mindray. Siemens Healthineers has some buffer from innovation. Philips is less exposed overall, but still faces competition in ultrasound.
EN_A: So it sounds like a pretty clear negative for all these Western players. But are there any questions the report doesn't fully answer?
EN_B: Two big ones. First, how deep will the price cuts actually be? The report mentions a 45 percent cut for CT scanners in a previous provincial round. But can you apply that to radiotherapy machines? Probably not, because the technology and service costs are very different. Second, can local players really fill the gap at the high end? United Imaging is strong, but there is still a technology gap in advanced radiotherapy.
