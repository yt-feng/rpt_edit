EN_A: I came across a research report that opens with a quote from Charles Dickens — "It was the best of times, it was the worst of times." And it’s about the US restaurant industry. What’s the story here?
EN_B: The core argument is that restaurant spending in the US is splitting sharply along income lines. High-income consumers are eating out more than ever, while lower-income diners are pulling back.
EN_A: So this isn’t just a slowdown, it’s a structural shift in who’s driving demand?
EN_B: Exactly. The report calls it a K-shaped recovery. Credit card data shows that households earning over 100k a year increased their total restaurant spending by roughly 70% since 2022. Their visit frequency also jumped 55%.
EN_A: And the other side?
EN_B: Households earning under 45k have been declining since 2023. Their spending and transaction counts dropped 25% to 35% from the 2022 baseline.
EN_A: That’s a massive divergence. Is it about how much they spend per visit, or how often they go?
EN_B: This is the key insight. The divergence is driven by frequency, not ticket size. Rich and poor customers spend about the same per visit. But affluent customers visit much more often.
EN_A: So what does that mean for brands?
EN_B: It means rebuilding lower-income traffic is harder than just cutting prices. Once people change their habit from frequent visits to occasional ones, reversing that is tough.
EN_A: Which brands are most affected by this shift?
EN_B: The report created a K-Shift Score to measure this. The highest scores belong to Wingstop and Chipotle. Their customer mix has moved heavily toward higher-income diners over the past three years.
EN_A: Is a high score good or bad?
EN_B: It’s mixed. It could mean they successfully attracted wealthy customers. But it could also mean they lost so many lower-income customers that the remaining base is just wealthier. Cava is the only brand where all three income groups grew, but its high score is still driven by affluent growth.
EN_A: So how should investors think about this?
EN_B: The report doesn’t expect the K-shaped dynamic to reverse soon. So it prefers brands with high-income exposure, like Starbucks, Cava, and Chipotle. Or companies that are more insulated, like Darden Restaurants and food distributors.
EN_A: What about the brands that could benefit from a turnaround?
EN_B: If lower-income consumers ever bounce back, the biggest winners would be McDonald‘s, Domino’s, and Yum! Brands. But the report leaves one question open — could a recession or policy change break the K-shape?
