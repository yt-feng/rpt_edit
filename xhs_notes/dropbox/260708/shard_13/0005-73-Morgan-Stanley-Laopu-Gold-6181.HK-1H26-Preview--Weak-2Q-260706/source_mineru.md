Ground checks and anecdotal feedback from mall operators and jewelry brands point to softer demand for fixed-price jewelry in 2Q.

Gold prices remain key near-term swing factor for revenue growth and valuation multiple. Low market penetration + ongoing brand building support LT growth.

1H26 results preview: We forecast revenue growth of 85% YoY to Rmb22.9bn and net profit growth of 112% YoY to Rmb4.8bn. 2Q fixed-price gold jewelry demand appears weaker than expected, based on softer store traffic and anecdotal feedback from mall operators. Peer data suggest a widening gap between fixed-price and weight-based products, with Chow Tai Fook (1929.HK) reporting +3% SSSG for fixed-price jewelry in April–May, versus +35% for weight-based gold jewelry. ## 1H26 results preview

We expect Laopu to report its interim results in late August. Before then, the company may announce preliminary results through a profit alert between late July and mid-August.

1H26: We project revenue growth of 85% YoY to Rmb22.9bn and net profit growth of 112% YoY to Rmb4.8bn. Growth has moderated from 2025 levels given a much higher YoY comparison base.

Growth is primarily driven by a robust 1Q26, followed by a significantly weaker 2Q26. We assume revenue of approximately Rmb2bn per month in 2Q26, below the March 2026 run rate (Rmb3bn sales and Rmb2.6bn revenue ex-VAT). This could further weigh on market confidence in the demand outlook, as March is traditionally a peak season.

Margin expansion plays a more important role in earnings growth. We project gross margin to expand from approximately 37% in 2H25 to 42% in 1H26 (1Q26e: 41%; 2Q26e: 45%), while net margin expands from 17.4% in 2H25 to 21% in 1H26. This reflects aggressive price increases, with tag prices up 78% YoY and 44% HoH, while gold prices rose only 15% YoY and declined 10% HoH.

We expect the company to maintain its high dividend payout policy. We assume a 75% payout ratio, implying an interim DPS of Rmb20.4. This would bring the trailing dividend yield (2H25 + 1H26) to 8.5%. We project a dividend yield of more than 10% in 2026e.

• Management's guidance for 2H26.

\- Strategy during a gold price down-cycle – whether management aims to maintain current pricing despite potential volume pressure, or lower prices and adjust the product mix to support demand.

\- Store network strategy – whether the company plans to rationalize its network or continue upgrading and expanding its store base.

## More about valuation

The current share price of approximately HK\$380 has returned to its January 2025 level, when gold traded at around US\$2,600/oz or Rmb600/g – roughly 35% below current levels. At that time, the capital market was still relatively unfamiliar with Laopu given its short listing history (IPO in June 2024) and limited brand track record. Since then, Laopu has strengthened its brand equity through market share gains and expansion into leading luxury malls across China, increasing confidence among both consumers and investors that it could become the first domestic brand capable of challenging international luxury brands. While gold prices have undoubtedly contributed to Laopu's rapid success, we believe the company has also established a premium brand image in consumers' minds, which should warrant a valuation premium to cyclical stocks.

The stock trades at less than 7x NTM consensus P/E, below the trough valuation of key peer Chow Tai Fook, which traded at approximately 8x in mid-2024. At that time, Chow Tai Fook was still navigating an operational downturn marked by store closures and declining retail sales, with revenue falling 18% in FY25 (April 2024–March 2025). In contrast, Laopu remains in the brand-building and customer acquisition phase, and we continue to expect medium-term growth.

# What's Changed?

## EPS

We lower our 2026–28 revenue estimates by 10–22%, as 2Q26 demand appears weaker than expected, highlighting the risk of softer brand momentum during a gold price down-cycle. A weaker gold price environment also reduces the company's ability to implement product price increases. We now expect Laopu to adopt a more conservative store expansion strategy in 2027–28 following the weaker-than-expected demand backdrop. As a result, our revenue cuts are larger for 2027–28 than for 2026.

However, lower gold prices also reduce raw material costs and support margin expansion. Net-net, we leave our 2026 EPS estimate largely unchanged (+1%), while lowering our 2027–28 EPS estimates by 12–14%.

## PT and scenario values

A weaker gold price environment is negative for valuation multiples, as it reduces near-term demand and earnings visibility.

PT – cut from HK\$1,010 to HK\$590: We apply an 11x 2026 P/E multiple (vs. 20x previously), implying a 0.4x PEG ratio based on 2025–27 EPS CAGR. This is well below the China consumer sector average of 1.5x and, in our view, appropriately reflects the lower growth visibility. The target P/E also implies a dividend yield of approximately 5%, broadly in line with the China consumer sector average. An 11x target P/E is also around 1.5 standard deviations below the average of Chow Tai Fook's valuation range since 2016. We view this as reasonable in a volatile gold price environment.

Bear case – cut from HK\$550 to HK\$295: We apply 8x 2026 bear-case EPS (vs. 15x previously). In this scenario, gold prices continue to decline gradually and demand for Laopu's products weakens further. Investors continue to view the stock as a cyclical name and assign a low valuation multiple.

Bull case – cut from HK\$1,675 to HK\$1,420: We apply 25x 2026 bull-case EPS (vs. 30x previously). We see two potential bull-case outcomes: (1) gold prices resume their up-cycle, supporting jewelry demand and driving a re-rating of the stock; or (2) gold prices remain weak, but demand for Laopu's products remains resilient and the company continues to gain market share, demonstrating the strength of its brand. In this scenario, the market begins to assign a valuation premium to Laopu as a premium consumer brand.
