EN_A: So there’s this new report on China’s metal inventories, and it says copper and aluminum have been drawing down for three weeks straight. What’s the big takeaway here?
EN_B: The big takeaway is that China’s base metals consumption might actually be picking up. The pace and persistence of the inventory declines, especially for copper and aluminum, are stronger than what we’d normally expect for this time of year.
EN_A: Let’s start with copper. The report says inventories hit a multi-year low. How low are we talking?
EN_B: As of the week ending July 3, total visible copper inventory in China dropped to 163 kilotons. That’s the lowest for this time of year in several years. And the drawdown has been accelerating—last week alone, we saw a drop of about 13 kilotons.
EN_A: So what does that actually mean? Is this a demand story or a supply issue?
EN_B: The report leans toward demand improvement. For one, the Yangshan copper premium climbed back above 70 dollars per ton, which suggests buyers are willing to pay more for spot metal. Also, the US is considering copper import tariffs, so metal is being pulled into COMEX warehouses, which indirectly tightens supply in China.
EN_A: And what about aluminum? The numbers there seem even bigger.
EN_B: Yes, aluminum saw its biggest one-week destocking since Chinese New Year—75 kilotons. Over the past eight weeks, aluminum inventories have fallen by more than 300 kilotons. That’s a pretty strong signal.
EN_A: Sounds bullish. But are there any cautionary notes in the report?
EN_B: Definitely. The report cites JPM’s China economists, who argue that the demand recovery is narrow. It’s mostly driven by high-end manufacturing supply chains, not broad consumer demand. So policy support may still be needed to widen the recovery.
EN_B: Zinc also saw its first significant destocking since Chinese New Year, down 11 kilotons last week. But here’s the catch—total zinc inventory is still at its highest level since 2022. So one week of drawdown doesn’t confirm a trend yet.
EN_A: So what’s the bottom line from this report?
EN_B: The bottom line is that copper and aluminum destocking is accelerating, which could signal improving demand. But the improvement is uneven—steel and iron ore inventories are still high, and steel mill margins are shrinking. So it’s not a broad-based recovery.

EN_B: The report doesn’t fully answer how long this destocking can last, or how US tariff decisions will reshape global metal flows. Also, whether China will roll out more stimulus is still an open question. Those are the key variables to watch in the full report.
