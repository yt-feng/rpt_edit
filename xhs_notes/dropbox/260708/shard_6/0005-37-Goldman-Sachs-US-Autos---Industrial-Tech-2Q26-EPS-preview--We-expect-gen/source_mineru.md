# 2Q26 EPS preview: We expect generally better guidance from industrial tech companies, and in line outlooks in autos

Heading into 2Q26 earnings the median stock in our Autos & Industrial Tech coverage is about flat since reporting 1Q26 results and up 3% YTD (with Auto exposed stocks up 7% since earnings and up 3% YTD, and Industrial Tech stocks down 5% at the median since reporting 1Q results and up 33% YTD).

With this backdrop, we expect 2Q26 results and forward guidance to generally be better than consensus from the Industrial Tech companies in our coverage, and we expect more in line outlooks from auto OEMs and suppliers. In Industrial Tech, this is due to what we think is ongoing strong hyperscale/AI capex spending, and a recovering industrial market more generally (e.g. the ISM index has been >50 the last six months). In autos, we expect modestly lower yoy global auto production and US auto sales, but this is consistent with what auto suppliers and OEMs commonly assumed in guidance.

## In terms of potential stock moves this earnings season:

For Industrial Tech, there has been a high bar for datacenter exposed Industrial Tech stocks to trade up in the first session post reporting over the last several earnings seasons. If that trend continues, there could be more upside specifically around earnings for stocks that have been more debated recently in this cohort (we highlight APH in particular as a name we're positive on).

For core autos, with both US SAAR and global production near historical averages, we think the focus will be on idiosyncratic growth drivers and EPS sustainability. This has been partly from energy and datacenter opportunities, which has helped BWA, APTV, and F to trade higher intra-quarter (as detailed in our Platforms & Power III report, we see BWA as best positioned to benefit from this theme). We also see upside to TSLA Street (FactSet) estimates, consistent with the stronger 2Q deliveries. In terms of stocks we'd be more cautious on specifically around earnings, we believe 2026/2027 estimates could be revised lower on MBLY to reflect costs for its robotaxi effort, as we think the company will need to increase its investments before its direct approach could lead to incremental earnings growth (and we believe that new design-win traction and/or wins would be needed for the stock to work around the quarter after the recent move higher in shares).

Key topics and themes we expect more generally this quarter include:

1) Trends by end market including auto sales and product cycles, industrial demand, and datacenter capex

2) China OEM exposure for auto suppliers, including risks from softer China auto sales YTD (which are -19%) but also the impact of growing exports and share gains, including in Europe.

3) The USMCA and how/if trade rules will change especially for autos as the White House has sought higher US specific content rules (per media reports). We expect companies in general to point to an ability to partner with suppliers/customers and price-cost discipline. However, the USMCA will remain in effect for another decade provided no country decides to exit per Bloomberg, so we do not expect any incremental actions to be taken unless companies have visibility into rules changing.

4) The degree of input cost inflation and ability for companies to offset this. Some costs have continued to rise, including copper and memory. We expect suppliers to continue to take steps to offset this. With memory costs likely to rise again in 2027, we expect the ability for this to continue next year to be a debate amongst investors. Our base case view is that the supply chain can pass on these costs over time, albeit at times on a lag. Conversely, aluminum and crude prices have moderated from recent highs, which could help lower resin/metals costs if this is sustained (which would be a positive for auto OEMs including limiting how much yoy input/material cost headwind GM and Ford may see in 2027).

5) AI related efforts including manufacturing efficiency, automation/robotics, and AV/robotaxi efforts. For Tesla, we believe FSD adoption/penetration, the pace of its robotaxi roll-out, and how Optimus development is going will be key topics on its earnings call.

We still expect US auto sales to be down low single digits yoy in 2026, with SAAR in line with the historical average of \~16 mn.

One reason is based on our auto leading indicators (ALI) analysis, which tracks key inputs that correlate with new vehicle buying such as consumer sentiment, Google Search traffic, and housing starts (Exhibit 10). The ALI overall picked up in the last few months, and our 16 mn SAAR assumption does imply some measured degree of 2H improvement. If the ALI were to remain strong in the coming months, we could be more positive on the US auto outlook.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

Each region manages Regional Conviction Lists, which are selected from Buy rated stocks on the respective region's Investment Lists and represent investment recommendations focused on the size of the total return potential and/or the likelihood of the realization of the return across their respective areas of coverage. The addition or removal of stocks from such Conviction Lists are managed by the Investment Review Committee or other designated committee in each respective region and do not represent a change in the analysts' investment rating for such stocks.

Total return potential represents the upside or downside differential between the current share price and the price target, including all paid or anticipated dividends, expected during the time horizon associated with the price target. Price targets are required for all covered stocks. The total return potential, price target and associated time horizon are stated in each report adding or reiterating an Investment List membership.
