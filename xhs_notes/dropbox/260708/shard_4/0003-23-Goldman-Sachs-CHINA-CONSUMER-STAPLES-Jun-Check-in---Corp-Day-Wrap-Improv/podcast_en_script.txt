EN_A: So there’s this new report on China consumer staples from a major investment bank. Everyone’s been waiting for a read on the second quarter. What’s the big takeaway?
EN_B: The headline is: June showed month-over-month improvement, but the overall picture is still soft. No V-shaped recovery. But the worst might be behind us.
EN_A: “Month-over-month improvement” sounds like good news. But if I talk to people on the street, nobody feels better. Where’s that improvement coming from?
EN_B: Two things. One, the Dragon Boat Festival shifted into June this year, which gave a temporary boost. Two, the comparison base from last June was very low. So it’s more of a statistical improvement than a demand rebound. The report also says weather was a big drag, especially for beer and beverages.
EN_A: So it’s not a real recovery. What about individual companies? Who’s winning, who’s struggling?
EN_B: Divergence is the keyword. Wanchen is opening stores faster than expected, with decent same-store sales. Mengniu, Tingyi, and Yihai all maintained their full-year guidance. But on the other side, Eastroc, Budweiser China, and Weilong all cut their first-half or second-quarter expectations. Reasons vary from weather to destocking to weak sentiment.
EN_A: What about costs? Raw material prices have come down. Does that help margins?
EN_B: It does, but unevenly. Most companies said cost pressure is easing. PET prices have dropped, so first-half gross margins were at least stable. Tingyi and Eastroc even saw margin expansion because they locked in lower costs earlier. But beer companies are cautious because logistics and aluminum costs are still rising. So the cost tailwind is not a blanket benefit.
EN_A: Are companies cutting prices then, since costs are lower?
EN_B: Actually, no. Most brands are very cautious about pricing. They’re afraid consumers will push back. Instead, they’re focusing on launching new products with higher margins. CR Beer and CR Beverage only adjusted prices on a few SKUs in select regions. Broad-based price increases are off the table.
EN_A: So if they can’t raise prices, where’s the growth?
EN_B: That’s the most interesting part of the report. Channel expansion is the real driver right now. Value retailers, like discount stores and snack chains, are the fastest-growing channel. Tingyi is seeing double-digit growth there, and it now accounts for 3% to 7% of their sales. CR Beverage even signed a partnership with Wanchen to supply exclusive products.
EN_A: So everyone is piling into the value channel. Is that sustainable?
EN_B: That’s the question the report raises but doesn’t fully answer. Store openings are accelerating, but per-store GMV is under pressure because of weather and competition. In June, many value retailers saw their daily GMV per store decline. So more stores doesn’t automatically mean more profit. That’s something to watch in the third quarter.
EN_A: What about shareholder returns? Dividends and buybacks are a big theme now.
EN_B: Yes, that’s a key support for stock prices. Yihai and Tingyi are committed to a payout ratio of around 100%. CR Beer and Mengniu promise year-over-year DPS growth. Eastroc is even considering a long-term dividend commitment plus buybacks. But the report doesn’t fully explore whether these commitments are sustainable if earnings decline.
EN_A: So looking ahead to Q3, what are you watching most closely?
EN_B: Three things. First, the base for on-trade channels like restaurants was very low from July to September last year. That could be a tailwind for beer and snacks. Second, weather and consumer confidence remain the biggest swing factors. Third, PET prices are lower, but pricing power is still limited. The report has one line I haven’t mentioned: for some companies, Q3 could be the make-or-break quarter for their full-year thesis.
