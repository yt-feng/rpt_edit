ZH_A: 最近有一份关于AI和零售业的研报，说AI对零售商来说机会大于风险，你怎么看这个判断？

ZH_B: 这个判断确实有点反直觉，因为很多人一提到AI和电商，首先想到的是亚马逊或者平台会不会被取代。但这份报告的核心观点是，AI更像是一个新的发现渠道，而不是颠覆者。

ZH_A: 所以它真正要回答的问题是什么？

ZH_B: 它要回答的是，在AI代理购物的时代，零售商到底是会被“去中间化”，还是能保住自己的地位。报告认为，目前风险其实被高估了。

ZH_A: 为什么这么说？AI代理不是可以直接帮用户完成整个购物流程吗？

ZH_B: 对，但这正是关键。报告指出，零售商已经重新控制了交易环节。早期OpenAI曾经推出过“即时结账”，由AI平台控制交易，但现在已经被放弃了，结账权回到了零售商手里。

ZH_A: 这意味着零售商可以自己掌握数据和交易？

ZH_B: 没错。而且，零售商还有另一个护城河——履约能力。AI平台再聪明，也不像沃尔玛或亚马逊那样拥有庞大的供应链和物流网络。很多品牌也不愿意自己去做直营配送。

ZH_A: 那风险最大的品类是什么？

ZH_B: 报告明确点出了两个高风险领域。第一个是生鲜和日用百货。因为这些商品是低考虑度的、重复购买的，AI代理很容易帮你自动补货。第二个是那些已经有成熟直营渠道的品牌，比如苹果和耐克。

ZH_A: 所以像耐克这样的品牌，如果决定通过AI直接卖给消费者，零售商就可能被跳过了？

ZH_B: 对。历史上耐克就多次绕过迪克体育这样的零售商。报告认为，如果品牌想赚回完整的零售利润，这种“去中间化”的风险是真实存在的。

ZH_A: 那哪些品类是安全的？

ZH_B: 高度情感化的品类，比如美妆、宠物用品，还有那些需要浏览和搭配的家居装饰。这些品类里，消费者的决策过程本身就很享受，不容易被AI替代。

ZH_A: 所以零售商应该怎么应对？

ZH_B: 报告的核心建议是“两面下注”。一方面，要积极和大型语言模型平台合作，比如沃尔玛、塔吉特、百思买都已经加入了谷歌的通用商务协议。另一方面，要大力投资自己的平台内AI代理。

ZH_A: 自己开发AI代理有什么好处？

ZH_B: 可以收集专有数据。比如你可以问“Sparky，感恩节大餐吃什么”，然后AI直接帮你加购。这种数据是零售商自己的，不会被平台拿走。而且还能用来做客服，比如“Olive，我的沙发送到哪了”。

ZH_A: 听起来沃尔玛走在最前面？

ZH_B: 是的，沃尔玛确实是领先的。塔吉特紧随其后，其他零售商在快速追赶。但报告也提示了一个还没完全展开的问题。

ZH_A: 什么问题？

ZH_B: AI平台到底怎么赚钱。目前它们的注意力似乎转向了数字广告，而不是从交易中抽成。但广告盈利模式是否可持续，以及是否会侵蚀零售媒体网络，报告认为还需要继续观察。

ZH_A: 所以整体上，你对零售商的未来是乐观的？

ZH_B: 报告给出的判断是，机会大于风险。但前提是零售商必须现在就开始投资和布局。如果等到AI代理真的成熟了再行动，可能就晚了。

EN_A: So this report from a global investment bank says AI is more of an opportunity than a risk for retailers. That sounds almost too optimistic. Is that really the case?

EN_B: It does sound counterintuitive, especially when everyone is worried about AI agents replacing shopping platforms. But the report’s main argument is that AI will act more as a new discovery channel, not a disruptor.

EN_A: What’s the core question the report is trying to answer?

EN_B: It’s asking whether retailers will get disintermediated in an agentic commerce world. And the answer, surprisingly, is that the risk is likely overstated.

EN_A: Why? If an AI agent can just handle the whole shopping trip from search to checkout, why wouldn’t it cut out the retailer?

EN_B: That’s the key insight. The report points out that retailers have already taken back control of the transaction. OpenAI initially had an “Instant Checkout” feature where they controlled the payment, but that’s been deprecated. Now checkout happens through retailer app integrations.

EN_A: So retailers keep the data and the transaction?

EN_B: Exactly. And they also have another big moat: fulfillment. AI platforms don’t have the supply chain networks that Walmart or Amazon have. Most brands also don’t want to handle direct-to-consumer delivery themselves.

EN_A: So which categories are most at risk?

EN_B: The report flags two high-risk areas. First, grocery and CPG, because those are low-consideration, repeat purchases. An AI agent could easily auto-replenish your milk or detergent. Second, brands that already have a strong DTC channel, like Apple and Nike.

EN_A: So a brand like Nike could just sell directly through an AI agent and bypass the retailer?

EN_B: Exactly. Nike has a history of doing that with Dick’s Sporting Goods. The report says if those brands want to capture the full retail margin, the disintermediation risk is real.

EN_A: What about the safe categories?

EN_B: Highly emotive categories like beauty and pet supplies, and categories where browsing and basket-building are part of the fun, like home decor. Those are low risk because the shopping experience itself matters.

EN_A: So what should retailers actually do?

EN_B: The report’s advice is to double down on both fronts. First, partner with the large language models. Walmart, Target, Best Buy, and others have already joined Google’s Universal Commerce Protocol. Second, invest in your own on-platform AI agents.

EN_A: Why build your own agent?

EN_B: It’s about owning the data. You can ask “Sparky, what’s a great Thanksgiving dinner?” and the AI adds items to your cart. That data stays with the retailer. It’s also great for customer service, like “Olive, where is my sofa delivery?”

EN_A: It sounds like Walmart is leading the pack.

EN_B: Yes, Walmart is the furthest along. Target is next, and others are fast-following. But the report also highlights one big open question.

EN_A: What’s that?

EN_B: How will the AI platforms themselves make money? They seem to be shifting focus to digital advertising rather than transaction fees. But whether that model is sustainable, and whether it will cannibalize retail media networks, is something the report says still needs to be verified.

EN_A: So the overall takeaway is cautiously optimistic?

EN_B: The report’s conclusion is that it’s more opportunity than risk. But only if retailers start investing now. If they wait until agentic commerce is fully mature, it might be too late.
