LS Electric is emerging as the standout performer in the current cycle, and the numbers are becoming too large to ignore. The company is expected to report new orders exceeding KRW 2 trillion in the second quarter, a dramatic acceleration from approximately KRW 1 trillion in the first quarter. This is not a one-time spike. It reflects sustained order intake momentum from US data centers, which have become the dominant driver of the company's growth trajectory.

The implications for full-year guidance are significant. LS Electric already raised its 2026 new orders forecast from KRW 4 trillion to KRW 5 trillion earlier this year. But based on the second-quarter run rate, a further upward revision to KRW 5-6 trillion appears increasingly likely. If the company delivers on this trajectory, it would represent year-over-year order growth of roughly 25-50%, depending on the final number.

What makes this particularly compelling is the nature of the demand. These are not small-scale or speculative orders. They are coming from data center operators who are committing capital to AI infrastructure at an unprecedented scale. The US capacity expansion in Utah, which the company has already announced, is a direct response to this demand. The critical question for the earnings call will be whether management signals additional capacity additions for other product lines in the United States.

The operating profit forecast of KRW 167 billion, representing 54% year-over-year growth, is likely to exceed consensus by more than 5%. But the real story is not the beat. It is the possibility that management revises its full-year operating profit target of KRW 600 billion upward. If new orders continue at this pace, the margin trajectory should also improve as fixed costs are spread over a larger revenue base.

The strategic implication is clear: LS Electric has positioned itself as the primary beneficiary of US AI data center buildout among Korean power equipment companies. Its product mix, capacity expansion, and customer diversification are aligned with the most powerful demand driver in the sector.

## Hyosung Heavy's Earnings Will Miss Consensus, but the Real Risk Is the Middle East Exposure and the 765kV Project Delay

Hyosung Heavy presents a more cautionary picture. The company is expected to report operating profit of KRW 266 billion, which is 6% below consensus. While this still represents impressive year-over-year growth of 62%, the miss relative to expectations is the first sign that the company's growth trajectory is not as smooth as its peers.

The primary headwind is the Middle East conflict. Approximately 5-10% of Hyosung Heavy's new orders in fiscal 2025 came from the Middle East, and the ongoing geopolitical instability is creating execution risk. Projects may be delayed, contracts may be renegotiated, and the overall contribution from this region could be lower than initially expected.

But the Middle East is not the only concern. The company faces a high base effect for new orders. After a first quarter in which orders doubled to approximately KRW 4 trillion, the sequential momentum is naturally ebbing. The question is whether this is a temporary normalization or the beginning of a more sustained deceleration.

The most strategic risk, however, relates to the 765kV transformer market. Hyosung Heavy has been pursuing large-scale orders for ultra-high-voltage equipment, particularly for projects in Texas. Delays in these projects are creating uncertainty around the timing and magnitude of future order wins. The 765kV segment is a high-margin, high-barrier-to-entry business, and any sustained delay would have a disproportionate impact on earnings quality.

On the positive side, the company's joint venture with Quanta Services represents a strategic move to strengthen its position in the US market. The partnership could provide access to a broader set of customers and projects, particularly in the AI data center space. But the details remain sparse, and observers will be looking for concrete evidence that this joint venture is translating into order wins.

The core tension for Hyosung Heavy is that its earnings quality is being affected by factors outside its control—geopolitics and project delays—while its competitors are benefiting from the same macro tailwinds. This divergence is likely to persist until the company can demonstrate that its US strategy can offset the headwinds from other regions.

## Hyundai Electric's Hyperscaler Win Is a Breakthrough, but the Market Is Underestimating the Second-Order Implications for Margins and Competition

Hyundai Electric's second-quarter results are expected to be broadly in line with consensus, with operating profit of KRW 301 billion. But the headline number masks a more important development: the company's reported win of a >$700 million order from a hyperscaler AI data center operator.

This is a breakthrough moment. Until now, Hyundai Electric's order book has been heavily weighted toward utilities and grid operators, with limited exposure to the direct data center market. The hyperscaler win changes that narrative. It signals that the company's products and capabilities are now being validated by the most demanding customers in the AI infrastructure ecosystem.

The implications for margins are significant. Hyperscaler orders tend to be large, standardized, and repeatable. They also come with higher quality requirements and tighter delivery schedules. Companies that can serve this market effectively should be able to command premium pricing and achieve higher margins over time. The question is whether Hyundai Electric can replicate this win with other hyperscalers and build a sustainable revenue stream from this channel.

The Middle East exposure is a smaller concern for Hyundai Electric than for Hyosung Heavy, with approximately 10% of total orders coming from the region. But it is still a factor that observers will want to understand. The company's ability to manage this exposure while scaling its US data center business will be a key determinant of its relative performance.

The most important question for the earnings call is whether management will revise its full-year new orders guidance upward. After a strong first quarter and the hyperscaler win in the second quarter, the company has a strong case for raising expectations. But the guidance revision, if it comes, will need to be supported by evidence that the order pipeline is diversified and sustainable.

## The Report Does Not Fully Answer How Tariff Rebates, Margin Expansion, and Capacity Constraints Will Interact Over the Next 12 Months

The earnings preview provides a detailed view of order momentum and earnings estimates, but it leaves several critical questions unanswered. These are the questions that will determine whether the current cycle is a multi-year structural shift or a temporary demand surge.

First, the tariff rebate situation remains opaque. Korean power equipment companies have been navigating US tariff policies, but the full impact on margins and pricing is not yet clear. If tariff rebates are granted or if companies successfully pass through costs to customers, margins could expand faster than currently forecast. If not, the margin trajectory could differ.

Second, capacity constraints are becoming a binding factor. LS Electric is expanding in Utah, but the broader industry faces limits on manufacturing capacity for transformers, switchgear, and other critical components. The companies that can add capacity most quickly will capture disproportionate market share. Those that cannot will face order book constraints even as demand accelerates.

Third, the competitive dynamics between Korean companies and global peers are shifting. Chinese power equipment manufacturers are becoming more aggressive in export markets, and European players are also expanding. The Korean companies' ability to maintain pricing power and market share in the face of increased competition is an open question.

Finally, the interaction between AI data center demand and traditional utility grid investment is not fully understood. If utility spending accelerates alongside data center buildout, the total addressable market could be much larger than current estimates. If utility spending is adjusted or delayed, the growth trajectory could be more modest.

These are not weaknesses in the report. They are the natural boundaries of any forward-looking analysis. But they are the questions that observers should be thinking about as they position for the next phase of the cycle.

**Geographic diversification** determines resilience to regional shocks. Companies with significant US exposure benefit from the strongest demand environment and the most predictable regulatory framework. LS Electric and Hyundai Electric are well-positioned here. Hyosung Heavy's Middle East exposure is a relative disadvantage.

**Capacity expansion trajectory** determines the ability to capture market share. Companies that are investing in new manufacturing capacity, particularly in the US, will be able to scale their order books faster than those relying on existing facilities. LS Electric's Utah expansion is a clear positive. Hyundai Electric's capacity plans are less clear and will need to be clarified.

Using this framework, LS Electric appears best positioned for the current cycle, but its valuation is already reflecting this premium. Hyundai Electric offers a more compelling risk-reward if the hyperscaler win can be replicated. Hyosung Heavy requires more patience and a clearer resolution of its Middle East and 765kV issues.
