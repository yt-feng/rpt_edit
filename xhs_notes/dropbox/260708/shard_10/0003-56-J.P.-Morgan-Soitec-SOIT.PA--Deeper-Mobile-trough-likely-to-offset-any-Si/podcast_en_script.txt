EN_A: Soitec is down about 40% from its peak this year. A lot of investors are asking if this is a good entry point for a medium-term position.
EN_B: That is exactly the question a recent report from a global investment bank tries to answer. Their conclusion is to stay neutral. The core argument is that while the silicon photonics business could beat expectations, the mobile business is facing a deeper and slower recovery than most people think.
EN_A: So the real question is, which of Soitec's two engines is more powerful right now?
EN_B: Exactly. Soitec is running at two speeds. Silicon photonics is booming because of AI infrastructure. But mobile, which is still their biggest revenue driver, is heading into a deeper downturn. The report tries to figure out whether these two forces cancel each other out.
EN_A: Let's start with the good news. How big is the silicon photonics opportunity?
EN_B: The report sees it as the main growth engine for the next few years. They model a 50% revenue CAGR from fiscal 2025 to 2030. By fiscal 2030, silicon photonics revenue could hit over 500 million euros. This is driven by the shift to silicon photonics in optical transceivers and the ramp of CPO, or co-packaged optics, from around 2028.
EN_A: So can Soitec capture all of that growth?
EN_B: Not necessarily. The report highlights a key risk: market share erosion. Soitec currently has close to 100% of the silicon photonics wafer market, but the report expects that to drop to about 80% by fiscal 2030. Competitors like Global Wafers are catching up. Also, long-term supply agreements will lock in volume but put some pressure on pricing.
EN_A: So silicon photonics is high growth, but margins might not be as good. What about the mobile business? The report seems more worried than the market.
EN_B: That is the bearish core of the report. Mobile is still about 52% of Soitec's revenue. But the report forecasts a 16% drop in global handset shipments in 2026 and another 3% in 2027. That directly hits demand for their RF-SOI products.
EN_A: But management says they will be more disciplined on pricing to protect margins.
EN_B: That could be a double-edged sword. The report argues that stricter pricing might support gross margins, but it also increases the risk of losing market share to cheaper competitors. It could also prolong the destocking cycle. The report now assumes destocking will continue into fiscal 2028. Their mobile revenue forecasts are 14% to 17% below consensus.
EN_A: What about the auto and industrial business?
EN_B: That segment is still working through inventory and will be a headwind through fiscal 2027. There are some signs of restocking in the auto supply chain, but the report thinks IDMs will benefit more than wafer makers. Their SmartSiC business is also likely to shrink further due to competitive pressure.
EN_A: So the overall margin picture is not great either?
EN_B: The report sees a slower margin recovery than the market expects. They forecast group gross margin to only reach 30.2% by fiscal 2029, versus 32.2% consensus. The headwinds include a higher depreciation base, pricing pressure, and currency effects.
EN_A: Are there any questions the report leaves open that investors should dig into?
EN_B: Two things. First, the report mentions a potential portfolio review in the first half of fiscal 2027, but it does not fully explore what that might mean. Second, there are some early-stage incubator projects, but the report says the market is reluctant to give them any valuation credit yet. Those could be surprises down the road.
EN_A: So what is the bottom line?
EN_B: Silicon photonics could surprise to the upside, but mobile is a bigger drag. The report stays neutral, cutting the price target from 130 euros to 92 euros. They think the downside in mobile is not fully priced in, but being short is also risky because silicon photonics could trigger earnings upgrades.

EN_B: Exactly. We just highlighted the key tensions. The full report has much more data and scenario analysis to help you form your own view.
