## The Dual Engine of HBM and Chinese OSATs Is Reshaping the Demand Landscape

Disco's shipment data reveals two distinct but complementary demand drivers. The first is AI-related demand, specifically for HBM. Multiple memory makers are investing heavily in HBM production, which requires advanced packaging techniques that are heavily dependent on back-end processing. HBM stacks involve vertically integrating multiple DRAM dies with through-silicon vias (TSVs) and microbumps, all of which require precise grinding and dicing. This is a structural demand driver that is independent of the broader semiconductor cycle, as AI investment continues to accelerate.

The second driver is OSAT companies, with a particular emphasis on those in China. The report notes that shipments to OSATs were strong, reflecting "progress in outsourcing of some packaging processes and strong investment appetites." This is a critical insight. The semiconductor industry has long been dominated by integrated device manufacturers (IDMs) and foundries that handle both front-end and back-end processes. But the increasing complexity of advanced packaging is driving a wave of outsourcing to specialized OSATs. These companies are investing aggressively in capacity, particularly in China, where the government is prioritizing semiconductor self-sufficiency.

The combination of these two drivers creates a demand profile that is more resilient than historical patterns. AI demand is technology-driven and relatively insensitive to macroeconomic cycles, while Chinese OSAT investment is policy-driven and similarly insulated from global demand fluctuations. Together, they provide a buffer against the cyclical downturns that have historically affected the semiconductor equipment industry. For observers, this suggests that the risk profile of back-end equipment companies like Disco may be structurally improving, warranting a reassessment of valuation multiples.
