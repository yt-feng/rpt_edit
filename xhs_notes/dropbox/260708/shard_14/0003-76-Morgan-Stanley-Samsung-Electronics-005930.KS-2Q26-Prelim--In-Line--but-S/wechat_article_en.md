## Capacity Constraints Create a Structural Tailwind That Favors Incumbents

One of the most underappreciated dynamics in the current cycle is the role of capacity constraints. The memory industry has been through a period of disciplined capital expenditure, and supply growth is not keeping pace with demand, particularly from hyperscale data centers and AI workloads.

The report notes that significant upside exists once observers adjust to earnings growth in a period of unprecedented capacity constraints. This is not a temporary phenomenon. Building new memory fabrication facilities takes years, and the technical complexity of advanced nodes is increasing. The result is a market where pricing power is concentrated among the few players who can deliver the most advanced products.

Samsung is uniquely positioned here. Its leadership in advanced DRAM and logic base die nodes gives it a competitive advantage that is difficult to replicate. The report's reference to the compute-to-power ratio is a technical way of saying that Samsung's products deliver more performance per watt, which is a critical metric for data center operators who are increasingly constrained by power availability.

The imminent long-term agreements referenced in the report would further strengthen this position. LTAs lock in volume and pricing over multi-year periods, reducing earnings volatility and improving visibility. For Samsung, this means that the current cycle is not just about spot pricing but about establishing a more stable and predictable earnings base.
