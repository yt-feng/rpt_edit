## Spotlight on Unsecured Consumer Vintage Performance

The performance data of unsecured consumer/MPL ABS looks strong this year, a different story than in auto ABS which continues to show weakness vs prior years. Aggregate delinquencies in MPL have been falling for a number of years, with 60+ DQs now well below prior years' highs. Easing of lending standards during Covid saw performance deteriorate rapidly. A common heuristic has been that lenders rapidly tightened standards in the aftermath, and to be sure that is indeed what reporting suggests (see Exhibit 7). However, we find that improved 2025 vintage performance is a more nuanced story, with changes in the universe as the MPL ABS market has grown contributing meaningfully as well.

## New Issuance, Outstanding Balance, and Trading Volumes

ABS issuance volumes set a new record in 2Q26 with \$117bn issued, +6% over 1Q26, bringing the YTD totals to \$228bn (+26% vs 2025 YTD). June was a slightly slower month than May, with \$37bn issued, but still 20% higher than June 2025 volumes and with a decent mix of both consumer (\$24.5bn) and commercial supply (\$12.5bn). A handful of digital infrastructure deals also priced in June for July settle, ending a 2-month drought in data centers with new deals from QTS and Aligned and bringing fiber YTD totals to nearly \$10bn with deals from Uniti and FirstLight. New issue spreads in both data centers and fiber tightened vs previous deals across the stack, with demand enthusiastic.

Consumer ABS spreads finally halted their rally with prime auto AAA widening \~8bps vs the May tights and subprime auto AAA \~13bps wider. We were surprised last month that spreads continued to hit new YTD tights despite the very heavy supply; the ongoing heavy issuance, not just in consumer ABS but in resi, helped to finally push spreads slightly wider across the consumer sec credit complex.
