# Nike: Key Takeaways from Bernstein's Retail Forum

Last week, we hosted Nike Investor Relations at our Bernstein Retail Forum for meetings with market participants. The company's repositioning continues, with a constructive tone and what appears to be improving visibility.

FY27 remains a transitional year, with the Lifestyle ("Sportswear") segment experiencing modest declines until clearance comparisons are lapped and new products arrive in the Spring. While the Performance segment continues to grow across all regions, the approximately 60% of company sales represented by Nike and Jordan Lifestyle products continues to contract, driven primarily by year-over-year declines in Classics (a 4 percentage point headwind in FY26; we model 2 percentage points in FY27). A broad set of new Lifestyle products in Spring 2027, along with moderating year-over-year declines in Classics, should begin to drive growth in Lifestyle during calendar 2027 (we model late FY27 and into FY28). Margins should improve before sales do, as Nike laps last year's heavy clearance across all regions and overhead dollar costs continue to decline.

## Regional Observations

**North America:** Wholesale continues to grow and direct-to-consumer (DTC) declines should moderate. North America Wholesale is improving as order books with existing partners (e.g., Dick's Sporting Goods, Foot Locker) and re-established partners (e.g., Run Specialty) continue to grow, and business with new partners (e.g., Amazon) keeps building. As DTC sales finish lapping last year's heavy clearance, the disparity between DTC and Wholesale trends should narrow over the coming quarters.

**China:** The region continues a multi-quarter reset: reducing lower-quality sales, renovating stores, and building a local team. While marketplace sell-through is positive, sell-in remains negative as Nike reduces lower-quality discounted online sales and invests into the market (e.g., store renovations, consumer insights across 20 cities, local design and product team).

## Investor Day

The upcoming Investor Day will include medium-term guidance and a calendar 2027 product showcase, under new CFO Dave Denton (starting August). We view this as a potential catalyst, with a concrete medium-term framework and visibility into the product roadmap for the next 12-18 months.

## Detailed Takeaways

### Cadence of Growth Through the Year

- Lifestyle (approximately 60% of the portfolio) is still expected to decline in FY27 overall (we model mid-single-digit percentage declines for FY27). Lifestyle Footwear (including Nike and Jordan brands) together with Lifestyle Apparel comprise nearly 60% of sales, with the Nike brand alone roughly 50% and Jordan's Lifestyle portion about high-single-digit percentage. This part of the business includes the Classics franchises (Air Force 1, Air Jordan 1, and Dunk), which have been declining significantly and together drove approximately $2 billion in revenue decline (approximately 4 percentage point headwind on total sales) in FY26. They should continue to decline in FY27, though to a smaller extent and weighted to the first half, particularly Dunk as it laps a heavy FY25 holiday season, while AF1 and AJ1 have largely stabilized.

- We model a mid-single-digit percentage decline in Lifestyle for FY27, narrowing the drag on total company sales from 4 percentage points in FY26 to approximately 2 percentage points in FY27. As this drag on sales fades, we expect total revenue growth to return to positive territory, which we model happening in the second half of FY27.

- China and Europe weigh on sales for Q1, driving guidance for low-single-digit to mid-single-digit percentage declines. The impact of lapping last year's promotional activity in Europe, as well as intentionally reducing sell-in in China, are the main drivers for Q1's decline. North America is further along in the cleanup, but management is still managing sell-through volatility and order books partner by partner to keep the market clean before new Lifestyle products launch in the second half of calendar 2027. Q1's guidance also includes some conservatism given that Q4 exit rate was running below plan and management assumed a continuation of that trend rather than an improvement. On the positive side, as Nike pays more attention to quality of sales and full-price selling, gross margin should improve from Q1 onward.

- Q2's sequential deceleration is not an underlying deterioration, but rather a function of tough comparisons in North America and Europe. Nike noted that the underlying business is not expected to worsen in any way in Q2. However, two factors make the comparison tougher: (1) EMEA's Digital business is lapping last year's highly promotional record Cyber Week/Black Friday period, when Nike moved high volumes at steep discounts, and (2) North America's Wholesale business is lapping 24% growth (versus roughly 10% in other quarters) from opening up new accounts and timing of shipments last year.

### Regional Observations: North America

- North America Wholesale growth is coming from a mix of existing partnerships, re-established relationships, and new business. Existing partners (e.g., Dick's Sporting Goods, Foot Locker) are growing their book of business, with Foot Locker seeing its first quarter of positive sell-in and sell-through in several years. Re-established partners such as Running Specialty are also growing their order books with Nike after Nike re-engaged with them, offering localized service, dedicated account teams, and better access to new products. Finally, new business also continues to be an area of growth, including Amazon, where Nike managed volumes tightly at first to create room to grow on a multi-year basis without over-distributing product.

- The gap between sell-in and sell-through, and between Wholesale and DTC sales, should narrow. Market participants have noted this gap over the course of the year, with questions around over-selling into the channel as sell-through rates (based on real-time data) appear lower than Nike's reported sell-in. Management noted that the gap between the two trends this year partly came from the fact that sell-in was lapping prior year cancellations and returns to vendor, which acted as a contra-revenue to depress prior-year sales (hence making FY26 sell-in appear higher). Over the coming quarters, management expects the spread between sell-in and sell-through to narrow. As the year-over-year reductions in Classics inventory anniversary, DTC sales should stabilize as well. As DTC sales stabilize and Wholesale sell-in rates normalize, the gap between Wholesale and DTC trends should also narrow.

- New Lifestyle product in calendar 2027 should drive both Wholesale and DTC growth. Nike is managing sell-in carefully this calendar year ahead of planned launches of a range of new Lifestyle (Sportswear in Nike nomenclature) franchises in Spring 2027, which should hit Q4 FY27 and then into FY28. Management expects these new launches in the Spring to drive a boost to Wholesale sell-in, especially for retailers that tend to skew more heavily Lifestyle versus Performance, as well as a boost to DTC sales. As noted earlier, Lifestyle sales growth is expected to be negative for the entire fiscal year 2027, but management comments suggest that Q4 Lifestyle growth may be positive from this new product (our assumption, not explicitly guided).

### Regional Observations: China

- What corrections is Nike making to China distribution? Given some recent media coverage around Nike's distribution presence in China, and several questions from market participants, Nike shared additional color on the situation in the region. Nike still plans to have a considerable physical retail presence in China (currently approximately 5,000 stores, mostly partner-owned) as well as an online presence, both DTC and via platforms including WeChat, Tmall, Taobao, Douyin, JD, and Weibo, the vast majority of which are also partner-managed. None of that is expected to change. However, Nike is pulling back from lower-quality, promoted touchpoints that in their perception dilute the brand. Examples include: reducing lower-margin sales in deep-discount shopping events like 6.18 and 11.11; actively mitigating the amount of Nike product appearing on random discount websites through resellers; and avoiding discounting products to match domestic competitors' price points just to maintain or gain share.

- The result will be a more tightly managed, premium, sport-led brand. Management is not stepping back from broader distribution—in fact, they specifically want a wide price architecture that includes not just premium product but also affordable/value-led product in different channels. However, they want to ensure a better rate of full-price sell-through, together with a more premium, sport-led presentation and positioning consistent with how Nike wants the brand to be perceived in China. Management points to the positive traffic and comparable store sales in the 100+ remodeled doors in China, which are refocused on sport, local storytelling, and newer innovation, as an indication that this repositioning should be successful.

- New local management and local design model. Nike has added a local product creation leader in China and expanded its coverage to local teams in approximately 20 cities to get closer to consumers, gather better local insights, and move more responsively by city and territory. Both on the recent earnings call and during our meetings, Nike spoke about the move toward a more localized China operating model as part of the repositioning of the China market, including more localized product design and creation, which should help Nike become more nimble and responsive in market.

### Margins

- Gross margin should improve before the top line turns, as Nike prioritizes high-quality sales over discount-driven sales. Management expects gross margin to move directionally positive starting in Q1, supported by lower discounting, better full-price realization, reduced off-price/liquidation headwinds, cleaner inventory, and improved channel quality. Particularly in China and Europe, Nike is intentionally pulling back from heavy promotions and reducing year-over-year discounting, which is a drag on FY27 sales but supports gross margin. North America margins have already been improving on an underlying basis, excluding tariff noise, as the marketplace cleanup is further along. Over time, lower liquidation and less promo-driven Digital revenue should support gross margin, with channel mix shift toward Wholesale becoming less of a headwind as DTC comes back into growth.

- Tariffs will be a factor in FY27, with guidance assuming a 15% rate after July. With the Q4 2026 tariff accrual effectively neutralizing the year's tariff costs, tariffs overall will be a margin consideration in FY27 versus FY26. Guidance assumes a step-up from the current 10% rate to 15% after July. This implies that for Q1, tariffs should be a tailwind (assuming 10% for two-thirds of the quarter and then 15%, lapping last year's rates above 20%). For Q2-Q3, it should be a modest tailwind (assuming 15% this year, lapping last year's rates above 20%), and then for Q4 it should be a headwind (lapping the large tariff accrual boosting Q4 2026 margins). Of course, these effects could vary depending on the current Section 301 tariffs, which could end up above or below the 15% rate embedded into guidance post-July.

- SG&A discipline is the main reason Nike can stabilize earnings per share while revenue is still declining. Management reiterated roughly flat year-over-year EPS growth over the 9-month guided period (Q4 2026 through H1 2027) despite revenue guided down over that period, reflecting fixed cost discipline and productivity benefits. Q1 Operating Overheads dollars are expected to be down, netting out a high-single-digit percentage increase in marketing dollars (World Cup investment), while for the remainder of FY27, marketing is expected to moderate. Management remains focused on managing Overheads tightly in the coming year, which should help support earnings growth until a revenue inflection occurs.

### Expectations for Investor Day

- Market participants should expect second-half and medium-term guidance at the November Investor Day. Management reiterated that Nike plans to issue guidance for not only the second half but also the medium term, including a growth framework to help market participants think through the next phase of top-line and earnings growth for the company.

- Investor Day will also include a detailed look at the 2027 pipeline, particularly product showcases of the new Lifestyle (Sportswear) footwear collections launching in Spring 2027, as well as more on the Sport Offense and category-specific product launches upcoming.

- Capital allocation will likely remain conservative until the new CFO lays out the framework. Nike has a strong balance sheet and net cash position, remains committed to the dividend, and has paused buybacks near term despite historically being active in repurchases. There are two tranches of Covid-era debt maturing in FY26, and paying down debt is an option management is considering, given the higher cost of refinancing. At the Investor Day, the new CFO will clarify capital allocation priorities going forward, including how Nike expects to balance dividend commitment, debt maturities, buybacks, and reinvestment.

- The lack of second-half guidance is tied to the CFO transition, rather than a lack of visibility. Nike provided an unusual 9-month guide (Q4 through H2) at Q3 results, rather than a full-year guide, which raised questions about whether this was an indication of continued lack of visibility. Management noted two reasons for this unusual guide: first, to set up the Investor Day as the catalyst for laying out the broader growth framework, and second, to allow incoming CFO Dave Denton to issue the forward guidance rather than inheriting it.

- In the CFO search, Nike prioritized public-company experience and consumer/retail experience rather than Sportswear expertise. Nike was looking for someone with extensive CFO experience and consumer sector experience, not necessarily someone with a background in Sportswear. Dave Denton comes from CFO roles at CVS and Lowe's within Consumer, as well as Pfizer. The remainder of Nike's senior leadership team has significant Nike-specific expertise and tenure, including CEO Elliott Hill, who has 30+ years at Nike.

We view this as a potential catalyst for the company, as concrete medium-term numbers and a strong product preview should improve visibility and provide a framework for medium-term expectations.
