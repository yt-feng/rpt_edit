# Nike: Key takeaways from Bernstein's Retail Forum

Last week, we hosted Nike IR at our Retail Forum for investor meetings. The turnaround is ongoing, though the tone was constructive and visibility seemingly improving.

FY27 remains a transition year with Lifestyle (“Sportswear”) in modest decline until clearance is lapped and newness comes in the Spring. Last week, we hosted Nike Investor Relations at our Bernstein Retail Forum for investor meetings.

We recap the key takeaways below. Bernstein clients are welcome to reach out to us for more detailed takeaways and a discussion of our views on Nike.

## CADENCE OF GROWTH THROUGH THE YEAR

\- Lifestyle (\~60% of the portfolio) is still expected to decline in FY27 overall (we model \~MSD% declines for FY27 - see Exhibit 1). Lifestyle Footwear (including Nike and Jordan brands) together with Lifestyle Apparel comprise nearly 60% of sales, with Nike brand alone roughly \~50% and Jordan's Lifestyle portion about HSD%. This part of the business includes the Classics franchises (Air Force 1, Air Jordan 1 and Dunk) which have been severely declining, and together drove a \~\$2b revenue decline (\~4 pt headwind on total sales) in FY26. They should continue to decline in FY27 though to a smaller extent and weighted to the first Half, particularly Dunk as it laps a heavy FY25 holiday season, while AF1 and AJ1 have largely stabilized.

\- We model a MSD% decline in Lifestyle for FY27, narrowing the drag on total company sales from 4 ppts in FY26 to \~2 ppts in FY27. As this drag on sales fades, we expect total revenue growth to return to positive territory, which we model happening in H2 FY27.

\- China and Europe weigh on sales for Q1, driving the guidance for -LSD% to -MSD%. The impact of lapping LY's promotional activity in Europe, as well as intentionally cutting sell-in in China, are the main drivers for Q1's decline. N.Am is further along in the cleanup, but Mgmt is still managing sell-through volatility and order books partner by partner, to keep the market clean before new Lifestyle products launch in H2'27. Q1's guide also includes some conservatism given that Q4 exit rate was running below plan and Mgmt assumed a continuation of that trend rather than an improvement. The flip side is that, as Nike pays more attention to quality of sales and full-price selling, gross margin should improve from Q1 onward.

\- Q2's sequential deceleration is not an underlying deterioration, but rather a function of tough compares in North Am and Europe. Nike noted that the underlying business is not expected to worsen in any way in Q2. However, two factors make the compare tougher: (1) EMEA's Digital business is lapping LY's highly promotional record Cyber Week / Black Friday period, when Nike moved high volumes at steep discounts, and (2) North America's Wholesale business is lapping +24% growth (vs roughly +10% in the other quarters) from opening up new accounts and also timing of shipments LY.

\- Exhibit 1 shows our view of the moving parts for FY27 total: Lifestyle (aka Sportswear) declining as detailed above; Performance Footwear growing above market from continued newness in Running, Football and soon Basketball (Caitlin Clark signature product coming in Fall 2026); Performance Apparel growing slightly above market benefiting from World Cup apparel; Converse declining.

## REGIONAL COLOR: NORTH AMERICA

\- North America Wholesale growth is coming from a mix of existing partnerships, re-established relationships, and new business. Existing partners (e.g. Dick's, Foot Locker) are growing their book of business, with Foot Locker seeing its first quarter of positive sell-in and sell-through in many years. Re-established partners such as Running Specialty are also growing their order books with Nike after Nike is now reengaging with them after many years, with localized service, dedicated account teams, and better access to new products. Finally, new business also continues to be an area of growth, including Amazon where Nike managed volumes tightly at first in order to create room to grow on a multi-year basis without over-distributing product.

\- Gap between sell-in and sell-through, and between Wholesale and DTC sales, should narrow. Investors have noted this gap over the course of the year, with concerns around over-selling into the channel as sell-through rates (based on real-time data) look lower than NKE's reported sell-in. Mgmt noted that the gap between the two trends this year in part came from the fact that sell-in was lapping prior year cancellations and returns to vendor, which acted as a contra-revenue to depress prior-year sales (hence making FY26 sell-in look higher). Over the coming Qs, Mgmt expects the spread between sell-in and sell-through to narrow. As the YoY reductions in Classics inventory anniversary, DTC sales should stabilize as well. As DTC sales stabilize and Wholesale sell-in rates normalize, the gap between Wholesale and DTC trends should also narrow.

\- New Lifestyle product in calendar 2027 should drive both Wholesale and DTC growth. Nike is managing sell-in carefully this calendar year ahead of planned launches of a range of new Lifestyle (aka Sportswear in Nike nomenclature) franchises in Spring 2027, which should hit Q4 FY27 and then into FY28. Mgmt expects these new launches in the Spring to drive a boost to Wholesale sell-in, especially for retailers that tend to skew more heavily Lifestyle vs. Performance, as well as a boost to DTC sales. As noted in the prior section, Lifestyle sales growth is expected to be negative for the entire fiscal year 2027, but Mgmt comments suggest that Q4 Lifestyle growth may be positive from this new product (our assumption - not explicitly guided).

## REGIONAL COLOR: CHINA

\- What corrections is Nike making to China distribution? Given some recent media coverage around Nike's distribution presence in China, and several investor questions, Nike shared some more color on the situation in the region. Nike still plans to have a considerable physical retail presence in China (currently \~5k stores that are mostly partner-owned) as well as presence online, both DTC and via platforms including WeChat, Tmall, Taobao, Douyin, JD and Weibo, the vast majority of which are also partner-managed. None of that is expected to change. However, Nike is pulling back from low-quality, promoted touchpoints that in their perception dilute the brand. Some examples: reducing low-margin sales in deep-discount shopping events like 6.18 and 11.11; actively mitigating the amount of Nike product appearing on random discount websites through resellers; avoiding discounting products to match domestic competitors price points just to maintain/gain share.

\- The result will be a more tightly managed, premium, sport-led brand. Mgmt is not stepping back from broader distribution - in fact they specifically want a wide price architecture that includes not just premium product but also affordable / value-led product in different channels. However, they want to ensure a better rate of full-price sell-through, together with a more premium, sport-led presentation and positioning, that is consistent with how Nike wants the brand to be perceived in China. Mgmt points to the positive traffic and comp store sales in the 100+ remodeled doors in China, which are refocused on sport, local storytelling and newer innovation, as an indication that this repositioning should be successful.

\- New local management and local design model. Nike has added a local product creation leader in China and expanded its coverage to local teams in \~20 cities to get closer to consumers, gather better local insights, and move more responsively by city and territory. Both on the recent earnings call and during our meetings, Nike spoke about the move towards a more localized China operating model as part of the repositioning of the China market, including more localized product design and creation, which should help Nike become more nimble and responsive in market.

## MARGINS

\- GM should improve before topline turns, as Nike is prioritizing high-quality sales over discount-driven sales. Mgmt expects GM to move directionally positive starting in Q1, supported by lower discounting, better full-price realization, reduced off-price / liquidation headwinds, cleaner inventory and improved channel quality. Particularly in China and Europe, Nike is intentionally pulling back from heavy promotions and reducing YoY discounting, which is a drag on FY27 sales, but supports GM. N.Am margins have already been improving on an underlying basis, excluding tariff noise, as the marketplace cleanup is further along. Over time, lower liquidation and less promo-driven Digital revenue should support GM, with channel mix shift towards Wholesale becoming less of a headwind as DTC comes back into growth.

\- Tariffs will be a headwind in FY27, with guidance assuming a 15% rate after July. With the Q4 2026 tariff accrual effectively neutralizing the year's tariff costs, tariffs overall will be a margin headwind in FY27 vs. FY26. Guidance assumes a step-up from the current 10% rate to 15% after July. This implies that for Q1, tariffs should be a tailwind (assuming 10% for 2/3 of the quarter and then 15%, lapping last year's >20% rates). For Q2-3, it should be a modest tailwind (assuming 15% this year, lapping last year's >20% rates) and then for Q4 it should be a headwind (lapping the big tariff accrual boosting Q4 2026 margins). Of course, these effects could vary depending on the current Section 301 tariffs which could end up above / below the 15% rate embedded into guidance post-July.

\- SG&A discipline is the main reason Nike can stabilize EPS while revenue is still declining. Mgmt reiterated flattish YoY EPS growth over the 9-month guided period (Q4'26 - H1'27) despite revenue guided down over that period, reflecting fixed cost discipline and productivity benefits. Q1 Operating Overheads dollars are expected to be down, netting out a HSD % increase in marketing dollars (World Cup investment) while in the remaining of the year FY27, marketing is expected to moderate. Mgmt remains bullish about being able to manage Overheads tightly in the coming year, which should help salvage earnings growth until a revenue inflection comes through.
