## INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS

The upcoming 1H26E updates could represent a turning point for the sector. Many long-only investors have been sitting on the fence waiting and seeing for global luxury demand to improve. Major companies providing a combination of better than 1Q26 growth, (slightly) positive beats, and a more than decent 1H26E profit performance could be a catalyst for investors to engage or at least put a toe in the water. The sector is 20% cheaper than the 10-year history, and the market may be looking for alternative places for funds to work as the tech boom seems to be reaching a peak.

In this context we have a bias for quality, as the first port-of-call for long-only investors, as well as self-help stories with more promising trajectories:

\- High Quality (see Global Luxury Goods: The Six Rings of Quality): In this group, we find continue to see Richemont as our top-pick. In the near-term, jewellery momentum remains strong and could well persist for longer than the market expects. In the medium-to-long-term, improved governance and a structural advantage in an increasingly polarized consumer environment remain underappreciated (see Global Luxury Goods: Preparing for consumer polarisation and Part 2). Brunello Cucinelli is also favored for its quality and potential for mean reversion. It is more difficult to be positive on Hermès in the short-term, but one or two weak quarters can be forgiven by the market, if they return to high single digit growth (see Hermès: Valuations already discount a 'Ferrari reset'). We believe Hermès could do more to achieve this (see Hermès: Stretching upwards).

\- Promising Self-Help: We find LVMH sitting between high quality and self-help. Lingering concerns around the W&S turnaround and the Arnault family's ‘Darwinian’ succession process are counter-balanced by Dior’s revival, cost efficiencies, and LVMH’s traditional position as a sine quo non within any luxury portfolio. Turnaround at Burberry is well on track. One-year anniversary of the Burberry Forward strategy has paid off, in the form of improved brand momentum and stronger full-price sell-through. With a solid foundation in place, the next leg of the turnaround will see focus shifting to driving store productivity, by capitalizing on the extended momentum in other categories from the core outerwear & scarf; Ferragamo is at the earlier stage of and trading at the lower end of the trading range. Recent conversation with management suggests a possible turnaround in the making, as a team of “old hands” are tackling key issues around the brand, assortment and retail store network.

## METHODOLOGY

We augment our alternative dataset tracking luxury shopping mall sales in Mainland China, with region retail sales data in Hong Kong, Korea, Japan, and France. We run correlations with reported regional performance for LVMH F&LG (on semi-annuals), Richemont (on quarters), Moncler brand (quarters), and Gucci (quarters). Details of the regressions can be found in the appendix (Exhibit 22 to Exhibit 32).

## In order to arrive at a single predicted value for 2Q26 at group/ division level:

\- We use the predicted value from the regressions for each region for the non-F&LG company/ divisions, combined with consensus estimates for regions where we don't have a reliable correlation (e.g. Americas and MEA for Richemont). We then apply a set of weights based on the reported regional revenue mix in the prior year if available (independent variable for Richemont APAC); Otherwise, we take a simple average (e.g, independent variable for Gucci APAC regression).

\- For LVMH specifically, given they only provide regional sales performance for F&LG on a half-yearly basis, the process is slightly different: we first arrive at a predicted total revenue for 1H26e, then subtract reported 1Q26 revenues. This then allows us to calculate the predicted reported growth for 2Q26. One can then arrive at a predicted OSG by taking into account the MtM FX impact, which is likely to be c. -3pts for 2Q26.

## We flag the following caveats to our data and regressions:

\- Despite the high correlations between reported regional performances and our data, there are still certain level variances around the predicted value, which could be substantial when expectations are low (e.g. F&LG).

\- All our datasets, bar French retail data, are only available up to May-26. To fill the gap,

\- For HK, we assume that the trend observed in April and May 2026 has persisted into June 2026 for the HK market.

\- For Japan, we use the regression implied growth rate from the preliminary footfall growth. This is based on the high correlation between preliminary footfall (e.g., the May report, released on 25 $^{th}$ June 2026, note that footfall was up +7.9% YoY for June period up to 18 $^{th}$ June 2026) and reported YoY category growth (e.g., apparel sales growth for Jun-26, to be reported in the last week of Jul-26).

\- For France, only April 2026 data is available, which casts greater uncertainty for predicted values derived from the related regression (i.e. Richemont Europe and LVMH F&LG France performance) before taking into account the added uncertainty from conflict in the Middle East.

\- Our China alternative dataset is based on estimated sales from stores located across a wide range of Chinese shopping malls, spanning different cities, tiers, and sizes. However, it excludes Hang Lung malls (e.g. Plaza 66 in Shanghai).
