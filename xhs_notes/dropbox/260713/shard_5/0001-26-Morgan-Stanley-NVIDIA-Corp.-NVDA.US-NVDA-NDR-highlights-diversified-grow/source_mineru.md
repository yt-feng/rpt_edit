# NVDA NDR highlights diversified growth opportunities

NVIDIA NDR with the executive team highlighted high confidence in an accelerating and diversifying growth story that should show appeal to value investors as well as growth.

## Key Takeaways

■ Accelerating growth still the focus from here

Citing growth from AI lab share gains, from hyperscalers, but also from a broader base of neoclouds, enterprise, and sovereigns

■ Hyperscale remains a strong growth vector with networking & CPU adding to SAM

As space, power, and geopolitics are central to decision making sovereign and neoclouds could outpace over time

\- Increasing focus on value based investors - in addition to core growth - could be key to closing value gap vs. peers; reiterate OW/top pick in semis

This week, we hosted a number of investor meetings in California as part of an NDR with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, CFO Colette Kress, and head of IR Toshiya Hari. The meetings were upbeat, as expected, with the company describing "accelerating growth rates" even as revenues approach the \$100 bn per quarter mark.

Orienting the commentary around the new segmentation, the company described 3 segment based growth drivers:

1) Growth within AI labs, which they said make up about 20% of total demand currently. Notably, one of the more prominent frontier models was developed mostly on an ASIC, so NVIDIA had minimal exposure, but that has risen to close to 50%, while other frontier models continue to be mostly on NVDA.

2) Growth within traditional hyperscalers, which (per the new segmentation) is about half of the revenue. Increasingly growth in this segment is limited by land space and power, but growth remains very strong, and NVIDIA is seeing SAM expansion based on networking, and more recently CPU. The company repeated its \$20 bn cpu target for this year, with a sense that close to half of that is outside the head node - meaning a very substantial presence in standalone CPU racks with Vera in the second half, due to the chip being purpose built for single threaded workloads with a large die, smaller core counts, and memory optimization for AI.

We continue to believe that two things can be true at once: hyperscalers will look to develop and deploy custom silicon alternatives (ASICs), while NVIDIA will retain a very large portion of the business. 3) Growth in the "AI clouds, Industrial, and enterprise" segment, now about half of data center revenue. The company emphasized that limitations on power and space, as well as geopolitical/reshoring initiatives, will drive dramatic growth in this segment, where there is likely to be minimal competition.

Management emphasized the breadth of demand within sovereign and industrial applications, citing among other things retail, finance, and biotech users. Each region is working hard to develop its own AI capacity given various reshoring pressures, driving a fragmented opportunity that can take time to get started - but with substantial scale when the time comes.

The growth from neocloud customers also remains quite strong, Last week, the company announced a new business model which would allow for co-investment, credit assurance, but also revenue sharing. Investors have mostly looked at the cautious aspects of NVIDIA backstopping credit, but revenue sharing should drive a bit more focus on the upside opportunities from those deals, as the company potentially could become a partial owner of a large network of GPU clouds.

Other important takeaways from the meeting include:

A focus on value investors. Part of this road show was designed to broaden the investor base to include more value-centric investors. We highlight this because it could be important for a stock that feels like it is struggling with boundaries of being widely owned - in some cases with maximum position size - across a wide range of growth investors. This remains a growth company, obviously, with very high growth visibility for the next couple of years and a strong set of longer term drivers. But with 50% or more of cash flow moving to cash return starting now, it's also a very clear value name amid large caps, and broadening the investor base could be an important factor. The management team has navigated from small cap to mid cap to large cap to megacap and is now the largest cap company in the world, and has adapted at each stage.

Product roadmap tracking expectations. We see a strong Vera Rubin cycle driving the next 12 months. With regards to recent stories that Rubin ultra could slip to 2028, the company said that is not true and that Rubin Ultra will ship next year - but also acknowledged that there would be form factor changes as the kyber rack is being replaced by a "better solution", potentially supporting a larger single rack scale up domain. The company presented this as a positive; they continue to take risks with the product roadmap but also will quickly course correct to derisk the ramps. Important technologies including 800 volt and optical scale-up between racks remains on schedule.

Memory shortage causing adjustments. The memory shortage looks to last for several years. But if an industry requires 10x token growth each year and memory supply is "basically static" relative to that ramp, something has to give and the

industry will find ways to keep compute supply on track. This may in part be a reference to something that we have written about in the past - that NVIDIA cut the LPDDR5 content per rack in half so that they can meet rack demand - or could imply future shifts. They said that compute, networking, and memory are all inter-related, and it is possible to use the other two to optimize a shortage of the third. The company also highlighted the importance of Groq technology, which uses SRAM that "used to be much more expensive than DRAM". While some of this commentary could dampen enthusiasm for memory stocks, we would highlight the starting place that the offsets are happening due to a multi year shortage.

## Importance of "nemotron" and open source model development. NVIDIA

developed the nemotron model to enable more application specific AI designs that can't use more generic closed models; they used the example of circuit design. Open model development allows enterprise customers to own the full stack, with the ability to customize that usage around their own expertise.

Thoughts on the stock: NVIDIA remains our top pick in semis, which has been a tough call since we made it in March the stock is higher but has underperformed a sharp runup in the peer group - but our conviction remains high. We understand that there is more leverage in other parts of the supply chain - in fact that was our reason for rotating our top pick on NVDA last year to first Sandisk, then Micron - but we now feel that this is the best value in the group. To be sure, the multiple is likely constrained by the large market cap and index weighting, but in our view the value disparity will revert over time. The points made here reinforced our enthusiasm, and we reiterate our call.
