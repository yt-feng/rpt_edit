We continue to believe that two things can be true at once: hyperscalers will look to develop and deploy custom silicon alternatives (ASICs), while NVIDIA will retain a very large portion of the business. Other important takeaways from the meeting include:

A focus on value investors. Part of this road show was designed to broaden the investor base to include more value-centric investors. We highlight this because it could be important for a stock that feels like it is struggling with boundaries of being widely owned - in some cases with maximum position size - across a wide range of growth investors. This remains a growth company, obviously, with very high growth visibility for the next couple of years and a strong set of longer term drivers. But with 50% or more of cash flow moving to cash return starting now, it's also a very clear value name amid large caps, and broadening the investor base could be an important factor. Product roadmap tracking expectations. We see a strong Vera Rubin cycle driving the next 12 months. With regards to recent stories that Rubin ultra could slip to 2028, the company said that is not true and that Rubin Ultra will ship next year - but also acknowledged that there would be form factor changes as the kyber rack is being replaced by a "better solution", potentially supporting a larger single rack scale up domain. The company presented this as a positive; they continue to take risks with the product roadmap but also will quickly course correct to derisk the ramps. Important technologies including 800 volt and optical scale-up between racks remains on schedule.

Memory shortage causing adjustments. The memory shortage looks to last for several years. But if an industry requires 10x token growth each year and memory supply is "basically static" relative to that ramp, something has to give and the

industry will find ways to keep compute supply on track. This may in part be a reference to something that we have written about in the past - that NVIDIA cut the LPDDR5 content per rack in half so that they can meet rack demand - or could imply future shifts. The company also highlighted the importance of Groq technology, which uses SRAM that "used to be much more expensive than DRAM". To be sure, the multiple is likely constrained by the large market cap and index weighting, but in our view the value disparity will revert over time. The points made here reinforced our enthusiasm, and we reiterate our call.
