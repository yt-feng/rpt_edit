# Japan Technology: Semiconductors: Investor visit feedback: Expectations for front-end firms seem high, including for price hikes

Many investors were bullish on front-end equipment companies in particular within SPE

We held around 40 investor meetings in Hong Kong from July 6 to 10. Although the upward share price trend seen until June has changed somewhat, many investors generally maintained a bullish stance on the sustainability of AI demand and the semiconductor/SPE cycle. In terms of sub-sector preferences, we had the impression that many investors favored (1) front-end over back-end, (2) memory capex over non-memory capex, and (3) CPU-related over GPU/ASIC-related, with a large number of investors bullish on front-end equipment companies in particular (Tokyo Electron (TEL), SCREEN Holdings, Kokusai Electric) due to expectations for WFE market expansion. On the other hand, we came away with the impression that there were not many bullish investors on back-end companies (Disco, Advantest) or on companies with relatively low memory exposure (Lasertec, Ebara). For Kioxia Holdings, while many investors saw the recent share price correction as a good opportunity to add to positions, our impression is that there were also quite a few concerns that earnings surprises are becoming less likely compared to before due to the lower rate of change in NAND prices.

## Many questions regarding equipment price hikes

Frequently asked questions from investors included (1) expectations for the WFE market, (2) the stance on price hikes at front-end equipment companies and their progress, and (3) the sustainability of memory price increases and views on the supply/demand balance. On the WFE market, our impression is that investor expectations are already around US\$200 bn for CY27 (our estimate is US\$187 bn), and expectations for sustainable growth from CY28 onward were also high, partly due to the recent announcements of new fabs by memory companies. As for the possibility of equipment price hikes, with TEL proactively pushing ahead with them, expectations also appear to be rising for the possibility of this spilling over to other companies. On memory prices, we got the sense that expectations for CY3Q (Jul-Sep) NAND prices are for an increase of c.+15-20% qoq, but there was also strong caution about the possibility of the rate of change gradually slowing going forward.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired.
