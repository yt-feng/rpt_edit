# The Case for CNH Outperformance Is Structural, Not Just Cyclical

The renminbi is not merely benefiting from a favorable moment. It is being propelled by a confluence of durable forces that most market participants have yet to fully price. Since late February 2026, the US dollar index has risen 1.7 percent, yet USD/CNH has actually fallen 0.9 percent. This decoupling is not a statistical anomaly. It reflects a fundamental shift in how the offshore renminbi responds to global risk events, and it signals that the currency's appreciation path may have more room to run than consensus expects.

The asymmetry in USD/CNH sensitivity to dollar moves is the most telling signal. When the dollar strengthens, USD/CNH rises only about 28 percent as much as the dollar's move would imply. When the dollar weakens, USD/CNH falls roughly 62 percent as much. This means the renminbi is absorbing dollar strength with remarkable resilience while capturing the full benefit of dollar weakness. For investors positioning in Asian FX, this asymmetry creates a compelling risk-reward profile that is not available in most other emerging market currencies.

A global investment bank report with a modest conviction level of 3 out of 5 on a short USD/CNH trade targeting 6.60 by end-August 2026 represents a measured but clear directional view. The target implies roughly a 4 percent gain from current levels. The question for institutional investors is whether the structural drivers behind this view are strong enough to withstand the inevitable periods of dollar strength that lie ahead.

## China's Corporate FX Repatriation Is Running at Levels That Create a Self-Reinforcing Appreciation Cycle

The most powerful near-term driver of CNH strength is the scale of onshore corporate FX repatriation. April 2026 data showed net FX trade settlement of USD 47.7 billion, a surplus that represented 82.1 percent of the trade surplus after adjusting for renminbi settlement. While this ratio is slightly below the first-quarter average of roughly 65 billion, it remains extraordinarily high by historical standards.

The mechanism works as follows. Chinese exporters are bringing home a large proportion of their foreign currency earnings and converting them into renminbi. This creates a steady bid for the currency that is largely independent of speculative flows or portfolio investment decisions. The exporter remittance ratio, measured as a percentage of export value, stood at 50.1 percent in April. While this is down from the first-quarter average of 55.8 percent, it remains elevated relative to the pre-2025 period.

What makes this dynamic self-reinforcing is the expectation of further renminbi appreciation. When exporters believe the currency will continue to strengthen, they have an incentive to accelerate their repatriation rather than delay it. This front-loading of FX conversion creates additional upward pressure on the renminbi, which in turn validates the original expectation. The cycle is virtuous for the currency but creates a potential trap for investors who are short renminbi or long dollars.

The underlying trade surplus provides the foundation for this cycle. China's net trade surplus in the first quarter of 2026 totaled USD 247 billion. The economics team behind the report has raised its full-year export growth forecast to 8.6 percent from 4.0 percent, with the trade surplus projected at USD 1.16 trillion. These numbers are large enough to sustain the repatriation flow even if the remittance ratio declines modestly from current levels.

## The Geopolitical Catalyst Has Shifted from a Headwind to a Tailwind for Capital Inflows

The US-Iran stalemate and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz created a natural experiment in currency resilience. China's energy security position is among the strongest in Asia, and its relative insulation from the supply shock meant that the renminbi could outperform even as other Asian currencies weakened. This is not a one-off event. It demonstrates that the renminbi is now being priced with a geopolitical risk premium that is lower than its regional peers.

The more consequential geopolitical development is the recent Xi-Trump summit. The meeting has created what the report describes as a constructive backdrop for US-China relations in the coming months. This matters for capital flows because geopolitical stability is a necessary condition for sustained foreign portfolio investment into Chinese assets.

The data on China-focused equity ETF flows provides a clear signal. Foreign inflows into these funds totaled USD 2.2 billion from May 1 to May 19, with a notable daily inflow of USD 811 million on May 18, the first trading session after the summit concluded. The last comparable episode of large monthly inflows occurred in February and March 2025, following the release of Deepseek-R1, which triggered a wave of enthusiasm about Chinese technology capabilities.

The pattern is instructive. Foreign capital flows into China are episodic and catalyst-driven. They do not follow a smooth trend. But each catalyst that attracts inflows creates a base of investors who are now long renminbi-denominated assets and who will be reluctant to exit quickly. This stickiness of foreign holdings provides a buffer against sudden capital outflows.

The trade component of the summit outcome is also material. China's Ministry of Commerce has confirmed details of a tariff reduction on approximately USD 30 billion worth of goods. China has also signaled that it will not retaliate against new Section 301 tariffs that the US has imposed as replacements for previous IEEPA tariffs. This restraint reduces the risk of an escalating trade conflict and removes a key source of uncertainty that has historically weighed on the renminbi.

## The Fixing Mechanism Is Drifting Lower Despite Apparent Official Restraint

The daily USD/CNY fixing provides one of the clearest windows into official thinking about the renminbi. On May 21, the fixing came in at 6.8349, its lowest level since February 2023. This decline has occurred even as the dollar has strengthened. Since May 8, when the latest upswing in DXY began, the daily fixing has averaged 14 pips lower.

The persistence of positive model errors is worth examining. The actual fixing has exceeded the model projection by an average of 417 pips. This means the authorities are setting the fixing weaker than a pure mechanical model would suggest, but they are doing so within a context where the overall trend is still toward renminbi appreciation.

The interpretation that best fits the data is that the authorities are managing the pace of appreciation rather than resisting it. They are using the fixing mechanism to smooth the path of the renminbi, preventing the kind of rapid moves that could destabilize markets or trigger speculative feedback loops. But the direction of travel is clear. The fixing is drifting lower, and the drift is accelerating when geopolitical conditions improve.

For investors, the implication is that official policy is not a barrier to renminbi appreciation. It is a moderating influence that reduces volatility but does not change the underlying trend. The question is whether the authorities would step in more forcefully if the pace of appreciation accelerated beyond their comfort zone, and what that comfort zone might be.

## The Undervaluation Argument Provides a Multi-Year Backdrop for Structural Appreciation

The most compelling structural argument for renminbi appreciation is the scale of its current undervaluation. Based on four different FX valuation models, the renminbi is undervalued by an average of 9.5 percent. The productivity-adjusted real effective exchange rate shows an undervaluation of 19.3 percent.

These numbers are large enough to matter for strategic asset allocation decisions. An undervaluation of this magnitude means that even if the renminbi appreciates significantly from current levels, it would still be below its fair value by most measures. This creates a margin of safety for long renminbi positions that is absent in most other currencies.

The productivity-adjusted measure is particularly relevant because it captures the structural improvements in China's economy that are not reflected in standard purchasing power parity calculations. As China's manufacturing sector continues to move up the value chain, its export basket becomes less price-sensitive, and its currency can appreciate without damaging competitiveness. The experience of Japan and Germany in their high-growth phases provides historical precedent for this pattern.

The open question is whether the market will eventually converge to fair value or whether structural factors such as capital controls and policy intervention will keep the renminbi persistently undervalued. The evidence from the past decade suggests that convergence is slow and uneven, but that the direction of travel has been toward appreciation.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Vulnerability to a Dollar Resurgence

The report's analysis is thorough on the drivers of CNH outperformance, but it leaves several important questions unresolved. The most significant is the vulnerability of the renminbi to a sustained dollar resurgence driven by factors outside China's control.

The report acknowledges that a stronger dollar backdrop could temporarily slow the downtrend in USD/CNH. But it does not fully explore the scenario where the dollar strengthens by 5 to 10 percent rather than the 1.7 percent move seen since late February. In such a scenario, would the asymmetry in USD/CNH sensitivity hold, or would it break down as dollar strength becomes more extreme?

The answer depends on whether the corporate repatriation flow can withstand a significant dollar rally. If exporters begin to expect dollar strength to persist, they might delay their repatriation, waiting for even more favorable exchange rates. This would reduce the flow of FX conversion that has been supporting the renminbi. The report's data shows that the exporter remittance ratio has already declined from the first-quarter average, which could be an early signal of this behavior.

Another unresolved question is the sustainability of the trade surplus. The report's economics team has raised its export growth forecast, but this projection assumes that global demand remains robust and that trade tensions do not escalate further. If the US economy slows or if new tariffs are imposed, the trade surplus could narrow, reducing the flow of FX settlement that underpins the renminbi.

Finally, the report does not address the risk that foreign portfolio inflows could reverse if global risk appetite deteriorates. The ETF flow data shows that inflows are concentrated around specific catalysts. If the next catalyst is negative rather than positive, the same mechanism that brought money in could take it out just as quickly.

## A Decision Framework for Positioning in CNH

For institutional investors, the question is not whether the renminbi will appreciate, but how to position for the appreciation in a way that accounts for the risks. The following framework translates the report's analysis into actionable decisions.

The first decision is the time horizon. The structural drivers of renminbi appreciation are strongest over a 6 to 12 month horizon. The trade surplus, the undervaluation, and the corporate repatriation cycle are all medium-term forces that are unlikely to reverse quickly. For investors with a longer time horizon, the case for being long renminbi or short USD/CNH is compelling.

The second decision is the conviction sizing. The report assigns a conviction level of 3 out of 5 to the short USD/CNH trade. This is not a maximum conviction call. It reflects the reality that the path of the renminbi will be volatile and that there are identifiable risks that could cause temporary setbacks. Position sizing should reflect this measured conviction level.

The third decision is the hedge. The most significant risk to the renminbi appreciation view is a sustained dollar rally driven by a global risk-off event. Investors can hedge this risk by maintaining exposure to other currencies that also benefit from dollar weakness, or by using options structures that limit downside risk while preserving upside participation.

The fourth decision is the entry point. The report's target of 6.60 by end-August implies that the appreciation will occur gradually. Investors should be prepared for periods of consolidation and even temporary reversals. The key is to maintain the position through these periods rather than trying to time the exact entry and exit.

## The Open Questions That Require Deeper Analysis

The report provides a coherent and data-rich case for CNH outperformance, but it also raises questions that only deeper analysis can answer. How would the renminbi behave in a scenario where US interest rates rise sharply and the dollar strengthens by 5 percent? Would the corporate repatriation flow hold up, or would it weaken as exporters adjust their expectations? How sensitive is the trade surplus to changes in global demand, and what is the probability of a significant slowdown?

What is the official comfort zone for USD/CNY? The fixing mechanism is drifting lower, but the authorities have not signaled a specific target. Understanding the implicit policy framework would help investors gauge how much further the renminbi can appreciate before official resistance becomes a factor.

How sustainable is the foreign portfolio inflow? The ETF flow data shows that inflows are concentrated around specific catalysts. If the next catalyst is delayed or does not materialize, the flow could slow or reverse. Understanding the composition of these flows and the marginal buyer of Chinese assets would provide greater confidence in the sustainability of the trend.

These questions are not answered in the report, and they cannot be answered without access to more granular data and a deeper understanding of the policy framework. For investors who want to build a more complete picture, the full report and its original charts provide the necessary foundation.
