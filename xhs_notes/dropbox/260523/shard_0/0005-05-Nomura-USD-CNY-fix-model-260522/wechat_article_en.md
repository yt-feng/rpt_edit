# The PBOC's Fix Model Is Sending a Clear Signal: The Counter-Cyclical Factor Is Being Withdrawn, Not Tightened

The People's Bank of China sets the daily USD/CNY reference rate through a transparent, model-driven process that incorporates overnight currency moves, but it retains a discretionary tool called the counter-cyclical factor. For most of the past year, that factor has been applied aggressively to lean against depreciation pressure. The latest model projection, however, tells a different story. The raw model fix, without any adjustment, stands at 6.7979, a full 370 pips lower than the previous fix of 6.8349. When the counter-cyclical factor is included, the projected fix rises only to 6.8138, which is still 211 pips below the prior level. The gap between the raw model and the adjusted fix has narrowed sharply. This is not a sign that Beijing is tightening control. It is a sign that the PBOC believes the pressure has eased enough to allow the fix to move lower, and that the counter-cyclical factor is being dialed back. For market participants, the implication is immediate and consequential: the central bank is testing whether the market can handle a weaker renminbi without destabilizing expectations.

This shift matters now because the window for a gradual, orderly depreciation may be closing. The calendar of political and economic events through the end of 2026 is dense. China will host the APEC leaders' meeting in November, and President Xi is expected to visit the United States toward the end of the year. These high-stakes diplomatic moments create a natural incentive for the PBOC to avoid volatility in the exchange rate. But they also create a deadline. If the central bank wants to use the exchange rate as a shock absorber before trade negotiations intensify, the next several months are the most favorable period to do so. The model data suggests the PBOC is already moving. The question is whether this is the beginning of a sustained trend or a tactical adjustment that will be reversed if capital flows turn adverse.

The analysis that follows draws on a detailed report from a global investment bank's Asia FX strategy team, which provides a daily model for the USD/CNY fix and tracks the application of the counter-cyclical factor. The report is data-rich and technically specific, but the strategic story it tells is much broader. It is about how the PBOC manages the tension between its domestic objectives and external constraints, and how sophisticated market participants can read the central bank's intentions from the crumbs of daily fix data. This article does not attempt to summarize the full report. Instead, it extracts the core analytical insight, explores its second-order implications, and identifies the questions that the report leaves open.

## The Narrowing Gap Between Model and Fix Reveals a Deliberate Policy Shift, Not a Mechanical Adjustment

The most important number in the report is not the projected fix level itself, but the difference between the raw model projection and the projection that includes the counter-cyclical factor. That gap has shrunk to 159 pips, down from a much wider spread in previous periods. To understand why this matters, one must understand how the fix model works. The PBOC uses a basket-based approach that tracks the overnight movement of major currencies, particularly the dollar, euro, yen, and a weighted set of Asian and emerging market currencies. The raw model projection is a mechanical output based on those moves. The counter-cyclical factor is a discretionary add-on that the PBOC can use to smooth volatility, lean against one-sided market expectations, or signal its policy stance.

When the counter-cyclical factor is large and positive, it means the PBOC is deliberately setting the fix stronger than the model would suggest, effectively resisting depreciation pressure. When it is small or negative, the central bank is allowing the model to do more of the work. The current data shows that the counter-cyclical factor is being reduced, not because the model has moved in a favorable direction, but because the PBOC has chosen to apply less of a buffer. The raw model projection has dropped by 370 pips from the previous fix, which is a large move. The adjusted fix has dropped by only 211 pips, but the key point is that the central bank allowed most of the model-driven depreciation to pass through. This is a deliberate choice.

The implication for investors is that the PBOC is signaling a higher tolerance for a weaker renminbi, provided the move is orderly and driven by market forces rather than speculative attack. This is consistent with a broader policy strategy of using exchange rate flexibility as a buffer against external shocks, including tariff risks and capital flow volatility. The narrowing gap also suggests that the PBOC is less concerned about the psychological impact of the fix breaching certain levels. In previous episodes, a move of this magnitude would likely have triggered a larger counter-cyclical adjustment. The fact that it did not is a signal worth watching.

## The Composition of Overnight Contributions Reveals Which Currency Pairs Drive PBOC Decision-Making

The report breaks down the top four weighted contributions to the projected change in the fix, and the composition is revealing. The Russian ruble contributed 18 pips, the euro contributed 11 pips, the Korean won contributed 3.5 pips, and the Australian dollar contributed negative 8 pips. This is not a random list. It reflects the PBOC's implicit weighting of different currencies in its basket, and it tells a story about which external factors are most influential on Chinese exchange rate policy at the moment.

The ruble's large positive contribution is notable. It suggests that the PBOC's fix model is picking up significant movement in the ruble, which in turn reflects the impact of sanctions, energy prices, and geopolitical risk on Russia's currency. For a Chinese policymaker, the ruble is a double-edged signal. A weaker ruble can indicate broader emerging market stress, which tends to put depreciation pressure on the renminbi. But it can also reflect a specific geopolitical dynamic that China may want to decouple from. The fact that the ruble contributed the most to the projected fix change means that events in Eastern Europe are indirectly affecting China's daily exchange rate setting.

The euro's contribution is less surprising, given its weight in global trade and reserve portfolios. But the negative contribution from the Australian dollar is worth examining. The Australian dollar is often considered a proxy for Chinese commodity demand and risk appetite. A negative contribution means the Aussie strengthened overnight relative to the model's expectations, which would normally pull the USD/CNY fix lower. This suggests that commodity markets or risk sentiment were supportive of the renminbi in the latest session. The Korean won's small positive contribution reflects the close trade and financial linkages between China and Korea, but its modest size indicates that regional FX stress is not the dominant driver at this moment.

For an investor trying to anticipate future fix moves, the composition of the overnight contributions is a leading indicator. If the ruble continues to drive the model, it will inject a volatile and geopolitically sensitive element into Chinese exchange rate policy. If the Australian dollar or euro begin to contribute more heavily, it would signal a shift in the underlying drivers. The report does not provide a full basket breakdown, but the top four contributors are enough to suggest that the PBOC is currently more exposed to geopolitical than to trade-cycle factors.

## Model Errors Have Been Persistent and Asymmetric, Raising Questions About the PBOC's Reaction Function

The report includes a chart of daily model errors, which measures the difference between the raw model projection and the actual fix set by the PBOC. The data shows a striking pattern. From January 2025 through April 2025, the model error was persistently negative, reaching as low as minus 1,800 pips in January and minus 1,200 pips in April. This means the PBOC was consistently setting the fix stronger than the model would suggest, by a very large margin. Then, from July 2025 onward, the error narrowed dramatically, oscillating around zero and occasionally turning positive, with errors of plus 600 pips in both January and April 2026.

This pattern tells a clear story about the PBOC's reaction function. During the first half of 2025, the central bank was in full defense mode, using the counter-cyclical factor aggressively to prevent the fix from reflecting the full force of depreciation pressure. The errors were large and one-sided. Starting in mid-2025, the PBOC began to relax that defense, allowing the fix to move closer to the model. By early 2026, the errors had become small and occasionally positive, meaning the PBOC was sometimes setting the fix weaker than the model would suggest.

The strategic implication is that the PBOC's reaction function is not static. It changes based on the perceived balance of risks. When depreciation pressure is acute and capital outflows are a concern, the central bank leans hard against the model. When pressure eases, it allows the model to do more of the work. The current data suggests we are in the latter phase. But the persistence of even small errors indicates that the PBOC has not fully abandoned the counter-cyclical factor. It is still managing the fix, but with a much lighter touch.

The open question is what would cause the PBOC to revert to a heavy-handed approach. The report does not answer this directly, but the data implies that a sudden spike in model errors would be the trigger. If the model begins to project rapid depreciation again, and the PBOC responds by widening the error back toward minus 1,000 pips or more, it would signal a return to crisis management mode. Investors should watch the error metric as a real-time indicator of central bank stress.

## The Calendar of High-Stakes Events Creates a Narrow Window for Exchange Rate Adjustment

The report includes a calendar of upcoming events that is deceptively simple but analytically rich. The list includes the Politburo economic work meeting in late July, the APEC leaders' meeting in Shenzhen in November, the Central Economic Work Conference in mid-December, and a planned visit by President Xi to the United States toward the end of the year. Each of these events creates both opportunities and constraints for exchange rate policy.

The July Politburo meeting is the earliest inflection point. It typically sets the economic policy agenda for the second half of the year. If the leadership signals a greater tolerance for depreciation as a tool to support exports or manage debt, the PBOC will have cover to continue reducing the counter-cyclical factor. If the meeting emphasizes stability above all else, the central bank may pause or reverse its current trajectory.

The APEC meeting in November is a different kind of constraint. China will be the host, and a volatile renminbi during a major international summit would be diplomatically awkward. The PBOC will likely want the fix to be stable or even slightly stronger in the weeks leading up to the meeting. This means the window for depreciation is essentially the period between now and late October. After that, the bias may shift back toward stability.

The Xi visit to the United States is the most consequential event on the calendar. A bilateral meeting at the presidential level creates enormous pressure for both sides to avoid disruptions. A sharp depreciation of the renminbi ahead of or during the visit would be interpreted as a hostile act by the US administration, potentially derailing trade talks or triggering new tariffs. The PBOC is acutely aware of this dynamic. The report does not speculate on the outcome of the visit, but the calendar alone suggests that the second half of 2026 is likely to be characterized by a period of adjustment followed by a period of stabilization. Investors who want to position for a weaker renminbi should be aware that the window may close sooner than the model data alone suggests.

## Translating the Fix Model into a Decision Framework for FX and Rates Investors

For a serious investor, the daily fix projection is not a trading signal in itself, but it is a crucial input into a broader decision framework. The report provides the raw data, but the user must build the framework. Based on the analysis above, a useful framework has three layers.

The first layer is the trend in the raw model projection. Is the projected fix moving consistently lower or higher? A sustained downward trend in the model, as we are seeing now, indicates that external factors are aligning to push the renminbi weaker. This is the most basic signal, but it is often the most reliable. An investor should track the week-over-week and month-over-month change in the raw projection, not just the daily level.

The second layer is the size and direction of the counter-cyclical factor. Is the gap between the raw model and the adjusted fix widening or narrowing? A narrowing gap, as we have now, suggests the PBOC is stepping back. A widening gap suggests it is stepping in. This is the most actionable signal because it reveals the central bank's intention, not just the market's pressure.

The third layer is the model error. Is the PBOC setting the fix consistently above or below the model? A persistent positive error means the central bank is deliberately allowing a weaker fix than the model would dictate. A persistent negative error means it is defending a stronger fix. The error metric is a lagging indicator, but it confirms whether the PBOC is following through on its apparent stance.

An investor using this framework would currently conclude that the PBOC is in a phase of managed depreciation, with a light touch on the counter-cyclical factor and a willingness to let the model drive the fix lower. The appropriate positioning would be to short the renminbi or to hedge USD/CNY exposure, but with a clear awareness that the window is limited. The July Politburo meeting and the November APEC summit are the two key dates that could trigger a reversal. The framework does not tell you when to exit, but it tells you what to watch.

## What the Report Does Not Answer: The Unresolved Questions About Capital Flows and the Dollar Cycle

The report is excellent at what it does, which is to model the fix and track the PBOC's discretion. But it leaves several critical questions unanswered, and these are precisely the questions that a strategist needs to explore. The most important is the relationship between the fix and actual capital flows. A lower fix does not automatically trigger capital outflows, and a stable fix does not prevent them. The PBOC's ability to manage the exchange rate depends on its willingness to use reserves, adjust interest rates, or impose capital controls. The report does not address any of these tools.

A second unresolved question is the role of the US dollar cycle. The raw model projection is heavily influenced by the dollar's movement against a basket of currencies. If the dollar enters a new weakening phase, the model will project a stronger renminbi, and the PBOC may need to decide whether to resist that or allow it. If the dollar strengthens again, the model will project a weaker renminbi, and the PBOC's current tolerance for depreciation will be tested. The report provides a snapshot of the current model inputs, but it does not forecast the dollar or the broader macro environment.

A third question is the political economy of the counter-cyclical factor. Who within the PBOC or the State Council decides when to apply it? What is the threshold for intervention? The report treats the factor as a black box, which is appropriate for a quantitative model, but it means the user must supplement the analysis with qualitative judgment about Chinese policymaking.

These are not criticisms of the report. They are the natural boundaries of a model-based approach. The report provides the data; the strategist must provide the context. For investors who want to move beyond the numbers and understand the full picture, the report is an essential starting point but not a complete answer.
