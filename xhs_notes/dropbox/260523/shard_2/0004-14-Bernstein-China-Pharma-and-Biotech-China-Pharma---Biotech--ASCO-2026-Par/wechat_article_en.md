# The Best Keytruda Booster Has Arrived, and It Reshapes the Logic of IO 2.0 Investing

The most important insight from the first batch of ASCO 2026 abstracts is not about which drug will replace pembrolizumab. It is about which drug will make pembrolizumab irrelevant by making it better. The question of "I/O 2.0" has dominated oncology investment debates for two years, with investors searching for the next checkpoint inhibitor that could surpass Keytruda in efficacy. That framing has been a distraction. The real strategic opportunity is not a replacement; it is an augmentation. And the data now suggests that the most powerful augmentation mechanism is a TROP2 antibody-drug conjugate combined with PD-1 blockade.

Kelun-Biotech's sac-TMT, when paired with pembrolizumab in first-line PD-L1 positive non-small cell lung cancer, has delivered a progression-free survival hazard ratio of 0.35 and an overall survival hazard ratio of 0.55 against pembrolizumab monotherapy. These are not incremental improvements. They are structural shifts in the efficacy frontier. If these numbers hold with data maturity, the standard of care for first-line NSCLC will be redefined, and the commercial logic of every competitor in this space must be re-evaluated.

This article interprets the ASCO 2026 readouts through a strategic lens, not a clinical one. It identifies which data points change the competitive landscape, which questions remain unresolved, and how investors should build a decision framework around these developments. The report from which this analysis is drawn contains the full data tables and subgroup analyses. What follows is the strategic synthesis.

## Sac-TMT Combined with Pembrolizumab Achieves the Strongest Early Efficacy Signal Ever Seen in First-Line PD-L1 Positive NSCLC, Making It the Definitive Keytruda Booster

The OptiTROP-Lung05 trial compared sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab against pembrolizumab alone in patients with PD-L1 tumor proportion score of at least 1%. At a median follow-up of 10.5 months, the combination arm had not reached median PFS, compared to 5.7 months for the monotherapy arm. The hazard ratio of 0.35 is the lowest ever reported in this setting. To understand what this means, one must look at the historical benchmarks.

Pembrolizumab monotherapy in this population typically delivers a median PFS of 5 to 6 months. Pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy delivers 8 to 11 months. Other TROP2 ADCs combined with PD-1 inhibitors have produced median PFS in the range of 9 to 13 months. The sac-TMT combination is trending toward a median PFS above 16 months, based on a simple approximation using the control arm median and the hazard ratio. This is not a marginal gain. It is a doubling of the efficacy bar.

The overall survival hazard ratio of 0.55, while still immature, is the best early trend observed in any trial of a PD-1 combination versus PD-1 monotherapy. The subgroup analysis is equally instructive. The combination performed better in patients with PD-L1 expression of 1 to 49 percent than in those with expression above 50 percent, and better in non-squamous histology than squamous. Both of these subgroups represent larger patient populations. This means the combination is not merely effective in a narrow niche; it has broad applicability across the most common presentation of NSCLC.

The strategic implication is clear. Any company developing a PD-1 bispecific antibody or a novel checkpoint inhibitor with the ambition of replacing Keytruda must now contend with the fact that the Keytruda plus sac-TMT combination may set a new efficacy standard that is difficult to match. The question is no longer whether a new IO agent is better than Keytruda alone. The question is whether it is better than Keytruda plus sac-TMT. That is a fundamentally higher hurdle.

## Innovent's IBI363 Shows Promising Durable Survival in Heavily Pretreated Patients, but the Sample Size Limits the Strength of the Conclusion

Innovent presented updated data from its phase 1 trial of IBI363, a PD-1/IL-2 bispecific antibody, in patients with advanced NSCLC who had progressed on prior immunotherapy. The data are encouraging but require careful interpretation.

In non-squamous patients, the median overall survival was 17.5 months at the lower dose level. In squamous patients, the median overall survival reached 18.2 months at the 3 mg/kg dose. For context, docetaxel, the standard of care in this setting, typically delivers a median OS of 9 to 12 months. The 24-month OS rate provides a more robust measure. In the IBI363 treated groups, 40 to 50 percent of patients were still alive at 24 months, compared to a typical 15 to 20 percent with docetaxel.

The caveat is the sample size. Each treatment group contained between 20 and 30 patients. With such small numbers, the median OS figures are highly sensitive to the inclusion or exclusion of a single patient. The 24-month OS rate is less volatile, but it too is based on a small denominator. The data are directionally positive and support further development, but they do not yet constitute a definitive proof of superiority.

The more important development from Innovent may be the first-line data. In a proof-of-concept study of IBI363 combined with chemotherapy in treatment-naive NSCLC patients, a high-to-low dosing regimen produced an objective response rate of 81.8 percent. This is the highest ORR reported across all similar trials. Innovent has also initiated a randomized head-to-head study of this regimen against pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy in first-line advanced NSCLC across all PD-L1 expression levels.

This head-to-head trial will be the decisive test. If IBI363 plus chemotherapy can demonstrate superiority over the current standard of care, Innovent will have a legitimate claim to a first-line IO 2.0 position. If it cannot, the company will need to find its competitive advantage in the second-line setting or in specific biomarker-defined subgroups. The partnership with Takeda will be critical. Takeda's willingness to initiate a global phase 3 trial will signal whether the data are convincing enough for a large partner to commit significant resources.

## The Report Does Not Fully Answer Whether the Sac-TMT Efficacy Signal Will Hold with Maturity or Whether the Safety Profile Will Limit Adoption

Every investor looking at the sac-TMT data must confront two unresolved questions. The first is durability. The median follow-up of 10.5 months is short for an OS analysis. The hazard ratio of 0.55 is impressive, but early OS signals in oncology trials have a history of attenuating as data mature. The question is not whether the signal will remain positive. It is whether the magnitude of the benefit will shrink to a level that is still clinically meaningful but commercially less transformative.

The second question is safety. The grade 3 or higher treatment-emergent adverse event rate for the sac-TMT combination was 55.3 percent, compared to 31.4 percent for pembrolizumab alone. This is a meaningful increase in toxicity. In a first-line setting where patients are relatively healthy at baseline, tolerability matters. Physicians may be willing to accept higher toxicity for a large efficacy gain, but the risk-benefit calculus will be scrutinized by regulators, payers, and prescribers. The full safety profile, including the types of adverse events and their manageability, will determine whether this combination becomes a broad first-line standard or a therapy reserved for specific patient subsets.

The report also does not address the question of sequencing. If sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab becomes a first-line standard, what options remain for patients who progress? Can they receive a different ADC? A different checkpoint inhibitor? The answers to these questions will determine the total addressable market and the durability of the commercial opportunity.

## Investors Should Use a Three-Dimension Decision Framework: Efficacy Magnitude, Data Maturity, and Competitive Positioning

The ASCO 2026 data create a clear framework for evaluating Chinese biotech companies in the NSCLC space. The framework has three dimensions.

The first dimension is efficacy magnitude. This is not simply about whether a drug works. It is about how much better it is than the current standard. Sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab has demonstrated a PFS hazard ratio of 0.35. IBI363 in the first-line setting has shown an ORR of 81.8 percent. Both are best-in-class or near-best-in-class numbers. Other assets in the space must be measured against these benchmarks.

The second dimension is data maturity. Kelun-Biotech's sac-TMT data come from a phase 3 trial with a randomized control arm. The sample size is large enough to provide statistical confidence, even if the follow-up is short. Innovent's IBI363 data come from a phase 1 trial with small sample sizes. The first-line head-to-head trial is ongoing but has not yet reported PFS or OS data. Kelun is closer to the finish line. Innovent has more to prove.

The third dimension is competitive positioning. Kelun-Biotech benefits from the fact that its drug is a booster to the existing standard of care. It does not need to replace Keytruda. It needs to convince physicians that adding sac-TMT to pembrolizumab is worth the additional toxicity. Innovent is attempting to replace the Keytruda plus chemotherapy regimen entirely. This is a higher-risk, higher-reward strategy. If IBI363 plus chemotherapy can beat pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy in a head-to-head trial, the commercial opportunity is enormous. If it cannot, Innovent will need to find its niche in later lines of therapy or in biomarker-selected populations.

The decision framework also requires investors to consider the partnership dynamics. Kelun-Biotech is developing sac-TMT largely independently, with a clear path to registration in China and a partnership strategy for global markets. Innovent's global ambitions for IBI363 depend on Takeda's willingness to fund and execute a global phase 3 program. The timing and terms of that partnership will be a critical catalyst.

## The Most Important Open Question Is Whether the Keytruda Booster Strategy Will Work with Future IO 2.0 Agents or Only with Keytruda Itself

The sac-TMT data raise a strategic question that the report does not fully address. If sac-TMT is the best Keytruda booster, does it also work with the next generation of checkpoint inhibitors? The answer to this question will determine whether Kelun-Biotech's asset has a long commercial tail or is tied to the lifecycle of a single drug.

There are two plausible scenarios. In the first scenario, sac-TMT is synergistic with PD-1 blockade in general, regardless of which PD-1 inhibitor is used. In this case, Kelun-Biotech could eventually pair sac-TMT with a next-generation IO agent if one emerges. The asset would be a platform, not a single-regimen product.

In the second scenario, the synergy is specific to the pharmacology of pembrolizumab, and a different PD-1 bispecific or IL-2 fusion protein might not produce the same additive benefit. In this case, the commercial value of sac-TMT is tied to the continued dominance of Keytruda. If a superior IO agent emerges that does not pair well with sac-TMT, the asset could lose relevance.

The data do not yet allow a clear distinction between these scenarios. The phase 2 trial OptiTROP-Lung01 tested sac-TMT with a different PD-L1 inhibitor and reported a median PFS of 15.4 months. This suggests the combination effect is not unique to pembrolizumab, but the sample size was small and the trial design was different. Investors will need to watch for data on sac-TMT combined with other checkpoint inhibitors to resolve this question.
