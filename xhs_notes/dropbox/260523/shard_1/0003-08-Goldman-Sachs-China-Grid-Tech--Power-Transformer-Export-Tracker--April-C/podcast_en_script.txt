EN_A: So there’s this new report tracking China’s power transformer exports for April. What’s the headline?
EN_B: Total transformer exports above 10 MVA grew 27 percent year on year in April. That’s a slowdown from March’s 56 percent.
EN_A: 27 percent is still solid, but why the drop? Is demand weakening?
EN_B: Not exactly. The report says it’s mostly base effects and regional shifts. But here’s the interesting part: switchgear exports surged 77 percent in April, compared to just 5 percent in March.
EN_A: That’s a huge jump. What’s driving it?
EN_B: It signals underlying demand strength. US switchgear prices are up 14 percent year on year, while transformer prices are only up 4 percent. So Chinese manufacturers are likely pivoting to where pricing power is stronger.
EN_A: Makes sense. What about the US market specifically?
EN_B: Exports to the US grew 95 percent year on year in April. Slightly down from March’s 118 percent, but still very strong. The report estimates the US power transformer supply gap is currently 72 percent and will only narrow to 60 percent by 2028.
EN_A: A 72 percent gap is massive. So China’s role is secure?
EN_B: The report thinks so, despite US local capacity expansion. But pricing is volatile. For example, 220-330 MVA transformer prices to the US dropped 14 percent in April alone.
EN_A: That sounds worrying. Is it a price war?
EN_B: Not necessarily. The three-month rolling average for February to April was still up 23 percent year on year. So the monthly dip could be due to product mix changes, not a trend.
