# NOM's view: Strong chip prices support the BOK's growth optimism

We expect the strong AI capex cycle to sustain strong double-digit growth throughout this year, which would support growth optimism. Indeed, the BOK raised its 2026 GDP growth forecast sharply to $2.6\%$ from $2.0\%$ , and indicated that the strong semiconductor cycles may help lift domestic demand through corporate profits, wages and tax revenue.

# Still K-shaped growth though

While we flagged Korea's twin-super cycle last year, and we believe a recovery is on the way, we remain cautious about a more broad-based recovery.

- K-shaped exports: Korea's export engine is running very hot, but with a narrow high-powered core: AI-related semiconductors, storage and infrastructure, while exports outside semiconductors are struggling with weak demand and geopolitical disruptions (Figure 2). Indeed, although higher oil prices lifted exports of oil-related products, the volume growth plunged.   
- Easing export volume growth: More importantly, custom-based export volume growth is declining sharply. Indeed, custom-based export volumes continued to fall by $18\%$ y-o-y in May again after a $17.7\%$ decline in April, posting double-digit y-o-y declines for the second consecutive month. The BOK's export volume index also likely slowed in Q2, although it is likely to maintain a double-digit pace (Figure 4).

# Divergence in export volume measures

Interestingly, the two export volume measures started to diverge in 2025, and now show more diverging trends, reflecting the growing weight of high-value, low-weight semiconductor exports. The divergence mainly reflects differences in how export prices are measured. Both indicators are based on the same customs export data, but they use different price deflators to derive real export volumes.

# ... largely due to a slower reflection of the recent chip price surge

Customs-based volumes rely on product level unit values, while the BOK's export volume index is deflated using the export price index with a 2020 benchmark structure (Figure 5). Thus, a plausible explanation for the divergence is that the BOK's export price index may be slow to reflect the recent surge in HBM and AI-related semiconductor prices. This could result in stronger measures of export volume growth than implied by customs-based indicators, which may be exaggerating exports' contribution to real GDP.

This suggests that a recovery in the domestic economy may take longer than what the strong external sector suggests as export spillover effects are likely to remain limited. Thus, we maintain our 2026 GDP growth forecast of $2.4\%$ on a slower recovery in domestic demand.

That said, as the BOK's policy reaction function has become more hawkish on inflation and financial stability concerns amid the growth optimism, we expect the BOK to take the policy rate to the more restrictive $3.25\%$ in early 2027 by delivering three 25bp hikes with the first in July, followed by two 25bp hikes at each of the October 2026 and January 2027 meetings.
