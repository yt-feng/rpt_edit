# Our process continues to evolve

Oversight of the list rests with a subcommittee of our Investment Review Committee — a team of senior research managers who source top ideas from each sector analyst but own the decision to add or remove a name from the list. The list is updated at least once a month in a published note.

As highlighted in each summary write-up, the committee seeks to identify investment views with high conviction, unique points of differentiation, a visible catalyst path and high risk-adjusted return potential. Our goal has been to remain disciplined around our stock selection process, while limiting the list to between 20-25 Buy recommendations and inviting the “competition for capital” among our analysts that many investors have to contemplate.

Since inception, this active discipline has served us well — helping us avoid drawdowns in some names after stated catalysts had passed or market views had become more closely aligned with those of our analysts. But lessons were also learned, as in other instances we would have been better served rotating out of names sooner. As with any investment process we continue to hone our approach, learning from what we got wrong while trying to mimic what we got right.

# Market Commentary - doubt seeds faith

The S&P 500 is now up 84% since Chat GTP was introduced to the world on November 30, 2022. The move has not been straight up. We had to get through the March ‘23 regional banks crisis, the August 2024 Japan taper tantrum, April ‘25’s Liberation Day, and this year’s US-Iran War. But the bounces off of intermittent bottoms have been swift and determined —each time compelling the market back up to new all-time highs.

Underlying this 3½ year rally has, of course, been the AI infrastructure trade compelled by the world's overwhelming hunger for compute power (and everything that goes with it) to run new artificial intelligence models that hold the promise of improving corporate productivity, streamlining almost everything we do, and who knows what else. As with any demand surge, however, investors are left to assess whether the surge has a cyclical component, can actually be fulfilled, and whether the product underlying the demand holds the promise that so many feel it does today (remember iPads, the Metaverse, and ISP's?).

The 20th century Catholic Trappist monk, Thomas Merton, famously said: ‘You cannot be a man of faith unless you know how to doubt.’ And it is this doubt that sometimes extends and fuels stock market rallies like the one we have seen over the past 3+ years.

As for stocks today, Ben Snider points out that, so far, faith has not had to play as big of a role in the rally as you might think (see the May-26 note, “Earnings growth paves a bumpy path to higher returns”). Our recently raised 8000 year-end 2026 S&P 500 target requires no P/E multiple expansion from here — just the realization of the 24% EPS growth rate that we forecast we are on pace to achieve this year. And, indeed, earnings growth has fueled most of the 84% rally we have seen since November 30 2022. In the December 2, 2022 “US Weekly Kickstart,” David Kostin (our strategist at the time) was forecasting 2023 EPS of \$224 for the S&P 500. Fast forward to today, and Ben Snider is forecasting 2026 EPS of \$340 — 52% higher than the forward forecast before ChatGTP was introduced. So almost 2/3s of the gain in the S&P 500 over these past 3½ years is coming from plain old earnings. The rest: faith.

Is this faith sustainable? Well if you are a follower of Thomas Merton, there appears to be just enough doubt to keep fueling the faith. Doubts are likely to persist about how much of an impact AI can have outside of the AI ecosystem itself. Jim Covello, writes in a presentation-style report from Apr-30, “AI Spend vs Benefit,” that the key question that needs to be answered for investors to understand how and where to invest going forward is ‘where is the value going to accrue in the supply chain?’ Will value accrue to the enterprise layer, the model layer, the hyperscaler layer, or the chip layer?

And in a May-5 cross-sector Technology note, Jim Schneider, Eric Sheridan, and Gabriela Borges highlight how the next leg of the AI infrastructure cycle is poised to see an explosion in required compute power in “Decoding the Agentic Economy – The Coming Inflection in AI Usage and Margins.” Agentic AI should drive an increase in token consumption of 24-times the current level. And the types of semis to provide these tokens (a term used to describe what a computer needs to generate or consume the equivalent of 3-4 words of data) is likely to be more CPUs than the GPUs that have dominated the AI infrastructure build-out so far.

Finally, to provide further perspective on how difficult it is for markets to identify structural shifts versus cyclical impulses, the relentless march up in AMZN shares over the past 20 years provides another interesting illustration. On May 14, 2007, our then-internet analyst, Anthony Noto, was forecasting AMZN to generate \$1.44 in EPS for CY2007 (see the May 14, 2007 note, “Noto’s Internet Notables”). Since that note was published, however, AMZN underwent a series of stock splits equating to a 20:1 split, implying that the \$1.44 in 2007 is equivalent to \$0.07 on today’s AMZN share base. Fast forward to today, and Eric Sheridan is forecasting AMZN to generate 2026e EPS of \$9.02 — 129X what it was earning 19 years ago (see Eric Sheridan’s Apr-30 note, “AMZN: Q1’26 Review: Investing Behind Secular Growth Themes Across Compute, Commerce & Advertising”). On May 14, 2007 AMZN shares traded at a current equivalent of \$3.09. Today, the stock trades at \$272 — 88X higher. The stock still hasn’t kept up with the earnings growth it has realized over the past 19 years. Where there is faith, doubt resides.

Where does that leave us today? Ben Snider recognizes that the strength of the recent Momentum-driven rally and midterm election seasonality suggest some tactical hedging may be prudent at this time. But we also think the market moves higher from here. Lean into stocks with the strongest earnings growth momentum, including those exposed to the AI infrastructure build-out (see Schneider and team's note above). And also lean into equities with earnings tailwinds and minimal correlation to the AI trade.

We also sat down with Pete Callahan, the US Technology, Media and Telecommunications sector specialist within GS Global Banking & Markets, last week to discuss how the Tech trade is shaping up as we exited May and looked forward to June (see “The Markets: Why There May Be More Investment Opportunities in Internet Stocks”). Where’s the trade? Callahan likes Internet stocks. DASH is on our Conviction List.
