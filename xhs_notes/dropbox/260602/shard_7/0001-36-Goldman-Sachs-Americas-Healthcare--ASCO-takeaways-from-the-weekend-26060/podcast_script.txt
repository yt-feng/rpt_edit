ZH_A: 这份研报里提到的ASCO数据，最让你觉得“这真的不一样”的是哪个？

ZH_B: 最让我印象深刻的，是SMMT的合作伙伴康方生物公布的三期HARMONi-6总生存期数据。这组数据在全体大会上公布，同时发表在《柳叶刀》上。它首次在非小细胞肺癌一线治疗中，证明了一种新方案能击败PD-1加化疗这个全球标准疗法。

ZH_A: 击败标准疗法？具体数字是多少？

ZH_B: 依沃西单抗加化疗组的中位总生存期是27.9个月，而替雷利珠单抗加化疗组是23.7个月。风险比是0.66，也就是死亡风险降低了34%。这个数字超出了我们和医生交流时设定的0.72这个门槛。

ZH_A: 所以这意味着什么？对患者和投资来说，这个判断有什么不同？

ZH_B: 这意味着，依沃西单抗这个PD-1和VEGF双抗，可能成为一线鳞状非小细胞肺癌的新标准。而且，这个结果对SMMT正在进行的全球三期HARMONi-3研究也是个积极的信号。那项研究是直接对比依沃西单抗加化疗和Keytruda加化疗。

ZH_A: 研报里还提到了结直肠癌的数据，这个也值得关注吗？

ZH_B: 是的，SMMT和康方生物还展示了一个二期研究的早期数据，针对的是不可切除的转移性结直肠癌。在20毫克每公斤的剂量组里，客观缓解率达到了70.8%，疾病控制率是100%。9个月的无进展生存率达到了76.1%。

ZH_A: 这个数字在结直肠癌里算好吗？

ZH_B: 关键意见领袖对这个数据很认可。而且，它看起来不输给辉瑞的竞争产品。当然，高剂量组毒性略有增加，但医生们对这个很熟悉，因为现在的标准疗法贝伐珠单抗也有类似的副作用。这支持了正在进行的全球三期HARMONi-GI3研究。

ZH_A: 除了SMMT，研报里还提到了Immunome的硬纤维瘤数据。这个听起来相对小众，有什么特别之处吗？

ZH_B: 硬纤维瘤市场不大，但Immunome的varégacestat在二期数据基础上，又在三期RINGSIDE研究中展示了“同类最佳”的潜力。它不仅在无进展生存期上显著优于安慰剂，在疼痛缓解等关键患者报告结局上也表现优异。

ZH_A: 和默克雪兰诺的Ogsiveo比呢？

ZH_B: 虽然没有头对头研究，但讨论环节的专家指出，varégacestat的肿瘤体积缩小更深，疼痛缓解更快更好。更重要的是，卵巢毒性更低，在55.6%的绝经前女性中出现，而Ogsiveo是75%左右。而且它是一天一次服药，更方便。

ZH_A: 但研报里也留了个悬念，是不是？关于治疗时长的问题。

ZH_B: 没错。专家提到，对于这类伽马分泌酶抑制剂，最优的治疗时长还不清楚。Ogsiveo的三年数据显示，长期使用只增加了3个新应答。所以，持续治疗的获益到底有多大，这仍然是个开放性问题。

ZH_A: 最后，Gilead的TUB-040在卵巢癌中的数据也很亮眼。它和别的ADC有什么不同？

ZH_B: TUB-040靶向NaPi2b，在铂耐药卵巢癌中，确认的客观缓解率达到了61%，中位无进展生存期是11个月，远超历史标准的5个月。最关键的差异化在于安全性，它没有出现间质性肺病、眼毒性和周围神经病变这类典型的ADC副作用。

ZH_A: 所以它的治疗窗口更宽？

ZH_B: 对，这为联合治疗提供了可能。Gilead计划在2027财年启动注册性研究。不过，剂量优化仍在进行中，最终结果还需要验证。

ZH_A: 总结一下，这次ASCO周末的数据，让你觉得最值得深入研究的三个方向是什么？

ZH_B: 第一，SMMT的依沃西单抗在肺癌中击败PD-1标准疗法，这可能会重塑一线治疗格局。第二，Immunome的varégacestat在硬纤维瘤中展现出“同类最佳”的潜力，它的上市申请已经提交了。第三，Gilead的TUB-040在卵巢癌中展示了差异化的安全性，这可能是下一代ADC的突破方向。

ZH_A: 听起来，研报里还有很多细节，比如更完整的亚组分析和竞争格局对比。如果想深入了解，可以去阅读完整报告。

ZH_B: 是的，完整报告里会进一步展开这些悬念，包括HARMONi-3的后续数据，以及varégacestat的全球销售预期。欢迎加入我们的社群，获取更多深度解读。

EN_A: So, walking out of the ASCO weekend, what was the one data set that really made you stop and say, okay, this is different?

EN_B: Without a doubt, it was the overall survival data from SMMT’s partner Akeso for ivonescimab in first-line squamous non-small cell lung cancer. This was presented at the plenary session and published in The Lancet at the same time. It’s the first time any regimen has shown a statistically significant survival benefit over a PD-1 plus chemotherapy, which is the global standard of care.

EN_A: That’s a big claim. What were the actual numbers?

EN_B: The median overall survival was 27.9 months for the ivonescimab combo versus 23.7 months for the tislelizumab combo. That’s a hazard ratio of 0.66, a 34 percent reduction in the risk of death. And that beat the bar we had heard from investors and physicians, which was around 0.72.

EN_A: So what does this mean in practical terms, both for patients and for the investment thesis?

EN_B: It means ivonescimab, which is a PD-1 and VEGF bispecific antibody, could become the new standard of care in this setting. And it provides a strong positive read-through for SMMT’s ongoing global phase 3 HARMONi-3 study, which is testing ivonescimab plus chemo head-to-head against Keytruda plus chemo.

EN_A: The report also highlighted some colorectal cancer data. Is that worth paying attention to?

EN_B: Absolutely. SMMT and Akeso presented early phase 2 data in frontline metastatic colorectal cancer. At the 20 mg per kg dose, the objective response rate was 70.8 percent, and the disease control rate was 100 percent. The nine-month landmark progression-free survival rate was 76.1 percent.

EN_A: How does that stack up against what else is out there?

EN_B: The key opinion leader presenting the data was impressed. And it appears to be in line with or better than Pfizer’s competing PD-1xVEGF molecule. There was a modest increase in toxicity at the higher dose, but physicians are already used to managing those side effects because the current standard of care includes bevacizumab. So this supports the ongoing global phase 3 HARMONi-GI3 study.

EN_A: Moving on to Immunome, the desmoid tumor data seems like a niche area. What stood out there?

EN_B: Desmoid tumors are a rare disease, but Immunome’s varegacestat has a real chance to be best-in-class. The full phase 3 RINGSIDE results confirmed a massive progression-free survival benefit, and importantly, it also showed statistically significant improvements on key patient-reported outcomes like pain relief.

EN_A: How does it compare to the existing drug, Ogsiveo from Merck KGaA?

EN_B: The discussant at the session did a cross-trial comparison. He noted deeper tumor volume reductions, faster and better pain relief, and a lower rate of ovarian toxicity. About 56 percent of premenopausal women on varegacestat experienced it, compared to 76 percent on Ogsiveo. Plus, it’s a once-daily pill versus twice-daily. That’s a meaningful differentiation.

EN_A: But the report also hinted at a remaining question, right? About treatment duration.

EN_B: Exactly. The key opinion leader pointed out that the optimal treatment duration for this class of drugs is still unclear. Recent three-year data for Ogsiveo showed only three additional responses with long-term use. So the question is, does continuous treatment really add value? That’s not fully answered yet.

EN_A: Finally, Gilead’s TUB-040 in ovarian cancer. The efficacy looks strong, but what about the safety profile?

EN_B: That’s actually the most interesting part. The confirmed overall response rate was 61 percent in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer, and median progression-free survival was 11 months, which is double the historical standard. But the key differentiator is the safety. There were no clinically relevant cases of interstitial lung disease, no ocular toxicity, and no peripheral neuropathy.

EN_A: So it’s avoiding the classic toxicities of other ADCs?

EN_B: Right. That suggests a wider therapeutic window and opens up the possibility for combination therapies. Gilead is planning to start a registrational study in fiscal year 2027. But dose optimization is still ongoing, so we need to watch that.

EN_A: So if you had to summarize, what are the three most important storylines from this ASCO weekend?

EN_B: First, SMMT’s ivonescimab beating a PD-1 standard in lung cancer. That could reshape the frontline landscape. Second, Immunome’s varegacestat establishing a best-in-class profile in desmoid tumors with a clear differentiation on safety and dosing. And third, Gilead’s TUB-040 showing a potentially safer ADC profile in ovarian cancer, which could be a breakthrough for the next generation.

EN_A: Sounds like there’s a lot more detail in the full report, especially on subgroup analyses and competitive dynamics.

EN_B: Absolutely. The full report dives deeper into the remaining questions, like the upcoming HARMONi-3 data and the global sales potential for varegacestat. It’s definitely worth a read for anyone following these names closely.
