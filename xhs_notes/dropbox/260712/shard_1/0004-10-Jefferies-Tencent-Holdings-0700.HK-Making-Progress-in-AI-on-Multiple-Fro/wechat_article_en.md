# Tencent Is Trading Earnings Growth for AI Dominance — and the Market Is Underpricing the Strategic Logic

Tencent's second-quarter 2025 earnings will tell a story of divergence. Revenue is expected to grow at roughly 9.6% year-on-year to approximately RMB 200 billion, driven by advertising market share gains and solid domestic game performance. Yet non-IFRS net income is forecast to rise only 4% year-on-year, a compression that marks the widest gap between top-line expansion and bottom-line growth the company has reported in several quarters. This is not a sign of operational decay. It is a deliberate strategic trade-off.

The company is in the midst of a multi-front AI deployment that spans a new foundation model, an enterprise workspace agent, and a consumer-facing assistant embedded into Weixin. Each of these initiatives carries upfront cost. Research and development expense is projected to rise from RMB 85.7 billion in 2025 to RMB 108 billion in 2026. Capital expenditure on property, plant, and equipment is forecast to nearly double over the same period, from RMB 160.5 billion to RMB 330 billion. Non-IFRS operating margin is expected to peak in 2025 at 37.3% and then decline to 35.7% in 2026 and 33.9% in 2027.

The market has applied amid sector-wide sentiment shifts. But the core question for observers is whether this earnings sacrifice is buying an asset that will widen Tencent's competitive moat or merely funding a catch-up race with no durable return. The evidence from the company's product pipeline and market data suggests the former.

## Weixin AI Testing Signals a Shift from Chat to Agent-to-Agent Interaction, and the Stakes Go Beyond Search

In June 2025, Tencent began small-scale testing of Weixin AI, internally called Xiao Wei, across multiple scenarios including social communication, information search, productivity, entertainment, shopping, and tool generation. The assistant is accessible from two entry points: the top-left corner of the Weixin main page and as a feature within messaging itself. The underlying model is WeLM, developed by the Weixin team.

This is not merely another chatbot integration. The significance lies in the architectural choice. Tencent is positioning Weixin AI as an early implementation of Agent-to-Agent (A2A) interaction, a paradigm in which AI agents communicate with each other to complete tasks rather than simply responding to human prompts. In a messaging ecosystem with over one billion monthly active users, the ability for an AI agent to interface with other agents — whether for booking, payment, content discovery, or enterprise workflow — creates a network effect that is difficult for standalone large language models to replicate.

The official release of Weixin AI is expected in the fourth quarter of 2026. That timeline may seem distant, but it reflects the complexity of deploying AI at the scale of the region's largest social platform. The testing phase allows Tencent to refine hallucination rates — the company reported a 15 percentage point reduction in hallucination for its Hy3 model compared to the preview version — and to optimize token efficiency before a full rollout. For observers, the key metric to watch is not the launch date but the adoption curve of A2A interactions once the feature is live. If Weixin AI becomes the default interface for task completion within the Weixin ecosystem, it will deepen user stickiness and open new monetization vectors in search, commerce, and enterprise services.

## WorkBuddy Has Already Captured the Workspace Agent Lead, Validating Tencent's Enterprise AI Strategy

While consumer-facing AI garners more attention, Tencent's enterprise AI product WorkBuddy has quietly taken the lead in the workspace agent segment. According to third-party data from Analysys for March 2025, WorkBuddy recorded 8.85 million desktop monthly visits, placing it ahead of ByteDance's Trae, Tencent's own QClaw, and Alibaba's Qoder Work.

This lead matters because the workspace agent market is structurally different from the consumer AI market. In enterprise settings, switching costs are high. Once a company integrates an AI agent into its workflow for software development, office productivity, financial analysis, design, or game development, replacing that agent requires retraining, data migration, and process redesign. First-mover advantage in this segment is sticky.

WorkBuddy's traction also validates a broader thesis: Tencent's competitive edge in AI comes not from a single model but from its ability to offer a comprehensive product suite across different scenarios. At the Tencent Cloud AI Industry Applications Summit, the company emphasized that foundation models, products, and frontier technology exploration are all necessary, but that co-design between product and model teams is the differentiator. WorkBuddy benefits from this integration because it can draw on data insights from Tencent's existing enterprise tools, including WeCom, Tencent Meeting, and Tencent Docs, creating a feedback loop that improves model performance with each interaction.

The financial implication is straightforward. Enterprise AI revenue, while still small relative to advertising and gaming, has a path to high-margin recurring revenue. Cloud revenue is expected to accelerate year-on-year in the second quarter, and WorkBuddy is a key driver of that acceleration. If the product maintains its lead through 2026, it could become a meaningful contributor to the FBS segment's profitability.

## Hy3's Pricing and Performance Improvements Are Designed for Scale, Not Just Benchmarking

The official release of Hy3, Tencent's latest foundation model, incorporates feedback from more than 50 business units and includes several enhancements that reveal the company's strategic intent. The input/output price has been cut by half, from RMB 2/RMB 8 per million tokens in the preview version to RMB 1/RMB 4 in the official release. This is not a discount; it is a volume strategy. Lower pricing is designed to support more users and more scenarios, which in turn generates more data for model improvement.

The performance improvements are concentrated in areas that matter for real-world deployment. Task success rate improved from 72% in the preview version to 90% in the official release. Token efficiency gains are substantial: compared to a competing model, Hy3 achieved a 47.4% reduction in token consumption for word processing and 49% for PPT generation. In a blind test with 270 experts working on real tasks — including frontend development, data engineering, storage, and CI/CD — Hy3 scored better than GLM-5.1.

These numbers point to a model that is being optimized for cost efficiency and reliability rather than for headline benchmark scores. The reinforcement learning enhancements in post-training suggest that Tencent is prioritizing task completion over raw parameter count. The company explicitly noted that Hy3 performs better in long-context tasks compared to models with 2-5 times more parameters. For enterprise customers who care about total cost of ownership and error rates, this positioning is more relevant than a leaderboard ranking.

The strategic implication is that Tencent is building its AI stack to be economically viable at scale. As AI inference costs fall, the addressable market expands. Hy3's pricing and efficiency improvements lower the barrier for small and medium enterprises to adopt AI tools, which in turn expands the data pool and strengthens the network effects that underpin Tencent's platform advantage.

## A Decision Framework for Evaluating Tencent's AI Investment Cycle

For observers trying to assess whether Tencent's earnings compression is justified, a structured framework is more useful than a binary bet on AI adoption. The framework rests on three variables: deployment velocity, monetization path, and competitive positioning.

Deployment velocity measures how quickly AI products move from testing to mass adoption. Weixin AI is in small-scale testing with an official launch expected in 4Q26. WorkBuddy is already live and leading its segment. Hy3 is officially released with a pricing model designed for rapid adoption. The velocity is highest in enterprise AI, moderate in consumer AI, and accelerating in the model layer. The risk is that Weixin AI faces delays or that user adoption is slower than expected. The mitigation is that Tencent controls the distribution channel, which historically has enabled rapid scaling once a product is ready.

Monetization path refers to the mechanism through which AI investment translates into revenue. For advertising, AI-driven ad tech upgrades are already contributing to market share gains, with marketing services revenue expected to grow 18% year-on-year to RMB 42.4 billion in the second quarter. For cloud and enterprise, WorkBuddy and Hy3 generate revenue through usage fees and subscription models. For Weixin AI, the monetization path is less clear but could include search advertising, transaction fees, and premium features. The risk is that consumer AI monetization takes longer than expected. The mitigation is that Tencent's advertising business provides a near-term return on AI investment while the longer-term bets mature.

Competitive positioning assesses whether Tencent's AI investments are creating durable advantages. The company's comprehensive product offerings across scenarios, its integration of product and model teams, and its control over distribution give it structural advantages over pure-play AI companies. The risk is that competitors with larger AI budgets or faster iteration cycles erode these advantages. The mitigation is that Tencent's ecosystem — Weixin, WeCom, Tencent Cloud, and its gaming portfolio — creates data and distribution moats that are difficult to replicate.

Applying this framework to the current data points suggests that the earnings compression in 2025 and 2026 is a calculated investment in widening the moat, but the base case target still implies 60% upside from the current price. The downside scenario of HKD 450 assumes unsuccessful game launches, slower ad growth, and aggressive subsidy campaigns — outcomes that would indicate the AI investment is not yielding returns. For now, the product-level evidence points toward the base case.

## The Earnings Story Is About Timing, Not Trajectory

Tencent's second-quarter earnings will show a company that is deliberately sacrificing near-term margin expansion to build infrastructure for the next phase of growth. The advertising business is already benefiting from AI-driven ad tech upgrades, with market share gains that are visible in the revenue numbers. The gaming business remains resilient, with domestic games growing faster than international games and evergreen titles like Honor of Kings and Roco Kingdom World performing well. The AI investments are concentrated in areas — Weixin AI, WorkBuddy, and Hy3 — that have clear pathways to scale.

The risk is that the investment cycle extends longer than anticipated, or that competitive dynamics shift in ways that reduce the return on capital. The company's own guidance implies that non-IFRS operating margin will decline from 37.3% in 2025 to 33.9% by 2027, a compression of over three percentage points. That is a material cost. But the offset is that revenue is expected to grow from RMB 751.8 billion in 2025 to RMB 894.2 billion in 2027, and the absolute level of non-IFRS net income is still projected to rise from RMB 259.6 billion to RMB 277.3 billion over the same period.

The tension in the stock is between observers who see margin compression as a signal of deteriorating business quality and those who see it as a necessary investment in future growth. The product-level data supports the latter interpretation. Weixin AI is testing at scale. WorkBuddy is leading its market. Hy3 is priced for adoption and performing well in real-world tasks. These are not speculative bets. They are operational realities that are already generating user engagement and revenue.

The question for observers is not whether Tencent's AI investments will pay off. It is whether the payoff arrives before the market's patience with margin compression runs out. Based on the current trajectory, the answer depends on the pace of Weixin AI's rollout and the continued adoption of WorkBuddy in enterprise accounts. Both are measurable, observable, and within the company's control. That is a risk worth pricing, not avoiding.
