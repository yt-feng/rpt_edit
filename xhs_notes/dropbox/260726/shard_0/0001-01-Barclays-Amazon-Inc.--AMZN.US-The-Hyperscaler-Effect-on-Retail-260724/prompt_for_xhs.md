As a follow-on to our recent note Hyperscalers: Too wide to ignore, too much supply to chase, we examine how Amazon and its recent issuance cadence (in line with other hyperscalers) has affected the performance of the retail sector.

## Amazon Effect on Retail Sector Performance

Retail sector composition has become more concentrated in the US and Europe as Amazon has grown to account for 35% and 25% of the USD and EUR IG retail sectors, respectively. Similar to our finding in terms of the effect that hyperscalers are having on the broader market, Amazon's issuance has been a drag on retail sector performance, resulting in excess returns versus Credit of -89bp and -70bp over the LTM and YTD periods. Ex-Amazon, the rest of the retail sector generated excess returns of 11bp and 3bp relative to Credit respectively. Over the past three months, retail ex-AMZN has also underperformed Credit by 8bp, driven by a drag in May, which coincided with the spike in gas prices (see Figure 7) and marked a month in which AMZN outperformed.

## Amazon Issuance Dynamics

## Size begets diversification, but further issuance remains on the horizon

The company's \$62bn in supply thus far this year has lifted it to the ninth-largest corporate issuer, accounting for 1.2% of the USD market. While its €14.5bn issuance led it to account for only 0.4% of the EUR-denominated portion of the Pan European Corporate Agg, making it the 54th-largest issuer, its foray into that market, along with Google's, has led the two to account for 7.88% of the long end of it (ie, €-IG 10yr+ bucket $^{1}$ ), as long bonds accounted for one-third of AMZN's € issuance. The company also entered the CHF and CAD markets this year, accounting for 5.8% of the CHF-denominated portion of the Pan European Corporate Agg, making it the fourth-largest issuer.

And with USD still accounting for 81% of the company's bond profile, we would expect additional issuance to persist in non-USD markets. AMZN has yet to tap some of the markets that Google has already entered, such as GBP or JPY, with neither having yet tapped the AUD market.

We expect AMZN to continue to look to issue in the USD market 2-3x per year, while EUR could be a twice-per-year occurrence. Given the more nascent size of the other currency markets, we estimate that scope for issuance there could be more limited (once per year at most). This would leave some reliance on the bank market, potential for equity issuance, and data center financing as other sources that could be utilized for funding.

We see scope for EUR issuance post earnings. As noted in European Investment Grade: The hyperscaler funding machine knows no borders, with the spread cost between EUR and USD issuance having become negligible, EUR issuance screens more favorably than earlier in the year.

## Issuance across currencies is creating market saturation

This is most evident in the USD market, as each of the past three USD deals that AMZN has done has come with successively higher new issue concession and lower new issue coverage, reflecting the directional trend across hyperscalers. This reflects a higher required concession to compensate for near-term spread, as well as mark-to-market risk related to the likelihood of further issuance. In addition, given the rising concentration of AMZN and other hyperscalers, many investors already hold sizable exposure and, as such, require more attractive yields to generate incremental demand. This trend is likely to persist and could be adversely affected by future deals coming in close succession (from other issuers in the space) or if there is competition from hyperscaler-adjacent supply (ie, data center bonds).

\- Of AMZN's most recent three deals, coverage ratios (ie, level of oversubscription) have generally been lower than those of other issuers (Figure 9), despite new issue concessions moving sequentially higher with each deal (Figure 10).

## Framing further issuance needs and what to look for next week

We estimate that the company could have need for incremental debt of up to \$90bn in 2027 (if GlobalStar is fully debt funded) and \$125bn in 2028. This assumes capex of \$275bn in 2027 and \$360bn in 2028. While it has already issued \$92bn of USD-equivalent debt this year, in line with our prior estimate, there is still scope for it to consider other currencies, as discussed above, or pull forward some of its 2027 needs. In addition, the company has a \$17.5bn delayed draw TL that could be fully utilized by September; in our view, this would serve as a backstop in the event it chooses to delay issuance until 2027. As such, we estimate that there is room for \$25-35bn of further debt to be added to the balance sheet by YE26 (which assumes the potential for \~\$10bn more issuance outside the USD market).

\- Given year-to-date issuance, we think that there is scope for the company to raise its capex outlook for 2026 (from \$200bn currently), with Google having recently raised its outlook by \$15bn, or suggest a more tempered CFO performance. Our sense is that neither, however, is part of market assumptions.
