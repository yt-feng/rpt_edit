EN_A: So the mid-year earnings preview for Chinese property companies just came out. The headline numbers look pretty rough. What is the main takeaway from this report?
EN_B: The core message is that first half 2026 earnings for developers are expected to drop 26 percent year on year. But the report is not asking you to panic. It is asking you to look through the numbers and find the structural winners.
EN_A: 26 percent is worse than last year's first half decline of 18 percent. What is driving that?
EN_B: Two main pressures. First, revenue booking is declining. Second, margins are getting squeezed. The earnings this half mainly reflect presales from 2025, when property prices dropped by about 15 percent. So the profit pool is naturally smaller.
EN_A: So is every developer suffering equally?
EN_B: Not at all. The report breaks them into three groups. The first group has investment property portfolios, like shopping malls. CR Land, for example, is expected to report flat earnings because of disposal gains and stable mall income. The second group can lean on asset sales to cushion the blow. The third group relies mostly on residential development, and they are seeing the biggest declines.
EN_A: What about the property management side? Is that also struggling?
EN_B: It is a mixed picture. CR Mixc is expected to grow earnings by 10 percent, driven by its commercial segment. But if you exclude CR Mixc, residential property managers are looking at near zero growth, maybe even a slight decline. The issues are weaker cash collection, vacant properties, and slower growth in value-added services.
EN_A: So the residential management space is not going to recover quickly?
EN_B: The report is cautious on that. Industry completion is declining, and vacant properties remain a headache for cash collection and margins. But there is one standout: Greentown Service, which is expected to deliver 13 percent earnings growth, thanks to double-digit revenue growth and margin improvement.
EN_A: From an investment perspective, what is the report recommending?
EN_B: Among developers, China Overseas Land and CR Land are the top picks. They are gaining market share and offer attractive dividend yields. CR Land's 2026 dividend yield is estimated at 5.6 percent. In property management, CR Mixc is the preferred name.
EN_A: So there are opportunities. But does the report leave any questions unanswered?
EN_B: Yes, and it is a big one. The report notes that developers' land acquisitions dropped 18 percent year on year in the first half. That means less saleable resources in the pipeline. The report hints that this could impact future sales, but it does not fully explore the chain reaction.
EN_A: So if you only focus on the earnings decline, you might miss the fact that supply is shrinking, which could eventually support prices?
