EN_B: The big takeaway is that the smart money is not one group. It’s actually the combination of two very different types of investors: Southbound investors from mainland China and global institutional players. When you blend their signals, you get a much more consistent edge.

EN_B: It is, and it’s a structural shift. Historically, Hong Kong was a large-cap market. Most liquidity was in giants like Tencent and Alibaba. But since late 2024, we’ve seen a significant increase in trading activity flowing into smaller and mid-cap stocks. The market has become much more tradable.
EN_A: So, stock picking is actually viable again for smaller names?
EN_B: Exactly. The report shows that the number of stocks with decent daily liquidity — over 5 million US dollars — jumped from around 200 to 500 in just one year. And a big driver of that is Southbound investors, especially retail traders from China.
EN_A: But everyone assumes Southbound money is smart money. Is that still true?
EN_B: Not entirely. Here’s the twist. While Southbound inflows have accelerated, their stock-picking effectiveness has actually faded since 2025. Why? Because more and more of that inflow is going into passive ETFs, not active stock selection.
EN_A: So the crowd is getting a bit dumber? Where’s the real alpha then?
EN_B: The report strips out the passive ETF flows and focuses only on the active part of Southbound. Then it adds two other signals: the top 20 best-performing brokers and custodians in Hong Kong, and a proprietary crowding factor that tracks global institutional positioning. Combine all three, and you get a composite strategy.
EN_A: And how does that composite perform?
EN_B: Impressively. Since 2019, the long-only version has generated an annualized excess return of 12%, with a max drawdown of only 6%. That’s a risk-adjusted return of 1.7, which is much better than any single source alone.
EN_A: So how do these groups actually differ in what they buy?
EN_B: Southbound investors lean into high-volatility tech names. Global participants prefer value stocks with high dividends, like utilities and telecoms. Their low correlation is what makes the combination so powerful for diversification.
