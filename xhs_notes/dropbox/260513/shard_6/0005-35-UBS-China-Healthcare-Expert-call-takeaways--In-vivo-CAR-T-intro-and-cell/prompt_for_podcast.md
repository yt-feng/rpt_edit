# China Healthcare

# Expert call takeaways: In vivo CAR-T intro and cell therapy market update

# Key topics from the expert call

On 12 May, we invited Mr. Kong, Director of Clinical Operations at Starna Therapeutics, to share his insights into CAR-T industry trends. Key topics included: 1) the advantages and challenges of in vivo CAR-T technology, and how its two delivery systems differ from each other, 2) the outlook for in vivo CAR-T business development (BD) in China; and 3) sales trends for CAR-T products since their inclusion in the commercial insurance drug list (CIDL).

# In vivo CAR-T: lower cost and greater efficiency than traditional CAR-T

As an emerging, still-developing technology, in vivo CAR-T offers potential advantages in both cost and efficiency compared with traditional ex vivo CAR-T. By engineering a patient's T cells directly within the body, in vivo CAR-T eliminates the need for complex, costly and time-consuming ex vivo manufacturing. According to the expert, large scale industrial production could reduce manufacturing costs to hundreds of US dollars per vial. In China, most in vivo CAR-T programmes are currently conducted under investigator-initiated trials (IITs), with a lack of robust late-stage clinical validation. Current development is concentrated in oncology and autoimmune diseases, focusing on validated targets such as CD19 and BCMA. There are two mainstream delivery systems: viral vectors and LNP-mRNA. For viral vectors, early exploratory studies have reported objective response rates (ORR) exceeding 80%, albeit based on limited sample sizes. From a safety perspective, higher incidences of grade 3+ CRS and neurotoxicity remain key concerns, underscoring the need for improved clinical management strategies. For LNP-mRNA, its strength lies in lower CRS incidence and manageable side effects, but its duration and depth of response need to be proved.

# Early-stage sector showing increased licensing interest

Recent landmark transactions and early clinical data disclosures within the industry have significantly increased market interest in in vivo CAR-T. While companies in China benefit from the ability to rapidly generate human efficacy data through IIT, MNCs typically require robust preclinical datasets to support BD transactions. This is why we have not seen a deal between MNCs and leading in vivo CAR-T companies in China.

# High pricing of traditional CAR-T continues to constrain sales ramp-up

Despite the inclusion of all five CAR-T therapies in the CIDL in January at c.30% price reduction, sales have not meaningfully accelerated, as the revised pricing remains unaffordable for most patients. Looking ahead, per unit manufacturing costs could potentially decline to approximately Rmb200,000 if key raw materials were domestically sUBStituted.
