EN_A: So today we are digging into a research report with a pretty bold title: "China Property: Ride on AI Supply Chain; Three Reasons to Upgrade." But let me ask you this — what is the one big question this report is really trying to answer?
EN_B: It is actually quite simple. The question is: is this property recovery different this time? Over the past five years, we have seen several false alarms. Every time people said recovery was coming, it did not happen. But this report argues that something fundamental has changed.
EN_A: What kind of change are we talking about?
EN_B: It is about breaking a negative feedback loop. In the past, a decline in property prices would drag down industrial profits. Lower profits meant lower income expectations, and that further reduced housing demand. It was a vicious cycle. But the report says this cycle is now unwinding.
EN_A: How? What is driving that change?
EN_B: The AI supply chain. The report found that industrial enterprise profits grew 15 percent year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026. And the biggest contributor was the computer, communication, and electronic equipment sector — basically AI-related industries. That single sector contributed 8.2 percentage points of the total profit growth.
EN_A: So AI is boosting corporate profits, and that is somehow linked to housing demand?
EN_B: Exactly. The report highlights a key data point: the correlation between industrial profits and tier-1 city property prices is 0.63. In the past, property dragged down industry. Now, industry profits are driving property demand. That is the fundamental difference.
EN_A: And where are these profit-generating companies based?
EN_B: The report did a really interesting mapping exercise. They looked at 514 companies with a market cap over 10 billion yuan and a share price gain of more than 50 percent over the past year. Then they checked where their headquarters are. The result: they are heavily concentrated in Beijing, Shanghai, and Shenzhen, followed by Suzhou and Chengdu. And that perfectly matches the stronger secondary transaction volumes in those Cities.
EN_A: So this recovery is not about policy stimulus. It is about real purchasing power coming from industrial upgrading?
EN_B: That is exactly the argument. The report even calls it a wealth effect. When companies are profitable and their stocks are up, employees and executives have more money and are more willing to buy homes. This is a completely different logic from past recoveries that relied on rate cuts or loosening purchase restrictions.
EN_A: What about the supply side? Has anything changed there?
EN_B: Yes, and it is quite important. The report shows that vacancy rates in suburban districts of tier-1 Cities have dropped significantly compared to three years ago. They used a clever proxy: the share of bare-shell listings in total secondary listings, because bare-shell units are definitely vacant.
EN_A: That is a smart way to measure it. So what does a lower vacancy rate mean?
EN_B: It means the shadow inventory built up over the past few years is being digested. In the past, many people bought suburban homes as speculative investments. Those units sat empty and then were dumped into the rental or secondary market, creating a supply shock. Now, new completions are declining and those vacant units are gradually being absorbed. Going forward, the supply side should be much healthier.
EN_A: So both demand and supply are improving. What does the report predict for prices?
EN_B: The forecasts are quite specific. For 2026, tier-1 city property prices are expected to stabilize, compared to an earlier forecast of a 10 percent decline. Tier-2 Cities are still expected to fall, but only by 5 percent, compared to a previous forecast of minus 10 percent. By 2027, tier-1 Cities could even see a 2 percent increase, and tier-2 Cities should stabilize.
EN_A: And what about investment recommendations? Which stocks does the report favor?
EN_B: The core logic is high exposure to tier-1 Cities. Several stocks were upgraded to Buy, including COLI, BEKE, Jinmao, and CMSK. CR Land is also favored, not just for its tier-1 exposure but also for potential value unlock through asset securitization.
EN_A: Are there any questions the report leaves open?
EN_B: Definitely. For example, how sustainable is the AI supply chain profit growth? If the global tech cycle turns, could this whole driver disappear? Also, the report is cautious on tier-2 Cities, but which specific tier-2 Cities could benefit from industrial spillover? Those are questions that the full report explores in more depth.

EN_B: If you want to see the specific target price changes, valuation multiples, and the detailed vacancy rate data by district, I highly recommend reading the full report.
