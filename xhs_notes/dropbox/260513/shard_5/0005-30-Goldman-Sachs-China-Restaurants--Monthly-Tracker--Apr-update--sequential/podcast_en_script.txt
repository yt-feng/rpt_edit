EN_A: What is the core question this report is trying to answer?
EN_B: It is about the pace and structure of recovery. The report tells us that April same-store sales growth was supported by the spring break holiday. But during Labor Day, growth slowed down noticeably.
EN_A: So does that mean the recovery is just a holiday-driven sugar rush?
EN_B: Not exactly. The key word here is divergence. For example, Tai Er saw double-digit SSSG in mainland China in April, with both old and new store formats performing well. But on the other hand, freshly made drink brands generally saw a deceleration.
EN_A: Why did the tea and coffee brands suddenly lose momentum? They were the hot story last year.
EN_B: It is mostly a base effect. During last year's Labor Day, many tea brands used heavy delivery sUBSidies to boost sales. This year, those sUBSidies are normalizing. Add in bad weather, and you get a sharp decline. For Nayuki, SSSG dropped by about 30% during the holiday.
EN_A: 30% drop? Does that mean full-service dining chains like Haidilao are more resilient?
EN_B: Not exactly. Haidilao's table turn growth in April was mid-single-digit, but that was from a very low base. During Labor Day, its table turns were roughly flat year-on-year. So it is stable, but not a breakout.
EN_A: So what are the main dimensions of divergence the report highlights?
EN_B: First, brand divergence. Tai Er outperformed most peers. Second, time period divergence. April was much better than Labor Day. Third, category divergence. Full-service and fast food held up better than tea. Fourth, cost side. Delivery sUBSidies are normalizing, but brands remain disciplined on pricing. No price war.
EN_A: So the recovery is real, but very uneven. Are there any big questions the report leaves open?
EN_B: Yes, a couple of important ones. First, how long will the high base effect last, especially for tea brands? The report suggests their SSSG could stay negative in Q2 and Q3. Second, the effectiveness of new store formats. Tai Er's new model is working, but other brands are still experimenting. That needs more time to validate.
EN_A: So what should investors be watching right now?
EN_B: Two things. First, the resilience of everyday demand between Labor Day and summer. Second, whether new product cycles can kick in. There is one big question the report does not fully answer yet: after sUBSidies and promotions fade, how strong is the consumer's true willingness to spend?
