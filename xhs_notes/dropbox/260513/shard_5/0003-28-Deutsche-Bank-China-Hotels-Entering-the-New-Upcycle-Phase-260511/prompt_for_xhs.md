# China hotel sector: structural upcycle commences as oversupply fades

The primary driver of our positive outlook on the China hotel sector is the anticipated shift toward more balanced supply-demand dynamics in 2026, which we believe marks a structural turning point for the industry's upcycle. As industry supply growth begins to decelerate and demand rebounds, the sector is well-positioned for a sustainable recovery starting in 2026.

# Slower industry supply since 4Q25

Following a period of significant oversupply between 2023 and 2025, our channel checks indicate that hotel supply growth began to decelerate as of late 2025. The earlier supply surge has absorbed the property pipeline accumulated during the Covid-19 pandemic. Meanwhile, the ongoing downturn in the commercial real estate sector has also constrained the availability of new assets suitable for hotel development.

For properties launched during the recent supply surge, weak RevPAR and unhealthy price competition among independent hotels has severely pressured individual operator profitability. This diminished return on investment, exacerbated by intense price competition for traffic, has significantly reduced the incentive for franchisees to open new hotels. Thus, the China hotel industry is starting to move toward a more favorable competitive landscape with supply growth gradually slowing in 2026, resulting in a more balanced supply demand dynamic.

Our expectation for disciplined supply growth in 2026 is reinforced by STR industry data. Following a multi-year downcycle and oversupply, the industry enters 2026 with disciplined supply growth of +2.3% in 1Q26, trailing a robust demand growth rate of +3.6% according to STR. This evolving supply-demand imbalance is expected to bolster industry ADR by mitigating the oversupply pressures that previously triggered aggressive price wars.

# Leading player expansion guidance

We have seen a decelerating pace of new openings from major players such as H World, Jinjiang, and Atour since 4Q25. Beyond the moderated growth of these market leaders, our channel checks indicate an even more pronounced slowdown in the independent hotel segment. This broader deceleration across the independent landscape implies a systemic tightening of industry supply. As independent players either exit the market or stall their expansion, irrational price volatility is expected to diminish.

# 2026 RevPAR growth forecast

The industry's full-year RevPAR is projected to achieve 2% YoY growth in 2026 (YTD: +4.3%), a significant improvement from the 2024-2025 declines. Thus, we foresee 2026 marking a turnaround year for the China hotel industry, signaling the beginning of a new upcycle. Our forecast is underpinned by:

ADR Trend: Unlike previous cycles where occupancy led the recovery, the current RevPAR upturn is primarily driven by ADR expansion as oversupply pressures gradually recede. Since 4Q25, the industry has reached a critical consensus: aggressive price discounting during off-peak seasons fails to meaningfully stimulate demand, serving only to erode the profitability of all participants. This shift in strategy has led industry leaders to prioritize price, ensuring more sustainable operating margins and brand image. Furthermore, increasing chain penetration and continuous product upgrades—such as Ji 5.0 and Hanting 4.0—have significantly enhanced the pricing power of market leaders. We observed stabilizing ADR trends among top-tier chains like H World and Atour starting in 4Q25. This is corroborated by STR industry data, which indicates positive ADR growth since 4Q25, distinguishing the hotel sector from other consumer categories that remain mired in margin-dilutive price competition.   
Occupancy Ratio Trend: While Occupancy (OCC) has trailed ADR performance, we expect continued improvements in both leisure and business traffic to drive a recovery throughout 2026. Holiday periods remain a standout strength, bolstered by favorable government holiday policies and a structural shift in consumer spending toward experiences. However, we believe the critical catalyst for a sustained RevPAR uptrend lies in the stabilization of business demand. A consistent recovery in corporate travel would provide the necessary foundation to lift occupancy rates during non-peak periods, significantly improving the industry's overall operating leverage.
