# Hotel industry enters upcycle as oversupply begins to fade

The core of our positive thesis on the China hotel sector is the shift toward balanced supply-demand dynamics in 2026. We foresee a structural transition from the oversupply environment that previously triggered irrational price competition to a more favorable operating environment, characterized by slower supply growth (+2.3% in 1Q26) and a solid demand rebound (+3.6% in 1Q26). This deceleration in supply is reinforced by a visible slowdown in new openings from major players in 4Q25. We expect this shifting landscape to bolster industry ADR by mitigating the oversupply pressures that fueled aggressive price wars throughout 2024 and 1H25.

Demand across all sub-sectors also remains constructive: business travel has stabilized following the conclusion of budget downgrades in 2025; leisure travel continues to demonstrate resilience as consumers pivot spending from goods to experiences, further supported by favorable holiday policies in 2026. Additionally, inbound tourism is expected to continue growing (2025: 82 million arrivals), driven by expanded visa-free policies, with arrivals from Hong Kong already exceeding 2019 levels and expected to continue their upward trend.

Following a multi-year downcycle since Covid-19, we believe industry RevPAR bottomed in 4Q25 and has now entered a structural upcycle. While we project full-year industry RevPAR growth of 2% for 2026 (+4.3% YTD trend), we expect market leaders to outpace the broader sector driven by ongoing consolidation and superior operational execution. Therefore, we believe the combination of improving RevPAR and accelerating industry consolidation provides a clear path for industry leaders to deliver stable, high-quality net profit growth.

# Near-term RevPar volatility creates attractive opportunity

We believe that recent RevPAR fluctuations and the impact on consumer sentiment due to geopolitical tensions and elevated fuel costs creates an attractive entry point for investors. While some investors may highlight near-term traffic weakness, specifically the impact of higher airfares on Labor Day holiday data, we view these recent disruptions as non-threatening to our full-year thesis. Our positive view is supported by the unchanged structural shift of leisure travelers towards experiences over goods, coupled with improving business demand during non-holiday periods, and a favorable low base in 2Q-3Q25.

Our medium-term optimism remains anchored by our view of a clear, cyclical bottom and accelerating consolidation led by industry leaders. These structural factors not only enhance the resilience of major hotel groups relative to other consumer categories but also provide high visibility, in our view, to deliver sustainable, double-digit EPS growth over the next three years.

# China hotel sector: structural upcycle commences as oversupply fades

The primary driver of our positive outlook on the China hotel sector is the anticipated shift toward more balanced supply-demand dynamics in 2026, which we believe marks a structural turning point for the industry's upcycle. As industry supply growth begins to decelerate and demand rebounds, the sector is well-positioned for a sustainable recovery starting in 2026.

# Slower industry supply since 4Q25

Following a period of significant oversupply between 2023 and 2025, our channel checks indicate that hotel supply growth began to decelerate as of late 2025. The earlier supply surge has absorbed the property pipeline accumulated during the Covid-19 pandemic. Meanwhile, the ongoing downturn in the commercial real estate sector has also constrained the availability of new assets suitable for hotel development.

For properties launched during the recent supply surge, weak RevPAR and unhealthy price competition among independent hotels has severely pressured individual operator profitability. This diminished return on investment, exacerbated by intense price competition for traffic, has significantly reduced the incentive for franchisees to open new hotels. Thus, the China hotel industry is starting to move toward a more favorable competitive landscape with supply growth gradually slowing in 2026, resulting in a more balanced supply demand dynamic.

Our expectation for disciplined supply growth in 2026 is reinforced by STR industry data. Following a multi-year downcycle and oversupply, the industry enters 2026 with disciplined supply growth of +2.3% in 1Q26, trailing a robust demand growth rate of +3.6% according to STR. This evolving supply-demand imbalance is expected to bolster industry ADR by mitigating the oversupply pressures that previously triggered aggressive price wars.

# Leading player expansion guidance

We have seen a decelerating pace of new openings from major players such as H World, Jinjiang, and Atour since 4Q25. Beyond the moderated growth of these market leaders, our channel checks indicate an even more pronounced slowdown in the independent hotel segment. This broader deceleration across the independent landscape implies a systemic tightening of industry supply. As independent players either exit the market or stall their expansion, irrational price volatility is expected to diminish.

# 2026 RevPAR growth forecast

The industry's full-year RevPAR is projected to achieve 2% YoY growth in 2026 (YTD: +4.3%), a significant improvement from the 2024-2025 declines. Thus, we foresee 2026 marking a turnaround year for the China hotel industry, signaling the beginning of a new upcycle. Our forecast is underpinned by:

ADR Trend: Unlike previous cycles where occupancy led the recovery, the current RevPAR upturn is primarily driven by ADR expansion as oversupply pressures gradually recede. Since 4Q25, the industry has reached a critical consensus: aggressive price discounting during off-peak seasons fails to meaningfully stimulate demand, serving only to erode the profitability of all participants. This shift in strategy has led industry leaders to prioritize price, ensuring more sustainable operating margins and brand image. Furthermore, increasing chain penetration and continuous product upgrades—such as Ji 5.0 and Hanting 4.0—have significantly enhanced the pricing power of market leaders. We observed stabilizing ADR trends among top-tier chains like H World and Atour starting in 4Q25. This is corroborated by STR industry data, which indicates positive ADR growth since 4Q25, distinguishing the hotel sector from other consumer categories that remain mired in margin-dilutive price competition.   
Occupancy Ratio Trend: While Occupancy (OCC) has trailed ADR performance, we expect continued improvements in both leisure and business traffic to drive a recovery throughout 2026. Holiday periods remain a standout strength, bolstered by favorable government holiday policies and a structural shift in consumer spending toward experiences. However, we believe the critical catalyst for a sustained RevPAR uptrend lies in the stabilization of business demand. A consistent recovery in corporate travel would provide the necessary foundation to lift occupancy rates during non-peak periods, significantly improving the industry's overall operating leverage.

# Potential Impact of OTA anti-monopoly measures

Based on our discussions with leading hotel groups, we believe the operational impact of recent anti-monopoly investigations into Trip.com will be limited. While regulators may scrutinize current commission rates (typically 25–30%), Trip.com is likely to mitigate any direct fee caps by leveraging algorithmic optimizations and expanding its suite of value-added services. While it is possible that overall commission rates may decline slightly, we do not anticipate a significant structural change. Furthermore, major hotel groups like H World have proactively insulated themselves from platform volatility by strictly managing their OTA reliance within a 20–25% range.

Regarding the rise of AI-generated itinerary recommendations, hotel operators anticipate a minimal long-term threat to their business models. Although consumers are increasingly using AI tools for hotel discovery, industry leaders believe that high brand equity and entrenched membership reward programs will continue to drive direct bookings. By focusing on superior customer experiences and loyalty ecosystems, major hotel groups remain well-positioned to maintain customer retention, regardless of the underlying search technology.

# H World: a rapidly growing multi-brand hotel group with deep roots in China

Founded in 2005, H World is a leading and rapidly growing multi-brand hotel group, operating 12,858 hotels as of the end of 2025 in 21 countries under Huazhu and Deutsche Hospitality. This portfolio includes 573 leased and operated (L&O) hotels and 12,285 manachised and franchised (M&F) properties, including multiple-brands in different price segments.

H World Group stands out as one of China's few hotel chain operators to have successfully developed a multi-brand portfolio covering the full spectrum of market segments. H World's journey began with its entry into the economy segment via the Hanting brand, followed by a strategic expansion into multi-brand offerings and its current penetration of the mid-to-high-end market. With 20 years of operations, H World's consistent growth underscores its deep understanding of Chinese hospitality and its long-term strategic vision as a global operator. Its core brands, Hanting and Ji Hotel, have surpassed 4,556 and 3,565 hotels respectively, establishing themselves as benchmark brands for their target price segments.

To enhance its global footprint, H World officially completed its 100% equity acquisition of Deutsche Hospitality (DH) on January 2, 2020. This strategic move transformed H World into a global player by adding five major European brands—including the luxury Steigenberger Hotels & Resorts and the urban IntercityHotel—to its portfolio.

# Asset-light transformation towards the M&F model

H World Group has successfully driven margin expansion through its asset-light transformation, shifting towards a M&F model. As of 2025, revenue from the asset light model reached 46% of total revenue, compared to 32% in 2022, as Huazhu continues to expand its network through the M&F model in China and close the underperforming L&O hotels which further improves its operational efficiency.

By strategically positioning itself across both economy and mid to high end segments, Huazhu also maintains a steady pace of new hotel openings from its M&F model. # CRS and membership to drive customer loyalty

A key component of H World's marketing efforts is H Rewards, the company's loyalty program. As of 2025, H Rewards boasts over 311 million members, with 73% of room nights booked under Huazhu being sold to H Rewards members. Members enjoy benefits such as discounts on room rates, complimentary breakfast (for Gold and Platinum members), and late check-out privileges. H Rewards members can accumulate points to offset future room charges, which encourages the members to book via its membership program instead of via OTAs.

H World also benefits from very solid customer traffic, thanks to its strong brand recognition and established customer loyalty. In 2025, 77% of room nights sold for Huazhu were booked through its own direct sales channels, with only 23% coming from intermediaries. The majority of direct sales are made via its CRS system, while a smaller proportion is made by walk-in and offline channels. According to management, H World aims to maintain the intermediary contribution to total sales below the 25% level, and continue to strengthen its membership networks through more efficient direct sales.

# Financial forecast and key assumptions

With over 20 years of expertise in China and an expanding international footprint, H World is a strong long-term compounder well-positioned to lead the structural consolidation of the Chinese hospitality market. We view the company as an ideal selection for investors seeking to capitalize on the cyclical bottoming of the industry while maintaining exposure to a best-in-class compounder within the China consumer space.

We forecast H World's consolidated revenue to grow by 7.4%, 7.7%, and 6.7% from 2026-2028E, respectively. This growth is driven by its China operations (Huazhu), projected to grow by 9.3%, 9.3%, and 7.9% during the same period, while Deutsche Hospitality (DH) is expected to contribute -0.9%, 0%, and 1% growth. Our forecast for China RevPAR growth is 2.8%, 3.0%, and 3.1% for 2026, 2027, and 2028, respectively, reflecting our view that the overall industry is bottoming out in 4Q25, thus providing a strong foundation for an industry upcycle.

For adjusted net profit, we factor in significant growth of 23.1%, 16.7%, and 11.8% from 2026-2028E. This accelerated profitability is driven by continuous margin improvement resulting from H World's strategic mix shift towards an asset-light model and robust cost control initiatives.

From a three-year perspective, we believe H World is capable of delivering 7% revenue and 17% adjusted net profit CAGRs. This positions it as one of the few players in China able to compound growth despite fierce industry competition and macro uncertainty.

# Key risks

# Our thesis is subject to several key risks, including:

1. Macroeconomic Headwinds and Demand Weakness in China

Slower-than-expected business demand recovery due to a weak macroeconomic environment, coupled with potential weakness or downgrade in leisure travel demand, could put pressure on our industry assumptions and negatively impact H World's China RevPAR growth.

2. Intensifying Competitive Landscape

China's hotel market is highly competitive, with H World competing against both domestic and international players who also try capture the opportunities in the mid-to-high-end and lower-tier city segments. Should competitors offer comparable high-quality products at lower price points, H World's customer acquisition could be adversely affected. Furthermore, any failure in H World's execution could limit its competitive edge.

3. Franchisee Management and Growth Execution

H World's growth is significantly dependent on robust franchisee relationships, which are critical for ensuring product quality, service consistency, and continuous new store expansion. Should H World fail to effectively manage its franchisees, it could lead to a deteriorating brand image and reduced customer loyalty. Similarly, an inability to attract sufficient new franchisees could result in lower-than-expected store openings, thereby impacting H World's growth estimates.

4. Geopolitical and Macroeconomic Factors in Europe

Geopolitical tensions could adversely affect business-related travel demand in Europe. Additionally, high commodity prices may limit overall travel interest, both of which would negatively impact H World's European operations via Deutsche Hospitality (DH).

# Atour: A lifestyle brand driving mid-to-high-end expansion with dual growth engines

Atour, a Chinese mid-to-high-end hospitality group, has positioned itself as a lifestyle brand rooted in hospitality but extending well beyond the traditional hotel business. Since its inception in 2013, Atour has astutely identified evolving market demands: capturing business travelers adjusting to budget cuts (those migrating from five-star hotels) and retail consumers seeking experience-driven upgrades. After a decade of growth, Atour has emerged as China's leading listed hotel group by scale within the mid-to-high-end segment.

Atour is currently in a favorable position for network expansion, given its relatively smaller footprint—operating 2,015 hotels in 2025 compared to the 10,000+ locations managed by top-tier chains. The closure of numerous underperforming independent hotels over the past two years has released a significant volume of high-quality properties in prime business districts and transportation hUBS. Combined with superior operating efficiency and a robust Central Reservation System (CRS) for user acquisition, industry leaders like Atour are gaining significant traction over independent competitors.

# Atour's "24-Hour Lifestyle" Approach: Beyond Just Accommodation

Unlike traditional hotels that primarily sell 'a room for the night,' Atour offers a '24-hour lifestyle,' effectively transitioning from a 'space provider' to an 'experience operator.' Atour has shifted its focus from 'selling rooms' to 'selling sleep,' establishing the 'deep sleep' scenario as a competitive brand moat that creates a superior customer experience. This emphasis on experience significantly differentiates Atour from its peers and serves as a crucial edge for cultivating member loyalty.

# Atour's multi-tier strategy: capturing the full mid-to-high-end spectrum

While many larger hotel groups operate multi-brand portfolios, Atour's primary strategy centers on its singular 'Atour' brand, encompassing both its hotel and retail businesses. Despite this core focus, the company has strategically expanded its sub-brands since 2022, to include different price segments under different Atour's sub-brands to capture the full mid to high end spectrum in China.

One of Atour's strategic sub-brands is Atour Light, which is positioned to compete directly in the mid-end hotel segment at a more accessible price point than Huazhu's signature Ji Hotel. By offering a comparable quality of service and design at a more competitive price, Atour Light serves as an important tool for expanding the company's customer base.

Conversely, Atour S and Atour X are positioned at a premium relative to the flagship brand, featuring upgraded facilities, elevated interior design, and prime locations. This comprehensive segmentation allows Atour to capture a broader range of consumer demographics and accelerate network expansion while effectively mitigating the risk of internal brand cannibalization.

# Atour's franchise-centric asset light model

Atour utilizes a franchise-centric, asset-light model, where franchisees serve as the primary customers and partners for network expansion. This model generates highly predictable income streams; as of 2025, each newly signed and operational hotel contributes an estimated RMB 3 million annually to Atour's top line. We project this per-hotel revenue to improve steadily through 2028E, driven by a structural upturn in RevPAR and enhanced ancillary service monetization.

Under Atour's business model, approximately 50% of franchise revenue is derived from recurring management and franchise fees, representing a robust gross take rate of 10.5% on hotel gross revenue. A significant portion of the remainder (48–49%) is generated through the sale of hotel supplies and integrated products, while transaction fees account for less than 1%. Looking ahead, we model a consistent expansion of the take rate through 2028E, alongside enhanced monetization of its supply chain services to franchisees.

# Atour's CRS: enhancing membership engagement

Similar to H World, Atour leverages a robust membership ecosystem to cultivate brand loyalty and anchor its unique Atour Planet lifestyle platform. Mmebers' benefits include exclusive discounts, extended check-out privileges (up to 16:00), and complimentary breakfast for elite tiers. A standout feature is the signature "Late Night Hearth" (complimentary late-night porridge), a service touchpoint that significantly enhances the guest experience and differentiates the brand in the mid-to-high-end segment.

From 2023 to 2025, Atour's direct sales channels accounted for approximately $63\%$ of total room nights. This high direct-booking ratio drives sUBStantial operational efficiency by minimizing third-party acquisition costs. Notably, while Trip.com remains a major shareholder and strategic partner, it contributes less than $10\%$ of Atour's GMV. This underscores Atour's limited dependence on external OTAs and highlights the strength of its proprietary distribution network.

# Offline channel is built on its extensive hotel networks

Beyond online distribution, Atour has developed a unique, scenario-based retail model seamlessly integrated into its extensive hotel networks. Atour's guest rooms serve as fully immersive product experience spaces, allowing guests to explore and try retail products during their stay, which in turn fosters brand loyalty. Atour equips specific hotel room types with its Atour Planet deep sleep products, enabling guests to opt for a sleep trial as part of its APLUS service. This allows guests to personally experience Atour's retail offerings during their hotel stays. Despite the strong online presence, offline channels contributed significantly to Atour's retail business GMV, accounting for approximately 19.7%, 9.3%, and 6.9% in 2023, 2024, and 2025, respectively.

# Atour's retail strategy starts from "deep sleep pillow"

Based on our estimates, Atour holds a 23% market share in the pillows market on e-commerce platforms. The ratio rose from 6% in 2023, to 21% in 2025, and 23% in 1Q26. Despite Atour's growth decelerating given its scale, its market share continues to rise against the fierce competition in the category. We believe that Atour's branding and unique premium and high-quality positioning help it maintain competitiveness despite increased market entrants.

# Competition in the memory foam pillow market

Following the strong growth of Atour's deep sleep memory foam pillows between 2023 and 2025, traditional home textile giants have aggressively entered the market. These competitors are now introducing similar memory foam pillows, often at lower price points, to seize market share from Atour Planet.

Traditional home textile players possess advantages in their supply chains and lower cost structures due to their ownership of factories, rather than relying solely on outsourcing. Their full-chain control, from fabric research and development to finished product delivery, results in comprehensive production costs that are 15-20% lower than Atour's OEM model, making their products more cost-effective. Key players such as Mercury, Mlily, Luolai Home, and Fuana, all boast mature supply chains and production capaCities. Historically, these brands dominated offline channels. Their major products, equivalent to Atour's pillows, are generally priced at a 10-20% discount to attract new users.

Furthermore, starting in 2025, these leading home textile brands have sUBStantially increased their investment in online marketing and IP collaborations to attract younger consumer demographics. While their historical strength lay in extensive offline distribution networks, catering to traditional shopping and trial preferences, these brands, with over 1000-2000 stores across China, are now actively strengthening their online presence to capture opportunities presented by younger consumers who prefer online shopping over physical stores.

# Comforters as another growth driver for Atour's new retail business

Atour's retail business growth also hinges on its ability to replicate the success of its pillow offerings across other product categories. Beyond pillows, Atour's expansion is driven by a gradual move towards a comprehensive range of sleep products, including comforters, mattresses, and sleepwear. This strategy aims to facilitate the upgrade and conversion of users from "individual product purchases" to "full-room sleep solutions." Atour's temperature-controlled comforters, launched in 2023, have become the second-largest SKU within its deep sleep product categories. These comforters essentially combine existing features, resolving the traditional dilemma of "temperature control and convenient cleaning not being achievable simultaneously."

In 2025, Atour's total retail business achieved over RMB 3 billion in sales. We assume that from 2025-2028E, comforters can enjoy a 30% CAGR, followed by mattresses and other products at 27% and 25%, respectively. This growth trajectory is projected to drive Atour's total retail business to deliver a CAGR of 17%.

# Financial forecast and key assumptions

We hold a positive industry view on the China hotel sector, as we think that among all consumer spending categories, the travel market has proven to be resilient against the macro environment. Leisure demand has benefited from supportive policies (such as extended holiday periods) and a clear shift toward prioritizing services and experiences over physical goods since 2025. While business travel was previously dampened by slowing economic activity and geopolitical conflicts — a major concern given that over half of Atour's demand is business-related—the sector has stabilized since early 2026. This lack of further downward movement sets a solid foundation for a recovery trend throughout the year.

We forecast total revenue to grow by 26%, 16%, and 10% from 2026 to 2028, respectively. These projections are primarily driven by the continuous addition of new hotels under Atour's asset-light model and an anticipated expansion in RevPAR as the industry reaches its cyclical bottom. Conversely, we have factored in a gradual decline in the "retail & others" segment given its current scale. Our conservative forecast also provides potential upside, if Atour can maintain its strong retail momentum. For adjusted net profit, we project year-over-year growth of 25%, 16%, and 11% over the same period.

# Key risks

# Our thesis is subject to several key risks, including:

# 1. High Concentration in Upper-Midscale Segment

Unlike peers with diversified portfolios spanning economy hotels and mid to high end brands, Atour's growth is heavily tied to a single, premium-priced upper-midscale segment. A slowdown in the Chinese economy or a "trade-down" by consumers to more budget-friendly accommodations would leave Atour without a defensive brand to capture this shifting demand, making it highly sensitive to changes in middle-class discretionary spending.

# 2. Retail/E-commerce Execution and Competitive Pressures

A significant component of Atour's thesis is its robust retail business (including pillows, comforters, and accessories). However, this segment faces fierce competition from pure-play e-commerce brands and traditional home-goods retailers. Failure to effectively capture and capitalize on new growth opportunities within this evolving retail segment could lead to a decline in revenue.

# 3. Intensifying Competitive Landscape in Mid-to-High-End Hotels

China's hotel market remains highly competitive, with Atour needing to compete against numerous domestic and international players in the same price segment. Should competitors introduce comparable high-quality products at lower price points, Atour's market position and pricing power could come under significant pressure.

# 4. Franchisee Management and Network Expansion Risks

Atour's asset-light Management & Franchise (M&F) growth model is heavily reliant on robust franchisee relationships. These partnerships are critical for ensuring product quality, service consistency, and continuous new store expansion. Atour directly competes with established giants like H World (e.g., for its JI Hotel brand) for the same pool of franchisees. If competitors offer more attractive terms such as superior financing, lower fees, or higher guaranteed returns, Atour's pipeline for new hotel openings could significantly diminish, impacting its overall growth trajectory.
