EN_A: The consumer sector in China has been pretty soft lately, but hotels seem to be a bright spot. What is the main call from this report?
EN_B: The report says the China hotel industry is entering a new upcycle in 2026. The key driver is a shift in supply-demand dynamics.
EN_A: Supply-demand shift? I thought the market was oversupplied for years. What changed?
EN_B: It was oversupplied from 2023 to 2025. But starting in late 2025, supply growth started to slow down. In the first quarter of 2026, supply grew only 2.3 percent, while demand grew 3.6 percent.
EN_A: So demand is finally outpacing supply. Why did supply slow down so suddenly?
EN_B: Two main reasons. First, most of the hotel projects delayed during COVID have already opened. Second, the commercial real estate downturn made it harder to find good locations for new hotels. But the biggest signal is that franchisees are losing interest in opening new hotels.
EN_A: Franchisees are backing off? That sounds like a problem for the industry.
EN_B: Actually, it is a healthy correction. Many hotels opened during the boom were independent or small brands. They had weak RevPAR and were stuck in price wars. Returns got so bad that nobody wants to expand anymore.
EN_A: So supply is shrinking naturally. What about demand? What does the report say?
EN_B: Demand is recovering across the board. Business travel has stabilized after budget cuts ended in 2025. Leisure travel remains strong because consumers are spending more on experiences. And there is one segment that people often overlook.
EN_A: Which one?
EN_B: Inbound tourism. The report says foreign arrivals hit 82 million in 2025 and are still growing in 2026. Visitors from Hong Kong and Macau have already exceeded 2019 levels. Visa-free policies are making a real difference.
EN_A: So with better supply-demand balance, how does RevPAR look?
EN_B: The report forecasts full-year RevPAR growth of around 2 percent for 2026. The first quarter already showed 4.3 percent growth. But the interesting thing is what is driving this recovery.
EN_A: What do you mean?
EN_B: In the past, recovery was driven by occupancy. This time, it is average daily rate, or ADR, that is leading. Because oversupply pressure is fading, hotels are no longer slashing prices. The industry has realized that discounting during off-peak seasons does not bring in real demand, it just destroys margins.
EN_A: So which companies benefit the most in this upcycle?
EN_B: The report highlights two names. One is H World, which is shifting to an asset-light model and upgrading its brands. The other is Atour, which has a unique dual-engine model.

EN_B: It combines hotels with retail. Atour's retail business saw sales surge 45 percent year-on-year in the first quarter, way above the company's full-year guidance of 25 to 30 percent. That is impressive, but the market has not fully priced it in yet.
EN_A: What is the market worried about?
EN_B: Mostly the sustainability of the retail business and some near-term RevPAR volatility. But the report argues that these short-term fluctuations actually create a good entry point.
EN_A: And H World? How does its valuation look?
