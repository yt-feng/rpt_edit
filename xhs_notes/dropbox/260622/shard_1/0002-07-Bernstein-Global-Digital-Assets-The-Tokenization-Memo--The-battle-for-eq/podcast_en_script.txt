EN_A: What is the core question the report is trying to answer?
EN_B: It wants to understand how different players are trying to capture the market for tokenized equities, even while the regulatory framework is still being built.
EN_A: How big is the market right now?
EN_B: Tokenized real world assets have crossed 51 billion dollars in market cap, up 40 percent year to date. That is strong growth compared to the broader crypto market, which is down about 20 percent.
EN_A: What does that tell us?
EN_B: It tells us that traditional assets moving on chain is not just hype. There is real demand. Equity tokenization alone grew from 0.7 billion to 1.6 billion this year.
EN_A: So who is actually building this?
EN_B: The report identifies two main business models. One is neutral infrastructure, helping traditional financial firms issue tokens. The other is a trading model, using tokenization to offer multiple asset classes on one platform.
EN_A: Can you give me a concrete example?
EN_B: Robinhood offers tokenized US stocks to European investors. A third party buys the actual shares, holds them in a custodial account, and issues a token against them. But the token holder does not get voting rights or dividends.
EN_A: So that sounds like tokenization in name only?
EN_B: Exactly. That is the limitation of the first model. The second model goes further. Companies like Figure and Bullish use blockchain as the actual settlement layer. Investors get full ownership and protection rights.
EN_A: And what is Coinbase doing?
EN_B: Coinbase is pursuing a third path. It wants to become an everything exchange. It has launched tokenized equities, equity perpetuals, and even pre-IPO perpetuals for non US investors.
EN_A: So they are using crypto tools to wrap traditional assets?
EN_B: Right. They combine the liquidity of traditional assets with the 24/7 nature of crypto trading. And Coinbase is now the only CFTC regulated futures commission merchant offering US investors access to global crypto derivatives.
EN_A: Which model is going to win?
EN_B: The report does not pick a winner yet. It says regulation is the key variable. The SEC has recently taken some encouraging steps, like proposing to remove certain restrictions and approving pilot programs for tokenized securities on major exchanges.
EN_A: So what is the biggest open question?
EN_B: The report mentions something called an innovation exemption. If the US allows tokenized stocks to trade onshore, the whole landscape could shift. But the report does not fully answer what that would look like.
EN_A: Are there other things the report leaves hanging?
EN_B: Yes. For example, most tokenized equities are currently for non US investors. If the US opens up, how will compliance costs and competition change? That is still being figured out.
