# EV Sales Acceleration Poses Downside Risk to Global Oil Demand

To assess the potential hit from the Hormuz-related oil supply shock to long-term global oil demand, we document the acceleration in Electric Vehicle (EV) $^{1}$ passenger car sales. We estimate a potential hit to global oil demand in December 2027 of 0.13-0.32mb/d (or 0.1-0.3% of global demand), depending on the persistence of the realized EV car sales acceleration (Exhibit 5).

\- Our new nowcast $^{2}$ shows a broad-based acceleration in EV car sales as a percent of total car sales (i.e., seasonally adjusted EV penetration) since February:

☐ Global EV car sales penetration has increased by 3.4pp and reached its all-time high last month of 26.1%, excluding September 2025 (when US EV sales surged in advance of a tax credit expiry) (Exhibit 1).

☐ China has experienced the largest gains, with the EV penetration rate up 11.4pp (Exhibit 2).

This acceleration in global EV car sales suggests our downside oil price scenario is plausible (although potentially more persistent constraints on Hormuz flows pose significant upside price risks, too). In this downside scenario, global oil demand experiences a 1.5mb/d Hormuz-related persistent loss (vs 0.5mb/d in our base case), and Brent declines to the mid-\$50s/bbl in late 2027 (with supply beats also weighing on prices). ☐ Beyond Passenger Cars: We omit from this analysis all non-passenger car EVs, and the around 55% of oil demand not related to road fuels (e.g., petrochemicals, for which coal might be an alternative).
