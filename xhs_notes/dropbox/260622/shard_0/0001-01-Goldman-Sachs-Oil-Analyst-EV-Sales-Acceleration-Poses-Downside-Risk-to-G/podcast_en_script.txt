EN_A: So I just saw this report about EV sales accelerating and how it could hit global oil demand. Is that really a big deal?
EN_B: It actually is. The report found that since the start of the Hormuz crisis, global EV penetration has jumped by 3.4 percentage points, hitting a record high of 26.1 percent last month.
EN_A: Wait, the Hormuz crisis and EV sales? How are they connected?
EN_B: That is exactly the question the report tries to answer. Higher oil prices are pushing consumers toward electric vehicles, and this shift might not be temporary.
EN_A: So higher fuel prices are driving EV adoption. How big is the impact?
EN_B: The report runs two scenarios. If EV penetration just stays at May levels, global oil demand could drop by 130,000 barrels per day by December 2027.
EN_A: 130,000 barrels does not sound that huge.
EN_B: Right, but in the persistent acceleration scenario, the hit could be 320,000 barrels per day. That is about 0.3 percent of global demand.
EN_A: And where is this happening most?
EN_B: China is the biggest driver. The report shows EV penetration there rose by 11.4 percentage points, more than three times the global average.
EN_A: So what does that mean? Is China really speeding away from oil?
EN_B: Yes, but the report also says this is just the tip of the iceberg. They only looked at new passenger car sales, not the existing fleet or other types of EVs.
EN_A: Other types like what?
EN_B: Think two-wheelers and three-wheelers. In India, they make up 92 percent of EV sales. In Vietnam, 80 percent. Each one can displace one-third to one-half of the fuel a passenger car EV would.
EN_A: So the real impact could be much bigger than the report estimates?
EN_B: Exactly. The report itself says they have not fully explored that dimension yet. Plus, high fuel prices are also making existing car owners drive less, which further cuts oil demand.
EN_A: So the downside risk to oil is bigger than what first meets the eye?
EN_B: The report argues that the EV acceleration makes the bearish oil price scenario more plausible. But remember, supply disruptions from Hormuz could also push prices up, so the risk is two-sided.
EN_A: Sounds like there is a lot more in the full report. If people want to dig deeper, they should check it out?
EN_B: Absolutely. The full report has more on regional differences and policy changes. We only covered the core findings here.
