# The Renminbi Fix Model Is Sending a Clear Signal: Expect Controlled Depreciation, Not a Free Fall

The most important number in Asian currency markets this week is not a trade-weighted index or a bilateral spot rate. It is 6.7787 -- the model-projected USD/CNY fixing level produced by a leading Asia FX strategy team. This projection sits 343 pips below the previous model output and, more tellingly, 164 pips above the most recent official spot close. The gap between the model's unadjusted projection and the actual fixing, after accounting for the counter-cyclical factor, tells a story that every Asia-focused investor needs to understand. The message is not one of crisis or capitulation. It is one of deliberate, managed adjustment.

The context matters enormously. The previous model projection stood at 6.8130. The new projection of 6.7787 represents a significant downward revision in the model's fair value estimate for the renminbi. But the counter-cyclical factor adjustment pushes the effective projection to 6.8008, which is only 122 pips lower than the previous day's fix. This is the signature of a central bank that is allowing the currency to weaken gradually while simultaneously signaling that it will not tolerate disorderly moves. The authorities are walking a narrow path between supporting export competitiveness and maintaining financial stability. The model's output is the clearest quantitative expression of that balancing act currently available to the market.

Why now? The second half of 2026 is packed with political and policy events that will shape currency expectations. The Politburo's economic work meeting in late July, the National Day Golden Week in October, the APEC summit in Shenzhen in November, the Central Economic Work Conference in mid-December, and the anticipated state visit by President Xi to the United States toward the end of the year. Each of these events creates a window for policy signaling or, conversely, a period during which the authorities will want to avoid excessive volatility. The model's current projection suggests that the depreciation bias is likely to persist through this calendar, but within a framework of tight control.

## The Model's Internal Logic Reveals That Overnight Market Forces Are Driving the Shift, Not a Change in Policy Preference

The most analytically useful detail in the report is the breakdown of the four largest weighted overnight contributions to the projected change. These contributions are the raw inputs that drive the model's output before any counter-cyclical factor is applied. Understanding which variables are moving the projection tells us whether the shift is being driven by external market conditions, domestic policy signals, or a combination of both. The report does not explicitly name the four contributions, but the analytical framework is clear: the model is reacting to observable market data, not to a discretionary policy decision.

This distinction is critical. If the model were simply reflecting a policy preference for a weaker currency, the adjustment would be visible in the counter-cyclical factor itself. Instead, the counter-cyclical factor is being used to *moderate* the market-driven depreciation. The authorities are not pushing the currency lower; they are allowing market forces to push it lower, but with a hand on the tiller. The 122-pip gap between the adjusted projection and the previous fix is the size of that moderating influence. It is neither trivial nor overwhelming. It is calibrated.

For an investor, this means that the primary driver of renminbi depreciation over the coming months is likely to be the same set of forces that are currently moving the model: interest rate differentials, trade flows, and relative growth expectations. The policy response will be reactive, not proactive. The authorities will adjust the counter-cyclical factor to slow the pace of depreciation, but they are unlikely to reverse the direction unless market moves become disorderly. The model's error history, which the report includes in a separate chart, provides a further clue: the model has been systematically under-predicting the degree of depreciation in recent observations. That suggests the market has been running ahead of the model's fair value estimate, and the model is now catching up.

## The Counter-Cyclical Factor Is the Most Important Tool for Reading Beijing's Tolerance for Weakness

The counter-cyclical factor is not a fixed parameter. It is a discretionary adjustment that the People's Bank of China applies to the daily fixing calculation to lean against one-sided market expectations. When the factor is set to push the fixing higher than the model's unadjusted projection, it signals that the authorities want to slow depreciation. When it is set to push the fixing lower, it signals a desire to allow or even encourage weakness. The current adjustment, which produces a fixing 122 pips lower than the previous day's fix, is consistent with a stance of managed depreciation.

But the magnitude of the adjustment matters as much as the direction. The 122-pip gap is relatively modest compared to the 343-pip shift in the model projection. This tells us that the authorities are not trying to resist the market's direction. They are trying to control the speed. The analogy is not a dam holding back a flood; it is a valve regulating flow. The question for investors is whether the valve will be tightened or loosened as the year progresses.

The calendar of events provides a roadmap for when the valve is likely to be adjusted. Before the Politburo meeting in late July, the authorities will want stability to avoid distracting from the economic policy agenda. Before the APEC summit in November, and especially before President Xi's visit to the United States, the incentives for stability will be even stronger. A sharp depreciation ahead of a high-profile diplomatic engagement would be a political liability. Conversely, the period between the Central Economic Work Conference in mid-December and the end of the year could be a window for more aggressive adjustment, as the policy framework for 2027 is set and the diplomatic calendar clears.

This sequencing leads to a clear forecast: the renminbi is likely to weaken gradually through the third quarter, with the pace accelerating in the fourth quarter after the APEC summit, and then potentially stabilizing toward year-end as the diplomatic visit approaches. The model projection of 6.7787 is a snapshot, not a terminal target. The trajectory will be shaped by how the counter-cyclical factor is deployed at each inflection point.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Interaction Between the Fix Model and Capital Flow Dynamics

The report provides an exceptionally clear picture of the fixing model's mechanics and its current output. What it does not fully address is the feedback loop between the fixing level and actual capital flows. The fixing is the anchor for the onshore spot rate, but the spot rate, in turn, influences the behavior of exporters, importers, and speculative capital. A weaker fixing encourages exporters to hold onto dollar receipts rather than convert them, which creates additional downward pressure on the currency. It also increases the cost of hedging for importers, which can lead to a build-up of short-dollar positions that eventually need to be covered.

The model captures overnight contributions from observable market variables, but it does not explicitly model these second-order flow effects. The result is that the model's projection may systematically understate the degree of depreciation during periods when behavioral feedback loops are strengthening. The recent model errors, which show the model under-predicting depreciation, are consistent with this hypothesis. The market is behaving as if the feedback loop is already in play.

An investor who relies solely on the model projection without accounting for these flow dynamics risks being caught offside. The counter-cyclical factor can moderate the pace of depreciation, but it cannot eliminate the underlying pressure if capital outflows accelerate. The critical unknown is whether the authorities will respond to accelerating outflows by tightening the counter-cyclical factor further or by allowing the fixing to move more quickly to a new equilibrium. The report does not resolve this question, and it is the most important analytical gap for anyone positioning in Asian FX.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: How to Use the Fix Model to Position Across Three Scenarios

The report's analytical machinery can be translated into a practical decision framework. The framework has three scenarios, each defined by the behavior of the counter-cyclical factor relative to the model projection.

Scenario one is continued managed depreciation. This is the base case implied by the current data. The model projection weakens gradually, the counter-cyclical factor moderates the pace, and the fixing moves lower by roughly 100 to 150 pips per week. In this scenario, the appropriate positioning is to be short renminbi against the dollar, but with tight stop-losses around key political event dates. The risk is not that the direction reverses, but that the pace slows unexpectedly, making short positions expensive to carry.

Scenario two is a tightening of the counter-cyclical factor. If the model projection continues to weaken but the authorities respond by increasing the adjustment, the fixing could stabilize or even strengthen temporarily. This would signal that the authorities have reached the limit of their tolerance for depreciation, likely because capital outflows have accelerated or because a diplomatic event is approaching. In this scenario, the appropriate response is to reduce short positions and consider tactical long renminbi trades, but only for a limited duration. The tightening is likely to be temporary, not a reversal of the depreciation trend.

Scenario three is an abandonment of the counter-cyclical factor. If the authorities allow the fixing to converge fully with the model projection, the renminbi could weaken sharply in a short period. This would be a crisis scenario, triggered by a sudden loss of confidence or a policy decision to prioritize export competitiveness over stability. In this scenario, the appropriate positioning is to be aggressively short renminbi and to look for knock-on effects on other Asian currencies, particularly those with close trade linkages to China.

The decision framework is only as good as the investor's ability to monitor the inputs. The key variables to watch are the daily fixing relative to the model projection, the magnitude of the counter-cyclical factor adjustment, and the pattern of model errors. If the errors remain biased toward under-predicting depreciation, the probability of scenario three increases. If the counter-cyclical factor adjustment widens, the probability of scenario two increases. The base case is scenario one, but the probabilities are shifting in real time.

## The Unresolved Analytical Question That Should Drive Your Next Research Step

The most intriguing unresolved question from this report is whether the model's projection is converging toward a fundamental equilibrium or whether it is simply tracking a moving target. The model incorporates overnight market variables, but it does not explicitly incorporate the full set of fundamentals that determine a currency's long-run fair value: productivity growth, terms of trade, fiscal sustainability, and the stock of external assets and liabilities. The renminbi's equilibrium value is a subject of intense debate, with estimates ranging from modest undervaluation to significant overvaluation depending on the methodology used.

If the model's projection is converging toward a fundamental equilibrium, then the current depreciation is a healthy adjustment that reduces the risk of a future crisis. If the model is simply tracking a moving target that is itself being driven by speculative flows, then the depreciation could overshoot, creating the conditions for a sharp reversal or a policy intervention. The report does not take a stand on this question, and it is the most important analytical issue that the full report likely addresses.

Investors who want to position with conviction need to resolve this question for themselves. That requires going beyond the fixing model to analyze the broader balance of payments, the trajectory of China's current account surplus, and the behavior of non-resident portfolio flows. The fixing model is an essential tool for short-term positioning, but it is not a substitute for a comprehensive macro framework.
