# Electronic Components Sector

## Component Data Deposition: METI production statistics (electronic components, semiconductors) for May 2026: ABF substrates remain strong

METI released May 2026 production statistics on July 14. Domestic production value (in USD terms) for both electronic components and semiconductors fell broadly in line with seasonality in May, reflecting the Golden Week holidays. Within electronic components, MLCCs came in well below typical seasonal levels, suggesting a lack of momentum. That said, METI's MLCC production-value data and the Ministry of Finance's (MoF) MLCC export value appear to have diverged of late. Given the gradual uptrend in the MoF's MLCC export value, we see no need for undue concern at this point. Among passive components, aluminum electrolytic capacitors remain solid. Meanwhile, production value and ASP for rigid module substrates (including ABF substrates/FC-BGA) continue to trend upward, a positive signal for Ibiden. Our top picks in the electronic components sector are Rohm and Murata Manufacturing.

We expect a strong recovery in industrial machinery demand heading into the electronic components sector's April–June results, with automotive demand also broadly recovering from inventory corrections. By stock, we recommend Ibiden, Rohm, Murata Manufacturing, and Hirose Electric (all Overweight rated). Among passive-component stocks, Murata is our top pick. For Taiyo Yuden, Nichicon, and Nippon Chemi-Con, near-term quarterly earnings visibility is low, and with quarterly profit levels still low relative to share-price gains, volatility could rise depending on results. We initially look to confirm April–June earnings levels. For Ibiden, beyond the strength in ABF substrates, we are watching for any new capacity-expansion announcements. For MLCCs, a book-to-bill ratio of 1.3 has never persisted for two consecutive quarters in past cycles, but we believe it held at 1.3–1.4 in April–June as well, reflecting demand for AI servers. At Rohm, we expect strong sales and order trends, driven by a recovery in the analog and discrete markets, where its automotive and industrial machinery exposure is high. In particular, supply-demand for Tier 1 makers' Si MOSFETs and similar products is tightening, and we believe Tier 2 makers are beginning to benefit as well. That said, we think destocking continued through April–June, and we expect earnings to expand from July–September. At Hirose Electric, we expect April–June results to beat guidance, led by industrial machinery, and we maintain a bullish stance on fundamentals.

- Our view: In May, both electronic components and semiconductors fell broadly in line with the past 20 years' seasonality (MoM). Within electronic components, passive components (MLCCs in particular) undershot seasonality, and the trend line continues to show sluggish momentum. MLCCs are gradually recovering in volume terms, but production value remains sluggish. Production value for rigid module substrates (including ABF substrates/FC-BGA) rose considerably MoM even excluding the impact of the weaker yen, maintaining its uptrend. In semiconductors, memory stood out with a considerable jump in both production value and ASP.

- MLCCs: For MLCCs, the MoF trade statistics and METI production statistics appear to have diverged since December 2025. On value in particular, METI put MLCC production value in May at US$370 million (-17% YoY, -17% MoM), while MoF export value in May was US$411 million (+9% YoY, -12% MoM), with production value falling short of export value. The criteria for many of METI's production data are vague, while MoF trade data shows growth for both export value and average unit prices on a USD basis, so we place more emphasis on MoF trade data.

- Electronic components: Domestic production value (in USD terms) of electronic components in May 2026 fell 13% YoY and 11% MoM for passive components, was flat YoY and fell 4% MoM for mechanical components, rose 12% and 3% for electronic substrates, and fell 7% and 11% for electronic circuit mounting boards. Overall, in May, domestic production value (in USD terms) fell broadly in line with seasonality. Passive components fell short of seasonality, suggesting a lack of momentum. For MLCCs, production volume fell 19% MoM, production value (in USD terms) fell 17% MoM, and ASP (in USD terms) rose 2% MoM; production value declined considerably on the sharp drop in volume. The trendline is also moving from flat to slightly lower, likewise pointing to a lack of momentum. In contrast, aluminum electrolytic capacitors are performing well, with production value maintaining a gradual uptrend. Mechanical components fell in line with seasonality. Connectors likewise fell in line with seasonality, and the production-value trendline remains broadly flat. Electronic substrates outpaced seasonality, and the trendline continues to show gradual growth. In particular, rigid module substrates (including ABF substrates/FC-BGA) ran considerably above seasonality, sustaining strong growth even excluding the impact of the weaker yen. A further jump in ASP resulted in an even clearer uptrend. Electronic circuit mounting boards moved broadly in line with seasonality.

- Semiconductors: YoY and MoM changes in May 2026 domestic production value (USD) were -19% YoY and -5% MoM for discrete semiconductors, -21% and +1% for MCUs, +10% and flat for logic ICs, and +548% and +30% for memory. In May, output fell short of seasonality overall, except for memory. Memory saw a considerable jump in both production value and ASP, rising at an unprecedented pace.
