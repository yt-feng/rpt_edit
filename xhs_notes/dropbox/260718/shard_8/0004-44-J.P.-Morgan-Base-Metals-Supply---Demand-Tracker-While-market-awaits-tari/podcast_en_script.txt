EN_A: So the latest report on base metals says China is still buying copper, even though demand signals look weak. That sounds like a contradiction. What’s really going on?
EN_B: It is the central puzzle in the copper market right now. The report highlights a huge divergence between China’s apparent consumption of copper and its actual end-use demand.

EN_B: Exactly. End-use demand indicators dropped sharply in May, down 5% year-on-year, because last year’s base was incredibly high, especially for renewables and grid investment. But apparent consumption kept growing, up 8% in May.
EN_A: So what does that mean? Is someone just stockpiling, or is production the issue?
EN_B: Production is actually very resilient. Chinese refined copper output is running near 14 million tonnes per year. But the real story is not production. It’s inventories.
EN_A: What about inventories?
EN_B: Chinese copper inventories have fallen to the low end of the five-year range. SHFE plus bonded stocks are below 140,000 tonnes. That has pushed up China’s spot premium and opened the import window.
EN_A: So China is buying not because demand is strong, but because stocks are too low and they have to?
EN_B: Exactly. And it’s not just China. LME cancellations in Asian warehouses jumped by 65,000 tonnes this week. Global on-warrant LME stocks are down to about 130,000 tonnes. That is critically low.
EN_A: And what does that mean for prices?
EN_B: Low inventories can trigger sharp backwardation and price spikes. But the report argues the real catalyst for copper in the second half of the year is not fundamentals. It is policy.
EN_A: Policy? You mean the US copper tariffs under Section 232?
EN_B: Yes. The report says the key is how the policy communication shapes expectations around tariff escalation.
EN_A: So it’s a fork in the road. If tariffs escalate, what happens?
EN_B: If the market expects more escalation, the US will keep importing before tariffs hit. That pulls metal away from the rest of the world. China has to buy at higher prices. It creates a very bullish pinch point.
EN_A: And if they don’t escalate?
EN_B: Then the US import window closes. The US goes through a long destocking period. China gets its pricing power back. That is bearish for LME prices.
EN_A: So the market is basically betting on a policy outcome?
EN_B: That is the biggest uncertainty for the second half. The report has more detailed scenarios, but we won’t go into all of them here.
