EN_B: Global caviar production has been growing at 10 to 15 percent annually over the past five years. The key driver is the shift from wild sturgeon fishing to sustainable aquaculture. Xunlong is perfectly positioned in that trend.

EN_B: The company guided for 900 million RMB in revenue and 460 million in adjusted net profit for 2026, both up 15 percent year-on-year. Gross margin is expected to stay at 65 to 70 percent, and net margin at 45 to 50 percent. Those are exceptional numbers for a food company.
EN_A: So where is the growth coming from? Overseas or domestic?
EN_B: About 80 percent of revenue comes from exports. In the first half of this year, the US and Europe showed resilient growth, and Japan and South Korea actually beat expectations. The domestic market, however, saw some weakness after an anti-graft campaign in late 2025.
EN_A: So is the domestic market recovering now?
EN_B: Yes, by late Q2 2026, there were early signs of a recovery in high-end consumption. The company sees the domestic market as a higher-margin brand-building opportunity, because its own brand, Kaluga Queen, currently only accounts for about 35 percent of revenue. There is a lot of room to grow.
EN_A: How is it building that brand domestically?
EN_B: It is experimenting with two types of stores: large luxury boutiques in premium malls, and smaller pop-up concept stores. It has also launched caviar-plus products, like caviar ice cream and caviar chocolates, to attract younger consumers. Some stores are generating monthly GMV of 200,000 to 400,000 RMB.
EN_A: Caviar ice cream? That is quite a crossover. What are its competitive moats? Can others copy it?
EN_B: That is a key part of the report. The first moat is time. Sturgeon take 7 to 15 years to mature. Xunlong already has over 15,000 tons of fish inventory. A new entrant would need more than a decade to catch up. The second moat is technical efficiency. Its egg yield is 17 to 18 percent, versus an industry average of 10 to 12 percent. And its annual survival rate is over 97 percent, compared to 50 to 70 percent for the industry.
EN_A: What about tariff risks? How does the US-China trade war affect it?
EN_B: US tariffs on Chinese caviar peaked at 185 percent and are now at 55 percent. But management says those costs are fully absorbed by US importers. The company is also exploring overseas farming bases to hedge against policy swings.
EN_A: And what about RMB appreciation risk?
EN_B: That is a real concern, because most overseas sales are in USD. The company has already discussed price hikes of about 10 percent with overseas clients this year. But this is something that still needs to be monitored.
