The industrial gas industry's ability to price above inflation is well documented but often underestimated in its consistency. For the gas majors, pricing growth has historically tracked at or above inflation over multi-year cycles, supported by long-term take-or-pay contracts and the essential nature of oxygen, nitrogen, and hydrogen in industrial processes. What makes Air Products' current opportunity different is the productivity side of the equation.

Menezes has emphasized a target of 3–4% net gain from "price plus productivity minus inflation." We believe this is conservative. The cultural shift at Air Products—driven by the involvement of Mantle Ridge Capital and the appointment of a board with deep industrial gas experience—has already begun to produce cost savings that were previously unattainable under the prior megaproject-focused strategy. These savings come from optimizing the existing asset footprint, reducing operational complexity, and instilling a cost-conscious mindset across the organization.

The comparison with Linde EMEA is instructive. Menezes previously ran that region, which today has the highest margins in the entire Linde group. The same operational playbook—density optimization, disciplined capital allocation, and relentless productivity improvement—is now being applied at Air Products. We expect this to contribute roughly 3% to annual EPS growth from productivity alone, with pricing adding another 2.5%. Together, these two levers account for over half of the projected EPS growth, and they are the most controllable elements of the plan.

## NEOM's EPS Headwind Is Manageable and Temporary, and the Project Turns Positive by 2030

The NEOM green hydrogen project is the single largest source of investor concern about Air Products' earnings trajectory. Our proprietary model of the joint venture and Air Products' offtake obligations provides a clearer picture than the prevailing market narrative.

The joint venture itself is expected to generate a typical industrial gas internal rate of return of approximately 10.6%, meaning the project is fundamentally sound at the asset level. The risk lies in Air Products' ability to market its offtake volumes at a green premium. In our base case, we assume that Air Products and Yara (assuming the agreement completes) can market 75% of volumes at a green premium, with the remaining 25% sold at grey prices derived from our ammonia supply-demand model. Under this assumption, NEOM creates an annual EPS headwind of roughly $0.20, or about 1% per year, across fiscal 2027 through 2029.

This headwind is significant but not debilitating. It reduces our EPS estimates by approximately 1% annually during the peak investment period, yet we still forecast EPS above consensus for those years. The critical inflection point comes in 2030, when the TotalEnergies hydrogen offtake begins. That contract has more conventional industrial gas economics, and under the same 75-25 green-grey split, NEOM turns from a drag to a net positive contributor to EPS. If Air Products and Yara can market 100% of volumes at a green premium, the annual EPS upside would be approximately $0.35, or 2.5% per annum.

The breakeven point for NEOM to be EPS-positive is 85% green-premium sales. Given the trajectory of European hydrogen regulation and the growing demand for green ammonia in power generation and shipping, we view this as achievable over time. Investors should focus on the temporary nature of the headwind and the structural value of the asset once the TotalEnergies offtake is operational.

For investors evaluating Air Products, the thesis rests on three observable conditions that can be tracked over time.

Second, base volume growth needs to recover to at least 1.5% annually. This depends on global industrial production trends and the resolution of geopolitical uncertainty in the Middle East. Investors should monitor industrial production indices and the company's quarterly volume disclosures for confirmation.

Third, NEOM's green-premium trajectory must remain on track. The key milestones are the completion of the Yara agreement, the commencement of the TotalEnergies offtake in 2030, and the evolution of European hydrogen regulation. Any acceleration in regulatory support for green hydrogen would improve the probability of 100% green-premium sales, turning NEOM from a headwind into a tailwind faster than our base case assumes.

If all three conditions are met, the valuation gap to peers should close, and the stock should deliver compounding returns well above the market. If one or two conditions falter, the downside is limited by the current valuation floor. The risk-reward is skewed to the positive, but only if the cultural transformation proves durable.
