EN_A: So Uber just announced a deal to acquire Delivery Hero. How big is this thing, and why should we care?
EN_B: We are talking about a roughly 14.8 billion dollar deal. Uber gets 50 out of Delivery Hero's 64 markets. The big picture here is that this is not just an acquisition. It is a move to turn Uber into a true cross-platform super app.
EN_A: A super app? That sounds ambitious. What is the core question this report is trying to answer?
EN_B: The report asks: how can Uber use this deal to offer both rides and food delivery in more markets to drive user spending and loyalty? Right now, Uber has both services in about 40 percent of its markets. After this deal, that number jumps to 60 percent.
EN_A: Sixty percent is a huge jump. So what are the key takeaways?
EN_B: First, synergies. Uber expects 1.2 billion dollars in total synergies, mostly from headcount and tech platform integration. Delivery Hero runs a very local setup with more employees than Uber. Uber plans to migrate those brands one by one onto its own platform.
EN_A: That sounds risky. What could go wrong?
EN_B: Plenty. Data loss, downtime, and the migration itself becomes the gating factor for cost savings. Interestingly, the revenue synergies from cross-platform users are a very small part of that 1.2 billion. The real upside might come from scaling marketplace efficiencies.
EN_A: Okay, so the second takeaway must be about the markets that matter most.
EN_B: Exactly. The report highlights three regions: the Middle East, South Korea, and Argentina. For example, Delivery Hero's talabat is number one in the UAE, but Uber had no strong delivery business there. In South Korea, Baemin is the leader, but Uber's rides business is weak.
EN_A: So the cross-sell opportunity is huge. But is execution going to be tough?
EN_B: It is. The report notes that Korea could be especially challenging because a private company dominates the ride-hailing space. Argentina is the opposite. Delivery Hero's business there is much larger than Uber's, so the cross-sell potential is bigger.
EN_A: That leads to the third takeaway, which I assume is about competition.
EN_B: Right. In the UAE, talabat is number one, but Meituan's Keeta brand entered in 2024 and is investing aggressively. Also, DoorDash's acquisition of Deliveroo is improving operations there. In Korea, Baemin faces competition from Coupang Eats, which is backed by a strong e-commerce player.
EN_A: So this deal is not just about integration. It is going to spark new competition. What about the read-through for DoorDash?
EN_B: Two points. First, in the UAE, the integration period could create a window for DoorDash to grab market share. Second, 14 Delivery Hero markets are being sold to private equity, and those are currently loss-making. DoorDash might see an opportunity to expand there.
