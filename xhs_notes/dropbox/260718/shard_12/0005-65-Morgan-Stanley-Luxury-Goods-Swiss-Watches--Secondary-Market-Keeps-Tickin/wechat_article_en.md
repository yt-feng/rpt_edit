## The recovery is broadening, but the pace of price growth is moderating, and the gains are concentrated in a single month

The 1.5 percent quarterly gain in secondary prices is respectable, but it represents a deceleration from the 2.5 percent gains recorded in both the fourth quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026. More importantly, the timing of the gains reveals that the recovery is not yet self-sustaining. The WatchCharts Overall Market price tracker rose 2.5 percent in April alone, making it the single best month since March 2022, before falling by 1.0 percent across May and June combined. The April spike was driven almost entirely by anticipation for Watches & Wonders 2026, which generated record-high search interest on Google Trends, fueled by the anticipated discontinuation of the Rolex GMT-Master "Pepsi" and the 50th anniversary of the Patek Philippe Nautilus. Dealers flooded the market with new listings in March and April to capitalize on the hype, supply reached record or near-record highs for all tracked brands, and prices surged. But once the event passed, the excess supply had to be absorbed, and prices softened.

This pattern has two implications. First, it suggests that the recovery is still heavily dependent on event-driven catalysts rather than organic demand momentum. Second, it means that the supply overhang, particularly for Rolex, will need to be worked through in the third quarter. The data shows that supply levels reached record highs over the past two quarters, and while demand remained steady, the surplus of listings caused prices to stagnate in May and June. The third quarter is likely to see limited carryover from the first-half events, and the pace of price growth may moderate further as the market digests the accumulated inventory.

Within the Big Three, the performance was solid but not spectacular. Patek Philippe gained 2.2 percent quarter over quarter, Audemars Piguet gained 1.5 percent, and Rolex gained 1.0 percent.
