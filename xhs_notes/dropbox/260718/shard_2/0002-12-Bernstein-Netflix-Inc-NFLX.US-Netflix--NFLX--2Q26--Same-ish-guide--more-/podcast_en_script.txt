EN_A: Netflix just reported earnings and the stock dropped about 9% after hours. What happened?
EN_B: The numbers themselves were actually fine. Revenue and profit were mostly in line. But the market is worried about something deeper: growth is slowing, and there is no obvious positive catalyst on the horizon.
EN_A: So the report itself was okay, but the market read it more negatively?
EN_B: Exactly. Take the revenue guidance. The full year outlook still shows 14% growth. But on a constant currency basis, the implied growth range narrowed from 11-13% down to 12%. That is a subtle but important shift.
EN_A: What does that mean? Is Netflix running out of new subscribers?
EN_B: That is exactly the question this report tries to answer. Subscriber growth in the first half of the year may have been weaker than expected. And by tightening the guidance range, management is essentially leaving investors to guess whether growth is decelerating faster than previously thought.
EN_A: What about margins? I remember management had a 31.5% target.
EN_B: They reaffirmed it. But they did not raise it. By now, they should have good visibility into second half content spending and advertising revenue. If they were confident, they could have upgraded the target. They did not.
EN_A: So investors assume management is being cautious for a reason?
EN_B: Right. And then there is the decision to reduce how often they disclose viewing hours. They are moving from semi-annual to annual reports.
EN_B: In an environment where growth signals are unclear, investors rely on those engagement numbers. Now that Netflix is providing less transparency, the market will turn to third party data, which is often noisier and less reliable.
EN_A: Did management explain why they made that change?
EN_B: Yes, and their logic makes sense. They argue that viewing hours do not have a linear relationship with revenue. For example, live events account for about 5% of content spend but only 1% of viewing hours. Yet they drive a disproportionate number of new sign-ups. So focusing only on hours would undervalue live content.
EN_A: That sounds reasonable. So what is Netflix doing next?
EN_B: They are exploring new formats. Video podcasts, for instance, are over-indexing on daytime mobile viewing, which suggests they are bringing in incremental engagement. They are also testing free trials and even a potential free ad-supported tier.
EN_A: A free ad-supported tier? That is the completely free version of Netflix people have been speculating about?
EN_B: Yes, but the report makes clear this is still in testing. It depends on whether ad monetization improves enough and whether it cannibalizes existing paid subscribers. That is a question the full report explores further.

EN_B: It is a signal. But the report also notes that the accelerated buybacks were partially offset by higher technology expenses. So buybacks alone do not mean everything is perfect.
