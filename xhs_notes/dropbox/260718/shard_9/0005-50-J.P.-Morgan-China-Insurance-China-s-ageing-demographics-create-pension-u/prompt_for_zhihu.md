## Snapshot on China's pension market

China's pension system is based on three pillars - government basis (Urban/Rural basic pension), corporate (Enterprise/Occupation Annuity), and individual pension with tax incentives. In the past decade, the underlying structure of pension system fell much on government-dominated and corporate pension categories (AUM of three pillars: US\$1.7T/1.0T/26B as of Dec-24). The China government first officially mentioned the pension sector in the 14th Five-Year Plan (2021 to 2025) in March 2021 including “build a pension service industry that balances between commercial and social value”. Hence, the Chinese government has conducted a series of pension reforms including the facilitation of the private pension business, allowing insurers, banks and wealth management firms to offer individual retirement. That said, the authority is aiming to address the looming shortfall in the private pension pillar and alleviate the high dependence on the first/second pillar as early as possible. In March 2026, the Chinese government newly announced the $15^{\text{th}}$ Five-Year Plan (2026 to 2030), outlining that it shall prioritize to improve a comprehensive multi-tier pension insurance system, including expanding the coverage of corporate pension, refining supportive policies for individual pension alongside vigorously developing commercial pension insurance. By 2030, the government targets a combined AUM scale of enterprises and Occupation Annuity nationwide exceeding Rmb9T (\~US\$1.3T).

\- First Pillar (AUM: Rmb11.6T or US\$1.7T, as of Dec-24). The composition of the first pillar included government-run basic pension Fund and National Social Security Fund. According to the Statistical bulletin on the development of Human Resources and Social Security in 2024 (link), the ending balance of the basic pension fund as of Dec-24 was Rmb8.7T. The other substantial supplement to this pillar is the National Social Security Fund (NSSF 全国社保基金). It worked as a reserve fund in case deficit of basic pension occurred. As of 2024, the net assets of NSSF were at Rmb2.9T, according to the annual report of the National Council for Social Security Fund (link).

\- Second Pillar (AUM: Rmb6.8T or US\$1.0T, as of Dec-24). Enterprise annuity and occupation annuity are fundamental elements of the second pillar. Based on the annual report disclosure from the Ministry of Human Resource and Social Security, the total AUM for corporate pension is Rmb 6.8T, as of Dec-24 (link), comprising approximately \~Rmb3.7T of enterprise annuity and Rmb3.1T of occupation annuity.

\- Third Pillar (AUM: Rmb182B or US\$26B, as of Dec-24): Supported by ongoing government policies, China's pillar 3 pension AUM has grown at 137% CAGR from 2020 to 2024, up to Rmb182B, according to China Construction Bank's pension finance white paper (2025). Over the past decade, China's pension system has remained heavily skewed towards government-led categories. Following pilot programmes for individual pension schemes, including tax-deferred commercial endowment insurance and pension target funds, the State Council initially proposed a private pension plan with tax incentives in April-2022. The plan encourages individuals to set up personal pension accounts, with current annual contributions of up to Rmb12,000, or approximately US\$1,717, as of July-26. Overall, the Chinese government is gradually building a multi-layered, multi-pillar pension system.
