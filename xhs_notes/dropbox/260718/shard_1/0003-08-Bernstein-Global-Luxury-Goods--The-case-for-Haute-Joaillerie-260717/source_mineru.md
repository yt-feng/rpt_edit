# Global Luxury Goods: The case for Haute Joaillerie

Luca Solca +41 582 723 126 luca.solca@bernsteinsg.com

Maria Meita +44 20 7170 0540 maria.meita@bernsteinsg.com

Eric Chen, CFA +852 2123 2628 eric.chen@bernsteinsg.com

Yi-Peng Khoo, CFA +44 20 7676 6822 yi-peng.khoo@bernsteinsg.com

Richemont just blew both sell-side and buy-side expectations out of the water, with JM posting +24% organic growth in 1Q27E. We estimate \~20% of those sales come from high jewelry. And with AI driving multiple pockets of wealth creation and spend becoming more polarized, we anticipate growth will continue to skew toward VICs, and in turn, towards high jewelry (Best of Times, Worst of Times: Global Luxury Goods - A Tale of Two Luxury Consumers). Hence, we thought it timely to provide an intro into the category - for those who have yet to be invited to spend north of €1 million on exceptional gemstones during Paris Couture Week.

Haute Joaillerie is basically couture for jewelry and it behaves as such: very high ticket, low volume, strong halo. It is creativity without limits in terms of both design and price. For most brands it is used as a brand-building and client-acquisition tool more than a profit-driving activity, but this dynamic could change in the wake of VIC demand for more unique pieces at any cost. Industry leaders (Chaumet's CEO as an example) talk about exceptional high jewelry demand as resilient, with lower priced items (around the €150k mark) necessitating more innovation to tempt clients.

Luxury brands have taken notice: Gucci unveiled its first-ever high jewelry collection in 2019 and Hermès is doubling-down on the category by presenting the biggest collection since its launch in 2010, ahead of its Haute Couture debut in January 2027. Chanel hired Cartier veteran Marie-Laure Cérède as the director of its jewelry studio, effective October 2026 and Tiffany & co. remains committed to high jewelry as a pillar of their turnaround efforts - see their exhibitions in London 2022, Tokyo 2024 or Seoul 2025.

While the Place Vendôme jewelers became part of the official Haute Couture Week calendar in Paris in 2010, recent Haute Joaillerie events have also traveled to exclusive locations such as Lake Como (Dior), St Tropez (Cartier) or Venice (Chaumet, Dior), with an intimate group of VICs being flown in for each event. Markets such as Greater China, Southeast Asia and the Middle East have driven recent growth, with North America emerging as a region with serious potential, similar to other luxury categories.
