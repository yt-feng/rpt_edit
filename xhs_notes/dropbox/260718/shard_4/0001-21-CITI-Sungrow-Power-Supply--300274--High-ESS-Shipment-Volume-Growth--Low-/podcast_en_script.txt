EN_A: Sungrow Power Supply just saw its stock drop more than 30 percent in July. What is the market so worried about?
EN_B: It is all about the second quarter earnings. The company is a giant in energy storage, and a recent global investment bank report argues that the market may have overreacted.
EN_A: So what is the main call from that report?
EN_B: They keep a Buy rating with a target price of 185 yuan, implying over 70 percent upside. The report believes the gross margin will indeed dip in Q2, but that is not a long-term trend. And the stock price has already priced in the bad news.
EN_A: What happened to the gross margin? I recall it was 30 percent in Q1 for the energy storage business.
EN_B: Right. The report estimates it will fall to around 26 to 27 percent in Q2. The reason is simple. Battery costs went up. Batteries make up 60 percent of the total cost of an energy storage system, and lithium accounts for 20 percent of battery cost. Lithium prices peaked in mid-May and have dropped 25 percent since then, but the impact takes time to flow through.
EN_A: So Q3 should look better?
EN_B: That is exactly one of the key insights. As lithium prices come down, Q3 margins will likely improve. And Sungrow only keeps about one month of battery inventory, which is much shorter than many peers. That means they can benefit from lower costs much faster.
EN_A: So the market might be too pessimistic about Q2.
EN_B: Exactly. The report points out that the 30 percent share price drop in the past month has already reflected the low expectations for Q2 earnings. At 11.6 times 2027 estimated PE and a 29 percent return on equity, the stock looks cheap.
EN_A: Beyond the near-term earnings, what else stands out in the report?
EN_B: The growth in energy storage shipments is very strong. The report expects global storage demand to grow 50 to 60 percent in 2026 and another 30 to 40 percent in 2027. Sungrow itself is confident, and they are working on a new 684Ah large battery, which is much more efficient than the current 314Ah model. It should go into production in the first half of next year.
EN_A: What about geopolitical risks? A lot of investors worry about the US market.
EN_B: Sungrow is not too concerned. They argue that if the US and Europe stop buying Chinese inverters, there simply is not enough alternative supply. New projects would be delayed. Also, their products do not have remote access functions, so they meet US regulatory requirements.
EN_A: I also noticed something about AI data centers in the report.
EN_B: That is a very important new angle. On July 9, Sungrow launched a new product for AI data centers, a 10kV solid-state transformer for 800-volt high-voltage DC power. They have already signed orders with two domestic clients, and companies like Google and Nvidia have shown strong interest.
EN_A: What does that mean for Sungrow?
EN_B: It means Sungrow is transitioning from a traditional energy storage and inverter company into a power solutions provider for AI infrastructure. The report believes high-voltage DC is just a temporary fix, while 800-volt DC is the sustainable solution. But how many orders this will actually generate, the report does not fully answer yet.
EN_A: So what are the big unknowns from this report?
EN_B: Two main ones. First, how bad will the Q2 earnings actually be? Could the real numbers be even worse than what the market expects? Second, how much revenue will the AI data center business really bring in? We will likely need to wait until the second half of this year or even next year to get clearer signals.
