## The Industry Is Undergoing a Simultaneous Three-Axis Expansion That Multiplies Optical Demand

The single most important structural driver in this market is that AI data center architects are no longer choosing between scaling approaches. They are deploying all three simultaneously. Scale-out connects thousands of accelerators within a single cluster. Scale-up links accelerators within a single server node at higher bandwidths. Scale-across connects multiple clusters across data center campuses. Each topology requires dense optical transceiver deployment, and the combined effect is multiplicative, not additive.

Previous industry forecasts assumed that one architecture would dominate and that optical demand would follow a linear growth path relative to compute capacity. That assumption has been invalidated. The new forecast of 150 million AI transceiver units in 2028, up from a prior estimate of 80 million, directly reflects the recognition that hyperscalers are building with all three architectures in parallel. The most dramatic upward revision comes in the 1.6T category, where the inflection point is arriving earlier and at higher volume than previously modeled.

This three-axis expansion creates a compound growth dynamic that is difficult to overstate. Each additional architecture deployed in a given data center adds optical ports that would not exist under a single-topology design. The transceiver content per GPU is rising faster than the GPU count itself. For investors and strategists tracking the AI supply chain, the optical layer has shifted from being a supporting component to being a binding constraint on cluster scale.

## Near-Packaged Optics and Optical Circuit Switching Open New Growth Vectors Beyond 2027

The most significant risk to the conventional transceiver model has been the prospect of co-packaged optics, where optical engines are integrated directly into switch silicon, potentially reducing the number of pluggable transceivers required. This concern has weighed on valuations across the optical sector. However, the emergence of near-packaged optics as a viable intermediate architecture provides a resolution to this debate that favors established transceiver manufacturers.

NPO retains the pluggable form factor but moves the optical engine closer to the switch ASIC, improving power efficiency and signal integrity without requiring a complete redesign of data center cabling infrastructure. Innolight is well positioned to supply NPO modules because the manufacturing expertise required is a direct extension of its existing 1.6T and 3.2T capabilities. The success of NPO as a bridging technology could delay or reduce the market impact of full CPO adoption, extending the addressable market for pluggable transceivers further into the decade.

Optical circuit switching represents a separate growth vector. As AI clusters scale to tens of thousands of accelerators, the electrical switching fabric becomes a bottleneck in terms of power consumption and latency. OCS uses optical signals to route data without converting to electricity at every hop, dramatically reducing power per bit. This technology is in early deployment at select hyperscale data centers, and adoption is expected to accelerate as cluster sizes continue to grow.

These two technologies together could support revenue growth beyond the 1.6T and 3.2T cycles. The report's earnings estimates for 2028 imply net profit of RMB 92.1 billion, but this figure does not fully incorporate NPO and OCS contributions, which are still in the early stages of commercial validation. If either technology achieves broader adoption, the current estimates may prove conservative.

The pattern of demand acceleration, speed node transitions, and emerging technologies described above can be distilled into a structured framework for strategic decision-making. Leaders evaluating their exposure to this market should assess opportunities and risks across four dimensions.

Companies that have qualified 1.6T products with hyperscale customers and demonstrated volume production capability are positioned to capture the majority of this growth. The second dimension is customer concentration and relationship depth. The hyperscale cloud providers are not interchangeable customers. They have distinct network architectures, qualification processes, and supplier relationship models. Companies with long-standing joint development agreements and a track record of meeting custom specifications have higher visibility into future demand and are less vulnerable to sudden vendor rotation.

The third dimension is technology roadmap optionality. The emergence of NPO and OCS creates both opportunity and risk. Companies that can supply both traditional pluggable transceivers and next-generation optical modules have downside protection against architectural shifts. Those committed exclusively to one form factor face disruption risk if hyperscalers adopt alternative approaches.

The fourth dimension is margin sustainability and capital efficiency. The gross margin expansion from 36.7% to 46.1% over five quarters demonstrates that early node leadership can produce expanding profitability even as volumes scale. However, this trajectory depends on continued yield improvement and cost reduction. Companies that fail to maintain time-to-cost advantages will see margin compression as competition intensifies at each node.

Applying this framework, the most attractive positions are held by companies that combine first-mover status at 1.6T, established joint development pipelines for 3.2T, and credible roadmaps for NPO and OCS. The least attractive positions are held by companies that are late to 1.6T qualification, dependent on a single customer, or reliant on legacy form factors with no clear next-generation strategy.

The risks to this thesis are concentrated in three areas. Co-packaged optics could disrupt the pluggable model if hyperscalers accelerate adoption beyond current expectations. Competitive intensity could compress margins if multiple suppliers achieve volume production simultaneously. Geopolitical and tariff risks could disrupt supply chains or limit access to key markets. These risks are real but manageable for companies with diversified customer bases, multiple manufacturing locations, and balance sheet strength to invest through cycles.

The optical connectivity market is entering a period of demand growth that has no close historical precedent in the semiconductor supply chain. The combination of three-axis architecture deployment, successive speed node transitions, and emerging optical technologies is creating a multi-year expansion that will reward companies with execution capability and punish those without it.
