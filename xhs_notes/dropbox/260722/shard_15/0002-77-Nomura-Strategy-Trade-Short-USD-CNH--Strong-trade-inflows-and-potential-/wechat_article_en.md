## Chinese Corporate FX Repatriation Has Reached a Critical Tipping Point,Creating Sustained Onshore Demand for RMB

The single most powerful near-term driver of RMB strength is the behavior of Chinese exporters. In June,the net FX trade settlement surplus by Chinese corporations reached USD 81.8 billion,up sharply from USD 53.6 billion in May and well above the Q2 average of USD 61.0 billion. This figure is striking not only for its magnitude but for what it represents as a percentage of the trade surplus. After adjusting for RMB settlement,the net FX trade settlement surplus was equivalent to 92.9 percent of the trade surplus in June,the highest ratio since December 2025.

What this means in practice is that Chinese exporters are choosing to convert their foreign currency earnings into renminbi at an accelerating pace. The exporter remittance ratio,which measures the share of export value that is repatriated and converted,rose to 52.5 percent in June from 46.2 percent in May. Importers' FX demand ratio also increased,to 47.0 percent from 44.4 percent. The gap between the two,which represents net conversion demand,has widened decisively in favor of the renminbi.

This is not a one-month anomaly. The underlying driver is structural:Chinese export growth remains elevated,generating a very large trade surplus that must eventually be converted. The question has always been timing. What changed in June was that spot USD/RMB broke above the 6.80 level during the hawkish post-FOMC environment,giving exporters a compelling incentive to lock in favorable conversion rates. The pattern is visible in intraday trading:spot USD/CNY has consistently drifted lower during Asian hours,which is precisely when onshore corporate FX settlement demand is most concentrated. This suggests that the conversion flow is not a passive reaction but an active,ongoing process that will likely persist as long as the trade surplus remains large and exporter sentiment stays positive.

## The PBoC's Fixing Strategy Has Shifted from Defending the Upside to Managing the Downside,a Critical Change in Policy Stance

The second pillar of the RMB appreciation thesis is a subtle but unmistakable shift in how the People's Bank of China manages the daily USD/CNY fixing. For much of 2025 and early 2026,the fixing was used as a tool to slow RMB depreciation during periods of dollar strength. That has changed.

The evidence is clear. Between February 27 and March 30,during the onset of the Iran War,the DXY gained 3.0 percent,yet the USD/CNY fixing barely moved,from 6.9228 to 6.9223. Similarly,between June 16 and June 24,DXY gained 2.1 percent,yet the fixing rose by only 13 basis points. The authorities are now capping the fixing's upside during dollar rallies.

More importantly,they are also allowing the fixing to move lower more aggressively when conditions permit. Since June 2026,on the 19 sessions when the fixing came in lower,the average decline was 33 pips. On the 17 sessions when it came in higher,the average increase was only 21 pips. This asymmetry is deliberate. The positive fixing error,which measures the gap between the actual fixing and the model projection,has narrowed sharply from an average of +437 pips between June 1 and 17 to an average of +196 pips since June 18. The last time such small positive errors were observed was in March 2026,during a period of USD strength that proved temporary.

The implication is strategic. The PBoC is no longer fighting to prevent RMB appreciation. It is now managing the pace of that appreciation to avoid excessive volatility. This shift matters because it removes a key source of uncertainty for investors. When the central bank is actively leaning against depreciation,any RMB rally faces a headwind from official intervention. Now,the headwind has become a tailwind. The authorities are signaling that they are comfortable with a stronger renminbi,and they are using the fixing mechanism to guide the market in that direction.

## USD/CNH Has Become Asymmetric to Dollar Moves,Amplifying Any Downside in DXY

The third factor is a technical characteristic of the USD/CNH pair that has become more pronounced in recent months. The offshore renminbi is now significantly more sensitive to declines in the dollar than to increases. Between June 16 and June 24,when DXY rose 2.1 percent,USD/CNH increased by only 0.84 percent,a sensitivity of 38 percent. But between June 24 and July 21,when DXY fell by just 0.64 percent,USD/CNH dropped by 0.68 percent,a sensitivity of 106 percent.

This asymmetry is not random. It reflects the underlying demand structure for CNH. When the dollar weakens,the combination of corporate repatriation flows,improving sentiment,and reduced hedging demand creates a powerful gravitational pull on USD/CNH to the downside. When the dollar strengthens,these same forces act as a buffer,limiting the extent of the rise.
