## Short USD/CNH: Strong trade inflows and potential DeepSeek 2.0-related inflows

RMB supportive factors include robust trade settlement, asymmetric sensitivity to USD downmoves, potential foreign inflows and stable US-China relations.

Trade: We maintain a conviction level of 3/5 on our short USD/CNH position, targeting 6.55 (\~3.0% gain) by end-October.

We raised (on 16 July) the conviction level on our short USD/CNH recommendation to 3/5 (from 2), as we are leaning towards a near-term softer USD view following the release of weaker-than-expected US June CPI and PPI data. Although there are still a few factors limiting USD's potential downside (i.e., renewed US-Iran attacks, inflows into US assets etc.), local developments should continue to support our CNH appreciation view.

## Drivers of CNH outperformance

1. A continuation of robust onshore Chinese corporate FX repatriation following the large trade surplus in June. We believe Chinese corporate FX selling activity is likely to remain strong in coming months, especially with still-elevated export growth supporting a very large trade surplus.

Latest June data on net FX trade settlement by Chinese corporates showed a surplus of USD81.8bn (May: USD53.6bn; Q2 average: USD61.0bn), which was equivalent to a robust $92.9\%$ of the trade surplus after adjusting for RMB settlement (Figure 1). This is also the largest monthly net FX trade settlement surplus since December 2025 (i.e., USD111.3bn).

The exporter remittance ratio (as a percentage of export value) rose to 52.5% in June (May: 46.2%; Q2 average: 49.6%), while the importers' FX demand ratio also increased to 47.0% in June (May: 44.4%; Q2 average: 46.6%).

This strong net FX trade settlement demand suggests Chinese corporates are increasingly willing to repatriate their USD holdings. Amid the USD bid environment after the hawkish June FOMC, Chinese corporates could have converted their FX earnings to RMB as spot USD/RMB broke above the 6.80 level. We note that spot USD/CNY has tended to drift lower during Asian hours, potentially owing to onshore FX trade settlement demand (Figure 2).

Similarly, between 16 and 24 June, DXY gained $2.1\%$ while the USD/CNY fixing rose only marginally by 13bp.

\- The Chinese authorities appear to be limiting both downside and upside in the daily fixing, the average move lower in fixing has tended to be larger than the average move higher.
