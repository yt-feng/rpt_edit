EN_B: Possibly, but not entirely. The report focuses a lot on leveraged ETFs. Their assets under management in Korea peaked at 50 billion dollars, which was four times larger relative to market size than in the US. That number is now down to 26 billion, but volatility is still unusually high.

EN_B: The VKOSPI, which is Korea’s volatility index, is still about five times the VIX level in the US. Normally it is around one-to-one. That kind of volatility scares away long-term investors and makes it very hard for asset managers to manage risk. Regulators have stepped in with new rules, like raising margin requirements and halting new leveraged ETF listings.
EN_A: And what about hedge funds? Are they still a big risk?
EN_B: Their leverage has come down. The long-to-short ratio in one major prime brokerage book dropped from over 5.5 times to below 4 times. But the problem is not fully solved. Trading volumes in Korea have surged six to seven times over the past year, and brokers are still struggling with capacity constraints.
EN_A: What about retail investors? Are they getting margin calls?
EN_B: Surprisingly, no. Margin balances in Korea are only about 0.5% of total market cap. In the US it is 1.9%, and in China it is 2.8%. Plus, Korean retail investors still have a lot of unrealized gains and cash. So the report is not worried about a systemic blow-up from this channel.
