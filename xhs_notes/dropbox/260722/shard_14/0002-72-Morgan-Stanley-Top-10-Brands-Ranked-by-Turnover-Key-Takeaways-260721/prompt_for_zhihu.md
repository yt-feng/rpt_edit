#7 – Rolex. In 2025, we estimate that Rolex brand sales stood at CHF 11.0bn (+4%), or \~€11.7bn (+5.4%), with units sold at 1.15mn (-2%). See our Ninth Annual Swiss Watcher. The growth in value was again primarily price-driven: ASP rose \~+6% YoY to \~CHF 14,000, more than offsetting the decline in volumes (see here). This is the first time in over 20 years that Rolex volumes have declined for two consecutive years, reflecting the brand's disciplined approach to scarcity. As measured by turnover, it rose from the fifth to the fourth-largest personal luxury brand in 2025, overtaking Dior (€11.2bn), and now only behind Louis Vuitton (€20.8bn), Chanel (€16.2bn) and Hermès (€15.7bn). Given that an estimated \~92% of Rolex sales continue to be generated via third-party retailers, Rolex sales in retail value were significantly higher: \~CHF 16.1bn (\~\$20.6bn or \~€17bn), we estimate it has now exceeded Apple Watch's estimated global revenues (\~\$19bn), reinforcing Rolex's leadership across the broader global watch market. This remains an impressive feat, given that Rolex is present in only one product category (Swiss watches), while the above-mentioned brands generally have a presence in 10 or more categories. No other brand dominates the category in which it competes more than Rolex. For 2025, we estimate the brand's share of the Swiss watch market at 33% (retail value), up from 26% in 2019. This year, we estimate Rolex volumes will remain broadly flattish, with further ASP-led growth likely enough to keep the brand among the very top ranks of the global personal luxury goods industry.

#8 – Cartier. Cartier's turnover for the calendar year ending December 2025 rose an estimated +8% to €11.0bn (from €10.3bn in 2024), keeping the brand in sixth place by turnover, just behind Dior (€11.2bn) and Rolex (€11.7bn). Retail sales stood at €12.3bn and EBIT at €3.5bn, both top six among the ranked brands. Cartier's fortunes have improved noticeably post the appointment of Cyrille Vigneron as CEO in 2016 (and have continued under the leadership of CEO Louis Ferla since 2024): between 2015 and 2025 we estimate that Cartier's turnover CAGR stood at \~+10% in euro terms, making it a clear outperformer in the jewellery category. So far in 2026, we believe the brand is up >+12% YoY in euro terms, with Cartier more than likely continuing to gain share in both the jewellery and Swiss watch segments. Should its top-line momentum continue at a similar pace in 2H26 vs. 1H26, Cartier's turnover is likely to exceed that of Dior in 2026.

#9 – Gucci. Gucci's turnover fell a reported -22% to €6.0bn in 2025 (from €7.7bn in 2024) – the sharpest decline in the top 10 for a second consecutive year, reflecting a deeper and more prolonged reset than peers. The brand nonetheless held its 7th-place ranking by turnover, with retail sales of €8.6bn and EBIT of €1.0bn.

#10 – Tiffany. We estimate that Tiffany's sales reached €4.9bn in calendar 2025, down -3% versus calendar 2024 (€5.1bn). Like Richemont, LVMH does not break down sales of individual brands, so this remains our estimate. This implies that Tiffany accounted for 47% of LVMH's Watches & Jewellery division sales in 2025 (other brands include Bulgari, Chaumet, Fred, Hublot, Tag Heuer and Zenith). Post completion of its acquisition of Tiffany in January 2021, LVMH has been working hard at elevating the brand, translating to important changes in terms of product, communication and distribution. However, changes are being implemented against a difficult backdrop, and while Tiffany's sales to the middle income consumer (particularly in the United States) have been under pressure (compounded by the very steep price increases Tiffany passed on in recent years, among the steepest in the jewellery industry), the brand has not yet been able to completely offset this shortfall by selling higher priced iconic gold lines (e.g. Lock, Tiffany T, Hardware, etc.). Tiffany drops out of the top 10 brands by retail sales and EBIT in 2025, having ranked 10th by retail sales (€5.6bn) in 2024.

#11 – Prada. Among the top 10 largest personal luxury brands (by turnover), the Prada brand was down -4.8% in euro terms to €3.8bn (from €4.0bn in 2024). Retail sales stood at an impressive €5.8bn in 2025 (mostly a function of large eyewear and cosmetics licences) and EBIT at €0.8bn in 2025 (a \~21% margin), we estimate.

#12 – Van Cleef & Arpels. Van Cleef's turnover expanded to an estimated €4.8bn in 2025 (12 months to December 2025), up +10% from €4.4bn in 2024. This continues its trajectory since joining the top 10 personal luxury brand ranking for the first time in 2024, having grown from \~€750m in 2013 under former CEO Nicolas Bos. Van Cleef's EBIT reached €1.5bn in 2025 and is now ahead of Gucci's despite Gucci's larger turnover, while retail sales stood at €5.8bn.

#13 – Saint Laurent. The brand is not in our top 10 ranking by turnover in 2025 (nor was it in the ranking last year). With reported sales of €2,643m in 2025, down - 8.3%, we estimate that Saint Laurent would only be ranked 13th. However, it is interesting to note how significant the brand is when measured by retail sales (which we estimate at €9.6bn in 2025, ranked 7th as can be seen on the next page). As per L'Oréal comments in recent months, we estimate that L'Oréal generated a turnover of \~€3.1bn with Saint Laurent Beauté (and thus retail sales of approximately €6.2bn, when taking into account third-party retailers' mark-up). Thus Saint Laurent is unique among large luxury brands in that Cosmetics turnover (\~€3.1bn) exceeded Fashion & Leather Goods turnover (€2.6bn) in 2025. Chanel, Dior, Gucci, etc. F&LG turnover all significantly exceeded cosmetics turnover last year.
