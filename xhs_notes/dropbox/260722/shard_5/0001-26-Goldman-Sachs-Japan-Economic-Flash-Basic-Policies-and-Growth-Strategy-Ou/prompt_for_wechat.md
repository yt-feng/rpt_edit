【金字塔原理写作原则】
1. 结论先行：文章开头先回答“这份报告最值得看的判断是什么”，而不是介绍背景。

4. 横向 MECE：每个一级小节必须彼此独立、共同支撑主判断，避免重叠。

## Basic Policies and Growth Strategy Outline Takaichi Administration's Fiscal Shift

Japan's Cabinet has approved the 2026 Basic Policies and the Japan Growth Strategy, a key policy of the Takaichi administration.

The Basic Policies are a document spearheaded by the Council on Economic and Fiscal Policy that outlines the government's key issues, budget formulation guidelines for the next fiscal year, and a medium- to long-term roadmap for national policies. It has been published around June every year since 2001.

The 2026 edition—the first under the Takaichi administration—outlines a major policy shift in the fiscal framework, including transitioning from single-year to multi-year budgets for investment projects, and replacing the fiscal consolidation target of delivering a primary surplus with steadily reducing the debt-to-GDP ratio through economic growth.

The key points of the 2026 Basic Policies are as follows:

For medium- to long-term key investments, there will be a shift from single-year budgets, where expenditures are recorded annually, to multi-year budgets.

These policies will be funded in the initial budget, and supplementary budgets will focus on emergency measures such as disaster response and economic crises.

Regarding fiscal discipline, the Basic Policies state that the government will not mechanically pursue a single-year surplus for the primary balances of the national and local governments, but will instead aim for a stable reduction in the government debt-to-GDP ratio.

On government bond issuance, the Basic Policies state that the government will clarify a full-year government bond issuance amount while ensuring market confidence, showing a certain degree of consideration for the market.

The document maintains that the government and the BOJ will work closely together to achieve the sustainable and stable realization of the $2\%$ price stability target. However, a footnote shows consideration for the BOJ's independence by stating that the specific methods of monetary policy that are employed are left to the Bank of Japan.

A policy for reducing consumption tax will be decided by early August, with the Basic Policies not including any specific tax rate.

\- On defense spending, the document does not mention any targets for the defense budget or for the ratio of defense spending to GDP.

Securing budgets for investment projects over multiple fiscal years and formulating budgets that are not bound by single-year primary balance targets create the necessary environment for promoting the medium- to long-term public-private growth investment strategy, which is PM Takaichi's core policy. This also creates room for the government to manage security and crisis management investments flexibly, as needed. On the other hand, by incorporating investments for the Growth Strategy and expenditure items from supplementary budgets into the initial budget, we expect that initial budgets from FY2027 onward will be larger than in the past over the long term.

The goal of a stable reduction in the government debt-to-GDP ratio represents, in our view, a “convenient” fiscal target for promoting the medium- to long-term investment policies advocated by the Takaichi administration. Raising the nominal growth rate via the government’s investment strategies should allow the ratio of spending to GDP to decline even if the fiscal deficit and government debt were to increase. Indeed, based on our estimates, even under a risk scenario of fiscal expansion where the consumption tax cut currently being debated in the National Council is introduced (assuming it becomes virtually permanent after two years) and defense spending is increased from 2% to 3% of GDP, there would be a grace period of about 10 years until the mid-2030s before the government debt-to-GDP ratio starts to rise at current interest rate levels. This suggests that if the financial market's concerns about public finances are disregarded by the government, the public debt-to-GDP ratio could rise via higher long-term interest rates driven by an increase in the risk premium. Put another way, market concerns about fiscal sustainability could potentially erode that sustainability.

## Japan Growth Strategy: Assumes ¥370 tn in Public-Private Investment and Nominal GDP of ¥1,100 tn by FY2040

The main points of the Japan Growth Strategy are as follows:

Regarding public-private investment, a draft public-private investment roadmap was formulated after discussions in a working group comprised of academics, think tanks, and corporate representatives in each field. As for additional fiscal spending, a new investment framework will be established for crisis management investment and growth investment, securing and managing financial resources over multiple fiscal years. However, specific fiscal spending amounts, the allocation of public and private investments, and the details of government spending (subsidies, loan quotas, etc.) are yet to be determined.

The government estimates that in a scenario where the effects of implementing the Growth Strategy are fully realized, the potential growth rate would rise from the current c.0.5% to over 1.5% in the 2030s on the contribution from an increase in TFP.

Furthermore, in this scenario, the government estimates private capex would expand to ¥250 tn and nominal GDP to ¥1,100 tn in FY2040.

The total public and private investment of over ¥370 tn cumulatively, private capex level of ¥250 tn in FY2040, and nominal GDP level of ¥1,100 tn included in the Japan Growth Strategy are merely assumptions and estimates, not necessarily targets that must be achieved. While the government will provide institutional support and funding for investments, it should be noted that it relies heavily on private investment, and ultimately depends on the decisions of private-sector companies.

Note that a nominal GDP of ¥1,100 tn would be achievable with annual nominal GDP growth of 3.5% through FY2040, but this is premised on the government's inflation expectation of 2% and a potential growth rate of 1.5% driven by an increase in TFP.

We consider the assumption that the potential growth rate will rise significantly, driven by TFP, to be quite an ambitious outlook, considering that Japan's TFP growth has only risen very gradually up to now. As such, it is uncertain whether a nominal GDP of ¥1,100 tn can be realized. We do consider private capex of ¥250 tn in FY2040 to be achievable. To attain this target, private capex will need to continue increasing by about 4% annually going forward. Capex in FY2025 has seen a nominal increase of about 5%. Current capex is being driven by labor-saving investments and digitalization to address intensifying labor shortages. Since the labor shortage is a structural issue, we expect corporates to maintain a proactive investment stance. Under such circumstances, if government support were to further boost corporate capex, along with the contribution from rising capex spending due to inflation, the private capex amount of ¥250 tn in FY2040 would appear achievable to us.
