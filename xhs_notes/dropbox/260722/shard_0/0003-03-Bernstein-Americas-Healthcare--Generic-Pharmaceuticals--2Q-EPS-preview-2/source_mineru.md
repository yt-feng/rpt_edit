# Americas Healthcare: Generic Pharmaceuticals: 2Q EPS preview

Ahead of 2Q earnings (TEVA 7/29, AMRX 7/30, VTRS 8/6), we refresh our estimates for the latest script data and recent company commentary (representing overall incremental changes). While we expect broadly in-line reports across the group on the top- and bottom-line, we highlight the potential for an AMRX beat on stronger margins (with top-line risk also skewed to the upside), albeit positioning likely reflects this to a certain degree given the recent out-performance (+44% YTD). With reports, beyond updates on the key commercial products/dynamics, we are most focused on branded portfolio execution, pipeline progress, and capital allocation strategies.

From a broader outlook perspective, the group has continued to out-perform both the healthcare sector (+18% vs. XLV) and broader market (+12% vs. S&P 500) in recent months (Exhibit 9), driven, in our view, by both: (i) fundamentals (e.g., earnings and pipeline execution, alongside strategic and meaningful BD), as well as (ii) macro/rotational dynamics where the broadening-out (AI/momentum unwind) trade has been a tailwind for healthcare, particularly therapeutics - where generic pharma continues to screen well from a valuation perspective (we highlight VTRS; Exhibit 10). Looking ahead, while macro/fund flow dynamics will likely create volatility, we continue to expect the fundamental drivers of stock performance to be earnings growth and pipeline optionality - where we see a highly favorable set-up into a catalyst-rich 2H26+ for the group (Exhibit 8). In particular, we highlight the set-up for TEVA, where the recent pull-back in shares represents a compelling entry point.

AMRX (Buy): Building commercial momentum as we monitor additional levers
AMRX shares have outperformed YTD (+34% vs. S&P 500; +40% vs. XLV), driven, in our view, by: (i) positive commentary at and following our June conference regarding the commercial business and pipeline - including commercial momentum for the generics segment (e.g., estrogen patch demand) and branded segment (Crexont and Brekiya), key upcoming launches (e.g., lanreotide and Iohexal), and the pipeline (new men's health specialty product and update to the PFE GLP-1 partnership to be announced in 2H26), (ii) gradual appreciation for the recently announced acquisition of Kashiv Biosicences (which vertically integrates the biosimilars segment), as well as (iii) macro dynamics (i.e., broadening-out trade as the AI/momentum trade has weakened - where AMRX likely

also screens well from a valuation/growth perspective). Overall, we continue to see a compelling diversified growth outlook driven by generics segment momentum, specialty product launches/pipeline, and an emerging obesity/GLP-1 franchise via the PFE partnership.

Ahead of the quarter, we make minimal changes to our estimates, which remain broadly in-line with consensus in FY26-27, but see the potential for a bottom-line beat in 2Q on stronger margins. We look for an updated outlook on the commercial business, including key specialty product launches (Crexont and Brekiya) and the generic segment dynamics (e.g., estrogen patch demand), and FY26 guidance, and will also be focused on: (1) key generic launches (lanreotide Q3 or earlier approval on track - AMRX expects limited competition over the next 2 years; additional dosages of lohexol recently approved with expectations to be the only generic on the market for the foreseeable future); (2) PFE-partnered obesity portfolio (management has expressed further conviction post the recent ADA updates and sees the partnership as a major growth lever not included in the long-term guidance; update on the partnership structure expected over the coming months); (3) key specialty launches (expect continued momentum for Crexont and Brekiya, where management has noted confidence in the upper-end of their respective long-term revenue targets; AMRX also expects to disclose a new men's health product over the near-term), as well as (4) Kashiv Biosciences integration progress (and updates on the biosimilars pipeline) and capital allocation strategy post the deal.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

## M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
