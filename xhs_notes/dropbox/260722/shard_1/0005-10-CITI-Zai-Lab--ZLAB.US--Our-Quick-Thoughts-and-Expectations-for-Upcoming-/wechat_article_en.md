## The Standard of Care Bar Is High, but the Weakness Is Durability and Tolerability, Not Response Rate

The existing first-line SCLC benchmarks create a deceptive challenge. On the surface, a 60 to 68 percent objective response rate looks like a high hurdle for any new agent to clear. But response rate alone is not the binding constraint in this disease. The real problem is that patients frequently cannot stay on treatment long enough to realize durable benefit. The Grade 3 or higher adverse event rate of 62 to 67 percent in the IMpower133 and CASPIAN trials reflects the toxicity burden of platinum-based chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy. Many patients discontinue early, and the median progression-free survival of approximately five months reflects that treatment-limiting toxicity, not intrinsic resistance to the mechanism.

This is where Zoci’s differentiated design becomes strategically relevant. As an antibody-drug conjugate targeting DLL3, a protein highly expressed on SCLC cells but minimally on normal tissue, Zoci has the potential to deliver cytotoxic payloads with greater selectivity than systemic chemotherapy. The key question for the ESMO data is whether that selectivity translates into a lower rate of severe adverse events, particularly Grade 3 or higher toxicities, while maintaining or improving efficacy. If Zoci can reduce the severe adverse event rate by 15 to 20 percentage points relative to the 62 to 67 percent range, that would be clinically meaningful not because the absolute number is low but because it would allow more patients to remain on treatment for longer. Extended treatment duration, in turn, could drive progression-free survival into the high single digits, a meaningful improvement over the current five-month median.

The report explicitly frames the expectation for first-line progression-free survival as a high-single-digit-month improvement, not a marginal one- to two-month gain. That is the correct bar. A one-month improvement in progression-free survival would be statistically underwhelming and commercially unconvincing. A three- to four-month improvement, however, would represent a genuine advance in a disease where treatment options have been stagnant. The ESMO poster will need to show that the progression-free survival curve is separating from historical controls early and staying separated, not that the median is slightly shifted by a few weeks.
