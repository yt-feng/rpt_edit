## Foundayo's Script Trajectory Is Underperforming Relative to Analog Launches, and the Gap Is Widening, Not Closing

The gap is not merely a function of lower absolute volume. It is a function of trajectory shape. Zepbound and Wegovy Pill both exhibited accelerating growth curves in their first twelve weeks. Foundayo's growth plateaued around week eight and then began to decline. This is unusual for a GLP-1 launch in the current environment, where demand is widely believed to outstrip supply. The implication is that the constraint is not on the patient side but on the physician and channel side.

There are two plausible explanations. First, Foundayo is a new molecular entity, not a line extension. Foundayo requires physician education from scratch. Lilly has acknowledged this, noting that DTC advertising cannot begin until physicians are sufficiently educated, which pushes the full marketing push into the third quarter. Second, the oral GLP-1 category itself may require a different adoption pattern than injections, because patients and physicians need to build confidence in efficacy and tolerability without the immediate feedback loop of injection-based delivery.

The data does not yet tell us which explanation is dominant, but it does tell us that the launch is not following the expected script. Observers and strategists should be watching the NBRx trajectory closely over the next four weeks. If NBRx does not rebound as DTC advertising begins to scale, the thesis that oral GLP-1s will be a rapid market expander will need to be recalibrated.

## The Continuing Patient Ratio Is Diverging from Prior Launches, Suggesting Potential Adherence or Titration Challenges

This divergence matters for two reasons. First, it suggests that Foundayo may be experiencing lower persistence or adherence in its early patient cohort. If patients are not continuing to refill at the same rate as they did for Zepbound or Wegovy Pill, the total prescription base will grow more slowly even if new starts remain stable. Second, it raises questions about the titration experience. Foundayo requires a gradual dose escalation across multiple strengths, from 0.8mg up to 17.2mg. If patients are dropping off during titration, either due to tolerability issues or to dissatisfaction with efficacy at lower doses, the long-term revenue potential of the product may be lower than current estimates assume.

The data is still noisy. Weekly fluctuations in IQVIA capture rates, holiday disruptions, and the small absolute number of patients in the earliest weeks all contribute to variability. But the pattern is consistent enough to warrant close monitoring. If the continuing patient ratio fails to converge toward the 55-60% range that Zepbound and Wegovy Pill achieved by week twelve, the market will need to ask whether Foundayo's profile is fundamentally different from prior launches in a way that limits its peak revenue potential.

Lilly's own commentary that the launch will play out over months, not weeks, is consistent with this interpretation. But that timeline also means that the second half of 2026 revenue estimates, which assume a sharp ramp in the third and fourth quarters, carry more execution risk than the consensus currently prices in.
