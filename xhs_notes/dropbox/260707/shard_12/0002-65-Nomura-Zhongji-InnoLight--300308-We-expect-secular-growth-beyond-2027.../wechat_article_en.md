## The Upgrade Cycle from 800G to 1.6T and Beyond Is Not Linear, and That Is Exactly Why It Is Underappreciated

Most observers understand that data center bandwidth requirements are growing. Few have internalized the pace at which optical transceiver speeds must increase to keep GPU clusters fed. The report's shipment forecasts are instructive. It now expects 71.5 million units of 1.6T transceivers in FY27, up from a prior estimate of 60 million. For FY28, it projects 126 million units of 1.6T, alongside 5 million units of 2.4T and 2 million units of 3.2T. These are not extrapolations of past trends. They represent a step-function increase in the number of high-speed optical links per data center.

The key insight is that the upgrade cycle is non-linear. Moving from 800G to 1.6T does not simply double throughput. It changes the architecture of the optical layer. Higher speeds require silicon photonics integration, advanced modulation formats, and new packaging approaches. The report explicitly calls out NPO and CPO as growth drivers. These technologies move optics closer to the switch ASIC, reducing power consumption and latency. They also increase the barriers to entry. A company that masters 1.6T with silicon photonics has a clear path to 3.2T. A company that struggles with 800G will find the next generation unattainable.

This is why the report's earnings revisions are so significant. Revenue in FY27 is now forecast at CNY 261 billion, up from CNY 204 billion. Normalized EPS jumps from CNY 50.87 to CNY 66.06. These revisions are not driven by volume alone. They reflect a product mix shift toward higher-value transceivers with better margins. The report does not break out segment-level margins, but the implied gross margin trajectory suggests that 1.6T and beyond carry significantly higher profitability than earlier generations. The market may be underestimating how fast this mix shift will occur.

These unanswered questions do not invalidate the positive case. They define the range of outcomes. The report provides a base case. Observers need to stress-test it.

On margins, the key variable is product mix. The report forecasts gross margins stabilizing around 46 percent. That is high for a hardware company, but it depends on the rapid ramp of 1.6T and beyond. If the mix shift is slower, margins could compress. Observers should track quarterly revenue by product generation. A rising share of 1.6T and 2.4T is a positive signal. A rising share of 800G at lower prices is a warning.

This framework does not produce a binary answer. It forces observers to form a view on each variable and update it as new data arrives. The report's strength is that it provides a clear base case. The observer's job is to decide whether the base case is conservative, realistic, or optimistic.
