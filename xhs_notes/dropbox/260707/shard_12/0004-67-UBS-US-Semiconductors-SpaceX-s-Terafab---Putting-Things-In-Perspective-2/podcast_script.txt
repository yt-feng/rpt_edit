Think back to 2014, when Tesla couldn’t get enough batteries from suppliers, so they built the Gigafactory. This is the same logic, but for semiconductors.

EN_A: So what exactly is this Terafab going to make? How is it different from what we already have?

EN_B: The key difference is integration. It aims to put everything under one roof: mask making, logic and memory fabrication, packaging, and testing. That allows for incredibly fast design-build-test-redesign cycles. The report mentions an initial investment of 55 billion dollars in Grimes County, Texas, which could expand to 119 billion if all phases are completed.

EN_A: 55 to 119 billion dollars. That is a staggering number. How does the report justify that kind of spending?

EN_B: It uses UBS’s forecast for SpaceX’s AI business alone, which is expected to generate about 1.1 trillion dollars in capex over five years. Roughly 20 percent of that, or 225 billion, is allocated to the Terafab. Assuming the typical 60 percent of fab capex goes to equipment, that implies 135 billion in wafer fab equipment spending over five years. That is roughly the size of the entire global WFE market this year.

It could act as the knowledge owner for the Terafab, licensing process technology and manufacturing know-how, similar to the old IBM-AMD model.
