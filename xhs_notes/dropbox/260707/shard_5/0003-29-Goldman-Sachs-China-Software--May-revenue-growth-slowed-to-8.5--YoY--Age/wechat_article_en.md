# China's Software Spending Shift: The AI Reallocation Story

China's software industry revenue growth moderated to 8.5% year-on-year in May 2026, marking the second consecutive month of single-digit expansion. This is not merely a cyclical adjustment. The data signals a structural reallocation of enterprise IT budgets away from conventional software procurement and toward AI foundational models and customized industry AI projects. For observers, the headline growth figure is increasingly misleading. What matters is not how much is being spent, but where it is going.

The aggregate numbers are noteworthy at first glance. After a 12.4% year-on-year spike in May 2025, growth has halved in relative terms. The five-month cumulative figure for 2026 stands at 10.5%, down from 11.2% in the same period a year earlier. The SMB PMI, a leading indicator for software demand, dropped to 48.2 in June 2026, below the 50 expansion threshold and down from 50.1 in April. The traditional narrative — that Chinese enterprises are simply tightening budgets — is too simple. The more accurate diagnosis is that they are re-deploying capital into a different kind of technology investment.

The critical insight from the May data is not the deceleration itself, but the composition of growth. IT services continue to dominate, contributing 68% of total software industry revenue in the first five months of 2026. Within that category, the fastest-moving sub-segments are cloud computing, big data, and semi-design services. These are precisely the areas where AI deployment is most concentrated. Meanwhile, traditional software products grew at only 3% year-on-year in May, and security software at 5%. The pattern is unmistakable: enterprises are prioritizing infrastructure and platform-level AI investments over packaged applications.

This reallocation is not a temporary budget adjustment. It reflects a fundamental shift in how Chinese enterprises think about technology ROI. The report identifies two specific use cases gaining traction: AI agents that retain institutional knowledge from experienced employees, and AI-driven diversification of production sites. These are not experimental pilots. They are operational deployments aimed at addressing chronic labor and efficiency challenges. The monetization of AI agents is accelerating, and the use cases are expanding beyond content generation into core business processes.

Net margin data provides a further clue. The industry-wide net margin held at 11.9% in May, nearly flat versus April's 12.0%. That stability, in the face of revenue deceleration, suggests that companies are not slashing prices or engaging in destructive competition. They are instead focusing on higher-value AI-enabled services that command better margins. The five-month cumulative net margin actually improved to 11.5% from 11.4% in the first four months. Profit discipline is holding up better than top-line growth.

The overseas revenue ratio ticked up to 3.2% in May from 3.1% in April, a small but notable increase. Chinese software firms are beginning to export AI-enabled services, particularly to markets in Southeast Asia and the Middle East. This is early-stage, but it points to a potential second growth vector beyond the domestic market.

## The SMB PMI Decline Is a Signal, But It Is Not a Contradiction

The June SMB PMI reading of 48.2 is the weakest since early 2025 and marks the second consecutive month below 50. This is a genuine point of attention. Small and medium-sized businesses are the primary consumers of standardized software products. When they pull back, it directly impacts the software products segment, which grew only 3% year-on-year in May. The PMI volatility — swinging from 50.1 in April to 48.5 in May to 48.2 in June — suggests that confidence is cautious and that procurement decisions are being deferred.

Yet the PMI weakness does not contradict the AI reallocation thesis. Larger enterprises, which are the primary buyers of customized AI models and foundational model services, operate on longer planning cycles and are less sensitive to monthly PMI fluctuations. Their AI investments are strategic, not discretionary. The divergence between SMB sentiment and enterprise AI spending is exactly what the May data reveals: two different demand regimes operating simultaneously.

The report expects sequential quarter-on-quarter improvement in the third quarter due to better seasonality. That is a reasonable mechanical forecast, but it should not be mistaken for a recovery in traditional software demand. The seasonal lift will likely be concentrated in IT services and AI-related projects, not in the software products or security segments. The growth trajectory is bifurcated, and observers need to track the right indicators.

## IT Services Are the Transmission Belt for AI Monetization

IT services contributed 73% of total software revenue in May 2026, up from 67% in April. This is not a one-month anomaly. Over the past twelve months, IT services have consistently accounted for 66% to 73% of monthly revenue. The segment grew 9% year-on-year in May, matching the industry average, but its sheer weight means it drives the overall number. More importantly, the growth within IT services is increasingly tied to AI.

The report highlights that service outsourcing contract values rose in May, with execution value up 14% year-on-year, driven by rising AI-related projects. This is the clearest signal yet that AI is not an add-on to existing IT service contracts; it is becoming the core deliverable. Companies that can package AI model customization, integration, and maintenance into their service offerings are capturing a disproportionate share of the budget.

The implication is straightforward: the IT services segment is the transmission belt for AI monetization in China. Traditional software product companies that lack a services component are being squeezed. Their revenues are flat or declining, while their margins face pressure from cloud-based alternatives. The winners in this environment will be firms that can embed AI capabilities into service delivery, not those that rely on standalone software licenses.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Timing and Scale of Enterprise AI ROI

The report is strong on trend identification but leaves several critical questions unresolved. First, it does not provide a clear timeline for when enterprise AI investments will translate into measurable productivity gains that show up in national statistics. The current data captures spending, not output. If AI projects are taking longer to deploy than expected, the spending may continue without a corresponding revenue uplift for software vendors.

Second, the report does not disaggregate the AI spending within IT services between one-time implementation fees and recurring service revenue. The distinction matters for evaluation. Recurring revenue supports higher multiples; one-time projects do not. If a significant portion of current AI spending is project-based, the revenue growth could be lumpy and may not sustain the current margin profile.

Third, the competitive dynamics within the AI services market are not addressed. As more IT service providers pivot to AI, pricing pressure could emerge. The current net margin stability may reflect a temporary period of scarcity pricing for AI talent and capabilities. As supply increases, margins could compress.

These are not criticisms of the report. They are the natural open questions that any serious observer should ask. The report provides the raw material for analysis; the synthesis requires judgment about timing, competitive intensity, and the shape of the adoption curve.

## A Framework for Observing the AI Reallocation

Based on the data and trends in this report, observers should evaluate Chinese software companies against three criteria.

First, assess revenue composition. Companies with more than 40% of revenue from IT services, particularly those with disclosed AI-related project backlogs, are better positioned than those reliant on software product licenses. The IT services share of total industry revenue has been stable or rising for over a year; this is where the growth is flowing.

Second, evaluate margin trajectory relative to revenue growth. The industry net margin has been remarkably stable, but individual company margins will diverge. Firms that can scale AI service delivery without proportional headcount increases will see margin expansion. Those that must hire expensive AI talent for each project will see margin compression. The key metric is revenue per employee in the AI services unit.

Third, monitor SMB PMI as a contrarian signal. A sustained PMI recovery above 50 would likely lift the software products segment and benefit companies that have been overlooked in the AI narrative. Conversely, continued PMI weakness reinforces the case for focusing on enterprise AI services. The PMI is not a direct indicator of AI demand, but it is a useful proxy for the health of the broader software ecosystem.

The report also identifies specific segments that outperformed in May: semi-design, IT services, and cloud computing and big data. These are the areas where AI investment is most concentrated. Observers should consider exposure to these sub-sectors and review traditional software products and embedded systems, which grew only 3% and declined 14% month-on-month respectively.

The May 2026 software industry data confirms that China's enterprise technology spending is undergoing a structural transformation.
