## Key Takeaways

VLCC TCE outlook: Since the US and Iran signed a MoU on Jun 17 $^{th}$ , VLCC TCE has rallied but peaked out on June 22 $^{nd}$ , which mgmt attributes to weak China restocking demand, while mgmt still expect the industry average VLCC TCE in 2H26 to be better than 1H26, as Gulf countries are ramping up oil shipment through the Strait of Hormuz. Mgmt forecast the avg industry VLCC TCE to reach US\$150k/day in 3Q26 considering the overall industry supply-demand has improved vs. Apr-May, before factoring in additional demand from China restocking/potential removal of sanctions on Iranian oil, and improved from US\$120k/100k per day in 2Q26/1Q26.

■ Restocking demand: Mgmt believe the market is underestimating upside risks to VLCC TCE from rigid restocking demand. As currently the restocking demand is only from non-China countries, mgmt believe China may gradually resume refined fuel export quota, and expect SOE refiners to do restocking in

July-August. That said, in the US, crude oil SPR has reduced by 89mn bbls to 326mn bbls as of Jun $26^{\text{th}}$ , 2026 vs. Feb'26-end and the US gov targets to replenish SPR with at least 1.2 barrels by end of 2027 for each released, which mgmt expect to be a tailwind to TCE.

Scenario for removal of sanctions on Iranian oil: Mgmt noted that a potential permanent removal of Iranian oil sanctions could act as a major structural upside catalyst for the compliant tanker market. Though a 60-day waiver for sanctions over Iranian oil is currently in place, mgmt observed that compliant fleets remain hesitant to participate in the Iranian oil trade, leaving the trade to shadow fleets. If sanctions on Iran oil are fully lifted, mgmt believe shifting Iranian oil seaborne volume to the compliant market could generate a 5pp in effective demand, which is in-line with our view (see Mar-26 report).

VLCC fleet development: Mgmt expect supply shortage for VLCC to persist until at least 2028. Bulker outlook: The dry bulk market is performing stronger-than-expected per mgmt heading into its peak 3Q-4Q, supporting freight rates above break-even. In major bulk, demand is being driven by 1) better-than-expected China demand for Iron Ore; 2) active iron ore shipments from Australia and Brazil; 3) urging bauxite exports from West Africa. Additionally, minor bulk shipments—including grain, steel, and fertilizers—continue to outperform expectations per mgmt, keeping the small-vessel market highly active.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
