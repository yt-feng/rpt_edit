## Indonesia Consumer

Navigating the sector through tough times

Consumer names under our coverage have outperformed the JCI by +1% to +62% YTD despite multiple headwinds. That said, absolute returns remain poor: only MAPI is up +30% YTD, while the rest are down -31% to +2% YTD. We acknowledge that investor appetite for Indonesian consumer exposure has been low this year, but we see a selective opportunity to accumulate quality names for longer-term holdings. Sector valuations have de-rated inline with market valuation (30% YTD) despite minimal earnings cuts for companies under our coverage. We believe the consumer sector will be one of the beneficiaries of a broader market rally and would urge investors to position selectively.

\- Top picks are Indofood Group (ICBP, INDF) and MAPA (sports retail). We downgrade AMRT and MAPI to Neutral, and maintain UW on UNVR. Our positive view on ICBP & INDF is underpinned by (1) an undemanding valuation for the world's largest instant noodle manufacturer, (2) cost headwinds (CPO, packaging) that should begin to abate, and (3) a likely moderation in negative earnings revisions. For MAPA, we see resilient sports demand (14% revenue growth CAGR in FY25–27E) alongside an undemanding valuation (\~10x P/E for \~13% EPS CAGR).

\- Headwinds are easing. We think the market has largely priced in (1) higher input costs (packaging and USD strength) and (2) a weak demand backdrop. We are selective in Indonesia's consumer space, preferring high-end retailers followed by select staples. Our selectivity stems from a continued K-shaped recovery that impacts the growth outlook for consumer staple companies. We believe the sector remains challenged on three key fronts: 1) rising competitive intensity that is compressing pricing power; 2) weak consumer purchasing power driving downtrading, resulting in 3) negative pricing impact at a time when input costs have increased and, ultimately, 4) subdued earnings delivery. These factors have led to a broad-based sector de-rating over the last 2 years and we are adjusting our valuation mental framework to accommodate for these factors.

Investing in Indonesia's consumer companies has historically promised double-digit topline growth, driven by strong pricing power and volume growth which is underpinned by a growing population and better distribution penetration. The situation has significantly changed as 1) inflation has remained subdued at $\sim 3\%$ in the last 5 years and 2) we have more competition coming in, especially from China (see our last year's assessment note). The resulting impact is not trivial. Staple companies under our coverage have undershot growth expectations and their valuation has significantly compressed (see Figure 5 for P/E multiples). Consumer discretionary companies that we cover have continued to enjoy growth. The dispersion in growth is largely attributable to a K-shaped recovery in Indonesia, and also Indonesia's strong affinity for foreign branded products. Having said that, multiple compression has also followed discretionary companies despite strong earnings delivery. Size, liquidity and also concerns on currency continue to negatively weigh the thesis for investing in MAPI.

How do we proceed from here? Three key developments have reshaped our framework over the past few months. Increases in commodity prices (CPO, wheat) alongside oil-derivative costs are likely to weigh on margins across our FMCG coverage in 2Q and 3Q26 results. Packaging costs in particular - which account for 5–15% of COGS for FMCG companies - could hurt HPC companies with higher packaging needs such as UNVR - though companies with pricing power are well-positioned to absorb these. Second, ownership dynamics have moved against us. Foreign ownership across our coverage has trended lower over the past 12-18 months (see Figure 4), following a broad-based selloff in the Indonesian equity market. Some companies under our coverage have also been excluded from MSCI's EM standard cap index in the last 1-year, with AMRT removed from the standard cap index in May -26 and similarly ICBP in Nov-25. There are currently no consumer companies represented in the MSCI EM index for Indonesia. The removal means that investors benchmarked to MSCI EM will be investing off-index for owning companies under our coverage. This off-index positioning, while a near-term headwind, sets up a powerful re-rating catalyst should flows return.

Local institutions are unable to entirely absorb foreign selling. Local institutions (mutual and pension funds) have begun selectively rotating back into consumer (see Figure 10). Local institutional ownership (% of free-float) has risen in our preferred names - ICBP (+3.1% y/y, +1.7% YTD) and MAPA (+2.8% y/y, +2.9% YTD) - and the YTD moves show this is still playing out. The buying has been selective - local ownership has fallen/ remained flat in the names where the outlook has weakened, namely MAPI (-17.7% y/y, -16.1% YTD) and AMRT (+0.7% y/y, +1.0% YTD), in line with our Neutral ratings on both. Two names run the other way - 1) INDF has seen local ownership drift lower (-3.7% y/y); and 2) UNVR has drawn local buying (+4.9% y/y, +3.3% YTD) off a low base, but we would not chase this given limited fundamental support. The bigger picture is constructive. With consumer names now out of the MSCI EM index, the marginal buyers have shifted from both active and passive foreign investors who are usually more sticky to local investors who are more myopic in nature.

Valuation is the silver lining, if there is any. Indonesian consumer companies are never yield plays. However, valuations have fallen to such an extreme that yields have become an integral component of the conversation. We remain selective on our picks in the space, preferring MAPA, INDF and ICBP. Our OWs screen does well on two filters: 1) the ability to pass on input cost inflation through price hikes and 2) earnings resilience that is not contingent on a broader consumer recovery. That said, a massive value unlocking will need foreign investors coming back to invest in the consumer space, in our view. To do that, we need better currency stability, a more sanguine outlook on growth and ultimately clear and consistent policy making. Until then, we believe there will be a cap on valuations and stocks could remain cheap for a long time. This is also why we downgrade AMRT to Neutral and also keep UNVR on Underweight. AMRT still trades at a lofty 14x 1-yr forward P/E multiple at a time when the entire sector is trading at single-digit multiples. Its growth outlook has also deteriorated with the company guiding down store expansion and margins looking tough with rising competition. Valuations relative to history look compelling, but we believe the precedent for historical valuations has no place today. We also downgrade MAPI to Neutral as the stock has outperformed the broader index by +62% YTD, driven by a mandatory tender offer at IDR1,550/sh.
