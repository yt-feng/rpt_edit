## Key Takeaways

\- Shipping rate: 2Q26 avg SCFI (spot rate) on US West Coast/East Coast routes rose by +19%/+6% YoY and Europe routes rallied by +45% YoY, and mgmt attributed this rally to front-loading ahead of potential higher tariffs and passing-through of higher fuel prices. Meanwhile, they also expect the earlier-than-usual peak season to end by mid-3Q26 and anticipate that 4Q26 rates will soften. On the US routes LT contracts finalized in Apr'26, mgmt noted West Coast/East Coast route rates are at c.US\$1.8-1.9k per FEU and US\$2.8-2.9k per FEU respectively, down c.US\$200 YoY due to oversupply concerns. On the EU routes LT contracts negotiated in late 2025, rates were down c.US\$200-300 YoY to US\$1.5-2.1k per FEU due to lower spot rates back then (see Jun-26 report).

\- Fleet expansion: OOIL is embarking on a major fleet expansion to remain in the first tier alongside COSCO Shipping Holding (1919.HK/601919.SS, Sell). OOIL's individual operated capacity is targeted to reach 2mn TEU by 2030, up from 1.1mn TEU as of March 2026. To support this, the company has US\$5.4bn remaining capex for 2026-2030, with further c.30 vessels to be delivered and

c.10 more to be chartered-in. The company's orderbook is shifting towards small-to-medium vessels (such as 16K TEU ships for Latin America, Africa, and other emerging markets). On the shipyards, OOIL placed these new ship orders with tier-1 Chinese shipyards due to cheaper prices vs. Korean shipbuilders and reliable delivery records, though mgmt mentioned it's hard to find slots delivered in 2028 among tier-1 shipyards.

Industry capacity outlook: When being asked about oversupply concerns, mgmt highlighted key factors mitigating this supply growth, including: 1) structural shifts toward de-globalization. Mgmt noted that more fragmented global trade routes are reducing overall shipping efficiency, which they believe is hard to reverse; 2) potential early retirement of aging vessels, where the avg age of scrapped container vessels is now more than 30 yrs (vs. past 2 decades avg of mid-20s and a low point of 18 yrs). Mgmt also noted that the possibility of returning to the Red Sea remains low due to safety concerns and geopolitical uncertainties, and they do not expect OOIL will be among the first to return.

■ Capital mgmt: The company has net cash on hand, which can fund new ships capex. Mgmt reiterated its dividend policy of a 30%-50% payout ratio for 2024-2026, having paid at the upper end in 2024-25. Share buyback is not being considered due to the low free float of shares (14%-15%).

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
