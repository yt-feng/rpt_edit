We also find that home price growth has been weaker in states with a greater immigration slowdown (bottom panel), suggesting a slightly greater hit to demand than supply. However, the relationship with home prices has only borderline statistical significance, and we did not find a meaningful relationship between slowdowns in immigration and changes in vacancy rates.

## Multifamily Homebuilding: Still Slowing

Multifamily construction is likely to fall further, albeit at a slower pace. Exhibit 9 shows that the backlog of multifamily units under construction has already declined $33\%$ after peaking $63\%$ above 2019 levels, as the pace of completions has remained elevated after jumping to multi-decade highs in 2024-2025 while the pipeline for new projects narrowed.

With a backlog that remains 9% greater than in 2019 and poor absorption rates pushing up the rental vacancy rate from a trough of 5.6% to 7.3%, we expect multifamily developers to continue to focus on clearing that large backlog before breaking new ground, albeit less uniformly than over the last couple of years because the backlog has shrunk. We forecast that multifamily starts will total 0.43mn in 2026 vs. 0.41mn in 2025 and 0.36mn in 2024. Combined with our expectation for single-family starts, we expect total housing starts to move roughly sideways year-over-year, totaling 1.35mn in 2026 vs. 1.36mn in 2025.

After growing sharply in 2021 and 2022, we estimate the gap is now $2 - 3\%$ narrower than it was pre-pandemic, as the slower pace of new-lease rent growth over the last few years has allowed continuing-lease rents to more than fully catch up.

Official shelter inflation has already slowed from a peak monthly rate of 0.76% in 2022 to 0.23% in the first quarter of this year (the April and May readings appear to have been boosted by separate distortions). We expect monthly shelter inflation to slow to an average of 0.19% over the rest of this year and 0.18% next year. Our forecasts imply that the year-on-year rate of PCE housing inflation will fall from 3.2% today to 2.9% by December 2026 and 2.2% by December 2027, which would mark the slowest pace since 2013.

As alluded to above, the official measures of shelter inflation capture rents for both new and continuing leases.
