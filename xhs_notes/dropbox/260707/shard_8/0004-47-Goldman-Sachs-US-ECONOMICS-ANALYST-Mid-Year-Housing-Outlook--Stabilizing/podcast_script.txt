ZH_A: 最近看到了某外资投行发布了一份关于美国房地产市场的年中展望报告，标题很有意思，叫“稳定但低迷”。这到底是什么意思？

ZH_B: 简单来说，就是房地产市场正在触底，但短期之内别指望它会强劲反弹。报告的核心判断是，高利率的锁定效应和需求疲软，会让市场在未来一到两年内都处于一种不温不火的状态。

ZH_A: 锁定效应？我经常听到这个词，但具体是指什么？

ZH_B: 就是字面意思。报告里提到，现在有将近80%的房贷借款人的利率，都低于当前的市场利率。其中有60%的人，他们锁定的利率比现在低了整整两个百分点。这意味着他们如果搬家，就要放弃这个超低利率，去背一个高得多的新贷款。这个财务成本太高了，所以大家都不愿意动。

ZH_A: 所以这直接导致了房屋成交量很低？

ZH_B: 没错。报告预测，今年下半年的二手房年化销售量只有420万套，虽然比上半年略高一点，但比2019年的水平低了22%。这就意味着市场上的交易流动性很差。

ZH_A: 那新房市场呢？开发商那边情况怎么样？

ZH_B: 新房市场稍微好一点，但也在放缓。报告里说，尽管房贷利率比2019年高了3个百分点，但单户住宅的开工量仍然比2019年高了4%。这听起来很矛盾，对吧？

ZH_A: 对，这很不寻常。为什么新房市场能扛住这么高的利率？

ZH_B: 核心原因还是房屋短缺。过去几年供应一直跟不上需求，所以开发商有底气继续建房。但报告也指出，这种支撑力正在减弱。因为供需缺口在慢慢收窄，开发商的利润率也在回归到疫情前的水平。所以，单户住宅的开工量预计今年会比去年下降2%。

ZH_A: 那租房市场呢？多户住宅的建设情况如何？

ZH_B: 多户住宅的情况更严峻。报告说，之前积压的在建项目已经清理了33%，但相比2019年仍然高出9%。因为新租约的租金增长非常慢，过去一年只有1%到2%，所以开发商现在的主要任务是消化库存，而不是开新项目。

ZH_A: 这意味着租金通胀会进一步降温？

ZH_B: 是的。报告预测，官方的PCE住房通胀率会从现在的3.2%，到2027年底降到2.2%。这会是2013年以来的最低水平。这对于美联储控制通胀来说，是个好消息。

ZH_A: 听起来，房价似乎也不会怎么涨了？

ZH_B: 报告预测今年全国房价只会上涨0.8%，明年上涨2.3%。这背后一个关键因素是，房主空置率在上升，从今年一季度的1.1%预计会升到明年底的1.2%。需求比供应弱，价格自然就涨不动了。

ZH_A: 报告里还提到了移民减少的影响，这部分很有意思。移民减少到底是打击了需求还是供应？

ZH_B: 这是一个很有意思的问题。报告专门做了分析，发现移民减少更多的州，房屋销售和建设都更弱。但有趣的是，它对房价的负面影响只是刚刚达到统计显著性。这说明移民减少对需求的打击，可能略大于对供应的打击。

ZH_A: 那报告有没有完全解释清楚，为什么移民减少对供应的影响没有想象中大？

ZH_B: 这个就是报告留下悬念的地方了。它提到了两个可能的原因，比如建筑行业的劳动力短缺已经缓解，但并没有给出一个决定性的结论。如果你想深入了解这个逻辑，以及它对不同州的具体影响，完整报告里还有更多的数据图表和模型分析。

ZH_A: 好的，那我们最后总结一下。这份报告的核心信息是什么？

ZH_B: 一句话总结：房地产市场正在从一个急剧下滑的阶段，进入一个漫长的低位盘整期。交易量低、房价涨幅极慢、租金通胀持续回落。对于投资者来说，这意味着短期内没有大的机会，但需要密切关注利率变化和移民政策这两个变量。

EN_A: So there’s a new mid-year housing outlook report from a global investment bank, and the title is "Stabilizing but Subdued." What does that actually mean for the US housing market?

EN_B: It means the market is bottoming out, but don’t expect any kind of strong rebound anytime soon. The core takeaway is that high mortgage rates are locking people in place, and demand is just too weak to push prices or activity much higher for the next year or two.

EN_A: That "lock-in" effect keeps coming up. Can you explain exactly how it works?

EN_B: Sure. The report shows that almost 80 percent of mortgage borrowers have a rate below the current market rate. And nearly 60 percent have a rate that is at least two full percentage points lower. So if you want to move, you have to give up that cheap mortgage and take out a new one at a much higher rate. That’s a huge financial penalty, so people just stay put.

EN_A: That directly hurts home sales, right?

EN_B: Exactly. The report forecasts existing home sales to run at an annualized pace of just 4.2 million in the second half of this year. That’s only 3 percent above the first half, and still 22 percent below where we were in 2019. So the turnover in the market is really low.

EN_A: What about new homes? Are builders feeling the same pain?

EN_B: Builders are in a slightly better position, but they are definitely slowing down. The report points out that single-family starts are still 4 percent above 2019 levels, even though mortgage rates are 3 percentage points higher. That sounds weird, right?

EN_A: It does. Why are they still building?

EN_B: The main reason is the long-standing housing shortage. There just aren’t enough homes, so builders have had pricing power. But that support is fading. The supply-demand balance is improving, and builder margins are back to pre-pandemic levels. So the report expects single-family starts to fall 2 percent this year compared to last year.

EN_A: And what about the rental market? Multifamily construction?

EN_B: That’s where the slowdown is more dramatic. The backlog of apartments under construction has dropped by 33 percent, but it’s still 9 percent above 2019 levels. And new lease rent growth is very weak, only 1 to 2 percent over the past year. So developers are focused on finishing what they’ve already started, not breaking ground on new projects.

EN_A: So does that mean rent inflation is going to keep cooling?

EN_B: Yes. The report predicts that PCE shelter inflation will fall from 3.2 percent now to just 2.2 percent by the end of 2027. That would be the slowest pace since 2013. For the Fed, that’s a welcome sign that housing inflation is no longer a big problem.

EN_A: And what about home prices? Are they going to drop?

EN_B: Not really drop, but barely rise. The forecast is for national home prices to increase only 0.8 percent this year and 2.3 percent next year. The key driver is that the homeowner vacancy rate is rising, from 1.1 percent to an expected 1.3 percent by 2027. With supply edging up and demand staying tepid, prices just don’t have much upward momentum.

EN_B: That’s a really interesting part. The report found that states with a bigger slowdown in unauthorized immigration saw weaker home sales and less homebuilding. But the impact on home prices was only borderline significant. That suggests the hit to demand might have been slightly larger than the hit to supply.

EN_A: So the report doesn’t fully explain why the supply impact wasn’t bigger?

EN_B: That’s one of the open questions. It offers a couple of possible reasons, like the fact that the construction labor shortage has eased. But it doesn’t give a definitive answer. If you want to see the full data and the state-by-state analysis, you really need to read the complete report.
