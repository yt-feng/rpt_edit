## HQ Mgmt Meeting & G.O.L.D. Store Takes; Overweight Establishing \$170 Price Target

We hosted a meeting and store tour at DLTR's Chesapeake VA headquarters with CEO Creedon, CFO Glendinning, CRO Konrad, and SVP of Investor Relations & Treasurer Delrosario and thought value-add to share our key takeaways and multi-year model implications.

I. Model Implications: Near-term, we raise 2Q EPS to \$1.19 (above the Street at \$1.09 / \$1.00-\$1.15 guide) based on +3.7% comps (> Consensus at +3.0%). We raise our FY26 EPS to \$7.18 (above Consensus at \$6.97 / \$6.70-\$7.10 guide), raise FY27 EPS to \$8.24 (> Street at \$7.67), and introduce a FY28 EPS estimate of \$9.29 (10% above Consensus \$8.46) with our model reflecting flow-through of mgmt's 6/25 \$500M share repurchase announcement.

Remain Overweight establishing a Dec '27 price target of \$170 based on 18x our FY28 EPS with incremental potential upside to our model across every line item. Specifically, we see (i) upside to 2Q & 2H26 same-store-sales (>3-3.5% Consensus), (ii) upside to 2Q/2H26 & FY27/28 Consensus gross margin, (iii) FY27/FY28 SG&A efficiency opportunity, and (iv) Capital allocation opportunity (\$4B+ incremental opportunity).

\- On Risk/Reward: Bottom-up built, our FY27/FY28 model points to \~11% annual net income growth and a +14% EPS growth profile. Translated to equity value - peer regression for a low-to-mid-teens EBITDA margin and mid-single-digit top-line profile points to an 18x multiple and \$170 equity value with linear regression (relative to DLTR's pre-pandemic 2017-19 top-line/ margin profile) translating to a 20x multiple and \$182 equity value.

\- Applying our peer regression (18x) and linear regression (19.6x) multiples to fully loaded FY28 EPS power of \$12+ (\$8.46 Consensus) points to an equity value upside range of \$220 to \$235.

## Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation

## Investment Thesis

We rate DLTR Overweight. Multi-year, we see DLTR returning to a double-digit EPS “compounder” with top- and bottom-line drivers in place at the core DT banner (MPP 3.0 format store expansion) and idiosyncratic bottom-line drivers through tariff mitigation strategies, MPP expansion, 1x cost recapture, and corporate expense leverage following the sale of the FDO business (with capital allocation representing an incremental catalyst).

We are establishing a Dec '27 price target of \$170 (vs. \$160 Dec '26 prior) based on 18x our FY28E EPS (= peer regression analysis).

... w/ Accelerating New Store Comp Waterfall Into FY27: Representing an incremental comp driver moving forward, our math points to an accelerating new store waterfall contribution to same-store-sales growth in FY27 of +125bps (vs. +100bps contribution the trailing 3 years on our estimates), which historically has been cited as a 4-year maturity curve with new stores outperforming vs. chain average in their first 3 years of maturity. Recall, DLTR in 2022 (in the midst of the FDO turnaround) had slowed the pace of its new store openings to less than 150 doors and \~350 openings in 2023, which has represented a constraint to the new store waterfall contribution (vs. DLTR sustaining a pace of at least 400 new openings in each year from 2024 onwards). Representing incremental upside to the potential new store waterfall lift – we note: (i) 3.0 MPP within new stores going forward (with the entire existing fleet also converted to 3.0 by 2026-end) and (ii) improving store standards with “massive learnings” from the customer over the past 12 months and continued progress on CRO Konrad’s G.O.L.D. standard.

Take #2: 2H June \$1 Price Point Reintroduction = Forward “Optionality”. On the reintroduction of select \$1 items in the store (launched on 6/12 as flagged in our 6/26 Boss’ Eve), CEO Creedon cited the initiative to date checks both the Value and Discovery buckets of the company’s 3 box merchandising grid (Value, Convenience, Discovery) specifically noting “if we like it, we can keep it”. Specifically, on progression, management noted the reintroduction of \$1 items tied to the company’s 40th Anniversary was cited as a small run today, with greater opportunity into the back half of the year with Back-to-school & Halloween in 3Q, followed by Thanksgiving & Christmas in 4Q. Further, CEO Creedon cited “a number of Oars in the water today” across (i) 40 $^{th}$ Anniversary price reinvestments tied to tariff refunds, with \$1 WOW! tables at the front of the store (i.e. seasonal dinnerware, glowsticks, summer toys), and (ii) seasonal endcaps noting size/scale advantages with vendor relationships translating to a “monthly new muscle” (i.e. Gatorade now \$1.75 vs. \$2.50 prior, Energizer at \$5.00 vs. \$8.50 prior, Hot Wheels at \$1.25 vs. Toy Category at \$1.75 opening price). Importantly, CEO Creedon cited learnings as most important noting the company will have a very good read on traffic and unit velocity by year-end. To that point, management noted DLTR’s back-end buying model allows the company to “architect” product to the same exact margin profile at the \$1 price point provided effective lead times. Tying the \$1 re-introduction back to the model, our work points to the potential for exit rate traffic to inflect to positive growth in 2Q26 (>2H expectations), with 3Q26 on tap to receive a “double” traffic catalyst on easier comparisons YOY (lapping trough sentiment tied to red sticking) and the WOW! Price reinvestment. Specifically, management noted continued improvement in price sentiment throughout 1Q with 80% of the assortment SKUs unique to Dollar Tree (= only 10% LFL & 10% similar to competitors) having exited 2025 with a much sharper price proposition (w/ JPM pricing analysis pointing to low-to-mid-single-digit price delta in consumables and mid-teens price delta in discretionary relative to mass).

Take #3: Structural Foundation Built For Multi-Price-Point Inflection: Looking ahead, management cited MPP 3.0 conversions across the fleet remain on-track to be completed by the end of FY26 (\~2,000 to be completed this year), and noted MPP stands at only 16% of the assortment today. Recall, 3.0 MPP conversions were first introduced in 1Q24 to include “in-line” configurations of MPP product placed within relevant aisles throughout the entire store (vs. a dedicated DT Plus section/aisle prior) creating a more cohesive and convenient shopping experience. Incremental to the current 3.0 doors today - management noted color-coding shelving strips to be rolled out across the store base by the end of July, coinciding with MPP units starting to move in-line with respective categories (i.e. detergent sitting on the bottom shelf with specific yellow strip coding signaling the pricing range). Overall, CEO Creedon cited the multi-price-point strategy as in “super early innings today” with clear runway referencing DOL in Canada at \~85% MPP (and previously citing Action in Europe at \~60% MPP). Representing the next leg of MPP - mgmt cited a strong foundation in place today after (i) 2.5 years of 3.0 MPP “learnings” taken to the store setup and curation of assortment driving sequential improvement in execution throughout each event (vs. the initial in-line configuration introduction in 1Q24), and (ii) merchants having a “larger field of play” (relative to 2 years ago only chasing available domestic product), importantly with 2+ year lead times in certain categories. On categories, our work points to opportunity across Hardware/Auto, Health & Beauty (w/ “weekly newness” & “untapped” social media/influencer activation in the category), and branded product noting size/scale advantages with its vendor base (i.e. DLTR introducing \$10 Tide Detergent representing a new high-price point).

Take #4: Multi-Year Gross Margin Runway w/ Expanding Drivers Into FY27 ... A key takeaway from our meeting and model build is an “under-promise” approach to multi-year gross margin relative to “up modestly” (\~10bps by our work) annual expansion from FY26-F28 as laid out at the October 2025 analyst day. To this point, management cited an “aggressive” approach to driving more volume through the box with the company’s back-end buying model sufficient to hold multi-year gross margins flat to FY26 levels alone. Two Accelerating GPM Drivers By Our Work: (1) Supply Chain efficiency: targeting \~800 stores through a single DC relative to \~600 prior (w/ 2029 timeline by our work), representing a more than 20% improvement in DC productivity by our math. In addition, mgmt previously cited at the 10/15 Investor Day replacing legacy tech systems with AI-enabled platforms to “flow product more reliably to stores, reduce out of stocks and improve our cost structure at scale” & higher capacity/productivity within existing facilities to increase the DC to Store ratio “with only limited capital investment”. (2) MPP Scale: CEO Creedon cited scale opportunity with multi-price point volumes tied to appropriate lead-times and knowledge today of customer reception per category relative to the initial year 1 rollout in 2019, which can lead to greater leverage with vendors as DLTR looks to scale volumes of MPP product. In addition, recall, management cited at the 10/15 Investor Day MPP items deliver higher margin dollars per item per basket, driving increasing fixed cost leverage within the box (w/ the Halloween ’25 assortment as an example drove +25% higher margin dollars despite selling 10% fewer units). (3) Shrink: Despite initial progress on shrink seen within 1Q26, mgmt cited continued opportunity remaining with Shrink, noting roughly 60% of product in the box is non-branded “private label-esque” carrying very little “Street value” in a resale scenario and our estimates pointing to +80bps of Shrink recapture opportunity relative to 2019 within gross margin. Translating this to the P/L - we raise our FY27/FY28 model to +10bps annual gross margin expansion (= October ‘25 algorithm) with >100bps of multi-year opportunity by our math (= upside to our model) associated with supply chain efficiency, multi-price-point scale, and shrink recapture representing incremental potential upside.

\- ... w/ 3 Areas of Potential Upside Cited Relative to the FY26 Flat “Baseline”: As a reminder, management’s 5/28 outlook embedded a “roughly flat” gross margin “baseline” outlook for FY26. Importantly, CFO Glendinning cited three areas of potential upside to FY26’s margin outlook: (1) Freight favorability specifically citing “fuel in a much better place” than at the time of the call with Oil prices now \~21% lower since the 5/28 EPS call (w/ mgmt embedding favorable tariff rates across 2Q/3Q fully offsetting incremental freight headwinds, by our math, pointing to \~50bps of freight upside by our model across 2H). (2) potential gross tariffs upside, if current

Section 122 rates extend past July (w/ mgmt embedded tariff tailwinds across 2Q/3Q before a reversion back to IEEPA rates in 4Q, pointing to \~50bps of incremental upside in 4Q). (3) tariff refunds, assuming reinvestments into the business (w/ \$110M received through 5/26 or \~\$0.40 EPS tailwind on 100% flow-through, and our gross profit dollar math pointing to mid-single-digit comp upside opportunity across 2Q-4Q. Put together, we see +40bps of potential gross margin upside (= freight + gross tariffs) relative to management's Flat guide for FY26 (2H-weighted opportunities), translating to \$0.30 of EPS upside relative to our published model.

Take #5: SG&A Efficiency Opportunity Into FY27 Improves Flow-Through: Regarding SG&A expenses, management cited labor as the largest contribution to expenses at \~60% of overall SG&A, with wage inflation favorably moderating already in FY26 (+3-3.5% forecasted by economists for 2026 < +MSD% increases experienced in the trailing 2 years) with mgmt noting a multi-year idiosyncratic opportunity to drive greater efficiency on labor from improved workforce management (potential low to mid single digit savings opportunity by our fieldwork). To this point (& not embedded in our model)—full flow-through of a 3.5% reduction (midpoint of low to mid-single-digits) in Store Labor expenses within SG&A would represent an incremental \~\$0.50 of EPS power vs. our current model reflecting 10bps of SG&A leverage in each of FY27/28E on our 3.5% comp. Representing a key inflection point moving into FY27, we note mgmt noted the workforce management system rollout underway and expected to go live by FY26-end. Further, mgmt noted pressure across both general liabilities and utilities having increased higher than inflation over the trailing 12 months, albeit noting “specific actions” now in-place today (similar to action plans around managing shrink) for general liabilities and energy management systems in place for utilities (= 30bps recapture opportunity on General Liabilities by our math, or \$0.25 incremental EPS opportunity, not in our model). Put together, assuming core wage inflation of \~3.35% (as forecasted by JPM economists in FY27/28), in addition to workforce management savings flow-through and general liability recapture, our math points to ability for 50bps of annual SG&A leverage at a 3.5% comp on full flow-through (= \$0.75 EPS power incremental upside to our model).

Take #6: Digging Deeper On Capital Priorities: On capital allocation, management noted a dividend as something the “board will consider” over time, though citing preference for share repurchases as “the best return” for shareholders at the moment. To that point, mgmt’s +16-23% EPS growth YOY outlook in FY26 excludes any additional share repurchases (outside of the \$98M shares that were repurchased after 1Q-end) with the 6/25 announcement of an incremental \$500M of repurchases translating to an implied EPS growth outlook of +19-26% YOY, by our math. Additionally, DLTR announced on 6/2 the board authorized a replenishment of the \$2.5B share repurchase authorization, with \~\$700M remaining following the \$500M repurchase announced on 6/25 (= \~\$1.8B replenishment). Tied to the release, CEO Creedon cited “The replenishment of our share repurchase authorization reinforces our commitment to disciplined capital allocation and reflects our confidence in Dollar Tree’s long-term growth”. Looking ahead, management’s FY26-28 algorithm by our work translates to \~\$2.4B in annual operating cash flow generation, offset by \~\$1B in annual capital expenditures, translating to \~\$1.3B of annual free cash flow generation or \$4B+ of FY26-28 buyback capacity (= +MSD% TSR accretion opportunity w/ DLTR’s leverage ratio at \~2x, below mgmt’s <2.5x target).
