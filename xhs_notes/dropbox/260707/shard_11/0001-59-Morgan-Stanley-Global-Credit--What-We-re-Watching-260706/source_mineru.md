## Global Credit: What We're Watching

A small favor: The Extel Global Fixed Income Survey is now open. If you have enjoyed our research, we'd appreciate your support in the Investment Grade, High Yield Strategy. and Credit Derivatives Strategy categories across US/EU/Asia. Thank you, and please reach out with any questions.

Key Recommendations: This week we outline our key recommendations across credit. In the US, we expect a gradual steepening of the 10s30s curve, driven by a modest decline in front-end yields. In Europe, we favor a barbell of sub-5y and long-end IG bonds. With worsening fiscal trends and rising election risk premiums on the horizon, we recommend buying protection on French banks versus selling iTraxx Senior Financials to hedge against further widening in OATs.

US Investment Grade: US IG spreads tightened 2bp last week, partially retracing the prior two weeks' widening and delivering excess returns of 0.1%. Spread curves bull-flattened and BBBs outperformed. Across sectors, transport, media, and utilities delivered the strongest excess returns, while banks, autos and telecoms lagged. US IG funds saw \$6.87bn of net inflows last week, the third largest flow this year, lifting YTD inflows to +\$90.75bn. Primary market activity was slowed down ahead of the holiday weekend, with \$18bn of bonds pricing last week. YTD supply is tracking at \$1,291bn (+28% YoY).

US Leveraged Credit: HY spreads tightened by 14bp last week, resulting in excess returns of 0.4% and total returns of 0.3%, outperforming both IG and loans. Loan spreads tightened by 2bp last week, delivering total returns of 0.2%. In HY, spreads tightened across all rating cohorts, with single Bs outperforming. Within loans, single Bs outperformed while CCCs lagged. Across HY sectors, media and tech outperformed, while autos and telecoms lagged. HY funds reported net inflows of \$2.1bn (-\$2.1bn YTD), while loan funds saw net inflows of \$430mn last week (+\$4.61bn YTD). HY and Loans each recorded \$1bn of issuance last week. YTD issuance now stands at \$181bn (+25% YoY) in HY and \$215bn (-5% YoY) in loans.

EU Investment Grade: EU IG modestly lagged US IG with spreads tightening by 1bp, resulting in excess returns of +0.1%. Spreads tightened similarly across the curve, while As outperformed across ratings. Autos and leisure outperformed, while insurance lagged. EUR IG fund flows modestly rebounded, but the levels remained subdued at +\$336m (0.05% of AUM). Both mutual funds and ETFs reported small positive net inflows. Primary market activity remained robust with €15bn of bonds issued last week, lifting YTD volumes to €448bn (+2% YoY).

EU High Yield: EU HY outperformed IG with spreads tightening by 2bp last week, resulting in excess returns of +0.2%. The moves were driven by compression in BBs, while CCCs traded flat and Bs underperformed. Basic industry and tech underperformed, while insurance, financial services, and consumer goods held in better. European HY fund inflows meanwhile accelerated, rising to \~\$800mln / 0.7% of AUM. ETFs saw very strong net inflows (strongest week since July 2025), but mutual funds also reported steady net inflows. Last week saw HY supply decelerate to €1bn of issuance, placing YTD supply at €84bn (-7% YoY).

Credit Derivatives: In synthetics, CDX IG and iTraxx Main each tightened by 1bp over the week, with XO outperforming and HY underperforming their respective IG counterparts. Implied volatility ticked lower over the week, with credit volatility continuing to trade near record lows relative to equities.

Asia Credit: Asia Credit spreads tightened by 1bp over the last week, while APAC Credit tightened by 3bp. In Asia credit, Asia HY (-11bp) outperformed Asia IG (-1bp). Similarly, in APAC credit, HY spread tightened by 12bp while IG spread tightened by 2bp.

## US - 10s/30s Spread Curves to Steepen

\- We expect gradual 10s–30s steepening, though less pronounced than at the beginning of the year, with recent flattening and modest yield declines led by the front end.

\- Long-end issuance has been concentrated in high-quality hyperscalers, limiting steepening, though incremental M&A supply may add some support further out the curve.

## EU – Barbelling <5y With Long-End

\- 3-5y in the sweet spot: This part of the curve continues to benefit from fixed maturity fund demand, and the cohort has cheapened up this year relative to longer-dated credit in spread terms, based on our bottom-up framework. Additionally, yield curves have bear-flattened since early March, adding a further boost for yield buyers to focus on the <5yr cohorts. Meanwhile, seasonal upswing in issuance should put more pressure on longer-dated spreads.

\- 15y+ spreads have steepened relative to the 10-15y: We like pairing the above positioning with adding risk through the very long-end, given the recent steepening of the curve.

\- As we discuss in our mid-year outlook, buying into new issues could be a good opportunity to implement such positioning, but we also provide screens of flattest curves between 3-5y and further out, as well as steepest curves between 10-15y and 15y+.

Flat curves in the belly provide opportunities to switch into shorter dated bonds without ceding significant carry and roll-down

## EU – Buy French Bank CDS vs. Sell iTraxx Senior Financials

\- French sovereign risks on the rise: Our economists recently noted that the fiscal picture is tracking worse than expected in France, prompting them to revise higher their deficit forecasts for this year, while also highlighting a busy July agenda on public finances (see France Macro: 2026 Public Deficit: More Than Expected). Our rates colleagues view these developments as likely to keep pressure on French sovereign spreads, and they recommend shorting OATs vs. an equal weighted basket of Italian and German government bonds.

\- French risk premia within corporate credit has broadly tracked the performance of OATs over the past two years, leaving these cohorts exposed to further widening in sovereign spreads. However, we think cash spreads have underperformed the moves in OATs. We prefer to position for this move by buying protection on French Bank CDS versus selling protection on iTraxx SnrFins, as the relationship has not fully priced in the recent widening in OATs, currently trading at only +4bp.
