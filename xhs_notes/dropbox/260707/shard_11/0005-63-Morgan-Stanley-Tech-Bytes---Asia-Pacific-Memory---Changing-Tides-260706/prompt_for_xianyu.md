## Three debates for memory now

When the biggest spender on AI reportedly has compute to sell, the story begins to change, but the potential motivation is not entirely clear, and our view remains constructive (5 Takes On The Neocloud vs Hyperscaler Opportunities). This – along with absence of re-rating post LTA announcements and peak rate of change in the memory cycle – were the most debated memory topics in our investor conversations over the past week.

## Compute – excess or no excess?

Markets are doing what they typically do – discounting the future ahead of the headlines. That headline (not confirmed by the company) is a narrative change last week on compute capacity. The bear read is obvious – if a hyperscaler has compute to spare, maybe the whole AI buildout is oversupplied. The other read is that this is just companies being smart and monetizing infrastructure to improve their capex returns, which is not the same as excess compute. The real tell will be 2Q26 reporting and whether hyperscale companies keep or raise capex – this becomes a good entry point for memory stocks. If they cut, then the oversupply narrative continues.

Token maxing vs. minimizing. The other debate on compute is on AI Agents. Many companies were trying to encourage their employees to generate as many tokens as possible, but this resulted in excessive spend in IT budgets, and companies are now starting to cut and/or look for cheaper alternatives on token use.

\- We now see companies figuring out how to address this (token maxing) problem with cheaper models – that could mean using more open source, and the best of those are LLMs coming out of China.

\- We are now seeing enterprises increasingly adding an orchestration layer on top of frontier models, where the basic AI queries are sent to open source models and more complex queries are sent to frontier models.

\- Amid token minimization, the investor focus is on how these dynamics show up when token providers report results and then guide.

Bottom line, the June quarter should be fine for the AI supply chain, but the market concern is moving to cheaper tokens and what that guidance might look like into 2H26. The implications for memory are significant, as the key beneficiary of where the AI money is being spent.

## Why not re-rate on LTAs?

Memory stocks are not expensive because the market is acting rationally – it knows that earnings are very cyclical and markets are pricing them appropriately (and not being euphoric). Many investors believe in the AI re-rating thesis (as do we), but we think the lack of multiple re-rating on the back of LTAs reflects past experience with LTAs that were either renegotiated or ultimately required customers to take inventory they did not need. We saw similar dynamics with analog semiconductor companies during COVID, where LTAs were cyclical – but one can argue that the current memory LTAs are structural (as long as

AI stays strong).

Can estimates continue to go up? Perhaps the biggest headwind for many investors we speak with is the very uncertain time line for when, how and to what extent memory prices can continue to surprise higher and move 2028 EPS. Within AI beneficiaries, memory-related stocks have had the best earnings revisions by far, but that is well understood by markets.

## Peak cycle vs. extended cycle

This reset on price does not mean the cycle is over. Instead, it is more likely that this is a necessary reset for the cycle to eventually extend. Bear in mind that we have had three such resets since Generative AI was launched in the fall of 2022. Further de-risking in crowded areas and intermittent volatility would be consistent with an overbought sector continuing to carve out a low – not entering a new bear market. Price momentum is starting to fade, led by the recent underperformance of the hyperscalers (key spenders for the boom). In our view, this rotation away from the hyperscalers may be a leading indication that memory (the key spending beneficiary) could now see a period of underperformance relative to the market as EPS revisions breadth for the space comes up against historical extremes, along with a tougher stretch seasonally for markets more broadly.
