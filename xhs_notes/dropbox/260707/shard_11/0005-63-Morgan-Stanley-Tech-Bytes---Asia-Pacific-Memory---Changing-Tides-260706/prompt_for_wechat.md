## Three debates for memory now

## Compute – excess or no excess?

Markets are doing what they typically do – discounting the future ahead of the headlines. That headline (not confirmed by the company) is a narrative change last week on compute capacity. The bear read is obvious – if a hyperscaler has compute to spare, maybe the whole AI buildout is oversupplied. The other read is that this is just companies being smart and monetizing infrastructure to improve their capex returns, which is not the same as excess compute. Token maxing vs. minimizing. The other debate on compute is on AI Agents. Many companies were trying to encourage their employees to generate as many tokens as possible, but this resulted in excessive spend in IT budgets, and companies are now starting to cut and/or look for cheaper alternatives on token use.

\- We now see companies figuring out how to address this (token maxing) problem with cheaper models – that could mean using more open source, and the best of those are LLMs coming out of China.

\- We are now seeing enterprises increasingly adding an orchestration layer on top of frontier models, where the basic AI queries are sent to open source models and more complex queries are sent to frontier models.

\- Amid token minimization, the investor focus is on how these dynamics show up when token providers report results and then guide.
