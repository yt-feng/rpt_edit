## THE SMR LANDSCAPE

Some definitions. We follow convention and argue that SMRs (Small Modular Reactors) are fission-based nuclear power plants intended to generate electricity at a scale of $< 300$ MW (specifically MWe). Recall that typical reactors are 1,000 MW (1 GW) such as the Westinghouse AP1000. Also recall that MWe is megawatts of electricity equivalent - a measure of power (the ability to generate energy, in this case electrical energy). We also quote MWth or megawatts of thermal equivalent (the ability to generate thermal energy). A good rule of thumb is a reactor's MWe is roughly a third of its MWth.

A large number of SMR projects exist across the world in different stages of maturity (Exhibit 3). We would expect the vast majority to either not move forward or to move forward in the mid 2030s at best.

We highlight that SMRs can be non-commercial (tests, pilots, demonstrations) or commercial (generating electricity from which revenue is earned). As equity investors, our focus is on the latter.

We also remind investors that non-commercial SMR technology has been deployed at scale by various militaries (see the appendix on nuclear vessels). There are currently two civilian operable projects: Rosatom's KLT-40s (providing power to a remote community by leveraging nuclear icebreaker technology) and CNNC's HTR-PM (a demonstration plant initially that has been connected to the grid).
