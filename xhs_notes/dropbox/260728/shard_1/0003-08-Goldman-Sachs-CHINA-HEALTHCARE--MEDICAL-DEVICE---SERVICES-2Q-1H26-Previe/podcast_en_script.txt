EN_A: So, the China healthcare sector has been under pressure lately. I saw a report saying medtech and services stocks dropped 21 percent on average over the past six months.
EN_B: That is right. It has been one of the weakest parts of the healthcare market. Even though the sector bounced back 4.5 percent in the last month, it is still lagging the broader healthcare universe.
EN_A: What is the main takeaway from this report?
EN_B: The report is broadly neutral heading into the second quarter and first half results. The key issue is that domestic demand recovery is still very uneven, and policy headwinds remain. But companies with global expansion potential stand out.
EN_A: What kind of policy headwinds are we talking about?
EN_B: Three big ones. Consumables VBP is becoming routine. The DRG and DIP cost control measures are still in place. And equipment VBP is coming in the second half of this year. On top of that, anti-corruption measures introduced in May could slow down hospital procurement and clinical volumes in the near term.
EN_A: So the domestic story is tough. Where is the market looking instead?
EN_B: Investors are very selective. They are only interested in two types of companies. One, those showing clear operational recovery. Two, those with a proven track record in overseas expansion. Angelalign is a good example. It was up nearly 17 percent in the past month because confidence in its international growth is improving.
EN_A: What about surgical robotics? That sounds like a hot area.
EN_B: It is getting a lot of attention, but the stock prices have not fully reflected the overseas commercialization progress yet. Investors are waiting for order confirmations and proof that overseas demand is sustainable. And Intuitive Surgical dropping 30 percent in three months has not helped sentiment.
EN_A: What about healthcare services? Any action there?
EN_B: Not much. The consumption backdrop is still soft. But the market is starting to focus on operational stabilization, margin resilience, and shareholder returns. Hygeia and Gushengtang both recovered some ground in the past month, partly because concerns about being removed from the Hong Kong Stock Connect program have eased.
EN_A: So what should investors watch next?
EN_B: The pace of demand recovery during the summer, especially for consumer-facing healthcare services. That will be key to judging whether earnings recovery in the second half of this year is sustainable.
EN_A: Let us get into the specifics. Which names stand out?
EN_B: Overseas execution is the biggest differentiator. In surgical robotics, Edge Medical had about 100 cumulative overseas systems installed or shipped by the first half. Utilization is ramping, with over 20,000 procedures done. MicroPort MedBot had around 300 cumulative global orders, with over 240 from overseas. They are targeting 200 installations this year. The commercialization momentum is real.
EN_A: Are any of these companies profitable yet?
EN_B: MicroPort MedBot hit a key inflection point. They issued a profit alert on July 22, saying they achieved profitability in the first half. Revenue grew 200 to 230 percent year on year. Overseas revenue grew over 450 percent. Gross margin expanded by more than 15 points. But investors still want to see if revenue is sustainable and how accounts receivable collection goes.
EN_A: What about AK Medical?
EN_B: They also issued a positive profit alert. First half revenue grew about 10 percent, net profit over 20 percent. Overseas revenue grew 40 percent, ahead of expectations. Their robot commercialization is gaining traction too. They secured 11 tenders as of June, five of them overseas.
EN_A: And Angelalign? How are they doing?
EN_B: Domestic and overseas growth are both on track. VBP exposure is limited because only 5 to 7 percent of revenue comes from public hospitals. The VBP rollout seems slightly delayed. And recent legal wins in China and Germany have reduced litigation overhangs.
EN_A: What about the service names like Gushengtang and Hygeia?
EN_B: Gushengtang is seeing solid volume growth, 15 to 20 percent in April and May. They are also ramping up shareholder returns, guiding for at least 450 million Hong Kong dollars in annual dividends and a 300 million buyback. Overseas, they are scaling in Singapore, targeting about 50 stores by year end.
EN_A: And Hygeia?
EN_B: Operations are improving. Outpatient visits grew 4 percent, surgical volumes up 7.6 percent. Pricing is stabilizing. But they are becoming more cautious on expansion, slowing heavy asset capex and focusing on buybacks and dividends. They plan to set aside about 500 million renminbi annually for shareholder returns from 2026 to 2028.
EN_A: Jinxin Fertility had a big loss before. What is happening now?
EN_B: They turned around. First half net profit was at least 90 million renminbi, compared to a loss of over a billion in the same period last year. New patient visits grew 9 percent. Overseas growth was the main driver, up 42 percent. But Chengdu was down 5 percent, so it is still a mixed picture.
EN_A: So there is a lot of divergence. What are the full year guidance numbers looking like?
EN_B: Angelalign expects total case volume growth of about 25 percent, with overseas monthly breakeven by late 2026. MicroPort MedBot targets over 200 new installations and revenue over 1.1 billion renminbi, with full year profitability. Edge Medical targets about 120 installations and revenue over 850 million, also profitable for the full year.
EN_A: What about the others like MicroTech and Weigao?
EN_B: MicroTech expects at least 35 percent revenue growth, with CGM growing over 50 percent. Net margin target is 15 to 20 percent in the medium term. Weigao is more cautious, guiding 5 to 8 percent revenue growth. First quarter was in line.
EN_A: Are there any big unknowns in this picture?
EN_B: Two things. First, equipment VBP only really kicks in during the second half, so the actual impact is not clear yet. Second, how much the anti-corruption measures will slow procurement in the short term still needs more data. The report says they are monitoring these risks.
