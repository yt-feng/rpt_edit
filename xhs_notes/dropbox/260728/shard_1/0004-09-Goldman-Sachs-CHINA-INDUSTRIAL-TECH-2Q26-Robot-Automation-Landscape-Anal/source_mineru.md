## 2Q26 key observations on the China Industrial Robot & Automation competitive landscape

Post MIR's estimated sales data (vs. production data reported by the NBS) as well as our coverage companies' business segment disclosures, we compile our latest landscape analyzer within.

Industrial automation (IA) market growth came in at +6% yoy in 2Q26, with the project/OEM (mainly discrete automation) market at -1%/+17% yoy, extending the visible OEM-led recovery observed in 1Q26. In this broader environment, Inovance again stands out as the clearest automation share-gainer: it retained No.1 market position in Servo, Low-voltage inverter and SCARA, with Servo share +5pp qoq/+5pp yoy to 38%, PLC share +4pp yoy, and SCARA share +6pp yoy with these gains concentrated in categories where it

already holds leadership, reinforcing our view that share consolidation is continuing around Inovance's strongest verticals.

2Q26 total industrial robot (IR) unit sales (according to MIR data) reached 101k, +17%/+15% yoy/qoq vs. 1Q26's +14%/-5% yoy/qoq; Cobots remained the fastest-growing segment at +43% yoy. Against this, NBS-reported IR production units grew 24% yoy/28% qoq, with the discrepancy between production and domestic sales widening sequentially. Export growth helped absorb part of the sequential increase in production. Across robot types, leadership was stable at the top but still competitive beneath, with ESTUN/Inovance retaining No.1/No.3 overall, Inovance sustaining the No.3 position it took from FANUC in 1Q26, and yoy share gains modestly consolidating around the top three (ESTUN/KUKA (Midea)/Inovance at 0pp/+1pp/+1pp).

By end market, growth was highly concentrated in high-tech verticals. Semiconductor sales accelerated to +65% yoy (the strongest growth since 3Q21); Auto Electronics and Lithium Battery both surpassed 30%

yoy growth in 2Q26 (vs. 20%+ in 1Q26). Solar reverted to a -9% yoy decline after a one-off positive quarter in 1Q26.

China domestic IR players significantly consolidated share in 2Q26, with domestic brands reaching $60\%$ total market share, up +5pp qoq/+5pp yoy. The localization gain was broad-based across robot and control categories, where domestic players gained share in Small 6-axis/Large 6-axis/SCARA/Servo by +5pp/+2pp/+7pp/+6pp yoy, respectively. At the company level, ESTUN and Inovance ranked No.1/No.3 overall, respectively - directionally consistent with Inovance's own share consolidation in Servo, PLC and SCARA, with Inovance likely a meaningful contributor to category-level localization gains in Servo and SCARA, given its +5pp yoy Servo and +6pp yoy SCARA share gains. The notable exception was Low-voltage Inverter, where Inovance remained No.1 but saw share decline -2pp yoy, alongside a -3pp yoy decline for domestic players overall, in contrast to the broad-based localization gains elsewhere.

\- Backed by a strong order intake of 40% yoy in 6M2026, assuming order lead time of \~1-2 months, we believe Inovance's 2Q IA revenue growth could trend at around 30%\~40% yoy, with much narrowed order vs. revenue gap compared to 1Q, supported by capacity expansion undertaken in early 2Q for better conversion. We expect Inovance to outperform peers and the broader market heading into 2Q26 earnings, supported by continued share gains in core control categories (inverters, servo and PLCs).

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

## M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
