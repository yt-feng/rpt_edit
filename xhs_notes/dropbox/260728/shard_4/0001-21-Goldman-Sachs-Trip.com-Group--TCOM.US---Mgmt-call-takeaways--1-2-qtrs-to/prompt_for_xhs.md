## Summary and key highlights of Q&As during the conference call: 1. With the delivery of SAMR decision, are regulatory risks fully behind TCOM?

The company views the conclusion of the SAMR investigation as an important milestone for TCOM's China domestic business practices. TCOM views this as an opportunity to further strengthen its governance framework and support long-term development of its online travel businesses. The company is also committed to creating long-term value for their business partners and users by fostering industry competition based on service quality and user experience. ## 2. What will be the new program replacing the former tier-1&2 delegated

## distribution programs? What could the impact on TCOM's ADR and take rate be?

As laid out from its 19 measures across 5 key areas, TCOM will adopt a new multi-tiered partnership framework with a new traffic allocation mechanism that promotes greater flexibility, transparency, and shared business growth. Under its G2 strategy (Globalization and Great quality), they will strive to build long-term value to hotel partners by helping them to reach a broad base of high-quality leisure/business travelers and driving incremental demand across multiple travel scenarios such as inbound travel, the silver generation travel, as well as event-driven travels. The company expects its ADR to remain primarily driven by market supply and demand, and does not anticipate these measures to fundamentally change such dynamic. Similarly, its take rate should not see any structural changes, but may experience normal fluctuations driven by product mix, promotional activities and partner incentive programs.

The company had discontinued its “automated price adjustment assistant” (自动调价助手) in Mar and the impact was already reflected in its 2Q26 guidance/outlook.
