Following China's State Administration of Market Regulation (SAMR)'s announcement that it has completed a 6-month anti-trust investigation into TCOM, charging the company Rmb5.179bn in one-off penalties fees (see SAMR's announcement on Jul 25 and our report on Jul 27), the company hosted a conference call where senior management team (CEO Jane Sun, CFO Cindy Wang) provided more details about the 19 measures the company is implementing to rectify any anti-trust practices and discussed the related financial implication. As previously stated, the company reiterated it fully accepts the regulator's decision and penalties imposed, and highlighted that the investigation is over without any further outstanding issues. We note, certain operational adjustments (e.g., discontinuation of the automated price adjustment assistant (自动调价助手) and exclusive partnership required for Premium category hotels (特牌酒店)) that the group has implemented were already reflected in their 2Q26 guidance.

Although its transition to a new merchant tiered cooperation model (i.e., somewhat similar to Booking.com's base/preferred/preferred plus tiering program, optional add-ons booster, etc.) could have a modest operational and GMV impact in 2H26, they do not anticipate any meaningful hit to its effective hotel take rate which is not that much different from other China OTAs (i.e., TCOM at $9.2\%$ vs. Meituan at $8.5\%$ in FY25). They still believe their ability to stay competitive hinges more on the value proposition they offer to hotel partners vs. peers, i.e., predictable booking volume, exposure to premium/higher-spending users, reliable 24/7 customer services etc. As discussed in our prior report (Jan 19), both Meituan's and Alibaba's take rates in China's food delivery and e-commerce markets have remained relatively stable in the 2-3 years after the conclusion of their investigations back in 2021. In the OTA space, TCOM/Tongcheng have maintained their hotel take rates largely unchanged despite various attempts by new entrants to compete by undercutting prices (e.g., zero take rate by JD, originally $4.5\%$ by Douyin). Similarly, outside of China, Booking.com's take rates were not much affected by regulators' legal disputes on its industry practices over the years.

From our discussion with investors earlier today, while many were pleased to see a removal of stock overhang after the conclusion of the 6-month investigation, there was also some pushback to our Buy call including (1) further downside earnings risk in 2H26 - other than the short-term impact from its transition to new hotel tiered model as mentioned above; China's hotel industry RevPar trend has softened from +3-4% yoy in Apr-May to -1% yoy in June and -6% mtd in July per STR data against less favorable base comparison, even though domestic air traffic showed a noticeable recovery from -6%/-7% in May/June to +3% mtd in July (Exhibit 3); (2) newsflow regarding potential increased competition from Meituan, Fliggy and other competitors which may make new attempts to grab GMV share; (3) limited scope for the stock to re-rate considering that China Consumer/Internet names' forward P/E multiples have contracted to 12-13x as of late. As laid out in Exhibit 5, we now forecast +8% revenue growth and -2% EBIT decline for TCOM in FY26E by assuming (1) steady +5% yoy revenue growth in 2H26E (vs. +17%/+5% in 1Q/2Q26) including -3% yoy domestic travel revenue decline, +4% yoy outbound travel revenue growth and +45-50% yoy growth from its Trip.com platform; (2) -3.1-3.2pts yoy EBIT margin decline taking into account -1pts dilution from unfavorable revenue mix shift toward loss-making Trip.com platform - even though Trip.com itself is expected to see narrower EBIT loss ratio. This is then be followed by +11% yoy revenue and +13% EBIT growth in FY27E. At the current share price, the stock is trading at 11.6x/10x FY26E/FY27E P/Es respectively, a deep 30+% discount to Booking.com's 17x/14x. By way of sensitivity, every -10% reduction in domestic hotel GMV and -1pts cut in domestic hotel take rate would lower our FY26E EBIT forecasts by TCOM by -7% and -11% respectively. We also highlight its EBIT/earnings sensitivity to China revenue growth and Trip.com's EBIT margin assumptions (Exhibit 1).

Overall, investors are likely to view the outcome of the investigation with no major surprise considering TCOM had in January ceased operating the two practices that the regulator identified (i.e., exclusivity partnership required for hotels joining the “Premium Category” and “lowest price platforms” for all other hotels. While it may take 1-2 quarters for the impact of its business model transition to be fully reflected in its financials, we think valuation suggests the stock has already discounted much pessimism toward its long-term growth trajectory - this we remain confident about esp. given its solid execution so far with rapid expansion into the rest of Asia via Trip.com platform. Maintain Buy with revised estimates (FY26E-FY28E EPS cut 1-24%) and 12-month target prices of US\$70/HK\$545 (from US\$71/HK\$560) after factoring in the one-off penalty and latest industry travel trend.

## Summary and key highlights of Q&As during the conference call: 1. With the delivery of SAMR decision, are regulatory risks fully behind TCOM?

The company views the conclusion of the SAMR investigation as an important milestone for TCOM's China domestic business practices. TCOM fully accepts the decision and will implement rectification measures in a timely and comprehensive manner. TCOM views this as an opportunity to further strengthen its governance framework and support long-term development of its online travel businesses. The company is also committed to creating long-term value for their business partners and users by fostering industry competition based on service quality and user experience. Like other leading technology and platform companies globally, they will work constructively with regulators while delivering value to shareholders, the industry and the society.

## 2. What will be the new program replacing the former tier-1&2 delegated

## distribution programs? What could the impact on TCOM's ADR and take rate be?

As laid out from its 19 measures across 5 key areas, TCOM will adopt a new multi-tiered partnership framework with a new traffic allocation mechanism that promotes greater flexibility, transparency, and shared business growth. Under its G2 strategy (Globalization and Great quality), they will strive to build long-term value to hotel partners by helping them to reach a broad base of high-quality leisure/business travelers and driving incremental demand across multiple travel scenarios such as inbound travel, the silver generation travel, as well as event-driven travels. The company expects its ADR to remain primarily driven by market supply and demand, and does not anticipate these measures to fundamentally change such dynamic. Similarly, its take rate should not see any structural changes, but may experience normal fluctuations driven by product mix, promotional activities and partner incentive programs.

## 3. Which operations adjustments were already factored in 2Q26 guidance and what additional financial impact should investors expect in the coming quarters?

The company had discontinued its “automated price adjustment assistant” (自动调价助手) in Mar and the impact was already reflected in its 2Q26 guidance/outlook. Meanwhile, the discontinuation of tier-1&2 delegated distribution programs, alongside other transition progress, may introduce performance fluctuations in the coming quarters. Coupled with ongoing macroeconomic uncertainties, the company’s visibility for 2H26 remains limited for now.

## 4. What is the financial impact of the penalty, and will it affect TCOM's operations and capital allocations?

From an accounting perspective, TCOM will recognize a one-off expense of RMB5.179bn and a counter revenue of RMB122mn in 2Q26. Its capital allocations priorities remain unchanged. With the share price relatively depressed of late and the company's commitment to buybacks and commentary during 1Q earnings, we see an increased likelihood that the group has deployed some capital in share repurchases ytd.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

## M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
