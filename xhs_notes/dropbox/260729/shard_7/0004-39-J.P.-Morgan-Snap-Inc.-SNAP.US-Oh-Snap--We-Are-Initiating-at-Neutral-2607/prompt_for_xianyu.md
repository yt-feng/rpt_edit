## Company Overview

Snap sees itself as a technology company that focuses on smartphone cameras for use in its main offering, Snapchat. Snapchat is a social media platform that allows users (called “Snapchatters”) to create a community of friends and then communicate with that community using pictures, videos, and text. The app allows customers to see where their friends are on a map, create stories for their communities, interact with augmented reality lenses and filters, and communicate with pictures and text that purposefully last for only a short while. Snapchat also includes a TikTok style feature called “Spotlight” that showcases the best of Snapchat, helping people discover new creators and content which becomes personalized using Snapchat’s AI. Snapchat’s ecosystem has expanded over time with a user base approaching one billion monthly active users, including 75% of the 13-34 year cohort in more than 20 countries. Users open the app 30–40 times per day on average, resulting in around 30 minutes of daily screen time globally (vs. TikTok and Instagram \~1 hour).

## Competition

From an industry standpoint, Snap operates at the intersection of digital media and advertising, social media, and augmented reality hardware, competing for user attention and ad dollars. The market for mobile engagement and advertising is crowded with deep-pocketed players such as Alphabet (including Google and YouTube), Apple, ByteDance (TikTok and affiliated joint ventures), Meta (including Facebook, Instagram, Threads, and WhatsApp), Pinterest, Reddit and X (formerly Twitter). Additionally, competition for engagement varies by region. For instance, SNAP faces competition from companies like Kakao, LINE, Naver (including Snow), and Tencent in Asia. Since the products are typically free, the businesses compete on brand and quality of product offerings to retain users' attention. At the same time, the businesses compete to retain partners and advertisers. Within this backdrop, Snap is a niche player with a compelling value proposition for advertisers looking to tap into younger demographics, as Snap reaches 75% of the 13-34 year cohort or most of Millennials/Gen Z.

## History

Snapchat began commercial operations in 2011, when Stanford University students Evan Spiegel, Bobby Murphy, and Reggie Brown launched what would become Snapchat as a mobile application focused on ephemeral photo messaging. As the user base grew rapidly among teenagers and young adults, Snapchat turned down takeover offers from larger players including Facebook's \$3bn offer in 2013 and Google's \$4bn offer in 2013. They began meaningfully monetizing Snapchat in 2015. As Snapchat matured beyond a single messaging app and looked to launch new initiatives such as Spectacles, the company rebranded from Snapchat to Snap in 2016.
