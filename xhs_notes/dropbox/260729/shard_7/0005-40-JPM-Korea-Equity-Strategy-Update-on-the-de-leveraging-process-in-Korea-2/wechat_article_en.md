This structural imbalance meant that any decline in the underlying index triggered forced deleveraging, which in turn created further selling pressure from other market participants. The feedback loop was powerful and destructive. Since the peak, leveraged ETF AUM has declined to $17 billion, a level that the data suggests is no longer problematic. More importantly, the previously rapid inflows into these products have stalled in recent days, with regulatory tightening likely keeping further inflows in check. The VKOSPI to VIX ratio, which had spiked to extreme levels during the height of the selling, has begun to decline and is expected to fall further as the structural overhang from leveraged products dissipates. The open interest in single stock futures, which served as the implementation channel for leveraged ETFs, continues to moderate, providing further evidence that the mechanical selling pressure has largely run its course.

## Retail Margin Leverage Is a Contained Risk, Not a Systemic Threat

Unlike the leveraged ETF complex, retail margin balances were never particularly high or growing rapidly during the market's ascent. Margin balances currently stand at approximately $20 billion, and as a share of market capitalization, they have actually been declining this year. This is a critical distinction because margin lending comes with buffers and discretion that leveraged ETFs lack. Korean retail investors still have substantial equity gains from prior periods, significant cash balances, higher incomes, and overseas assets that can be tapped to absorb margin calls. The risk of forced retail selling is therefore limited, and the retail investor base is unlikely to become a source of systemic selling pressure even if the market experiences further near-term weakness. This stands in contrast to the leveraged ETF structure, which forced selling regardless of investor discretion.

This concentration of selling in memory names, combined with the fact that memory prices continue to rise, suggests that the foreign outflow dynamic is more about portfolio rebalancing and mandate constraints than a fundamental rejection of Korea as an investment destination.
