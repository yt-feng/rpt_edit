EN_A: So you have two earnings alerts from the dairy sector at almost the same time, but they point in completely opposite directions. What’s the real story here?
EN_B: Exactly. This is the signal we have been waiting for. Youran Dairy turned profitable in the first half of this year, while Ausnutria issued a loss warning. And both have significant implications for Yili.
EN_A: So what is the single biggest question this report is trying to answer?
EN_B: It is about whether China’s raw milk supply and demand cycle is finally reaching an inflection point. Youran’s turnaround, combined with Modern Dairy’s recent profit alert, suggests upstream losses are narrowing. The industry is rebalancing.
EN_A: But doesn’t the Ausnutria loss warning kind of undermine that narrative?
EN_B: Not really. The report breaks it down clearly. Ausnutria’s loss is mostly driven by one-off items, like inventory destocking, higher shipping costs due to geopolitical tensions, and non-cash asset impairments. If you strip those out, its core operating profit is actually flat year on year.
EN_A: So for Yili, the positive from Youran might outweigh the negative from Ausnutria?
EN_B: That is exactly the math. Based on Yili’s 31.5% stake, Youran’s profit alert implies roughly 233 to 284 million renminbi in associate income for Yili in the first half. That is already above what analysts were expecting for Yili’s total associate income. The Ausnutria hit is around 551 million renminbi, but the key difference is that Youran’s improvement is structural, while Ausnutria’s problem is temporary.
EN_A: What about Yili’s core business? Could Ausnutria drag down its overall performance?
EN_B: The report addresses that too. Ausnutria accounts for about 22% of Yili’s infant milk formula and other segment, and about 6% of Yili’s total sales. A 20% drop in Ausnutria sales would shave off about 1.2% from Yili’s total revenue. But Yili’s own flagship brand, Pro-Kido, is still gaining market share, so the overall formula segment should still grow.
EN_A: So the market might focus more on the positive read-across from Youran?
EN_B: That is the report’s view. The market will care more about the upstream cycle turning. Because when upstream profitability improves, it eases the competitive pressure on promotions downstream. That is a medium-term positive for industry leaders with upstream assets.
EN_A: Are there any open questions the report doesn’t fully answer?
EN_B: Two big ones. First, can raw milk prices really stabilize? The latest price is 3.05 renminbi per kilo, basically flat from year end, but we need to see if that holds. Second, can Ausnutria really deliver a clean restart in the second half and return to profit? The report says inventory is clean, but demand recovery still needs more data to confirm.
EN_A: So if someone wants the full picture, they should read the original report?
EN_B: Absolutely. The full report has detailed financial models and valuation methodology, including projections for Yili’s revenue and earnings over the next few years. If you want the numbers, that is where to go.
