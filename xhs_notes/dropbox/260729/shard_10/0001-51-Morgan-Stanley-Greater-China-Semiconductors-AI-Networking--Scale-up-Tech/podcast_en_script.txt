EN_A: So there's this new report on China's AI networking, and it keeps talking about something called "scale-up." What exactly is that, and why should we care?
EN_B: Scale-up is basically about making multiple AI accelerators inside a single rack or across a few racks work together like one giant supercomputer. It's different from scale-out, which is about connecting more servers to grow a cluster. Scale-up focuses on high-speed, direct communication between accelerators so they share memory and process the same workload.
EN_A: Got it. So why is this report specifically zooming in on China's scale-up technology right now?
EN_B: The key thesis is that because Chinese AI chips are constrained at the wafer process level, the competition is shifting from standalone chip specs to system-level solutions. At this year's WAIC, we saw fewer chip launches and way more supernode solutions. Leading domestic vendors showed rack-scale or multi-rack supernodes that can connect 64 or more accelerators, mostly using their own proprietary scale-up tech.
EN_A: So they're using system architecture to work around the chip process limitations?
EN_B: Exactly. For example, Huawei's Atlas 950 uses its own UnifiedBus 2.0 to connect 1,024 NPUs, and it's designed to scale to 8,192. Biren's next-gen BR2xx architecture targets up to 1,024 GPUs. These numbers show that scale-up is becoming the main battleground for China's AI computing.
EN_A: How are these supernodes actually connected? Is traditional copper cabling enough?
EN_B: That's the second big insight. Inside a single rack, electrical interconnect is still preferred because it's cheaper and simpler. Moore Threads' C256 uses an all-copper cable tray design. But when scale-up goes across racks, copper hits limits on reach, signal loss, and power. That's where optical interconnect becomes critical.
EN_A: So optics is the key for cross-rack scale-up? Any concrete examples from the report?
EN_B: Yes. Huawei's Atlas 950 SuperPoD uses optical interconnect across racks. More notably, at WAIC, Lightelligence showed new NPO and CPO switch solutions, partnering with a domestic switch vendor we believe is Centec. Also, Enflame and Biren both unveiled NPO-based server solutions targeting 512 or more accelerators. These are real signs that optical tech is being integrated at both the accelerator I/O layer and the distributed switching layer.
EN_A: That sounds like it would create opportunities for a bunch of domestic suppliers. Did the report name any specific companies that could benefit?
EN_B: Yes. The report thinks Montage Technology will benefit from higher PCIe interconnect content in larger supernodes, meaning more demand for PCIe retimers and switch chips. It also expects AI GPU vendors like Hygon, along with its ecosystem partner Sugon, plus Cambricon and Iluvatar, to leverage scale-up tech to improve their rack performance.
EN_A: Are there any big open questions the report didn't fully answer? Like, what's still uncertain?
EN_B: Two big ones. First, how mature are these NPO and CPO solutions really for scale-up deployment? Most are still prototypes or demos. Mass production and cost control are unproven. Second, the report gives a TAM forecast for the US scale-up market but doesn't quantify the China market specifically. So we still need to watch actual capex data and customer procurement to figure out when and how big this market will get.
