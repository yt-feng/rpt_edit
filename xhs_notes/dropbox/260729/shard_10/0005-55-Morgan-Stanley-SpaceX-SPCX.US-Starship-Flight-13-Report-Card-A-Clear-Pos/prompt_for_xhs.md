# Starship Flight 13 Report Card: A Clear Positive in a Tide of Cautious Sentiment

While admittedly a qualitative assessment, if we're asked to assign a grade to SpaceX's Starship Flight 13 we would give it an overall grade of A-. While not a entirely flawless, Flight 13 included some extraordinary milestones that should leave investors with greater conviction in Starship's path to becoming operational. Most notably, Musk posted shortly after the flight that SpaceX will likely attempt its first-ever Ship catch on Flight 14, giving investors one of the first concrete potential upside catalysts to anticipate. Bears will isolate the off-nominal booster landing burn as reason for caution, but this was only the second landing attempt of a V3 booster and its all-new Raptor 3 propulsion, so a hard landing should not be a major surprise, and it still marked a clear step up from Flight 12 when the V3 booster was lost before it ever reached a landing. First lockup release after August 4 earnings is still an overhang, which likely keeps many would-be longs on the sidelines. Overall stock implications: Positive. Next milestone... catching ship. If they can do that, bears may need to consider cryogenic hibernation.

## Report Card in 7 Parts:

1. Countdown and launch readiness (B): Recovered from a T-0 pad abort on Jul 16 (moisture in the LOX turbopumps kept four Raptors from starting; six engines swapped), then went cleanly on July 24th. There was a scrub on July 23rd due to poor visibility from weather, but that was out of SpaceX's control.

2. Liftoff and ascent (A+): All 33 Super Heavy Raptors lit in a clean, full-thrust ascent with no engine-out.

3. Hot-stage separation (A+): Clean hot-stage separation and stage split, with no repeat of the $\sim90^{\circ}$ flip that doomed the Flight 12 booster.

4. Boostback & landing burn (C+): Boostback was clean, but the final landing burn lit only \~5 of 13 engines, forcing a hard splashdown in the Gulf of America. No catch was planned, but a soft touchdown is a prerequisite before any future tower catch of the V3 Booster.

5. Payload deployment (A+): First-ever deployment of 20 production Starlink V3 satellites (prior flights carried simulators). Arrays and antennas extended, laser links to the constellation successful. Satellites de-orbited shortly after as was planned- these sats weren't intended to serve customers. Satellites captured extensive imagery of the ship heatshield after deployment.

6. In-space maneuvers/Raptor relight (A+): Successful single-engine relight \~39 min into flight. This was the maneuver test abandoned on Flight 12 due to the loss of a Raptor vacuum engine on the ship.

## What now? Next launch catalysts to watch:

\- Flight 14 (expected \~1 month out, late Aug / early Sept): Shortly after Ship 40's splashdown, Musk posted that unless data review turns up problems, SpaceX will attempt to catch the Ship with the tower on the next flight. That would be the first-ever Ship catch on Mechazilla's arms at Starbase.

\- Implies Starship's first orbital flight. A tower Ship catch requires an orbital (not suborbital) trajectory, so Flight 14 would likely be Starship's first orbital attempt. Worth flagging: after 13 test flights and three years, SpaceX has yet to complete a full Earth orbit with Starship.

\- Likely no V3 booster catch yet. Given Flight 13's hard booster splashdown, SpaceX will want a clean soft splashdown of the new V3 booster before risking a catch, so Flight 14 should fly the booster to the water again.

• Potential first operational Starlink V3s. Flight 13's 20 V3s were a suborbital demo that demised on reentry about 20 minutes after deployment. An orbital Flight 14 could theoretically deploy V3s into a working orbit for the first time, the first genuinely operational deployment.

\- Flight 15 (roughly a month after that): If Flight 14 lands a Ship catch and a soft booster splashdown, Flight 15 could be the first to attempt a Ship catch and a V3 booster catch on the same mission.
