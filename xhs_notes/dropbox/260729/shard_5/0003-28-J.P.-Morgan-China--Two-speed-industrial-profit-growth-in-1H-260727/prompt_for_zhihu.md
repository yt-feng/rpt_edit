# China: Two-speed industrial profit growth in 1H

• 1H26 industrial profit growth expanded by 18.7% oya.

• Profit growth was concentrated in a narrow set of tech and upstream industries.

\- The recent Consumption 15FYP is directionally positive but lacks detailed support to revive domestic demand.

\- Accelerated fiscal deployment in 2H continues to benefit policy tailwind sectors, while energy risks cap the prospects for a broader profit recovery.

Headline industrial profit growth was 18.7% oya in 1H26, slightly below the 18.8% in the first five months, as the monthly pace of expansion moderated to June's 15.1% oya from 21.1% in May. In 1H26, sales revenue grew 6.5% oya, while costs rose more slowly at 5.9%, supporting net profit margin. That said, finished goods inventory climbed to the highest since early 2023 at 9.5% oya and accounts receivables edged up to 8.1% in June, pointing to slower turnover and cash conversion pressure.

The headline profit gains were driven by a relatively narrow set of tech-related and raw material sectors, accounting for nearly 93% of the headline profit growth in 1H. Electronics manufacturing profit rose 96.9% oya ytd on the back of a global AI upcycle. This new growth driver has lifted adjacent industries' profits, including ICs manufacturing, electronic specialty materials, and data center interconnectivity equipment. Rising demand from AI and new energy products has also benefited upstream materials, keeping non-ferrous metal prices elevated (e.g. copper, aluminum) and driving its profit up by 99.4% oya ytd. Despite a temporary moderation in global commodity prices in June, industries along the oil production chain accumulated solid profit gains in 1H, with the petro-processing industry swinging from last year's loss to profit, and chemical industry profit rising 67.8% oya ytd.

In contrast, rising producer prices have squeezed the profit of downstream and consumer-related industries. In June, consumer-goods PPI remained in deflation at -0.9% oya, despite the improvement in producer-goods PPI (+5.5%), reflecting weak pricing power amid still-soft domestic demand. In 1H, profits remained deeply negative in furniture (-52.7%) and autos (-19.5%), and the contraction widened in apparel (-28.0%) and cultural/sports/entertainment goods (-13.8%).

Looking ahead, we expect the two-speed profit growth to persist. Tech-related industries should continue to outperform, supported by the government's multi-year “AI+” and industrial upgrading agenda, as well as the global IP and AI cycle. However, spillovers to employment, income and household demand are expected to be limited in the near term, capping the prospects for a broader profit recovery across downstream and consumer-related industries. The 15th Five-Year Plan for Expanding Consumption is directionally positive, but it remains largely supply-side focused and lacks detailed implementation plans and explicit fiscal deployment.
