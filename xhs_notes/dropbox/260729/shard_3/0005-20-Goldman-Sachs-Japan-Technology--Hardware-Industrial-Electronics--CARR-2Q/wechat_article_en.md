## Carrier's Order Book Confirms That Data Center Cooling Has Entered a Multi-Year Expansion Phase

The most important data point from Carrier's quarter is not the revenue beat or the margin decline. It is the composition of the order book. Commercial HVAC orders doubled year over year, and data center-related orders within that category grew more than 300%. Carrier's backlog reached over $8 billion, representing roughly 14 months of sales at the current run rate. This backlog provides rare visibility into future revenue streams, and it is concentrated in the highest-growth end markets.

The Americas segment, which accounts for the majority of Carrier's revenue, saw residential channel inventory decline 25% year over year. That destocking creates a setup for second-half volume growth that is largely independent of macroeconomic conditions. Commercial orders doubled in the Americas, and the company explicitly cited data center demand as the primary driver. In Europe, heat pump sales grew 20% year over year, and the electrification trend remains intact despite regulatory uncertainty in some markets. Asia ex-China delivered broad-based strength, with India and the Middle East each growing 35% year over year, Southeast Asia up 20%, and Australia up 30%.

The pattern is clear. Data center construction is no longer a niche demand driver for HVAC companies. It is the dominant growth vector, and it is accelerating. For Mitsubishi Electric and Panasonic Holdings, the question is how to access this market profitably.

## Panasonic Holdings Faces a More Complex Equation, Where Data Center Gains Must Outweigh Headwinds in Residential China and Battery Competition

Panasonic Holdings' exposure to data center cooling comes through its HVAC business, but the company's earnings profile is more diversified—and therefore more vulnerable to cross-currents. Carrier's results showed that residential and light commercial HVAC in China declined 25% year over year. Panasonic has meaningful exposure to the Chinese residential market, and a prolonged adjustment there would offset gains in data center-related commercial HVAC.

The company's fixed-cost reduction program is more ambitious than Mitsubishi Electric's, but also riskier. The report identifies five companywide risks: weaker-than-expected progress on cost reductions, loss of key personnel during restructuring, delays in portfolio revamping, additional investment burdens from cylindrical battery demand, and adverse currency movements. The battery risk is particularly relevant because Panasonic Energy supplies cylindrical cells to Tesla, and any shift by Tesla toward other battery suppliers would increase pricing pressure on Panasonic's battery business while the company is still investing in capacity expansion.

The read-through from Carrier's results is most positive for Panasonic's HVAC segment in Europe and Asia ex-China. Heat pump demand in Europe grew 20% year over year, and Carrier expects continued strength. Panasonic has a strong position in European heat pumps, and the electrification trend supports multi-year growth. In Asia ex-China, Carrier's 20-35% growth rates across India, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia suggest that Panasonic's regional HVAC business should see similar tailwinds.

The challenge is that Panasonic's HVAC margins are thinner than Carrier's, and the company does not have the same scale advantages in data center cooling. Panasonic will need to demonstrate that its fixed-cost reductions are sufficient to offset raw material cost inflation and competitive pricing pressure. The report applies a 9.0x EV/EBITDA multiple to Panasonic, which is lower than Mitsubishi Electric's 14x, reflecting the market's perspective on execution.

## A Decision Framework for Observers: Three Conditions Determine Which Japanese Industrial Electronics Company Will Deliver on the Data Center Opportunity

The data center cooling opportunity is real, but it is not evenly distributed. Observers should evaluate Mitsubishi Electric and Panasonic Holdings against three conditions that determine whether top-line growth translates into earnings acceleration.

The key metric to watch is not revenue growth but operating profit margin trajectory.

Second, does the company have a credible path to US data center market share? Mitsubishi Electric's chiller expansion into the US is a higher-risk, higher-reward strategy than Panasonic's focus on Europe and Asia. The US market is larger but more competitive, with established players like Carrier, Trane Technologies, and Johnson Controls. Mitsubishi Electric's success will depend on its ability to build a service network and compete on total cost of ownership rather than upfront pricing.

Third, can the company manage its non-HCV exposures without creating earnings drag? For Mitsubishi Electric, the risk is a prolonged adjustment in FA orders from non-data-center end markets. For Panasonic, the risks are more numerous: Chinese residential HVAC weakness, Tesla battery competition, and currency headwinds. The company with the cleanest earnings profile—where data center tailwinds are not offset by other business line headwinds—will deliver the most predictable earnings growth.

The report does not resolve these questions. It provides the data points that allow observers to track them. Carrier's order book is a leading indicator for the entire HVAC supply chain, and the 300% growth in data center orders is a signal that cannot be ignored. But the margin compression at Carrier is equally instructive. In a period of rising raw material costs, the companies that succeed will be those that manage costs as aggressively as they pursue revenue growth.
