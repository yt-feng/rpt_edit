## Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation

Lufax is a leading technology-enabled platform focused on retail and SME financing. We upgrade Lufax to Overweight from Neutral, as its 2Q26 update showed an emerging fundamental recovery, with new loan growth returning to positive territory and leading asset quality indicators improving. We also believe Lufax is relatively resilient to further regulatory tightening, given its below-peer loan IRR. ## Valuation

Our Dec-27 PT of US\$2.00 is based on a P/B-ROE model, assuming a cost of equity of 19.8%, a normalized ROE of 7.6% and a terminal date of 31 December 2028. Our valuation is more sensitive to the normalized ROE assumption: a 1ppt increase/decrease in normalized ROE would raise/lower our PT by approximately 39%, while a 1ppt decrease/increase in the cost of equity would raise/lower our PT by approximately 7-8%.

## Upgrade Lufax to Overweight: Growth and asset quality turn at trough valuation

\- Distressed valuation despite an emerging fundamental recovery. Lufax trades at only a 0.1x forward P/B, close to its historical low (Figure 1), while the operating trajectory is improving. New loans returned to 4.6% y/y growth in 2Q26 vs. a 14.8% decline in 1Q26 and flat y/y in FY25, while the contraction in outstanding loans moderated sequentially (Figure 2). Asset quality also showed signs of turning: The leading indicator C-M3 flow rate declined 20bps q/q, to 1.0%, the 30-day delinquency rate improved 30bps, to 5.8%, and the consumer finance NPL ratio fell 10bps, to 1.3%. While near-term ROE remains subdued, we believe the inflection in volumes and asset quality reduces downside risk to the earnings trajectory and supports a re-rating from the current trough multiple.

\- Lower loan pricing provides greater resilience against potential further regulatory tightening. IRR-cap uncertainty remains the sector's key regulatory overhang, particularly whether the 4x LPR cap, currently applied to micro-lenders, could ultimately be extended to other funding providers. This concern was reinforced by a recent industry self-disciplinary meeting in June (link) that highlighted that overall pricing remains relatively high and requested major platforms to provide data for a sector-wide baseline assessment and a dedicated report to financial regulators. While this does not represent a new pricing rule, we believe the information-gathering exercise could lay the groundwork for future pricing guidance or a broader loan facilitation rate cap. Against this backdrop, we believe Lufax is better positioned than peers: Its blended new loan IRR was only 18.6% in 2025 vs. \~21.8% for peers on average (Table 1), while its consumer finance loan IRR was 17.2%, well below the current 20% cap. Lufax should therefore face less pricing and margin pressure than higher-IRR platforms if loan rate regulation tightens further.

\- Next catalyst in August from interim results, which could improve earnings visibility and offer shareholder return optionality. We expect Lufax to release its 1H26 interim report in August. Unlike the 1Q26 and 2Q26 operational updates, the interim results should provide key P&L information and greater clarity on profitability following the near-completion of the full-guarantee transition. This could help validate whether the recovery in loan growth, asset quality and take rate is translating into improved earnings. Lufax's balance sheet provides additional upside optionality: The current market capitalization is only around $37\%$ of end-FY25 cash, while the company generated positive operating cash flow in both 2024 and 2025. While we do not assume a capital return announcement in our base case, any dividend, buyback or clearer capital management commitment would represent additional upside and could accelerate a re-rating from the current distressed valuation, in our view. As a recap, Lufax previously had a semiannual dividend framework targeting an aggregate payout of $20 - 40\%$ of net profit, although it paid no FY25 dividend because of the reported loss.

## 2Q26 operational update

## What we like

\- New loan growth returned to positive territory, supported by consumer finance. Total new loans increased 5% q/q and 4.6% y/y in 2Q26, to Rmb51.1bn, reversing the 15% y/y contraction in 1Q26 (Figure 4). Consumer finance originations increased 26% q/q and 28% y/y, to Rmb36.9bn, and accounted for 72% of total new loans, up from 60% in 1Q26 (Figure 5). This suggests that the contraction following the 2H25 regulatory tightening is beginning to ease, helped by an ongoing business mix shift toward consumer finance.

\- Outstanding loan balances are showing early signs of stabilization. Total outstanding loans declined 3% q/q, to Rmb167.3bn at end-2Q26, an improvement from the 6% q/q contraction in 1Q26, although balances remained 13.5% lower y/y. Consumer finance loans increased 10% q/q and 20% y/y, to Rmb65.4bn, lifting their share of the total loan book to \~39% vs. 34% in 1Q26. Cumulative borrowers also increased 6% q/q, to 31.4mn.

\- Leading asset quality indicators improved. C-M3 declined 20bps q/q, to 1.0%, reversing the deterioration seen in 1Q26 (Figure 8). The improvement was broad-based: C-M3 for general unsecured loans declined to 1.0% from 1.2%, while secured loans improved to 0.9% from 1.0%. D30 also declined 30bps q/q, to 5.8%, with both unsecured and secured loan delinquency ratios improving. The consumer finance NPL ratio decreased to 1.3% from 1.4% at end-1Q26 (Figure 10).

\- Risk-bearing transition continued to advance despite the headline decline. Excluding the consumer finance subsidiary, Lufax's risk-bearing share increased 170bps q/q, to $95.7\%$ , indicating that the transition toward the full-guarantee model is close to completion. However, the ratio including the consumer finance subsidiary edged down 1ppt q/q, to $93.2\%$ , which we understand mainly reflects an increase in referral volumes under the consumer finance subsidiary, rather than a reversal of the underlying transition. This remains supportive of the credit business take rate, which had already risen to $12.7\%$ in FY25 from $9.6\%$ in FY24 as the risk-bearing transition progressed.

## What we don't like

\- Management turnover adds uncertainty at a sensitive stage of the governance reset. CFO and executive director Mr. Tongzhuan Xi resigned alongside three non-executive directors with backgrounds from parent company Ping An, effective 25 July. Although the departing directors cited personal work arrangements and confirmed no disagreement with the board, the simultaneous departures add to Lufax's recent management instability while the company is still responding to HKEX's comments on its investigations and internal control review. The appointment of Mr. Wai Kin Chim, who has extensive risk management, internal control and board experience, as an independent non-executive director is directionally positive, but a permanent CFO has yet to be appointed, with the internal team temporarily assuming the relevant responsibilities.

\- Lagging delinquency remained elevated despite improvement in leading indicators. D90 increased 30bps q/q in 2Q26, to 3.7%, including a 30bp increase for unsecured loans, to 3.9%, as earlier-stage delinquencies continued to migrate into later buckets. We therefore expect the improvement in C-M3 and D30 to translate into lower reported delinquency and credit costs with a lag. Nonetheless, the turning leading indicators increase our confidence that asset quality pressure is approaching a peak.
