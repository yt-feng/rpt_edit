# Coca-Cola's Q2 Results Prove Its Organic Sales Growth Is Structurally Superior—and the Stock Still Has Room to Run

Coca-Cola delivered 5% unit case volume growth in the second quarter, more than double the 2.2% consensus estimate and far above its own trailing nine-quarter average of 1.1%. The stock rose 5% on the day, but the magnitude of the beat—combined with a sustained competitive advantage that extends well beyond any single quarter's tailwinds—suggests the market has not fully priced in the structural shift underway in the company's growth profile.

The quarter was not without its quirks. An easy -1% year-over-year comparison, the World Cup, hot weather in Europe, and the America 250 celebration all contributed. But even stripping those factors away, the underlying operating momentum is unmistakable. Coca-Cola posted 3% unit case growth in Q1, which at the time was its strongest result since early 2023. The Q2 acceleration confirms that the company has entered a new volume regime, one in which pricing power and volume growth are operating in tandem rather than in tension.

The more important story, however, is what this means for the next several years. Coca-Cola's organic sales growth (OSG) of 7% in Q2 sits in a different category than its mega-cap staples peers. The company is managing a deliberate shift in the balance of its OSG toward volume from outsized price/mix contributions in recent years—and doing so without sacrificing margin or market share. This is not a one-quarter phenomenon. It is the result of multiple structural drivers that together create a durable growth advantage.

## Fairlife Is Becoming a 100+ Basis Point Annual Growth Engine That the Market Still Underappreciates

The most underappreciated catalyst in Coca-Cola's portfolio is Fairlife, the ultra-filtered dairy brand that Coca-Cola acquired full control of in 2020. In Q2, Fairlife grew 18% year over year, even as capacity constraints remained in place. Once those constraints are resolved, the contribution to corporate OSG becomes material.

The math is straightforward. If Fairlife ultimately represents 4% of Coca-Cola's total sales and grows at a 20% compound annual rate, it would contribute roughly 80 basis points to corporate OSG each year. At a 25% growth rate, that contribution rises to 100 basis points. At 30% growth—well within reach given the brand's momentum in high-growth segments like protein shakes and premium milk—the contribution reaches 120 basis points. For a company targeting mid-single-digit OSG, an incremental 100 basis points from a single brand is a structural advantage that few peers can replicate.

Fairlife's competitive moat is twofold. Its proprietary ultra-filtration process concentrates protein and removes lactose, creating a product that competes in both the dairy aisle and the growing functional nutrition space. And it benefits from Coca-Cola's distribution infrastructure, which gives it shelf placement and cold-chain reach that standalone dairy brands cannot match. As capacity ramps up in the coming quarters, the contribution to OSG will become increasingly visible—and increasingly difficult for the market to ignore.

## Coca-Cola's Pricing Power Is Bolstered by Peer Behavior and Structural Industry Dynamics

Coca-Cola's 4% price/mix growth in North America in Q2 exceeded the 2.9% consensus, even as global price/mix came in at 2% due to weakness in Asia-Pacific. The North America result is not an accident. It reflects a pricing environment in which key competitors—PepsiCo and Keurig Dr Pepper—have strong incentives to continue taking price.

Both PepsiCo and KDP face pressure in their ancillary businesses. PepsiCo's snack division is navigating input cost inflation and volume softness. KDP's coffee segment is dealing with volatile green coffee prices. For both companies, carbonated soft drink pricing is a critical profit lever. As long as they continue to raise prices to protect margins in their broader portfolios, Coca-Cola benefits from a rational pricing umbrella that allows it to maintain its own pricing discipline without fear of losing share.

This dynamic is reinforced by Coca-Cola's outperformance in U.S. scanner data. The company has consistently outpaced both beverage peers and mega-cap consumer staples peers in measured retail channels. In Q2 and early Q3, Coca-Cola's U.S. scanner sales growth was approximately 400 basis points ahead of beverage peers and nearly 300 basis points ahead of mega-cap staples peers. These data points matter because U.S. scanner data is the most visible and widely tracked indicator of brand health in the consumer staples universe. Sustained outperformance here drives sentiment, supports shelf space negotiations, and creates a virtuous cycle of reinvestment and share gain.

## The Asia-Pacific Pricing Weakness Is Manageable and Does Not Undermine the Broader Thesis

The most common pushback to the Coca-Cola bull case is the -9% price/mix result in Asia-Pacific in Q2. That number looks alarming on its own, but it breaks down into three roughly equal components: investment timing, affordability initiatives aimed at bringing in new consumers, and geographic mix dilution from faster-growing countries like China.

All three factors are either transitory or strategic. The investment timing element will normalize as the year progresses. The affordability initiatives are designed to expand the consumer base in a region with enormous long-term potential—unit case volume in Asia-Pacific grew 8% in Q2, the strongest of any region. And the geographic mix dilution reflects the fact that Coca-Cola is growing fastest in lower-price markets, which is a volume-first strategy that builds future pricing power as incomes rise.

The company will cycle much easier pricing comparisons in Q4, and full-year 2025 pricing in Asia-Pacific was solid at 4%. The region's 8% volume growth in Q2 suggests that the pricing investments are working: Coca-Cola is gaining share and building habits in markets where long-term consumption per capita remains well below developed-world levels. The temporary pricing drag is the cost of planting seeds for future growth.

## A Three-Part Decision Framework for Investors Evaluating Coca-Cola's Risk-Reward Profile

For institutional investors weighing whether the current valuation leaves room for further upside, a structured framework is useful. The bull-bear range implied by the options market and analyst scenarios suggests a wide dispersion of outcomes, but the base case—26 times 2028 estimated earnings—offers a mid-single-digit premium to mega-cap staples peers despite Coca-Cola's structurally higher OSG. The question is whether that premium is sufficient.

**Step one: Assess the sustainability of the volume growth regime.** Coca-Cola's 5% unit case growth in Q2 was helped by tailwinds, but the Q1 result of 3% was clean. The company's volume trajectory has shifted upward, driven by Fairlife, emerging market expansion, and a portfolio that spans sparkling, water, sports drinks, and tea—each of which grew at or above 4% in Q2. If unit case growth settles in the 2-3% range on a normalized basis, that alone supports mid-single-digit OSG when combined with 2-3% price/mix.

**Step two: Evaluate the Fairlife contribution trajectory.** The capacity ramp is the key variable. If Fairlife can sustain 20%+ growth for the next three years, it will add roughly 100 basis points to corporate OSG annually. That is a structural growth increment that no other mega-cap staples company can match from a single brand. Investors should track Fairlife's revenue growth and capacity expansion announcements as leading indicators.

**Step three: Monitor the competitive pricing environment in North America.** The rational pricing umbrella provided by PepsiCo and KDP is a tailwind, but it is not guaranteed. If either competitor faces a shock that forces aggressive price promotion—a snack volume collapse at PepsiCo or a coffee margin crisis at KDP—the pricing dynamic could shift. For now, the incentives are aligned, but this is the variable most worth watching.
