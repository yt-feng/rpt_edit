Third, can the operating margin reach 21.5% or higher in the second half? Structural efficiencies are running at an annualized run rate of over EUR 600 million, which provides the raw material for margin expansion. The risk is that a higher mix of Electronics revenue, which carries lower initial margins during the project ramp phase, dilutes the overall margin despite absolute profit growth.

Fourth, does cash conversion return above 90%? The 84% conversion ratio in 1H26 is below the levels that long-term observers of Air Liquide expect. If working capital normalizes in the second half and conversion moves back toward 90% or higher, the free cash flow story remains intact. If conversion stays below 85%, the quality of earnings will come under greater scrutiny.

These four questions collectively determine whether Air Liquide is in a transitional period that resolves upward or a plateau that justifies the current valuation. The data from the first half provides positive answers on Electronics growth and pricing momentum, mixed answers on margins and cash conversion, and a negative answer on the second-half growth trajectory relative to expectations. The balance of evidence does not yet support a decisive conclusion, which is precisely why the company faces a period of heightened scrutiny.
