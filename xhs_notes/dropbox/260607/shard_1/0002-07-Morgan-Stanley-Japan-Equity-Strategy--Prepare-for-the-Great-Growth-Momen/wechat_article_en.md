# Japan’s Great Growth Moment Is Not About Cheap Stocks Anymore

The most important shift in Japanese equities today is not whether the market will rise or fall. It is whether the structural bull case can finally transition from a story of valuation repair and corporate governance catch-up into a genuine growth-driven phase. The evidence from a major global investment bank’s June 2026 strategy report suggests that this transition is not only possible but already underway. The report’s base-case TOPIX target of 4,300 by end-June 2027 implies roughly 8% upside from current levels, but the more significant signal lies in the composition of that forecast. For the first time in years, the bull case is not anchored solely to multiple expansion. It is anchored to the idea that Japan’s corporate sector can deliver sustained earnings growth even as the global macro environment becomes more complex.

Why now matters is straightforward. The combination of accelerating AI investment, a stable political backdrop following the LDP’s landslide victory, and the ongoing evolution of the Corporate Governance Code creates a rare alignment of catalysts. The report identifies four structural drivers: sustained stable inflation, expectations of faster growth strategy execution under the current administration, progress in governance reform, and rising household equity investment via NISA. None of these factors are new individually. What is new is that they are now converging at a moment when global investors are reassessing their exposure to Japan. The question is no longer whether Japan has fixed its structural problems. The question is whether the market can price in the next phase of the story: growth.

This article unpacks the report’s core argument, examines the active debate around AI and value rotation, and identifies the unresolved questions that investors need to watch. It is not a summary. It is an analytical framework for understanding what the Great Growth Moment really means and where the risks lie.

## The Bull Case for Japan Equities Has Shifted from Repair to Expansion, and the Numbers Support the Move

For the better part of a decade, the bull case for Japan was built on corporate governance reform, share buybacks, and the slow but steady unwinding of deflationary psychology. The report makes clear that this phase is no longer sufficient. The base-case TOPIX target of 4,300 is supported by a forward P/E of 17.5x, which sits at the 93rd percentile of its 10-year range. That is not a cheap market. It is a market that is beginning to price in a regime change.

The report’s EPS forecasts are telling. For the fiscal year ending December 2026, the top-down estimate is 216, representing 9% year-on-year growth. For December 2027, the estimate rises to 235, another 8% gain. These numbers are below consensus, which stands at 227 and 253 respectively. The report explicitly states that its base-case EPS assumes a lag in pass-through from higher oil costs, roughly 2-3 percentage points below the street. This is a deliberate conservative bias. It means that if the energy shock proves less severe than assumed, the upside to earnings could be material.

The structural underpinning for this earnings resilience is the steady improvement in corporate margins. The report highlights that the sensitivity of TOPIX to USD/JPY has declined meaningfully over the past decade. The regression beta has fallen from around 1.1 in the mid-2010s to approximately 0.4 today. This is not a minor technical detail. It implies that Japanese companies have structurally reduced their dependence on a weak yen for profitability. Higher import prices post-COVID forced firms to improve pricing power and cost control. The result is that earnings are less vulnerable to yen appreciation than in the past. Investors who continue to treat USD/JPY as the sole driver of Japan equity returns are using an outdated playbook.

## The AI Rally Is the Core Growth Engine, But the Strategy for Playing It Has Become More Nuanced

The report’s positioning on AI is unambiguous. The continued AI rally remains the base case. Since the Liberation Day announcement in April 2025, the effectiveness of a strategy that sells expensive AI-related stocks and buys cheaper AI-related stocks has increased. This is not a blanket endorsement of the entire AI complex. It is a call for active differentiation within the theme.

The logic is grounded in the observation that the AI trade has become increasingly crowded and priced for perfection. The report explicitly warns that managing the risk of an AI-rally reversal is equally important as capturing the upside. It identifies puts on stocks that have been bid up on AI narratives but lack fundamental earnings support as one of the more effective risk-management tools in the current environment. This is a pointed message. Not every company that mentions AI in its earnings call will deliver results. The market is entering a phase where narrative alone will not sustain valuations.

The sector preference reinforces this view. The report favors upstream B2B sectors within the supply chain. These are the companies that provide the infrastructure, components, and services that enable AI adoption, rather than the high-profile end-user applications. This is consistent with the view that the most durable value creation in any technology cycle often occurs in the enabling layers, not the final consumer-facing products. The implication is clear: investors should look for companies with tangible revenue exposure to AI capex, not just thematic adjacency.

## Governance Reform Is Still a Catalyst, But the Easy Money Has Been Made

The report dedicates significant attention to the upcoming revision to the Corporate Governance Code, expected around June 2026, and the July shareholder meeting season. The headline message is that investors should be cautious about indiscriminately shorting governance-related stocks. This is a warning against assuming that the governance trade has peaked.

Since the Tokyo Stock Exchange market restructuring in April 2022, the Prime market PBR has risen by approximately 40%. That is a substantial move. But the report argues that the next phase of governance reform will focus on more effective use of corporate cash, which could drive further ROE improvement and support equity valuations through PBR and PER expansion. The NISA program, which is encouraging household equity investment, adds a demand-side tailwind. From January 2027, a new Kids NISA will allow individuals under 18 to invest up to JPY 600,000 per year tax-free.

The critical nuance here is that governance reform is no longer a low-hanging fruit trade. The stocks that have benefited most from the initial wave of buybacks and capital efficiency improvements may now be fairly valued. The next wave will require more fundamental operational changes. Companies that can demonstrate genuine strategic transformation, not just financial engineering, will be the winners. The report does not say this explicitly, but the implication is that passive exposure to the governance theme is no longer sufficient. Active stock selection will be necessary.

## The Bear Case Is Real and Undervalued by the Market, and the Report’s Scenario Gap Is the Key Analytical Tool

One of the most valuable contributions of the report is its explicit quantification of the bull-bear scenario gap. The base case is 4,300. The bull case is 4,900, implying 23% upside. The bear case is 2,600, implying 35% downside. That is a range of 2,300 points, or roughly 58% of the current index level. For context, the bear case forward P/E of 12.0x sits at the 4th percentile of the 10-year range. This is not a tail risk. It is a plausible scenario under specific conditions.

The bear case assumes an escalation of Middle East tensions that drives a sharp oil spike and triggers a global recession. Under this scenario, Japanese equities face both EPS pressure and valuation compression. The report’s macro forecasts already bake in an energy supply shock that modestly drags global growth in 2026, with recovery in 2027. The bear case simply takes this assumption to its logical extreme.

What makes this scenario gap so important is that the market is currently pricing in the base case, but the dispersion of outcomes is unusually wide. The report’s own EPS forecasts are below consensus, which suggests that the sell-side community may be too optimistic about the near-term earnings trajectory. If the macro environment deteriorates faster than expected, the downside could be severe. Investors who are positioned solely for the bull case are taking on significant uncompensated risk.

## The Report Does Not Fully Answer How to Navigate the Intersection of AI Momentum and Macro Risk

For all its analytical rigor, the report leaves one critical question unresolved. How should investors balance conviction in the AI-driven structural bull case with the very real macro risks that could derail it in the near term? The report provides tools for managing AI-specific risk, such as puts on narrative-driven stocks. But it does not offer a clear framework for reconciling the conflicting signals.

On one hand, the structural drivers for Japan are strengthening. On the other hand, the macro outlook is clouded by energy shocks, sticky inflation, and the risk of a global recession. The report’s base case assumes that these two forces can coexist: near-term earnings deterioration and mid-to-long-term earnings normalization. The data suggests that Japan equities are indeed pricing in both. But the sustainability of this coexistence is untested.

The report also does not address the potential for a regime shift in global interest rates. Its forecasts assume the Fed remains on hold through 2026, with 50 basis points of cuts in early 2027. The Bank of Japan is expected to raise rates gradually, reaching 1.3% by end-2027. If inflation proves more persistent in the US or Japan, this path could be disrupted. The impact on Japanese equities would be significant, particularly for growth stocks that are sensitive to discount rate changes.

## A Decision Framework for Positioning in the Great Growth Moment

The report provides the raw material for a decision framework, but it does not synthesize it. Here is a structured approach for readers to apply.

First, assess your conviction in the AI capex cycle. This is the most important variable. If you believe that AI investment will continue to accelerate regardless of the macro backdrop, then the right strategy is to overweight upstream B2B sectors and use options to hedge individual stock risk. If you are less confident, the framework suggests a more defensive posture, favoring companies with strong pricing power and low FX sensitivity.

Second, evaluate your tolerance for the bear case. The 2,600 TOPIX scenario is not a black swan. It is a plausible outcome under specific conditions. The report implies that the appropriate hedge is not to exit the market but to structure positions that can withstand a 35% drawdown. This could mean holding cash, using index puts, or shifting toward sectors with lower cyclical exposure.

Third, monitor the governance reform timeline. The July Corporate Governance Code revision is a catalyst, but its impact will be uneven. Companies that have already made significant progress on capital efficiency may see limited additional upside. Companies that are lagging may face renewed pressure. The report’s caution against indiscriminately shorting governance stocks suggests that the most attractive opportunities may be in the middle: companies that have room to improve but are not yet fully priced for the next wave of reform.

Fourth, do not ignore the NISA flow. The household equity investment channel is a structural demand driver that is often overlooked by institutional investors. The Kids NISA expansion in 2027 will add a new layer of retail participation. This is not a short-term catalyst. It is a multi-year trend that should support valuations, particularly for high-quality domestic names.
