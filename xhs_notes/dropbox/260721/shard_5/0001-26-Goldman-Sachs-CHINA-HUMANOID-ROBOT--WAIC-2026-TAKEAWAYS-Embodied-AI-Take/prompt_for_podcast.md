## Key takeaways

1. AI Model (“VLA + WAM” Convergence): The integration of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with World-Action Models (WAM) has solidified as the industry-wide mainstream standard. This dual-system architecture (“System 1” fast motor control + “System 2” slow cognitive reasoning) significantly improves humanoid task efficiency, spatial-physical reasoning, and cross-embodiment generalization.

2. Data Collection (The Egocentric Shift): Non-embodiment, egocentric video data collection methods have superseded traditional teleoperation as the preferred strategy. This is driving an aggressive volume race among players targeting a hundred-million-hour scale dataset to train robust foundation models.

3. Commercialization Maturity (Divergent Paths): While live demonstrations are proliferating, a stark, multi-tiered divergence exists across end-markets. Logistics handling and structured industrial environments show the highest commercial readiness (e.g., factory floor 1:1 demo zones), whereas household adoption remains a distant frontier due to safety, cost, and reliability constraints.

4. Ecosystem Dynamics (Interconnected Networks): The industry ecosystem is rapidly diversifying into an interconnected network of full-stack startups, traditional robotics vendors, cloud large-model giants, and industrial co-builders, moving away from isolated, vertically integrated silos.

5. Hardware & Supply Chain (Form Factors & Dexterity): Wheel-based form factors continue to dominate lower-body mobility for near-term commercial deployments. ## WAIC 2026 takeaways

VLA (Visual-Language-Action model) + WAM (World-Action-Model) with multiple layers of agents become industry's AI model mainstream, while different players' view on model training data strategy still diverge: "VLA + WAM" has become humanoid industry consensus as almost every embodied AI booth at WAIC 2026 was emphasizing that this convergent "VLA+WAM" stack would improve the overall task efficiency and accuracy, with VLA handling visual/semantic understanding and task grounding while WAM would handle physical dynamics, future-state prediction and action consequences. Robotic AI model players were highlighting the cross-embodiment model capability, which could drive heterogeneous third-party humanoid bodies to conduct long-horizon demos. Despite the pre-training foundations, fully "out-of-the-box" capabilities have yet to materialize, and any commercial deployments and real-world demonstrations still heavily rely on specific environmental trajectory collection and on-site fine-tuning.

Non-embodiment data collection is now the consensus (vs. teleoperation), driving data collection terminals toward diversified and lightweight form factors.

Lightweight devices are proliferating, led by Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) handheld dual-finger grippers, Egocentric (Ego) first-person vision hardware, and exoskeleton data collection gloves. That said, the data collection solutions for dexterous hands have yet to fully converge, with exoskeleton, pure finger-cot, and tactile glove designs coexisting in the market. Global non-embodiment data volume is projected to reach the hundred-million-hour scale within 3 to 5 years from the 10mn-hour scale in 2026E, aiming to trigger the Scaling Law in model capabilities. However, industry views on data standards are diverging, as select teams emphasize that high-quality data - featuring precise tactile and force-control feedback - matters much more than the mere stacking of data hours in enhancing model generalization.

## Demos are proliferating, but with significant tiered divergence in deployment

maturity across end-markets and technical performance among players. Overall, the commercialization progress is advancing rapidly vs. previous conferences but there is still a significant gap between short-term demonstrations vs. hours of continuous high-intensity operations in the real world, and how to break through bottlenecks such as low error tolerance and the autonomous recovery from external interference is the key to determining whether various applications can move from “display” to “mass production.” By end-markets:

In industrial application, logistics sorting and warehouse material handling stands out as one of the most commercially-ready applications, where some leading manufacturers' logistics sorting and palletizing solutions have achieved decent accuracy and tempo, enabling long-term and continuous automated operations with the potential to move from PoC (proof of concept) to volume adoption, in our view. On the other hand, some enterprises suffer from low efficiency (such as long single-box handling time) and frequently encounter positioning or grasping errors. Precision manufacturing and production line assembly show clear divergence as well, where few players can achieve high success rates and long-process coherent operations in specific precision workstations (e.g. wire harness assembly, fine mobile phone assembly, and battery module plugging), whereas some enterprises

experience high failure rates during detailed operations (e.g. screwing and unplugging parts) with operation times still significantly lagging behind human labor.

For retail settings (e.g. pharmacies, catering, and hotels), commercialization exploration is active but mostly stays in the “functional but inefficient” stage, for example, in smart pharmacy displays, although several players have demos that can achieve multi-SKU recognition and grasping, the single-item retrieval time is long (even reaching the minute level), and some food preparation or commercial greeting scenarios still carry strong display and gimmick attributes, with their capability to cope with high-frequency and high-intensity continuous commercial operation still awaiting verification.

■ Household and consumer scenarios, in our view, are still the most difficult sectors for embodied AI deployment due to the high uncertainty, dynamic changes, and non-standard characteristics of the home environment: in household chores (e.g. breakfast making, clothes folding, and item organization), most applications still appear immature. In live demos, tasks involving refined and soft-object operations (such as putting bread into a toaster, folding clothes while keeping them neat upon placement, and cleaning messy desktops) generally have low success rates, with limited gripper ranges of motion, insufficient perception of soft-object deformation, and frequent errors or item drops during operations, indicating that current technology is still far from commercialization.

## Market participant diversification is shaping an interconnected ecosystem.

Embodied AI startups represent the most high-profile group of players during the conference, with many of them claiming to have full-stack control on software and hardware through in-house foundation models, data infrastructures, and multi-form humanoid bodies. Legacy hardware vendors from different fields, such as logistics robots, collaborative robots, and rehabilitation robots, leverage existing customer trust as well as vertical-scenario engineering and focus on reliability breakthroughs in very specific applications. Internet/LLM giants are exploring cross-embodiment AI, i.e. "one brain, multiple bodies", which means a centralized AI that can operate and share learned skills across a wide variety of physical bodies and hardware, leveraging their advantages in computing infrastructure, multimodal large model APIs, and cloud training platforms. On the other hand, vertical industry companies, represented by automakers/auto component suppliers, electronics manufacturers, and pharmacy chains, are trying to transform from robot purchasers into co-builders, by binding with humanoid body makers or model vendors and opening up their production lines, warehouses, retail stores, and hazardous operational environments to jointly build embodied AI training hubs with data feedback loops.

## Wheel-based remains the dominant form factor for lower body, while dexterous hand is the key component in the spotlight from a supply chain perspective.

Wheel-based continues to be the most commonly seen form factor for lower bodies especially for products with live demos. Centaur-type robot is a novel form factor, which is a large quadruped robot base combined with a humanoid upper body with up to 60kg payload could address ultra-heavy-load, complex terrain application scenarios, according to several players. Looking at the upstream, the presence of dexterous hands grew visibly with more players (38 in WAIC 2026 vs. only 9 in 2025) and larger booths at more central locations than previous years. However, dexterous hand technology routes remain fragmented, with direct-driven, linkage and cable-driven designs all on display during the conference, featuring a wide range of 11 to 42 DoFs, and a growing number of dexterous hand makers now pursuing multiple form factors in parallel rather than betting on a single one. Meanwhile, the boundaries of the component-module-body value chain are blurring, with many players seeking a higher content value by expanding their product lines; for example, some dexterous hand makers released ego data-collection devices and even upper-body dual-arm embodied robots, and some reducer makers are also building integrated joint modules.
