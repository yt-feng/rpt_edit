Upgrade to Buy. We upgrade shares of URBN to Buy from Neutral with a 12-month price target of \$93. With Anthropologie brand execution risk now well-appreciated, we believe management actions to reset the assortment will continue to build into 2H, driving stronger consistency of comp and profit execution. More importantly, Anthropologie uncertainty is now embedded in expectations, and we thus see upside to shares as UO brand recovery bolsters profitability. Relative to other specialty softlines peers within our coverage universe, we believe URBN offers a stronger risk/reward.

Anthropologie still an execution risk, but expectations low: Anthropologie brand results have been choppy YTD, with slowing comps and higher markdowns as a result of an inefficient product assortment. We believe management's quick actions to read and react have enabled stabilization at a LSD-MSD% comp rate, and see opportunity for reads from early Fall promotions in July to drive stronger execution this fall/holiday. While we acknowledge the continued execution risk at this brand, we believe investor expectations are now more muted.

Expectations low, creating attractive risk/reward: Investor focus has increasingly shifted toward Anthropologie's execution challenges, resulting in a more cautious outlook despite continued strength elsewhere in the portfolio. We believe this has created a more balanced setup for shares. Recent data points suggest brand momentum trends are stabilizing, particularly among younger consumers where purchase intent has improved meaningfully.

Where could we be wrong? The Anthropologie brand remains the largest driver of near-term stock performance. While we see scope for continued stabilization ahead, we acknowledge several risks to the medium-term outlook for this brand including but not limited to: (1) Fading tailwinds from a favorable fashion cycle; (2) Intensifying competitive pressure from specialty competitors such as ATZ and value-oriented retailers such as Quince, which could erode margins as Anthropologie looks to compete on more fashion-oriented product or items with a higher degree of complexity; and (3) URBN has a history of delivering only limited time periods where all banners in the ecosystem comp positively. Beyond Anthropologie, any slowdown in growth at FP or UO brands could also be perceived negatively.

## Urban Outfitters brand recovery a key profit tailwind

Following several years of weak comp and profitability trends, the UO brand inflected positively in FY26 and is now showing signs of profit recovery. Our brand momentum trackers suggest continued improvement, which we believe will lead to stronger profit delivery as a result of markdown recapture and sales leverage. On our math, a recovery to historical levels of \~MSD% segment margins presents significant upside to consensus estimates by FY27.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired.
