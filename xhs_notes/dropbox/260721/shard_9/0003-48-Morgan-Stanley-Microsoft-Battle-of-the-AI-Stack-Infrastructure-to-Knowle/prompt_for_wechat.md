## Executive Summary

## Azure Insights:

Despite Azure's Strong Secular Position and Modest Acceleration in Growth, Investors Skew Negative Given the Significant Ramp in Capex vs. Still Unproven ROIC. We Think This Sentiment Improves As Near/Medium Term Supply Drives Higher Than Expected Azure Growth for Longer. Over the past year, Azure growth has been constrained by supply of capacity, not by demand. Though Microsoft has brought incremental infrastructure online, management has consistently emphasized that demand continues to exceed available supply, while also highlighting the need to balance available capacity across Azure, 1st-Party applications and internal R&D initiatives. Against that backdrop, management guided Azure to grow 39-40% YoY in CC in F4Q26, while also having enough confidence and visibility to guide to a modest acceleration in C2H26 relative to C1H26, suggesting newly deployed capacity is beginning to convert into revenue at scale. Importantly, this comes at a time when Microsoft has remained fairly out-of-favor with investors at least partly due to the latest Azure growth trajectory relative to both the pace of capex revisions and the accelerating revenue trajectories currently being delivered by other hyperscalers (see Exhibit 4 & Exhibit 5).

Importantly, Investors May Be Underestimating the Magnitude And Durability of the Upcoming Azure Acceleration – Supported by Our Capex-Implied AI Revenue and Our Rev/MW Analyses. Historically, Azure growth has been constrained by available capacity rather than customer demand, creating a backlog of workloads waiting to be deployed. As incremental capacity comes online, Microsoft is no longer simply filling current demand but also working through previously deferred demand, creating the potential for Azure growth to both inflect higher and remain elevated for longer than many investors expect. Given management's confidence in guiding to both near-term acceleration and a stronger growth profile in C2H26, we could potentially see a path beyond mid-40% Azure growth becoming increasingly plausible, particularly as broader cloud migrations remain healthy and AI workloads continue to scale. As such, we raise our Azure revenue estimates in F2H27 / FY28 / FY29 accordingly (see Exhibit 22 for more details).

What Would Make Us Wrong: A lower ceiling on AI margins due to capital intensity and less attach of higher level services causes a more significant gross margin headwind, too difficult to offset with scale and operating leverage, resulting in lower operating margins.
