# EU / Japan Semiconductors: Hybrid Bonding – Logic Adoption Trends and Market Outlook

## Executive Summary

Bonding technologies are essential to enable advanced packaging, and hybrid bonding (HB) represents a key advancement in this field. This report provides our latest view of the current bonding market as well as an update to our market size forecast.

We observe a clear trend for logic applications to adopt die-to-wafer (D2W) hybrid bonding to increase interconnection speed among logic chiplets and between logic and memory. For logic, we estimate 2028 HB market size of $887mn, with 2028 shipment of 237 units (103 prior), reflecting our outlook of stronger adoption at TSMC for SolC packaging. We expect TSMC's SolC capacity to grow approximately 8x from 10kwpm in 2025 to 85kwpm in 2028, for AMD, Apple, Google, and Nvidia applications. Intel is also expected to resume HB capacity expansion from 2027, with the ramp up of Clearwater Forest and Diamond Rapids.

HBM adoption timing is less clear. Samsung may adopt HB in HBM4E (16-hi) in 2027, but if HBM4E turns out to be 12-hi only per Korean media, HB adoption could be postponed to 2028 with HBM5, with few (maybe 1) initial layers only while the remaining stays on TCB. For HBM, we estimate 2028 HB market size of $489mn, and 2028 shipment of 130 units, based on our view that some portion of HBM4E will adopt hybrid, and HBM5 will migrate to hybrid bonding.

We raise our HB market size forecast. We estimate the hybrid bonder market size to reach $1.4bn in 2028, driven by adoption in both logic and HBM. Compared to Besi's market forecast, our 2028 unit shipment estimates of 367 units is 24% above the mid case and 8% below the high case. Our HB market size model is available for download here.

We raise Besi target price to €310, as a technology leader and structural beneficiary of hybrid bonding adoption. Assuming 2028 market share of 76% (80% logic, 70% HBM), Besi's revenue from HB would be $1.1bn, based on unit sales of ~281 units and ASP assumption of $3.75mn per unit. We raise 2027/2028 EPS by 3.6%/23% respectively and our 2028 EPS is now 16.6% above consensus. Based on 40x (45x prior, adjusted to reflect evolving visibility on hybrid bonding) 1-year forward P/E and rolling over a quarter, we set Besi's target price at €310.00 (€280 prior), with 39% potential upside. Reiterate Outperform rating.

For DISCO, we expect the real opportunity would come from hybrid bonding adoption, as dicing/grinding systems with high-cleanness specification would need to be adopted to ensure a particle-free bonding surface, enabling a high yield. Overall setup for DISCO seems favorable into 2027. Current drivers of generative AI in which DISCO partakes is mostly HBM and CoWoS. Whereas from 2027, we expect further adoption of NAND CBA bonding from more players such as Samsung, increased adoption of BSPDN, and die-to-wafer hybrid bonding. In addition, power semiconductors such as SiC is also likely to resume investment. Reiterate Outperform rating.

## LOGIC: CLEAR TRAJECTORY OF HYBRID ADOPTION

Hybrid bonding is expanding in adoption among the various interconnect technologies, especially for leading-edge chiplet architectures. The key driver remains to be the fact that it enables higher interconnect density while lowering power consumption than with conventional micro-bump based approaches.

We see three primary use cases for hybrid bonding:

- XPU-to-XPU chiplet integration (bonding logic on logic): to improve logic transistor count and boost computing power. Examples include AMD's Instinct GPUs utilizing advanced chiplet architectures, as well as future Nvidia multi-die GPU architectures such as Feynman GPUs. Apple's M5 Pro uses unique CPU+GPU chiplet integration named Fusion Architecture.

- XPU+Memory (bonding memory on logic): to break the memory wall, with several variations:
  - The most prominent is bonding XPU with SRAM, and AMD's 3D V-Cache architecture is a good example, already with sizeable production volume. EPYC (Milan-X/Genoa-X) and Ryzen X3D (7800X3D/9800X3D) series have logic die bonded to cache memory die, to realize improved cache capacity as well as lower latency. The Nvidia Groq LPU is also anticipated to use XPU+SRAM integration in the next generation. Intel is also working on their own architecture of HB named Foveros Direct 3D.
  - There is also XPU bonding with DRAM, as shown in China's LongShine CPU which integrates HBM and DRAM on chip.

- CPO (optical communication): most prominently TSMC's COUPE which integrates optical chip (PIC) and electrical chip (EIC) together with hybrid bonding.

For these applications, we expect hybrid bonding capacity to increase rapidly, leading to strong growth of hybrid bonding equipment revenue. In terms of capacity, TSMC and Intel are the leaders.

- For TSMC, current use cases are mainly for AMD CPUs and GPUs mentioned above, and some for Apple (M5 Pro/M5 Max). Going forward, we expect TSMC's HB capacity to nearly double for both this year and next year driven by increased adoption, for Broadcom's 3.5D packaging as well as for Nvidia's Feynman GPUs.

- Intel has already deployed hybrid-bonding based architectures through their Foveros Direct 3D platform and remains strategically committed to advanced 3D packaging. Intel's Clearwater Forest is an instance of their HB adoption, and we expect higher adoption going forward as Diamond Rapids and subsequent CPUs start ramping. As for Intel, we don't anticipate much ramp in HB capacity quite yet in 2026 as they already have 30 units of hybrid bonders, and Diamond Rapids seems to be delayed to 2027, but capacity expansion should happen again from 2027.

In terms of market size, we expect a strong ramp and model the logic hybrid bonding capacity (installed base) to grow 4.5x to 582 units in 2028 from 137 units in 2025.

- We believe the install base at TSMC could reach ~440 units as AP7 followed by AP8 ramps up — from the current ~65 unit fleet mostly in AP6. AP7 capacity expansion occurring in three phases: 1. Initial SolC applications 2. HPC chiplet deployment 3. More broad-based adoption such as wafer-level multi-chip module (WMCM) adoption.

- We expect TSMC's capacity grow ~8x from 10kwpm in end 2025 to 80kwpm in 2028, driven by the adoption of AMD, Apple, followed by Google TPU, Groq LPU, and Nvidia GPU (Feynman).

- At a higher level, we estimate that each kwpm SolC capacity addition requires around 5-6 hybrid bonders, and that the intensity will go down over time as hybrid bonding throughput improves.

- For Intel, we expect them to resume HB capacity addition from 2027 and model the install base to reach ~65 units in 2028 from the current install base of ~30 units, and others (including Samsung) doubling to ~68 units from ~33 units. As a result, we expect the logic HB market size to reach $887mn in 2028, effectively growing eightfold from 2025.

## HBM: UNCERTAINTIES STILL PRESENT IN HB PENETRATION

While hybrid bonding has become increasingly established in logic applications, adoption within HBM is likely to proceed more gradually. The primary reason is that the current TCB solutions remain highly effective and sufficient for many HBM requirements, especially given that 1. JEDEC seems increasingly likely to relax HBM height standards to ~900μm from the current 775μm, 2. HBM4E seems likely to stay mostly at 12-hi 3. Both Samsung and SK Hynix are coming up with new ways to better dissipate heat.

However, in the longer term, we believe factors such as increasing number of layers to stack as well as increase in I/O channels to 4,096 from the current 2,048 eventually would create an incentive to migrate selected layers toward hybrid bonding. We view Samsung to lead the HB adoption followed by SK hynix and Micron, although we believe the majority will continue to be TCB up to 2028. In terms of new capacity addition, we believe TCB shipment should drop after 2028.

Below are our current expectations and potential changes/risks in HBM adoption of HB:

- It looks likely that Samsung could start adoption of HB from 2027 with 16-hi HBM4E, but initially it seems only 1 layer out of 16 layers will use hybrid bonding. However, if HBM 4E only uses 12-hi, then the adoption of HB would be postponed to 2028 with HBM 5.

- Hynix and Micron are likely to follow in 2028, which we expect would be for HBM5. We expect that TCB will still be mainstream technology by then, but TCB shipment will start to decline in 2028 while HB increases steadily.

- As HBM is easier and has lower alignment accuracy requirement, it should be much faster than logic. Every kwpm HBM bonding capacity would only require ~2-3 bonders, less than half of that for logic. Assuming 15% blended penetration in total 1,202kwpm HBM capacity, we derive the HB revenue for HBM bonding to be $489mn and $1.8bn in total combined with TCB.

- Adding HBM and logic demand, we get total HB market size of $1.4bn in 2028.

## BESI: LIFTING 2028 FORECAST FOR HB ADOPTION AND RAISING OUR TARGET PRICE TO €310.00 (€280 PRIOR)

- For logic, Besi is currently the sole supplier, but we conservatively forecast some share loss and assume 80% for Besi in 2028, as Shibaura, ASMPT, TEL may take some share.

- For HBM, we believe the market share for Besi should be lower in HBM than in logic, as the requirement is lower and the Korean suppliers are likely to participate. SEMES, Hanwha, and Hanmi are all likely to become suppliers. We hence assume only 70% market share in HBM for Besi.

- We estimate Besi's HB revenue at €901mn in 2028 assuming 70% market share in HBM and 80% in logic. On the other hand, we expect the conventional business to recover to €843mn in 2028, from ~€484mn in 2025. In aggregate, we estimate 2028 revenue to reach €1.8bn, which is 11% above consensus. As a result, we expect operating margin to improve further due to scale, reaching 48% in 2028. Our operating profit estimate for 2028 is now €874mn, 10% above consensus.

- Compared to Besi's long term revenue model, our 2028 numbers of €964mn for "Engine 2 Revenue" (including hybrid bonding and TCB) is 13% above the midpoint, but 23% below for "Engine 1 Revenue" (conventional). Although, we note that this comparison is based on our 2028 numbers, but Besi's long term guidance does not have a target year.

- Based on 40x (45x prior, adjusted to reflect evolving visibility on hybrid bonding) 1-year forward P/E and rolling over a quarter, we set Besi's target price at €310.00 (€280 prior), with 39% potential upside. Reiterate Outperform rating.

## DISCO: RIDING THE ADVANCED PACKAGING WAVE

- One other major beneficiary from hybrid bonding migration is DISCO. While broad-based shift towards more advanced packaging has already helped DISCO, we expect the real opportunity would come from hybrid bonding adoption, as dicing/grinding systems with high-cleanness specification would need to be adopted to ensure a particle-free bonding surface, enabling a high yield.

- The setup for DISCO seems favorable into 2027. Current drivers of generative AI in which DISCO partakes is mostly HBM and CoWoS. Whereas from 2027, we expect further adoption of NAND CBA bonding from more players such as Samsung, increased adoption of BSPDN, and die-to-wafer hybrid bonding. In addition, power semiconductors such as SiC is also likely to resume investment.

- Disco's valuation is now at 36.5x and has pulled back 32% from the recent peak of 53.9x, but we remain positive on the name as we believe their growth drivers are still intact. Reiterate Outperform.
