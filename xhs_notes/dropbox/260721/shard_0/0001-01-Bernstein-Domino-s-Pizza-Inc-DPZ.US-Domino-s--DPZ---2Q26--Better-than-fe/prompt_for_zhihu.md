## OUR KEY TAKEAWAYS FROM CONVERSATIONS WITH MANAGEMENT

## OVERVIEW

Domino's delivered another mixed quarter, with meaningful order-count growth across both delivery and carryout offset by continued ticket pressure, resulting in a muted $0.1\%$ U.S. same-store sales increase. Management's tone notably shifted from prior quarters, placing less emphasis on competitive pressures and more focus on internal execution, specifically acknowledging that the Premium Series and Slice Sauce failed to resonate with consumers and weighed on ticket performance. At the same time, management expressed greater confidence in the underlying health of demand, highlighting strong transaction growth, increasing loyalty participation, and continued momentum across aggregator platforms as signs that the brand continues to attract new customers. We are encouraged by the strength in order count trends, the contribution from aggregators, and management's belief that upcoming innovation can expand consumption occasions rather than simply shift demand within the existing menu. That said, we think the central question surrounding the upcoming menu introduction is whether it generates a temporary trial benefit or creates a sustainable incremental occasion, particularly given the recent disconnect between test and actual market results. We note that Domino's does not (and has not historically) tested products in the market prior to full launch but has historically seen a high correlation between consumer test/studies and actual results. That said, with increasingly shorter consumer preference cycles and trend relevance we do wonder if management will tweak their testing approach. Additionally, the continued strength of independent pizza operators suggests some consumers may be gravitating toward more specialized pizza offerings, potentially making it more difficult for Domino's to fully capitalize on category growth despite its scale advantage. Overall, the quarter leaves us more cautious on the path back to Domino's long-term algorithm, and we expect the valuation debate to persist until management demonstrates more consistent execution and a clearer ability to convert transaction growth into profitable sales growth.

## PERFORMANCE

\- Operating income: Increased 2.6% in Q2, excluding the impact of foreign currency and refranchising gains from the sale of certain U.S. company-owned store markets in the second quarters of 2026 and 2025, primarily driven by higher U.S. and international franchise royalties and fees.

\- Global retail sales: Grew 3.0% in the quarter, excluding foreign currency impact, supported by global net store growth of almost 1,000 stores over the past 12 months.

• US retail sales growth: 1.9% in Q2, driven primarily by net store growth inclusive of 26 net new stores in the quarter.

\- Carryout comps: Increased 1.1% in Q2.

• Delivery comp: Declined 0.7% in Q2.

• US unit count: Added 26 net new stores in Q2.

\- International retail sales: Increased 4.1%, excluding the impact of foreign currency in the quarter, primarily driven by net store growth over the last year, inclusive of 183 stores in Q2.

## OUTLOOK / INITIATIVES

\- Order Count Growth: Domino's placed significant emphasis on its order count growth, which was driven by its strong carryout and delivery channel performance despite a challenging macroeconomic environment. Management is confident that the company's order count will continue to grow in 2H with aggregators and the loyalty program serving as incremental levers to support this upward trajectory. We think the meaningful increase in Domino's order counts compares favorably against an industry backdrop where QSR transaction trends remained largely unchanged.

\- Execution struggles: Management mentioned that the implementation of the Premium Series and Slice Sauce did not resonate with customers as anticipated and created a drag on ticket growth, as the quarter lapped the popular launch of Stuffed Crust from 2025. We expect greater scrutiny around upcoming product launches and marketing initiatives as the company works to achieve a better barbell balance between ticket and order count growth.

\- Innovative product introduction: The company highlighted a new product arriving in Q3 that is unlike anything previously offered at Domino's, hoping to address an unmet consumer need, expand occasions, and attract incremental customers without disrupting core occasions. We think expectations are now elevated given the significant role this launch appears set to play in the second-half outlook, especially as management appears to have greater clarity around the messaging and execution pitfalls that affected recent product launches.

\- Ticket dynamics: While U.S. same-store sales increased just 0.1% in Q2, management described order count growth as meaningful across both delivery and carryout channels. The primary headwind was average ticket, which was pressured by lapping the successful Stuffed Crust launch from the prior year and the weaker-than-expected consumer response to the Premium Series and Slice Sauce platform. Management expects this ticket drag to moderate in Q3 as the prior-year Stuffed Crust mix becomes less of a headwind, though the success of the upcoming pizza launch will be critical in determining whether the business can restore a healthier balance between transaction growth and ticket expansion.

\- Aggregator partnerships: Management discussed strong growth across Uber Eats and DoorDash, and Domino's is now the leading pizza brand on both platforms. Despite this progress, management believes the company still has substantial runway and market share to capture. We continue to view aggregators as one of the more important drivers of new customer acquisition and order count growth. However, this will require meticulous execution from both a margin preservation and product quality standpoint. Domino's maintains premium pricing on aggregators in effort to achieve profit neutral economics for the franchisees. In addition, the company's orchestration agent optimizes order timing, ensuring pizzas are made closer to pickup and delivered to customers as hot and fresh as possible.

\- Aggregator incrementality: Management indicated that aggregator orders are approximately 50% incremental to the business, suggesting that while some demand is being sourced from existing Domino's customers, a meaningful portion represents new transactions. We view the key debate as whether the incremental volume, customer acquisition, and future frequency opportunities generated through aggregators are sufficient to offset any migration from the company's higher-margin first-party channels.

\- Best Deal Ever: Domino's modified its Best Deal Ever LTO by incorporating Stuffed Crust, which is expected to drive order count growth with modest mix impact from the ticket, given the Stuffed Crust introduction has already been lapped. Management views the promotion as a form of "paid trial" designed to drive repeat purchases over time. We see this as a strategic effort to introduce new customers to one of the brand's strongest recent innovations at an attractive value point, with the goal of increasing long-term customer retention and frequency.

\- International: International same-store sales declined 0.1%, with management again identifying Domino's Pizza Enterprises as the primary drag. DPE remains focused on improving profitability and reducing lower-margin transactions, while China and India continued to perform relatively well. Management also expressed confidence in incoming DPE CEO Andrew Gregory, who joins in August with roughly 30 years of restaurant industry experience, primarily at McDonald's. We think international performance increasingly depends on the pace and effectiveness of the DPE turnaround and view DPE as a business that may need to reset to a lower but more sustainable growth profile.

\- Dine-in recovery: Management noted that QSR pizza category growth has remained consistent with historical trends, but an increasing portion of that growth is currently being driven by dine-in occasions as some consumers return to pre-COVID dining habits. We think this is an important shift, as it may alter where category growth accrues even if overall pizza demand remains healthy.

\- Independent operators: Management highlighted that independent pizza restaurants have been the primary beneficiaries of the resurgence in dine-in demand. We view this as notable because it suggests that consumers may be seeking more specialized or local pizza experiences, potentially limiting the degree to which Domino's can capture all of the category's growth despite its scale, value positioning, and convenience advantages.

\- Unit growth: Management modestly lowered its U.S. net unit growth outlook from 175+ net stores to approximately 175 net stores, citing some pressure on the development pipeline from the macroeconomic environment and weaker franchisee profitability following the challenging start to the year. Despite these pressures, U.S. retail sales increased 1.9% in the quarter, driven primarily by net store growth, including 26 net new store openings during Q2.

\- Franchisee economics: The quarter highlighted the importance of balancing transaction growth with ticket expansion. While strong order counts supported royalties and supply-chain volumes, weaker ticket performance limited the benefit to franchisees. We believe improving unit-level economics remains critical to sustaining the company's long-term development algorithm and franchisee confidence.

\- Guidance largely unchanged: Domino's maintained its full-year outlook, including low-single-digit U.S. same-store sales growth and mid- to high-single-digit operating income growth, while modestly reducing its U.S. net unit growth target from 175+ stores to approximately 175 net new stores as mentioned above. While maintaining the broader financial outlook may initially appear constructive, we believe it effectively raises the bar for the second half given the relatively soft first-half performance and increased dependence on upcoming innovation.

\- Little room for error: Because guidance was unchanged despite another quarter of weak comp performance, the company now appears to require a noticeable improvement in second-half trends. We think the unchanged outlook increasingly relies on successful execution of the upcoming product launch, stabilization in ticket performance, and sustained order-count momentum, leaving less room for additional setbacks over the remainder of the year.
