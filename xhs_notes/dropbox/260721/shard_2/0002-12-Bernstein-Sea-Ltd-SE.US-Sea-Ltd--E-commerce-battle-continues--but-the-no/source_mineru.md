# Sea Ltd: E-commerce battle continues, but the noise is fading

How fierce is e-commerce competition in Southeast Asia? We present thoughts based on the latest on-the-ground checks.

Our proprietary pricing tracker, which follows a fixed basket of products across platforms, continues to point to an industry choosing discipline over disruption. March quarter, prices rose broadly as merchants grappled with higher logistics costs. Importantly, both Shopee and TikTok Shop moved in the same direction. Rather than fighting for every order, they appeared increasingly focused on protecting economics. Sea's subsequent margin improvement only strengthened that narrative.

The latest three months tell a similar story. Prices are still rising across most markets, though the source of inflation has shifted. Previously, higher prices came from increases in post-discount listing prices. Today, the lift is coming less from what sellers ask and more from what platforms give away. Vouchers are fading into the background, and consumers are paying a little closer to sticker price.

Yet the two are following different scripts. Shopee has kept headline prices largely unchanged while quietly pulling back on coupon subsidies, allowing realised selling prices to climb. TikTok Shop has also eased promotional intensity, but softer delivery charges have absorbed much of the impact, leaving consumer prices comparatively contained. This has resulted in Shopee is appearing less often as the cheapest platform relative to TikTok Shop. But the gap is not wide and hence it does not represent any major change in competitive intensity. While growth focus remains - we are seeing attempts to keep margins intact.

## DETAILS

Price increases continued across ASEAN this quarter, although the drivers differed from those observed previously. Shopee kept listing prices broadly stable across markets, generally within a range of -5% to +5%, but sharply reduced coupon discounts, resulting in higher final consumer prices. This approach should be margin accretive, even if it comes at the expense of some headline growth. TikTok Shop took a different route. While it also reduced coupon discounts, the impact was largely offset by lower delivery charges paid by consumers, helping limit increases in final prices across several markets. Listing discounts meanwhile recovered after declining last quarter, potentially reflecting improving merchant confidence as concerns around elevated fuel costs eased.

These differing approaches translated into changes in relative competitiveness. The proportion of products priced cheaper on Shopee declined across all markets in our sample, reflecting the sharper increase in final prices on the platform. However, this does not appear to signal a meaningful shift in competitive intensity. Last quarter, we highlighted the platforms' ability to pass through higher costs, particularly fuel-related increases linked to the MENA situation. The latest data suggests a continuation of that trend, albeit through different mechanisms. After nearly two years of persistent price declines, Sea appears increasingly focused on balancing growth with margin improvement, while TikTok Shop remains more focused on protecting consumer value by offsetting lower vouchering with reduced delivery charges.

Despite the narrowing of its pricing advantage, Sea remains more competitively priced than TikTok Shop across most ASEAN markets in our sample. Vietnam was the only exception, where a sharper cut in TikTok Shop prices allowed it to regain some ground, although Sea remains more competitive overall. Across the rest of the region, higher price increases on Shopee shifted relative pricing modestly in TikTok Shop's favour, but Sea remains either the cheaper platform or broadly at parity in most markets. Overall, the data points to rational pricing behaviour rather than any meaningful escalation in competition.

## ECOMMERCE MARGINS

E-commerce margins at Sea Ltd have improved materially over the years, but have largely remained within a similar range over the past few quarters (September to March). This plateau reflects a deliberate choice to prioritise growth, with investments accelerating in logistics and customer acquisition. The key concern has been whether margins could deteriorate further, particularly if TikTok Shop adopts a more aggressive stance. However, both our view and market consensus are that margins should remain resilient despite ongoing investments. Recent price increases also suggest there may be scope for modest margin expansion ahead.

Sea Ltd has significant dependence on one game - FreeFire, hence a moderation in active user base as they move to other games is a key risk.
