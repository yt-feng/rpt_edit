# The 618 Results Reveal a Structural Shift: Premium and Foreign Brands Are Reshaping China's Beauty Market, Not Just Winning a Single Shopping Festival

The 2026 618 shopping festival on Douyin has concluded, and the data tells a story that goes far beyond a single sales period. Based on tracking of the top 500 cosmetics brands from May 15 to June 18, the platform grew by the mid-30s year-on-year. But the headline number masks a more important development: the market is undergoing a decisive consolidation toward the top, with a clear bifurcation between winners and laggards. The top 50 brands grew at roughly 40% year-on-year, while brands ranked below 100 managed only high-20s growth. This is not a broad-based recovery; it is a concentration of power.

What makes this 618 particularly significant is the reversal of a long-standing narrative. For several quarters, domestic brands had been gaining share, especially in the mass and mid-market segments. The 2026 618 data challenges that assumption head-on. Multinational corporations (MNCs) and higher-end brands are now leading the growth, and the gap is widening. This is not a temporary fluctuation. It reflects a fundamental shift in consumer preference, platform dynamics, and brand strategy that investors need to understand as a structural trend, not a seasonal blip.

The implications are stark. For domestic brands that built their success on mass-market appeal and aggressive discounting, the window is closing. For premium brands—both foreign and domestic—the opportunity is expanding. And for investors, the key question is no longer whether the market is growing, but who is capturing the value.

## The Premium Segment Is Decoupling from the Mass Market, and the Growth Gap Is Accelerating

The most important finding from the 618 data is the stark divergence between premium and mass-market performance. Luxury and premium brands on Douyin recorded solid results, accelerating from mid-20s to high-20s year-on-year growth in May to high-30s and even high-50s growth in June to date. In contrast, mid-high end and mass market brands moderated sharply, from 40s growth in May to high-teens or twenties in June.

This is not a one-month phenomenon. Looking at the combined Tmall, Douyin, and JD data from the broader report, the trend has been building since late 2025. MNC luxury brands have consistently outperformed domestic premium brands in recent months, and MNC premium brands have accelerated while domestic mid-to-high end brands have stagnated or declined. The 618 data confirms that the premium segment is not just holding up; it is pulling away.

The strategic implication is clear: consumers are trading up, not trading down. In a market that many assumed would see value-seeking behavior amid economic uncertainty, the opposite is happening. The premium segment is becoming a safe haven for growth, while the mass market faces margin compression and slowing volumes. Brands that cannot command a premium price point are increasingly fighting for a shrinking pool of value-conscious consumers, while premium brands are capturing the discretionary spending of a more resilient demographic.

## Multinational Brands Are Regaining Share, Driven by a Portfolio Strategy That Domestic Players Cannot Replicate

The 618 data shows that Western brands achieved a roughly 140% completion rate against their 618 targets, compared to 110% for local brands. Japanese brands were roughly on par with the prior year, but with significant variation underneath. This is not a story of a single blockbuster product. It is a story of portfolio power.

Take the Estee Lauder Group, which led MNCs with 56% year-on-year growth in Douyin for the full period, accelerating to 75% in June 1-18. The strength came from higher-end brands: Estee Lauder itself at 167% completion and La Mer at 154%. Similarly, L'Oreal Group achieved 33% growth and 130% completion, driven by Skinceuticals at 190% and YSL at 176%, while mass-market brands like L'Oreal Paris lagged. Beiersdorf delivered 146% completion, powered by La Prairie at 168%.

The pattern is unmistakable. MNCs are winning not because of any single brand, but because they have a ladder of brands that allows them to capture consumers at multiple price points. When a consumer trades up from L'Oreal Paris to Lancome, or from a domestic brand to Estee Lauder, the MNC captures that value internally. Domestic players, with their narrower portfolios, cannot do this. They win or lose on a single brand's performance.

This portfolio advantage becomes even more important as the market consolidates. The top 50 brands are growing faster than the rest, and MNCs dominate that tier. The implication for domestic brands is sobering: unless they can build a multi-brand portfolio that spans price points, they will struggle to retain consumers as they trade up.

## Mao Geping's Performance Proves That Domestic Brands Can Win at the Premium End, But the Strategy Is Different

There is one notable exception to the MNC dominance in premium: Mao Geping. The brand achieved 54% year-on-year growth for the May 1 to June 18 period, with a remarkable 91% growth in June 1-18 alone, leading to a 150% full-period completion rate. This is particularly impressive given the softer sales in May, which the report attributes to deliberate pacing and competitive strategy.

Mao Geping's success is instructive. It is a domestic brand competing at the premium price point, and it is winning. But the strategy is different from the mass-market domestic brands that grew rapidly in previous years. Mao Geping is not competing on price; it is competing on brand equity, product quality, and a disciplined approach to promotional events. The pattern of deliberate pacing during major shopping festivals has been observed before, including during Double 11, and it has consistently delivered solid top-line growth and margin profiles.

This suggests that domestic brands can succeed at the premium end, but it requires a fundamentally different approach. Mass-market domestic brands grew by riding the wave of platform subsidies and aggressive discounting. Premium domestic brands must build genuine brand value that justifies a higher price point. Mao Geping has done this, but the question is whether other domestic brands can replicate this model. The data suggests that most cannot. Brands like Bloomage and Chicmax remained under pressure, with completion rates of 77% and 63% respectively.

## The Data Raises Unanswered Questions About Sustainability, Platform Dependency, and the Role of Discounts

While the 618 data is revealing, it also leaves several critical questions unanswered. The first is sustainability. The 618 period is a concentrated promotional event. The question is whether the premium brand growth can be maintained in non-promotional periods, or whether it is pulled forward by heavy discounting and platform subsidies. The report notes that JD and Douyin are offering smaller discounts compared to last year's 618, which is a positive sign for margin sustainability. But the full picture will only emerge when we see the July and August data.

The second question is platform dependency. Douyin has been the fastest-growing platform for cosmetics, but its dynamics are different from Tmall or JD. Douyin is more influencer-driven and more volatile. The report mentions that it will host a cosmetics expert call focusing on Tmall and comprehensive operational metrics, including discounts, platform subsidies, government subsidies, and product returns. These factors could significantly alter the profitability picture. A brand might look strong on Douyin but weak on Tmall, or vice versa. The full 618 picture requires a multi-platform view.

The third question is the role of government subsidies. The report mentions that local brand rebounds in May were "likely on subsidies." If premium brand growth is being subsidized by government stimulus, the sustainability of that growth is questionable. Investors need to understand the extent to which the 618 results are organic demand versus policy-driven demand.

Finally, there is the question of Japanese brands. While the aggregate data shows Japanese brands on par with prior year, the June slowdown is notable. Shiseido Group declined 21% in June 1-18, and Decorte and SK-II saw weakness. This could be a temporary trend or the beginning of a structural decline. The geopolitical context cannot be ignored, and the report's data does not fully address this risk.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: How to Evaluate Cosmetics Brands in a Two-Speed Market

Given the structural shift revealed by the 618 data, investors need a new framework for evaluating cosmetics companies. The old framework, which focused on total market share and growth rates, is no longer sufficient. The market is splitting into two speeds: premium and mass. The following decision framework can help investors identify winners and losers.

First, evaluate brand portfolio breadth. A single-brand company is inherently riskier than a multi-brand company, especially if that brand is in the mass market. The MNCs are winning because they have brands at every price point. Domestic companies with a single mass-market brand face an uphill battle. Investors should ask: does the company have a credible premium brand, or a path to building one?

Second, assess pricing power. The 618 data shows that premium brands are growing faster and with better margins. Brands that rely on discounting to drive volume are in a structural decline. Investors should look at average selling price trends, discount depth, and the ratio of promotional to non-promotional sales. Companies that can maintain or increase prices while growing volume are the clear winners.

Third, analyze platform strategy. Douyin is not the same as Tmall or JD. A brand that over-indexes on Douyin may be more volatile and more dependent on influencer relationships. Investors should look for brands with balanced platform exposure and a clear strategy for each channel. The report's upcoming expert call on Tmall metrics will be critical for this analysis.

Fourth, monitor margin trends. The 618 data focuses on GMV, but the real test is profitability. Brands that grow GMV through heavy discounting and platform fees may be destroying value. Investors should look for brands that are growing GMV while maintaining or improving gross margins. The report's observation that Mao Geping delivers solid results in both top-line growth and margin profile is a positive signal.

Finally, consider the competitive moat. For premium brands, the moat is brand equity, product innovation, and distribution exclusivity. For mass-market brands, the moat is scale and cost leadership. The 618 data suggests that brand equity is becoming more valuable than scale. Investors should favor companies with strong brand equity, even if their scale is smaller.

## The Full Picture Requires Going Beyond Douyin and Beyond GMV

The 618 data from Douyin provides a powerful snapshot, but it is only one piece of the puzzle. The full report includes data from Tmall, JD, and Douyin, and it covers not just GMV but also operational metrics like discounts, subsidies, and product returns. These factors can dramatically change the profitability picture.

For example, a brand that achieves 50% GMV growth but does so through 40% discounts and high platform fees may be less attractive than a brand that achieves 20% growth with minimal discounting. The report's upcoming expert call, which will focus on Tmall and comprehensive operational metrics, is essential for understanding the true economics of the 618 period.

Moreover, the 618 data does not capture the full year. The report shows that 618 (May plus June) contributed roughly 75% of second-quarter online GMV and about 21% of full-year GMV. That means 79% of the year is still to come. The trends observed in 618 may or may not persist. Investors need to watch the July and August data closely to determine whether the premium shift is structural or seasonal.

There are also unanswered questions about the role of government subsidies, the impact of geopolitical tensions on Japanese brands, and the sustainability of Douyin's growth. These factors could alter the trajectory in the second half of the year.
