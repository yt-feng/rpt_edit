EN_A: What does that number actually tell us?
EN_B: It tells us that investment growth is tightening while output is still growing. In other words, companies are no longer blindly expanding. They are absorbing existing capacity. That is a sign that capacity rationalization is moving forward.

EN_B: It means credit risk in manufacturing is gradually easing. One key data point is that medium to long term industrial loan growth slowed to 5.9 percent year on year in the second quarter, down from 6.8 percent in the first quarter.
EN_A: Slower loan growth usually sounds like bad news though.
EN_B: In this context, it is actually the opposite. When you have overcapacity, slower credit growth means money is not flowing into inefficient expansion projects. It is flowing into more rational uses. That helps the financial system de-risk.
