## Investment thesis

## Earnings skepticism priced-in, Dividend upside ahead

Moving into the 1H26 reporting season in mid to late August, we prefer life insurers over non-life insurers, and H shares over A shares. Our H-share pecking order is Ping An-H, China Life-H, CPIC-H, PICC Group-H, PICC P&C and New China Life-H. For A shares, valuations are less compelling, with average multiples at a 35% premium to H-listed peers (Table 2), while A-share insurers' FY26E dividend yields of 2.6%-5.3% also look less attractive. Our A-share preference is Ping An-A, CPIC-A, China Life-A, New China Life-A and PICC Group-A.

Despite trading at 5x/6x FY26E P/E with a 5%/4% dividend yield for H/A-listed insurers, China insurers have remained out of favour YTD, with China Life-H and PICC P&C the only two outperformers across A- and H-shares. We think the key debate is no longer whether earnings are recovering, but whether investment-led earnings strength can convert into visible capital return.

We believe that the August interim results season could be the turning point, with dividend guidance, capital deployment and core earnings recovery likely to matter more than headline profit growth. We roll forward our PT timeframe from Dec-26 to Dec-27 and raise our PTs by 5% on average, reflecting steady business recovery, stronger solvency capital and a better core earnings outlook which drives enhanced cash generation abilities. Ping An-H remains our top OW, followed by China Life-H and Ping An-A.

## Time for capital return to do the talking

China's H-listed insurers trade 5x FY26E P/E on average with a $5.0\%$ dividend yield, which we view as attractive given still undemanding valuations. Yet the sector has remained out of favour YTD across both H- and A-shares, with the only exceptions of China Life-H and PICC P&C. This is notable given strong 1Q26 results across most key financial metrics, confirming that fundamentals remain on track.

In our view, sector underperformance reflects the gap between reported earnings strength and investor confidence in dividend delivery. Much of the earnings upside appears investment-led, helped by equity market gains, while investors remain unconvinced that these gains will translate into higher shareholder returns amid rising macro and political uncertainty.

Against this backdrop, the mid- to late-August interim results season could be an important window for the sector. We believe management commentary on dividends, capital deployment and core earnings recovery could prompt investors to revisit the risk-reward, similar to the sector rotations seen in 2H24 and 2H25 (Figure 5/Figure 6).

To frame the opportunity, we start with the positive profit alerts released by several major insurers in early July. In Table 3, we summarise the profit alert dates, companies' 1H26 earnings guidance versus FY26 consensus net profit, and share price reactions in the week after the alerts. As Table 3 shows, strong profit growth was not well perceived by the market, which we attribute mainly to concerns over earnings quality and equity market sensitivity.

More importantly, despite strong profit alerts suggesting that China Life and New China Life could achieve almost all current full-year consensus profit in 1H26, the pace of FY26E earnings upgrades has been surprisingly slow for both names (Figure 7/Figure 8). Based on current FY26E net profit consensus of Rmb139B and Rmb31B for China Life and New China Life, respectively, the market appears to be now assuming limited earnings delivery in 2H26.
