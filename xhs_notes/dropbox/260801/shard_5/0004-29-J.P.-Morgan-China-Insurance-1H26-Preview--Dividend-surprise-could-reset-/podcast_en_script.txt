EN_A: So I just read a really interesting preview on Chinese insurers, and the headline is pretty bold. It says a dividend surprise could reset the whole trade. What does that actually mean?
EN_B: It means the market is no longer asking whether insurers can grow earnings. They clearly can. The first quarter results were strong. The real question is whether that earnings growth is trustworthy, or whether it is just driven by the stock market going up.
EN_A: So the market is skeptical of the profit quality?
EN_B: Exactly. The report points out that if the Shanghai Composite Index moves 10 percent, it would swing consensus net profit by 45 percent on average. That is a huge sensitivity. So when insurers post big profit gains, investors just think it is the equity market doing the work, not the core business.
EN_A: And that is why the stock prices have not reacted even to strong earnings?
EN_B: Right. China Life and New China Life both issued very strong profit alerts in early July. But the market reaction was muted, and in one case the stock actually fell after the alert. That tells you how much skepticism there is.
EN_A: So what is the report saying could change that?
EN_B: Dividends. Specifically, interim dividends. The report forecasts that the sector could see an average 15 percent year on year increase in interim dividends. And that could be the catalyst that resets the whole investment case.
EN_A: Why dividends and not just higher profits?
EN_B: Because dividends are the proof that the profits are real. If insurers are willing to distribute part of their unrealized investment gains, backed by strong solvency, that shows confidence. It also gives shareholders a tangible return. Right now, the market does not believe the profits will ever reach shareholders.
EN_A: And how does the report see the consensus?
EN_B: Consensus is forecasting only 4 percent dividend growth for the full year. The report thinks that is too conservative and models 7 percent. For interim dividends, they see 15 percent growth. So there is a real gap between what the market expects and what could actually happen.
EN_A: That is a big gap. Which companies are best positioned?
EN_B: The top pick is Ping An H shares, followed by China Life H shares. Ping An has the highest dividend yield in the sector, around 5.8 percent on the H shares, and trades at only 6 times forward earnings. China Life has a lower yield, but the report sees much more upside to dividends, potentially 20 percent growth versus consensus at 7 percent.
EN_A: And why H shares over A shares?
EN_B: Because H shares are cheaper and offer higher yields. The report notes that A shares trade at a 35 percent premium to H shares on average, but with lower dividend yields. So from a risk reward perspective, H shares are simply more attractive.
EN_A: Is there anything else supporting the fundamentals?
EN_B: Yes. The report expects new business value to grow by double digits over the next 12 months, around 17 percent on average. Agent productivity is improving, headcount is stabilizing, and bancassurance is steady. In non life, PICC P&C is expected to keep its combined ratio around 94.7 percent, which is solid.
EN_A: So the fundamentals are fine, the valuations are cheap, but the market is just not buying it yet?
EN_B: That is exactly the situation. The market is waiting for proof. And the report thinks the proof will come in the form of dividends during the August results season. If insurers deliver a dividend surprise, the trade could reset quickly.
EN_A: What are the risks if that does not happen?
EN_B: Then the cheap valuation is just cheap for a reason. Without a catalyst, the sector could stay out of favor. The report also mentions that China Life could do buybacks, but that needs regulatory approval. So there are two potential catalysts, but they both need to show up in the results season.
EN_A: So what should investors be watching for?
EN_B: Two things. First, the interim dividend announcements, especially from China Life and Ping An. Second, what management says about capital deployment and buybacks on the earnings calls. If both are positive, this could be the start of a re-rating. If it is just profits with no dividend follow through, the market will stay skeptical.
EN_A: Got it. So the key takeaway is that profit growth alone is not enough. It has to turn into cash returns.
EN_B: Exactly. Dividends are the bridge between reported earnings and shareholder value. And the August results season is when we will see if that bridge is actually built.
