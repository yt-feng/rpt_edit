## Minor Beat of FQ3'26 with Soft FQ4 Outlook:

\- FQ3'26 delivered only a modest beat. Revenue at \$109.4B, up 16.4% YoY, toward the middle of management's guided range of 14-17% growth. EPS at \$2.02, but excluding the \$0.11 benefit from tariff refunds, underlying EPS of \$1.91 was only modestly ahead of consensus at \$1.89. Reported gross margin was 50.1%; adjusting for the roughly 200 bps benefit from tariff refunds, underlying gross margin was 48.1%, essentially at the midpoint of the company's 47.5-48.5% guidance range.

\- FQ4'26 guidance came in below expectations. Apple guided September quarter revenue growth to 9-11% YoY, implying a meaningful slowdown from the mid-teens growth seen in the past three quarters - driven by both FX headwind and supply constraint.

\- Supply constraints were primarily driven by the limited availability of the advanced nodes which SOCs are produced on. Into September quarter, Apple continue to expect high levels of demand. However, with less flexibility in supply chain, the impact from the supply constraints is expected to increase significantly. The projected supply constraints in the September quarter, will affect iPhone, Mac, and iPad broadly.

\- Gross margin is expected to be 47-48%, including a 100 bps benefit from tariff refunds. At the midpoint, this implies roughly 160 bps of sequential margin compression versus FQ3's adjusted gross margin of 48.1%, reflecting ongoing memory cost inflation and the exhaustion of benefit from carry-in inventory. Management also indicated that September quarter Services growth should track roughly 9.5%, consistent with June quarter growth excluding the 2.5% FX headwind, while iPhone revenue is expected to grow in the mid-teens, down from the 20%+ growth rates seen in recent quarters.

Product revenue reached \$78.7 billion (vs. consensus of \$77.2 billion), up 18% YoY, driven almost entirely by double-digit growth in both iPhone and Mac. Product gross margin was 40.1% (vs. consensus of 36.4%), improving 140 bps sequentially, including a favorable 250 bps benefit from tariff refunds. iPhone revenue at \$54.3 billion (vs. consensus of \$53.6 billion), up 22% YoY. Mac revenue was particularly impressive at \$10.4 billion (vs. consensus of \$8.6 billion), growing 29% YoY, supported by strong demand for the MacBook Neo and MacBook Pro. Management specifically highlighted two AI workload use cases for Mac during the earnings call: 1) using Mac mini as a powerful platform for agentic AI and 2) deploying clusters of Mac Studio systems to run frontier-class models locally. The good result in Mac and iPhone were achieved despite ongoing supply constraints, which were most pronounced in the Mac portfolio and, to a lesser extent, in iPhone and iPad. Looking ahead to the September quarter, supply constraints are expected to become more broad-based, affecting iPhone, Mac, and iPad, with limited flexibility across the supply chain to meaningfully mitigate the impact.

Services revenue reached \$30.7B (missed consensus of \$31.4 billion), up 12% yoy though decelerating from the 16% growth in the March quarter primarily due to FX, with softer mobile gaming, App Store business-model and regulatory changes, and a tough comp against the F1 theatrical release also weighing on growth. Despite the deceleration, the underlying franchise remains healthy: Apple set revenue records in every Services category, including all-time highs in cloud services and payment services, surpassed 1.5B paid subscriptions, and posted all-time-high transacting and paid accounts with double-digit growth in emerging markets. Management expects September quarter's underlying Services growth before FX impact to remain broadly similar to June (12%), but they anticipate an incremental 2.5pt FX headwind, which implies reported growth of roughly 9.5% yoy. Going forward, we expect Apple's 2.5B+ active-devices installed base to remain the key long-term support for this segment.

Total company gross margin of 50.1% (up 80bp qoq) came in better than the Street feared, largely driven by a roughly 2-point tariff-refund benefit; excluding this benefit, gross margin was \~48.1%, at the midpoint of prior guidance. Services margin softened 110bp qoq to 75.6% on mix, while product margin of 40.1% got the bulk of the tariff lift. The real story is Memflation: management confirmed costs stepped up sequentially again in the June quarter and expects a further increase in September, partially cushioned by carry-in inventory benefits and lower non-memory BOM costs, although the inventory benefit is expected to diminish beyond September. Management also explicitly framed the Mac and iPad price increases as a reluctant response to what Cook characterized as a “hundred-year flood” in memory pricing.

Management commented that the DRAM industry remains concentrated among three primary suppliers, and that additional suppliers could help alleviate supply constraints and potentially pricing. Apple is therefore exploring other options in DRAM sourcing. If the company decides to move forward with a fourth supplier we believe this may be CXMT, although such a move

could attract political backlash.

This memory pressure is already embedded in the September GM guide of 47%–48% gross margin, which includes a \~1-point tariff refund benefit versus \~2 points this quarter. At the midpoint, the GM guide reflects 260bps qoq decline, of which 100 bps reflect the lower tariff benefit, while tariff-adjusted GM declines 160bps. Management indicated that more than 100% of this adjusted decline is attributable to memory inflation, with other cost and mix benefits partially offsetting this pressure.

Tweaked estimates down reflecting Memflation, higher R&D costs, FX, and supply constraints. We lower our EPS to \$10.03 and \$11.28 for FY27 and 28 respectively on slightly weaker revenue, lower product gross margins due to memory costs and higher R&D costs. We remain 5% above consensus for FY28.

We rate Apple Outperform, incr. TP to \$370 on 33x FY28 EPS or 29.5x EV/FCF (prev. FY27). We worry about rising memory cost pressures but see Apple's position as unrivaled in consumer electronics with the potential to continue to gain share in smartphones and PCs. AI also presents a new opportunity for Apple which will take longer to play out.
