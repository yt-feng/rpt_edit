# The Japanese Cosmetics Sector Is Entering a Margin-Correction Phase That Core-Brand Strategies Alone Cannot Solve

The Japanese cosmetics industry is facing a structural test that its current earnings trajectory does not yet reflect. Based on April-to-May data, domestic shipments have improved sequentially from the January-to-March quarter, but this modest recovery is being undercut by a sharp rise in imports and a slowing sales environment in China. The result is a competitive landscape where market share is being contested on multiple fronts simultaneously. For the four major listed players—Shiseido, Kosé Holdings, Pola Orbis Holdings, and Rohto Pharmaceutical—the upcoming April-to-June earnings reports will likely meet guidance, but meeting guidance is no longer sufficient. The real question is whether any of these companies have a credible strategy to grow their core brands while simultaneously lifting overall margins in an environment where input costs, channel dynamics, and consumer behavior are all shifting.

This is not a cyclical dip. It is a structural rebalancing of how value is created in the sector. The report from a global investment bank covering these companies makes this clear through its detailed estimates and external data points. The numbers tell a story of divergence: some companies are gaining ground in specific sub-segments, but the aggregate picture is one of margin compression and rising competitive intensity. For investors, the critical insight is that the next quarter will not provide the catalyst many are hoping for. Instead, it will force a reassessment of which companies can execute on both growth and profitability simultaneously—a combination that has become increasingly rare in this sector.

The implications extend beyond Japan. Chinese consumer data shows a deceleration in cosmetics sales at large retail stores, and import volumes continue to climb. This means that the Chinese market, long a growth engine for Japanese premium brands, is becoming a source of margin pressure rather than expansion. Companies that relied on China for volume growth must now compete with both local players and other international brands in a market that is no longer expanding rapidly. The strategic question is not whether to be in China, but how to compete there without destroying margins.

This article will analyze the specific positions of the four covered companies, identify what the report does not fully answer, and provide a decision framework for investors navigating this uncertain landscape.

## The Modest Sequential Improvement in Domestic Shipments Obscures a Deeper Erosion in Pricing Power and Market Share

The report notes that April-to-May domestic cosmetics shipments likely improved versus the first quarter. On the surface, this is positive. However, the improvement is relative to a weak base, and it masks a more troubling trend: imports are rising sharply, meaning that domestic brands are losing share in their home market. The METI data and point-of-sale information cited in the report reveal a fragmented picture where no single company is clearly outperforming the market across all categories.

For Shiseido, the external data is particularly concerning. The company's mid- to low-priced cosmetics sales in April-to-May were slightly below the market average for both skincare and makeup. This is a warning signal for a company that has historically commanded premium positioning. If Shiseido is losing ground in its domestic mid-range segment, it suggests that the brand's equity is not translating into purchasing behavior at the price points where volume growth occurs. The company's core operating profit growth forecast of JPY 5.4 billion year-on-year to JPY 20.5 billion in the April-to-June quarter is driven by profit recovery in China and travel retail, plus the elimination of losses in the Americas. But these are restructuring gains, not organic demand improvements. They are one-time in nature and do not signal a sustainable margin trajectory.

Kosé Holdings presents a different but equally challenging picture. The report estimates that the company's April-to-May mid- to low-priced cosmetics sales slightly outperformed the market average for skincare but were in line to slightly below for makeup. This mixed performance comes after upfront marketing investments in January-to-March that were supposed to generate returns. The fact that the payoff is only partial suggests that marketing spend alone is not enough to drive sustained outperformance. Kosé's operating profit estimate of JPY 5.6 billion, up JPY 900 million year-on-year, represents an improvement from the first quarter, but the report explicitly states that this does not increase the likelihood of meeting the FY2026 guidance. This is a critical admission: the company is on track to deliver a better quarter, but not a better year.

Pola Orbis Holdings faces the most pronounced structural challenge. The report estimates that overall sales will grow only 1% year-on-year in April-to-June, with the flagship POLA brand declining 3% while the mass-market ORBIS brand rises 5%. This divergence within the same company highlights a broader industry tension: premium brands are struggling to maintain volume, while mass-market brands are capturing whatever demand exists. The estimated operating profit decline of JPY 200 million to JPY 3.9 billion underscores that Pola Orbis is not generating sufficient operating leverage to offset the mix shift toward lower-margin products.

Rohto Pharmaceutical stands out as the relative bright spot. The company's estimated 5% constant-currency sales growth in April-to-June, driven by Asia, and its operating profit increase to JPY 12.5 billion, suggest a more resilient business model. The report notes that domestic skincare sales trends in April-to-May were broadly in line with to slightly above the market average. But even here, there are questions. The pullback from new skincare launches in January-to-March and the demand for Hada Labo ahead of price hikes suggest that some of the growth is pull-forward demand rather than organic expansion. The contribution from the acquired firm Eu Yan Sang remains to be confirmed.

## The China Growth Engine Is Stalling, and the Margin Consequences Are Only Beginning to Be Priced In

The report includes two critical external data points: sales at large retail stores in China are decelerating, and cosmetics and toiletries imports into China continue to rise. The combination is toxic for Japanese brands. A slowing domestic market means that Chinese consumers are becoming more price-sensitive and more selective. Rising imports mean that foreign brands are competing more intensely for a smaller pool of incremental spending.

For Shiseido, which has historically derived a significant portion of its profit from China and travel retail, this is a double-edged sword. The report's forecast of rising profits in China and travel retail in the April-to-June quarter suggests that the company's restructuring efforts are yielding results. But these are profit recoveries from a very low base, not evidence of sustainable competitive advantage. The key watch point cited in the report is progress on innovative product launches and sell-out trends in China and the US. This is the right focus, but it also reveals the company's vulnerability: Shiseido needs innovation to drive demand, not just cost-cutting to restore margins.

Kosé Holdings and Pola Orbis Holdings are less exposed to China than Shiseido, but they are not immune. The overall slowdown in Chinese cosmetics demand affects the entire competitive landscape because it forces all players to compete more aggressively in Japan and other markets. When the largest growth market stops growing, the pressure cascades everywhere.

The report's forex sensitivity table provides another layer of insight. For Shiseido, a 1% yen depreciation against all currencies adds approximately JPY 500 million to operating profit. For Rohto Pharmaceutical, a JPY 1 depreciation against the USD adds approximately JPY 90 million, and against the CNY adds approximately JPY 170 million. These sensitivities are not large enough to offset the structural pressures on margins. Currency tailwinds can help, but they cannot compensate for a loss of pricing power or market share.

The strategic implication is clear: companies that relied on China for growth must now find new sources of demand or accept lower margins. The report does not provide a clear answer on where that new demand will come from, which is precisely why the upcoming earnings season is so important. Investors need to hear concrete plans for market diversification, not just cost-cutting targets.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Sustainability of Profit Recovery and the Role of Brand Investment

The report is thorough in its estimates and external data analysis, but it leaves several critical questions unresolved. First, the profit recovery forecasts for Shiseido and Kosé are based on cost improvements and the elimination of losses in certain regions. These are one-time gains. The report does not model what happens when the easy restructuring work is done and companies must rely on organic revenue growth to drive further margin expansion. If the top line does not accelerate, the margin improvements will plateau.

Second, the report does not fully address the role of brand investment in a deflationary competitive environment. Kosé's upfront marketing investments in January-to-March were supposed to generate returns in the April-to-June quarter, but the results are mixed. This raises a fundamental question: does marketing spend in the current environment generate a positive ROI, or is it simply a cost of maintaining market share? If the latter, then the entire industry is facing a structural margin headwind that no amount of cost-cutting can fully offset.

Third, the report does not provide a clear framework for evaluating the trade-off between volume and price. In a market where imports are rising and domestic demand is slowing, companies may be forced to choose between cutting prices to maintain volume or holding prices and losing share. The report's external data suggests that mid- to low-priced segments are where the competition is most intense, but it does not model the margin implications of a price war in these segments.

Fourth, the report does not address the long-term impact of changing consumer preferences. The data on domestic cosmetics shipments by product category shows shifts in mix, but the report does not interpret whether these shifts are temporary or structural. If consumers are permanently trading down to lower-priced products, then the entire premium segment of the Japanese cosmetics industry is at risk.

These unanswered questions are not weaknesses in the report. They are the natural boundaries of an analysis that focuses on a specific earnings window. But they are precisely the questions that investors need to answer for themselves before making portfolio decisions.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: Three Filters to Separate the Resilient from the Vulnerable

Given the uncertainty in the sector, investors need a systematic approach to evaluating the four covered companies. The following framework is derived from the report's data and logic, but extends it into a set of decision criteria that can be applied beyond the April-to-June quarter.

**Filter One: Core Brand Momentum.** The report makes clear that the key variable for each company is whether its core brands are gaining or losing momentum. For Shiseido, the external data showing below-average performance in mid- to low-priced cosmetics is a red flag. For Rohto Pharmaceutical, the above-average performance in skincare is a green flag. For Kosé and Pola Orbis, the mixed results require deeper investigation into specific brand-level data that the report does not provide. Investors should prioritize companies where core brands are outperforming the market average, because that outperformance is the foundation for pricing power and margin expansion.

**Filter Two: Geographic Diversification and Dependency.** The report shows that China and travel retail are critical profit drivers for Shiseido, while Rohto Pharmaceutical's growth is driven by Asia more broadly. Kosé and Pola Orbis are more domestic-focused. In the current environment, geographic diversification is a risk factor, not a benefit, if the diversification is concentrated in slowing markets. The ideal profile is a company with exposure to multiple growing markets, not just one large market that is decelerating. Rohto Pharmaceutical's Asia-driven growth appears more balanced than Shiseido's China-dependent recovery.

**Filter Three: Cost Structure Flexibility.** The report's estimates show significant variation in operating margins across the four companies. Rohto Pharmaceutical has the highest operating margin at 14.3% in the first quarter, while Kosé and Shiseido have much lower margins. The ability to absorb cost pressures without sacrificing brand investment is a critical differentiator. Companies with already-thin margins have less room to maneuver when input costs rise or when they need to increase marketing spend to defend market share. Investors should favor companies with higher base margins and demonstrated ability to maintain or improve them without relying on one-time restructuring gains.

Applying this framework to the four companies, Rohto Pharmaceutical emerges as the most resilient, with above-average brand momentum, diversified Asia exposure, and a high base margin. Shiseido is the most vulnerable, with below-average domestic performance, heavy China dependency, and a margin recovery that is largely restructuring-driven. Kosé and Pola Orbis fall in between, with specific challenges in their core brands and domestic cost structures that the upcoming earnings will need to address.

## The Unresolved Analytical Question That Will Define the Sector's Next Phase

Despite the detailed estimates and external data, the report leaves one critical question unanswered: can any of these companies grow their core brands and lift margins simultaneously in a market where imports are rising and domestic demand is slowing? The report's title itself poses this question, but the data does not yet provide a conclusive answer.

For Shiseido, the challenge is to prove that innovative product launches can reverse the domestic share loss. For Kosé, the question is whether the upfront marketing investment will eventually yield sustained returns or whether it was a cost of maintaining share in a competitive market. For Pola Orbis, the issue is whether the ORBIS brand's growth can compensate for POLA's decline without dragging down overall margins. For Rohto Pharmaceutical, the question is whether the Asia-driven growth is sustainable and whether the Eu Yan Sang acquisition will prove accretive.

These are not questions that one quarter of earnings can answer. But the April-to-June results will provide important data points. If Shiseido's China profit recovery is weaker than expected, the stock will likely reprice. If Kosé's marketing investments continue to show mixed results, the market will question the strategy. If Pola Orbis cannot stabilize POLA's salon channel sales, the structural decline may accelerate. If Rohto Pharmaceutical's Asia growth slows, the premium valuation may come under pressure.

The report is a valuable starting point, but it is not a conclusion. The real analysis begins when investors use the data to form their own views on which companies have the strategic depth to navigate this challenging environment. The report provides the map, but investors must choose their own path.
