ZH_A: 那报告里有没有提到具体的“绿色萌芽”？比如哪些区域或细分领域的数据是真正在好转的？

ZH_A: 但欧洲的情况呢？我们经常听到德国财政刺激和基建计划，报告里怎么评价？

ZH_B: 这里就有意思了。报告认为，欧洲基建和资本开支仍然面临挑战。德国的基建支出进度甚至落后于预期。欧洲的汽车制造商市场份额在下降，这也拖累了整个欧洲的工业资本开支。所以，欧洲并没有同步复苏。

ZH_A: 那具体到选股上，报告给出了什么方向？比如电气设备和机械这两个大板块，他们更看好哪个？

ZH_A: 说到利润率，这似乎是报告里一个很大的隐忧。能展开讲讲吗？

EN_A: So we’re looking at a fresh research report from a major global investment bank, focused on European capital goods. The title alone is pretty provocative: “Industrial capex over production. A case for selective broadening out.” So what’s the big idea here?

EN_B: The core message is that global industrial capital expenditure is proving more resilient than most people expected. But the resilience is not uniform. The report argues that investors need to move away from just betting on one or two thematic winners and start “broadening out” their stock selection. That’s the key strategic call.

EN_A: Okay, but why is capex more important than production right now? Help me understand the logic.

EN_B: The report points to three things protecting nominal sector earnings: data centers, strategic capex, and pricing power. But the bigger risks are actually on margins and valuation multiples, especially if interest rates start moving higher. So it’s not a blanket bullish call. It’s a call for selectivity.

EN_A: And where are they seeing the so-called “green shoots”? Any specific regions or sub-sectors?

EN_B: Yes. They highlight the US and Asia, excluding China. In the US, manufacturing construction starts are picking up again after a lull. In Asia, capital goods imports are rising. The interpretation is that customers are “looking through” the current uncertainty and continuing to invest.

EN_A: But what about Europe? We keep hearing about the German fiscal package and infrastructure spending. Is that showing up in the data?

EN_B: That’s where it gets interesting. The report says European infrastructure and capex still face headwinds. German infrastructure spending is actually falling behind expectations. And European auto manufacturers are losing market share, which is a big drag on overall European industrial capex. So Europe is not participating in the same recovery.

EN_A: Let’s get into stock selection. How do they view the two big buckets: electricals and mechanicals?

EN_B: They are selective within both. In electricals, they prefer Schneider, calling it the best balance of growth and valuation. In mechanicals, they like upstream mining names like Epiroc. But here’s a suspenseful point they leave hanging: they believe electrification order growth has likely peaked. So the future driver for these stocks might shift from order momentum to cost control and margin expansion.

EN_A: Margins. That seems like a big, unspoken risk in this report. Can you elaborate?

EN_B: Absolutely. The report explicitly says margin risk is arguably bigger than growth risk right now. Cost inflation is back, and this time it’s hitting electricals harder than mechanicals. Raw materials, freight, even electricity costs are rising. They estimate companies need to raise prices by about 4% this year just to offset the cost pressure. The big question they leave open is: can they actually pass through those price increases without losing volume?

EN_A: So an investor looking only at order growth could be misled if the margin story is deteriorating?

EN_B: Exactly. That’s the whole point of “selective broadening out.” The report also flags a fascinating valuation gap between US and European capital goods. US stocks are trading at a record premium to their European peers. The implication is that if geopolitical tensions ease, European names could have significant catch-up potential. But that’s a big “if” the report doesn’t fully answer.

EN_A: So if a listener takes one thing away from this report, what should it be?

EN_B: Don’t be scared by the macro headlines, but don’t be blindly optimistic either. The industrial capex story has shifted from “broad recovery” to “structural divergence.” You need to find companies with pricing power and structural growth drivers like data centers, automation, and energy transition. And you need to be wary of companies that are just leveraged to a cyclical production recovery.
