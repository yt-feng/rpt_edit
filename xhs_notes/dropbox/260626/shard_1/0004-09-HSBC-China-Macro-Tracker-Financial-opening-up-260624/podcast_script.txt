ZH_A: 这期节目我们来聊一份很有分量的宏观报告，它刚刚在陆家嘴论坛前后发布。你觉得这份报告最核心的判断是什么？

ZH_B: 报告的核心判断是，中国正在从一个“吸引资本”的阶段，转向一个“深度开放金融体系”的阶段。陆家嘴论坛上释放的信号非常明确，人民币国际化、资本市场的进一步开放，还有货币政策工具的升级，这三件事是同时推进的。

ZH_A: 同时推进？这听起来像是一个组合拳。那它要回答的根本问题是什么？

ZH_B: 它要回答的根本问题是：在外部环境不确定、内部消费偏弱的背景下，金融开放到底能不能成为支撑经济增长的新引擎？报告认为，可以，但路径跟以前不一样了。

ZH_A: 不一样在哪儿？以前我们讲开放，更多是让外资进来买股票、买债券。现在呢？

ZH_B: 现在更强调“制度型开放”和“工具型开放”。比如，报告提到要试点离岸金融业务，包括离岸贸易融资、自贸区离岸债券，还有离岸再保险。这不仅仅是让钱进来，而是让中国的金融市场规则和产品能跟国际接轨。

ZH_A: 这让我想到报告里提到的那个新工具——境外央行人民币回购。这是什么意思？

ZH_B: 对，这个FIMA回购安排很重要。它允许境外央行用人民币债券做抵押，从中国央行获得流动性。这意味着，人民币开始像一个真正的国际储备货币那样，承担起“最后贷款人”的职能。这为未来更多央行增持人民币资产铺了路。

ZH_A: 所以不只是开放，还是在为人民币国际化搭基础设施。那货币政策工具那边呢？报告提到央行调整了临时隔夜回购工具，把利率走廊收窄了。

ZH_B: 没错。以前利率走廊是70个基点，现在缩到50个基点，而且是对称的。报告的解释是，这能更好地控制银行间市场利率的波动，尤其是季末那种资金紧张的时候。更深层的含义是，央行可能在为最终转向以隔夜利率作为主要政策利率做准备，就像美联储那样。

ZH_A: 这很技术性，但也很关键。那回到消费端，报告用端午节和618的数据说明消费还是偏弱。这跟金融开放有什么关系？

ZH_B: 关系很大。金融开放是长期结构性的故事，但短期经济还需要消费和投资来托底。报告指出，人均端午消费比2019年还低14%，618电商销售基本没增长。这说明居民还是谨慎，不敢花钱。

ZH_A: 那财政这边呢？报告说5月财政支出又放缓了，特别是一般公共预算支出还在下降。

ZH_B: 对，但结构上有亮点。报告发现，今年财政资金更多投向了民生领域，比如教育、医疗，而不是传统的基建。这跟“投资于人”的政策方向一致。但报告也留了一个悬念：如果投资数据继续疲软，财政可能会重新加码基建，专项债发行会加速。

ZH_A: 这个悬念很好，那外资这块呢？报告提到一个吸引外资的新行动计划，说服务、金融、医药等领域都要扩大准入。

ZH_B: 这是报告另一个重点。它认为，中国吸引外资的逻辑变了。以前是靠低成本劳动力，现在靠的是创新能力和庞大的市场。报告引用了数据，今年前五个月有近4000家外资企业在中国扩大了投资，而且研发投入占比很高，医药行业尤其突出。

ZH_A: 所以外资不是不来，而是来的方式变了，更多是冲着研发和高端制造来的。

ZH_B: 对。而且报告提到，中美关系相对稳定也帮助提振了美国对华投资，前五个月美国对华直接投资增长了17.3%。中东资金也在加速流入，沙特投资增长了285%。

ZH_A: 285%？这个数字很惊人。那报告里有没有提到哪些问题还没有完全展开，值得我们去读完整版？

ZH_B: 有两个关键悬念。第一，货币政策的调整方向很清晰，但报告没有明确说央行今年会不会降息，它只是说工具更多了。第二，财政支出结构转向民生，但如果经济下行压力加大，这个转向会不会被逆转？基建会不会再次成为主力？这两个问题，完整报告里会有更详细的推演。

EN_A: So we just went through a major report from a global investment bank, focused on China's financial opening-up. What is the single most important takeaway you got from it?

EN_B: The biggest takeaway is that China is moving from simply attracting capital to actively reshaping its financial infrastructure. The Lujiazui Forum sent a very clear signal. They are pushing RMB internationalization, capital market reforms, and monetary policy upgrades all at the same time.

EN_A: All at the same time. That sounds like a coordinated strategy. So what is the core question this report is trying to answer?

EN_B: The core question is, can financial opening-up become a new growth engine when domestic consumption is weak and the external environment is uncertain? And the report says yes, but the path is different from before.

EN_A: Different how? In the past, opening up meant letting foreign money buy stocks and bonds.

EN_B: Right. Now it is more about institutional opening and new tools. For example, the report mentions piloting offshore financial activities, like offshore trade finance and offshore bonds in the free trade zone. It is not just about letting money in. It is about making China's financial rules and products compatible with global standards.

EN_A: That reminds me of the new repo facility for foreign central banks. What is that about?

EN_B: That is the FIMA repo facility. It allows foreign central banks to use their RMB bonds as collateral to get liquidity from the PBOC. This is a big deal. It means the RMB is starting to function like a true reserve currency, providing a lender of last resort function. It paves the way for more central banks to hold RMB assets.

EN_A: So it is not just opening up, it is building the infrastructure for internationalization. And on the monetary policy side, the report talks about the PBOC narrowing the interest rate corridor.

EN_B: Exactly. The corridor was 70 basis points, now it is 50 basis points, and it is symmetrical. The report says this helps control volatility in interbank rates, especially around quarter ends. The deeper implication is that the PBOC might be preparing to eventually use the overnight rate as its main policy rate, similar to the Fed.

EN_A: That is very technical but very important. Now, on the consumption side, the report uses Dragon Boat Festival and 618 sales data to show consumers are still cautious. How does that connect to financial opening-up?

EN_B: It is a big connection. Financial opening-up is a long-term structural story, but the short-term economy still needs consumption and investment. The report points out that per capita spending during the Dragon Boat holiday was still 14 percent below 2019 levels. 618 e-commerce sales were basically flat. People are still cautious.

EN_A: And on the fiscal side, the report says May expenditure slowed again.

EN_B: Yes, but there is a structural shift. The report finds that more fiscal money is going to livelihood areas like education and healthcare, rather than traditional infrastructure. This aligns with the "investing in people" policy direction. But the report leaves a cliffhanger. If investment data continues to weaken, fiscal policy might have to pivot back to infrastructure, with faster bond issuance.

EN_A: That is a good cliffhanger. What about foreign investment? The report mentions a new action plan to attract FDI.

EN_B: This is another key point. The report argues that China's appeal to foreign investors has changed. It is no longer about low labor costs. It is about innovation capacity and the size of the market. The report cites data that nearly 4,000 foreign companies expanded their investments in China in the first five months of this year, and R&D spending is a big part of that, especially in pharmaceuticals.

EN_A: So foreign capital is still coming, but in a different form, more focused on R&D and high-end manufacturing.

EN_B: Exactly. And the report notes that a relatively stable US-China relationship has helped. US FDI into China grew 17.3 percent in the first five months. Middle Eastern money is also flowing in. Saudi investment jumped 285 percent.

Will infrastructure spending come back as the main driver? The full report has a more detailed scenario analysis on both.
