EN_A: So air cooling might be phased out quickly? What about different climates?
EN_B: Air cooling is more efficient in warm, dry climates, while water works better in cooler ones. So it is not a one-size-fits-all. Delta sees more value in closed-loop cooling, offsetting extra energy with renewables.
EN_A: The report also talks about “prefabricated data centers.” That sounds like a fast deployment solution.
EN_B: Yes, it is Delta’s strongest product line. Their new AI modular solution cuts deployment time by 50% to 60%. They plan to grow capacity four times over the next six months. Interestingly, companies like AirTrunk follow this modular approach, while NXT and MAQ still use traditional methods because their customers are mostly enterprise.
EN_A: What about the hyperscalers? Shouldn’t they be the most eager to adopt new tech?
EN_B: Surprisingly, Microsoft and AWS lean toward traditional methods because they have standardized global designs. That is why they use colocation for regional deployments but self-build in the US, which accounts for about 90% of their total capacity.
EN_A: The last big topic is 800-volt DC power. Why is this such a big deal?
EN_B: It is a next-generation power system developed in close partnership with NVIDIA. It is designed to solve the problem of limited rack space in AI data centers. By using higher voltage, it reduces current, which means less copper and fewer cables. The total system cost could drop by 30% to 40%.
