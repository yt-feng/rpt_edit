ZH_A: 四月信贷数据又低于预期了，这次有什么特别值得关注的点吗？
ZH_B: 是的，这次数据信号非常明确——信贷需求持续疲软。四月新增人民币贷款甚至出现负增长，减少了10亿元，远低于市场预期的3000亿元。
ZH_A: 贷款负增长？这听起来挺罕见的。到底发生了什么？
ZH_B: 关键在于居民和企业两大部门的信贷都在萎缩。居民中长期贷款减少了3410亿元，企业中长期贷款减少了4100亿元。这说明私人部门的信贷需求非常弱。
ZH_A: 那政策层面有没有什么动作？比如票据融资是不是又上来了？
ZH_B: 没错，票据融资确实大幅增加到了1.2万亿元，同比多增了4090亿元。但这更像是政策推动的结果，并没有从根本上弥补其他部分的疲软。贷款增速进一步放缓到5.6%，比三月的5.7%和去年底的6.4%都低。
ZH_A: 所以这意味着什么？对整体经济有什么影响？
ZH_B: 这意味着实体经济动能仍然不足。我们估算的信用脉冲指标进一步恶化，从三月的负0.4%下降到负1.9%。换句话说，信贷对GDP的支撑作用在减弱。
ZH_A: 社会融资总量数据怎么样？是不是也低于预期？
ZH_B: 是的，四月新增社融只有6250亿元，远低于市场预期的1.25万亿元。除了贷款疲软，政府债券发行也比去年同期低，表外信贷也在收缩。社融增速放缓到了7.8%。
ZH_A: 那银行间市场流动性呢？是不是很宽松？
ZH_B: 确实很宽松。DR007利率最近一直徘徊在1.3%左右，低于7天逆回购操作利率1.4%。央行四月通过公开市场操作、MLF和PSL净回笼了超过1000亿元流动性，但市场资金依然充裕。
ZH_A: 央行回笼流动性，但市场还这么宽松，这听起来有点矛盾。为什么？
ZH_B: 关键原因有两个：一是信贷需求疲软，银行不需要借钱去放贷；二是出口商在人民币升值背景下结汇增加，提供了额外的流动性。这其实是一个有趣的结构性问题。
ZH_A: 所以央行现在是什么态度？是继续宽松还是收紧？
ZH_B: 我们认为央行仍然保持宽松姿态，目的是支持货币和信贷增长。但报告里也提到，金融监管部门可能会用行政手段敦促银行增加贷款。至于效果如何，完整报告有更深入的分析。
ZH_A: 听起来这份报告还留了很多悬念。比如，如果行政手段效果不好，下一步政策会怎么走？
ZH_B: 对，这正是报告没有完全展开的地方。比如央行会不会降准或降息？财政政策会不会加码？这些都需要结合更多数据来判断。建议听众去读完整报告，里面有更详细的推演。

EN_A: So April credit data came in below expectations again. What really stood out this time?
EN_B: The headline is pretty striking. New RMB loans actually turned negative in April, contracting by 10 billion yuan. That’s way below the market consensus of 300 billion.
EN_A: Negative loan growth? That’s unusual. What drove that?
EN_B: Both household and corporate lending weakened. Household medium- and long-term loans dropped by 341 billion, and corporate loans fell by 410 billion. It’s a clear sign that private sector credit demand remains very weak.
EN_A: Did the policy side try to offset that? I saw bill financing picked up.
EN_B: Yes, bill financing surged to 1.2 trillion yuan, up 409 billion year-on-year. But that looks more like policy-driven window dressing. It wasn’t enough to stop overall loan growth from slowing to 5.6 percent, down from 5.7 in March and 6.4 at the end of last year.
EN_A: So what does this mean for the broader economy?
EN_B: It means the credit impulse is fading. Our estimate shows the credit impulse turned more negative, from minus 0.4 percent of GDP in March to minus 1.9 percent in April. That suggests credit is providing less support to economic activity.
EN_A: And what about total social financing? Did that miss too?
EN_B: Big time. New TSF came in at 625 billion yuan, far below the 1.25 trillion consensus. On top of soft loans, government bond issuance was also lower than last year, and shadow credit continued to shrink. TSF growth slowed to 7.8 percent.
EN_A: Interbank liquidity seems pretty loose though, right?
EN_B: Yes, it’s been very loose. The DR007 rate has been hovering around 1.3 percent, below the PBOC’s 7-day reverse repo rate of 1.4 percent. The central bank actually drained liquidity in April through open market operations, MLF, and PSL, but the market still had ample funds.
EN_A: That sounds contradictory. How can they drain liquidity and still have it so loose?
EN_B: Two reasons. First, weak credit demand means banks don’t need to borrow to lend. Second, exporters have been converting more foreign exchange into yuan as the currency appreciates, which adds to liquidity. It’s an interesting structural dynamic.
EN_A: So what’s the PBOC’s stance here? Are they leaning dovish or hawkish?
EN_B: We think they remain accommodative on purpose, to support money and credit growth. But the report also notes regulators could use administrative measures to push banks to lend more. The big question is whether that will actually work.
EN_A: That sounds like a good cliffhanger. What does the full report say about policy options?
EN_B: The report doesn’t fully answer that yet. For example, will the PBOC cut reserve requirements or interest rates? Will fiscal policy step up? Those are open questions that require more data. The full report dives deeper into those scenarios.
