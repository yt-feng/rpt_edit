# AI Is Reshaping China’s Education Market Faster Than Offline Recovery Can Keep Up

The Chinese education sector is no longer a single story of regulatory recovery or online migration. The April 2026 data from a global investment bank’s tracker reveals a market bifurcating along a clear fault line: AI-native education products are scaling with compound momentum, while traditional offline tutoring and overseas study channels face structural headwinds that have not yet bottomed. ByteDance’s Doubao Study has accelerated its daily active user growth to 128% year-on-year, and TAL’s Xueersi.com has reaccelerated to 22% MAU growth. Yet at the same time, the number of offline non-academic tutoring licenses continues to decline, and student visas granted to Chinese students by key English-speaking destinations have fallen again. This is not a market where all boats are rising. It is a market where the strategic question has shifted from “when will regulation ease” to “which business models are structurally insulated from the headwinds that remain.”

The argument of this analysis is straightforward: The most defensible growth in China’s education market over the next 12 to 18 months will come from AI-powered software and hardware products that operate independently of the offline license regime and the overseas study pipeline. Companies that are able to combine proprietary AI capabilities with direct-to-consumer distribution—whether through super-app ecosystems or dedicated learning devices—are building moats that the legacy players cannot easily replicate. The data from April 2026 provides the strongest evidence yet that this transition is not hypothetical; it is happening in real time, and it is accelerating.

Why now matters is a function of timing. The market has spent the past two years anticipating a recovery in offline tutoring capacity and a rebound in overseas study demand. Those recoveries have been slow, uneven, and in some cases, are now reversing. The April tracker shows that the number of offline non-academic tutoring licenses continued its downward trend. Student visas from Australia and Canada declined 6% year-on-year in the first two months of 2026. The offline and overseas narratives are not merely delayed—they are being disrupted by structural forces that are unlikely to reverse quickly. Meanwhile, AI education apps and tablets are growing from a smaller base but at rates that imply they are capturing share of mind and wallet. The strategic implication is clear: investors and operators who continue to anchor their expectations to offline recovery risk missing the most significant value creation in the sector today.

## ByteDance’s AI Education Ecosystem Is Winning on Both User Scale and Monetization Efficiency

The most striking data point in the April tracker is Doubao Study’s DAU growth acceleration to 128% year-on-year. This is not a new product benefiting from a low base effect; Doubao Study has now sustained its position as the sixth-largest AI native app in China by daily active users. The scale is meaningful. Doubao Study is no longer a niche experiment within ByteDance’s broader AI portfolio. It is a mainstream education product with the kind of user engagement that typically precedes significant monetization.

The pattern is even more compelling when viewed alongside Gauth, ByteDance’s overseas education app. Gauth achieved monthly billings of approximately USD 1.0 million in April, representing 35% year-on-year growth, with last-twelve-month billings reaching USD 12.9 million. More importantly, Gauth’s monthly billings per average DAU grew 49% year-on-year to USD 0.35. This metric is critical because it indicates that Gauth is not just acquiring users—it is converting them into paying customers at an increasing rate. The introduction of Gauth Atlas, an interactive tool that transforms topics into dynamic visual knowledge maps, and the launch of an AI Live Tutor feature in Southeast Asia, suggest that ByteDance is systematically building a product suite that increases stickiness and willingness to pay.

The competitive implication for other education companies is uncomfortable. ByteDance possesses two structural advantages that are difficult to replicate: a massive existing user base across its ecosystem that can be cross-sold into education products, and a track record of investing aggressively in AI capabilities. Doubao Study benefits from the same AI infrastructure that powers ByteDance’s broader Doubao app, which itself has 154.5 million DAUs. This is not a standalone education startup; it is a division of one of the world’s most sophisticated AI-native companies. For legacy education players, competing on AI product quality alone will be insufficient. The distribution advantage that ByteDance enjoys means that any product parity in features will still result in a significant gap in user acquisition cost and speed to scale.

## TAL’s Dual-Track Strategy Is Working, But the Tablet Business Faces a May Test

TAL Education presents a more nuanced picture. On the software side, Xueersi.com’s MAU growth accelerated to 22% year-on-year in April, up from 15% in February and 10% in March. This is a meaningful reacceleration after a period of moderation. The Peiyou app, which handles enrichment course registration and online attendance, saw a slight moderation to 19% MAU growth, but this still represents healthy engagement. TAL’s ability to grow its online user base while the broader offline tutoring license environment continues to contract suggests that its hybrid model is gaining traction. The question is whether this online growth can translate into revenue at a sufficient scale to offset the slower offline expansion.

On the hardware side, TAL’s learning tablet business delivered 20% year-on-year GMV growth in April, with shipments up 27% year-on-year. The average selling price of TAL’s tablets saw its year-on-year decline narrow to 6%, while increasing month-on-month for four consecutive months to approximately RMB 4,000. This pricing trend is encouraging because it suggests that TAL is successfully moving customers toward higher-value products, likely driven by the launch of the X5 Ultra model priced at RMB 5,900. The narrowing of the year-on-year ASP decline indicates that the discounting pressure that plagued the tablet market in 2025 may be easing.

However, the April data also contains a warning signal. Combined GMV of major learning device brands dropped 2% year-on-year and 47% month-on-month across major e-commerce platforms. The month-on-month decline is partly seasonal—April typically follows the March peak around the Chinese New Year and school resumption period—but the year-on-year decline is a reversal from March’s 29% year-on-year growth. The divergence between TAL’s performance and the broader market is stark: while TAL grew 20% year-on-year, Xiaodu dropped 60%, BBK dropped 40%, and Zuoyebang dropped 24%. This suggests that the tablet market is consolidating around brands that can demonstrate differentiated AI capabilities, while generic hardware players are losing share.

The critical unknown for TAL is May. The report notes that May is the biggest quarter for tablet sales, and the March-April GMV growth rate tracks above the investment bank’s estimate of 11% GMV year-on-year growth for the May quarter. But one quarter does not make a trend. If TAL can sustain its growth momentum through May, it will validate the thesis that its AI-powered tablets have achieved product-market fit at scale. If the growth decelerates sharply, it will raise questions about whether the April performance was driven by the X5 Ultra launch effect rather than sustained demand.

## The Offline and Overseas Study Headwinds Are Structural, Not Cyclical

The April tracker provides the clearest evidence yet that the headwinds facing offline tutoring and overseas study are not temporary. The number of offline non-academic tutoring licenses continued its downward trend. This is not a blip; it is a multi-year pattern that reflects sustained regulatory scrutiny and a structural shift in how Chinese families consume education services. The days when offline tutoring centers were the default growth engine for education companies are over. The total addressable market for offline tutoring is shrinking, and it is unlikely to recover to pre-regulatory levels regardless of any future policy adjustments.

The overseas study data is equally sobering. Student visas granted to Chinese students by Australia and Canada declined 6% year-on-year in the first two months of 2026, with Australia down 5% and Canada down 9%. This is part of a broader trend that has seen Chinese student outflows to key English-speaking destinations decline or stagnate. The reasons are well-documented: geopolitical tensions, tighter visa policies, and the rising competitiveness of Chinese domestic universities. But the data suggests that the decline is accelerating, not stabilizing. For companies that built their business models around overseas study preparation and consulting, this is a secular headwind that will require fundamental strategic repositioning.

The strategic implication is that investors should not expect a near-term recovery in either offline tutoring or overseas study to drive growth for the sector. The most resilient education companies will be those that have diversified their revenue streams away from these channels and toward AI-powered products that are not subject to license constraints or geopolitical risks. The data from April reinforces that this transition is already underway, and the gap between the winners and losers in the sector is likely to widen.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Unit Economics of AI Education at Scale

The April tracker provides excellent data on user engagement, GMV, and billings, but it leaves several critical questions about unit economics unanswered. The most important of these is whether the AI education products that are scaling—Doubao Study, Gauth, TAL’s tablets—are doing so at sustainable unit economics. User growth and revenue growth are necessary conditions for investment, but they are not sufficient. The key metrics that are not fully visible in this data include customer acquisition cost, gross margin on AI education sUBScriptions, and the development cost of the AI models themselves.

For ByteDance, the question is whether Doubao Study and Gauth are profitable at the product level or whether they are being sUBSidized by the broader ByteDance ecosystem. The company’s ability to cross-sell users from its massive Doubao app gives it a significant cost advantage in user acquisition, but the AI inference costs for education products—which require real-time interaction, personalized content generation, and visual knowledge mapping—are likely higher than for general-purpose chatbots. If the unit economics are negative at scale, the growth story becomes dependent on future monetization improvements that may or may not materialize.

For TAL, the tablet business presents a different set of questions. The ASP is increasing, which is positive, but the gross margin on hardware is typically lower than on software or services. The real value in the tablet business is likely in the recurring revenue from content sUBScriptions and AI tutoring services that are bundled with the hardware. The report does not provide data on sUBScription attach rates, renewal rates, or average revenue per user for the tablet ecosystem. Without this data, it is difficult to assess whether the tablet business is a high-margin software play disguised as a hardware business, or a low-margin hardware business that is difficult to differentiate.

These unanswered questions are not a criticism of the report, which is focused on tracking leading indicators rather than conducting full financial analysis. But they are the questions that any serious investor or operator should be asking before committing significant capital to the AI education thesis. The user growth data is encouraging, but the path to sustainable profitability at scale remains unproven.

## A Decision Framework for Navigating the AI Education Landscape

For readers who are evaluating investments or strategic positions in China’s education sector, the April tracker suggests a three-part decision framework based on business model exposure to structural tailwinds and headwinds.

First, prioritize companies with AI-native product architectures that are independent of the offline license regime. The data shows that the offline tutoring license count is still declining, and there is no sign of a reversal. Companies that require physical learning centers to deliver their core value proposition face a shrinking addressable market. Companies that deliver value through software and AI—whether in the form of study companions, AI tutoring, or adaptive learning platforms—are operating in a market that is growing in both user base and willingness to pay. The key question is whether the company has proprietary AI capabilities or is relying on third-party models. ByteDance’s internal AI development gives it a clear edge, while smaller players may struggle to keep pace with the pace of model improvement.

Second, assess the company’s distribution advantage. One of the most striking patterns in the April data is the asymmetry between ByteDance’s education products and those of standalone education companies. Doubao Study benefits from being part of an ecosystem with 154.5 million DAUs. Gauth benefits from ByteDance’s global app distribution infrastructure. Distribution is becoming the primary competitive advantage in AI education because the underlying AI models are increasingly commoditized. A company that has to pay to acquire each user faces a structural disadvantage against a company that can cross-sell to an existing user base at near-zero marginal cost.

Third, evaluate the monetization trajectory through the lens of billings per user, not just total billings. Gauth’s 49% year-on-year growth in monthly billings per average DAU is a more telling metric than its 35% total billings growth, because it indicates that the company is improving its ability to convert engagement into revenue. TAL’s narrowing ASP decline and four consecutive months of ASP increases suggest a similar dynamic in the hardware business. Companies that are improving their monetization efficiency are building a foundation for sustainable growth. Companies that are growing total revenue only by adding users at constant or declining per-user revenue are more vulnerable to competitive pressure.

## The Unresolved Analytical Questions Point Toward Deeper Investigation

The data from April 2026 is rich, but it also opens more questions than it answers. The most pressing unresolved question is how the competitive dynamics will evolve as AI education products scale. ByteDance’s dominance in both domestic and international AI education apps raises the question of whether there is room for multiple winners, or whether the market will consolidate around a few platform players. TAL’s ability to grow its tablet business in the face of ByteDance’s broader ecosystem expansion is a test of whether specialized education companies can coexist with platform giants.

A second unresolved question is the impact of the May sales season on the tablet market. The April data showed a sharp month-on-month decline in combined GMV, but May is historically the largest quarter. If the May data reverses the decline and shows strong year-on-year growth across the market, it will validate the thesis that AI learning tablets are gaining mainstream adoption. If the weakness persists, it will suggest that the tablet market is facing demand saturation or that consumers are waiting for next-generation products.

A third question is the trajectory of overseas study demand. The decline in visas from Australia and Canada is clear, but the data does not cover other destinations such as the United States, the United Kingdom, or Singapore. If the decline in English-speaking destinations is being offset by growth in Asian destinations, the overseas study market may be undergoing a geographic rebalancing rather than a structural decline. If the decline is broad-based, it represents a more significant headwind for companies with exposure to this channel.

These questions are not answered in the April tracker, but they are the natural next questions for any serious analysis of the sector. The data provides a clear signal that the AI education transition is real and accelerating. The next step is to understand the sustainability and profitability of that transition.
