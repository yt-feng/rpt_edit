# Continuation of strategic stability

President Xi and President Trump met in Beijing. The U.S. delegation included a group of business leaders predominantly drawn from the technology and financial sectors.

Vice President HAN Zheng greeted President Trump at the airport. This could be considered a signal of respect since this is as senior as it gets.

The bilateral meeting lasted two hours and fifteen minutes, significantly longer than the roughly one hour and forty minutes of the meeting held in Korea in late October last year.

The two sides jointly proposed establishing a “constructive and strategically stable China–U.S. relationship”. This framework encompasses four dimensions: positive stability centered on cooperation, constructive stability characterized by managed competition, manageable stability based on controlled differences, and durable stability oriented toward long-term peace. The new positioning is intended to serve as strategic guidance for China–U.S. relations over the next three years and beyond (implicitly the current presidential term).

President Trump expressed his intention to "build the best-ever U.S.-China relationship".

China explicitly emphasized that the Taiwan question remains the most important and sensitive issue in bilateral relations, underscoring that maintaining peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait constitutes the greatest common ground between the two countries. Chinese media reported no comment from President Trump on the issue.

China reaffirmed its commitment to continued high-level opening-up, a key issue for visiting financial executives.

The two sides also exchanged views on major international and regional issues, including the Middle East, the Ukraine crisis, and developments on the Korean Peninsula. Chinese media reports provided no details.

Both sides confirmed that “overall balanced and positive outcomes” had been achieved in the trade negotiations. Secretaries Bessent and Rubio both used the word “constructive” when responding to a Chinese journalists questions in the People’s Hall. But there are no details from official sources.

According to Reuters, the U.S. Department of Commerce has approved approximately ten Chinese companies—including Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, and JD.com—to purchase Nvidia's H200 chips. This is not a breakthrough as the US side has previously eased exports control of H200 chips and these are not the most sought-after high-end chips by China.

President Trump leaves tomorrow. We will comment again when there are more details.
