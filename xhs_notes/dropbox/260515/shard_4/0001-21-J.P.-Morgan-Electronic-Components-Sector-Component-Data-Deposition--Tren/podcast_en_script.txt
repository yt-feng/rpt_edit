EN_A: We just got a fresh report on electronic components and hard drives, covering the first quarter of 2026. What’s the one thing that really stands out?
EN_B: The headline is simple. Hard drive shipments were flat quarter over quarter, but they jumped by double digits year over year. The big driver was nearline drives, which are the high-capacity drives used in data centers. They grew 28 percent year over year.
EN_A: So the narrative that hard drives are dying is not true for the data center market?
EN_B: Exactly. Consumer drives are losing to SSDs. But for cloud providers who need massive, cheap storage, HDDs still have a huge edge. The report shows that the cost per gigabyte gap between NAND flash and nearline HDDs widened to 12.7 times in Q1 2026. That’s the highest since 2020.
EN_A: 12.7 times? That means HDDs are actually becoming more competitive on cost for bulk storage?
EN_B: Yes. And that supports both higher shipment volumes and larger drive capaCities. Total shipped capacity grew 38 percent year over year. The average drive is now 14.5 terabytes.
EN_A: So hard drives are very much alive in the enterprise space. What about the components? The report mentioned HDD heads, right?
EN_B: Right. Head shipments grew 22 percent year over year, which is faster than drive shipments. Two reasons: more nearline drives being sold, and each drive is using more heads. The average is now 12.2 heads per drive, and for nearline drives it is 19.4.
EN_A: More heads per drive means more business for suppliers. Is that why the report highlights TDK?
EN_B: Exactly. The report says TDK is the top pick among HDD-related stocks. The logic is that captive manufacturers, who make their own heads, are being cautious about adding capacity. They are also shifting existing lines to next-generation HAMR technology. That opens the door for an independent supplier like TDK to keep gaining market share.
EN_A: So TDK benefits from its competitors’ capacity constraints. But what about the shift to HAMR? Does the report fully explore the risks there?
EN_B: That is the interesting part that the report does not fully unpack. Moving to HAMR is a big technology upgrade. It could lead to lower initial yields and supply constraints for nearline drives. How fast will the captive manufacturers ramp up HAMR? That uncertainty is something the full report digs into.
EN_A: Good point. We also saw data on spindle motors. What is the market structure there?
EN_B: That market is very concentrated. Nidec has 60 percent and MinebeaMitsumi has 40 percent. It is stable, unlike the head market where there is a clear share shift opportunity.
EN_A: So to sum up, the data shows strong HDD demand from data centers, a widening cost advantage, and a key opportunity in the head supply chain. But the full report has more on the technology transition risks. Thanks for the insights.
EN_B: Thank you.
