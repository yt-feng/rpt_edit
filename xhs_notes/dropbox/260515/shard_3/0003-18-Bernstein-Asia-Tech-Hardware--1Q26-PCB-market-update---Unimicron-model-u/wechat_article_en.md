# The AI PCB Supply Chain is Entering a Structural Upcycle That Most Investors Are Still Underestimating

The printed circuit board and IC sUBStrate market is not merely recovering. It is undergoing a structural transformation driven by AI infrastructure buildout, and the first quarter of 2026 has confirmed that the cycle has more runway than consensus expects. Sampled AI PCB companies delivered 38 percent year-on-year revenue growth in the first quarter, with sequential acceleration of 8 percent quarter-on-quarter. This is not a cyclical bounce from depleted inventories. This is a demand wave powered by rising AI server content per unit, accelerating adoption of advanced packaging sUBStrates, and a pricing environment that is becoming increasingly favorable for suppliers.

The critical insight that many market participants still miss is that the current upcycle is fundamentally different from the last peak in 2022. The 2022 cycle was driven by pandemic-era demand for consumer electronics and general cloud expansion, which proved unsustainable when inventory corrections hit. The current cycle is anchored to AI accelerator demand, CPU upgrades for AI-optimized servers, and the buildout of high-bandwidth networking infrastructure. These are multi-year investment cycles, not inventory-driven spikes. ABF sUBStrate revenue growth accelerated to 40 percent year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026 and is about to surpass the previous peak reached in the fourth quarter of 2022. The composition of that growth matters more than the absolute number.

Raw material tightness is reinforcing pricing power across the supply chain. T-glass, copper-clad laminate, and copper foil have all seen price increases since 2025, and ABF film costs are now reported to rise in the second half of 2026. This is not a transient bottleneck. It reflects a structural mismatch between capacity expansion timelines and the pace of AI demand growth. Suppliers who secured long-term pricing agreements with hyperscalers and AI accelerator designers are now in a position to compound volume growth with margin expansion. The companies that hesitated on capacity investment during the 2023 downturn are now scrambling to catch up, which further tightens the market for those who planned ahead.

The strategic question for investors is not whether the cycle is real. It is which companies have the positioning, the technology roadmap, and the pricing leverage to convert this cycle into sustainable earnings power rather than a one-time revenue spike.

## The ABF SUBStrate Market Has Entered a Pricing Super-Cycle That Is Only Beginning to Be Reflected in Consensus Estimates

The most underappreciated dynamic in the current market is the acceleration of average selling prices for ABF sUBStrates. A global investment bank report estimates that Unimicron's ABF sUBStrate ASP increased 5 percent quarter-on-quarter in the first quarter of 2026 and will accelerate to 7 to 10 percent quarter-on-quarter through the remainder of the year. The catalyst is a roughly 30 percent raw material cost hike starting from the second quarter, combined with a richer mix of high-end ABF products that command premium pricing.

This is not a cost-pass-through story. It is a value capture story. The suppliers who have invested in advanced packaging capabilities, who can deliver the fine-line, high-layer-count sUBStrates required for AI accelerators and next-generation server CPUs, are now in a position where their customers have limited alternatives in the near term. The report notes that some CPUs are beginning to adopt T-glass from second-source suppliers, but adoption by GPU and ASIC customers from tier-2 suppliers is expected to remain limited through the end of 2026. This creates a window of pricing power for incumbent leaders.

The implications for earnings are significant. For Unimicron, the report models EPS more than tripling to NT$14 in 2026, then growing roughly 80 percent in 2027 and 45 percent in 2028 to NT$36.6. Even with these aggressive growth rates, the report assumes a more gradual margin recovery than consensus, with gross margin reaching approximately 35 percent by the end of 2028. The report explicitly does not factor in a shortage-driven margin spike. If the pricing environment proves even more favorable than this conservative assumption, the upside to earnings could be sUBStantial.

The broader lesson is that the ABF sUBStrate market is transitioning from a volume-driven growth story to a volume-plus-pricing story. Investors who focus only on revenue growth rates are missing half of the equation. The margin trajectory, driven by pricing power and mix improvement, is where the compounding effect on earnings will come from.

## Capacity Expansion Is Accelerating, But the Timing Gap Creates a Multi-Year Opportunity for Incumbents

The capital expenditure cycle in the PCB and sUBStrate industry is entering an aggressive phase. Unimicron has guided for NT$34 billion in capex for 2026, representing 33 percent year-on-year growth. Kinsus has guided for 31 percent and 25 percent year-on-year capex growth in 2026 and 2027 respectively. Gold Circuit has guided for more than NT$17 billion in capex in 2026, more than double the prior year. Chinese MLPCB and HDI companies including Victory Giant, WUS Group, and Delton are scaling advanced production in China while accelerating overseas capacity in Southeast Asia to mitigate geopolitical risks.

This capex wave is a double-edged sword for investors. On one hand, it signals that management teams have high conviction in the durability of AI-driven demand. On the other hand, the sheer scale of capacity additions raises the question of whether the market will become oversupplied in 2028 or 2029. The report provides a framework for thinking about this. The key variable is not the total amount of capacity being added. It is the time lag between capacity announcements and actual production output, combined with the pace of technology migration in AI chips.

Building advanced ABF sUBStrate capacity requires 18 to 24 months from ground-breaking to volume production. During that period, demand continues to grow as AI server shipments accelerate and chip designers increase the sUBStrate layer count and density requirements for each generation. The report estimates that AI ABF plus HDI should reach half of Unimicron's revenue going forward, supporting a 30 percent revenue compound annual growth rate from 2025 to 2028. This implies that even with aggressive capacity additions, the market remains tight through at least 2027.

The strategic implication is that incumbents with existing capacity and established customer relationships have a multi-year window to generate superior returns on capital. New entrants and second-tier suppliers may add capacity, but they face significant qualification timelines with hyperscaler and accelerator customers. The report's channel check indicates limited GPU and ASIC adoption of tier-2 T-glass suppliers by the end of 2026. This qualification bottleneck is a structural moat that is not captured in simple supply-demand models.

## The Report Does Not Fully Answer How the Cycle Will End, and That Uncertainty Is the Most Important Analytical Question

Despite the depth of the analysis, the report leaves several critical questions unresolved. The most important is the timing and nature of the cycle's peak. The report models a gradual margin recovery and explicitly avoids assuming a shortage-driven margin spike. But if the pricing environment continues to accelerate, and if raw material tightness persists longer than expected, the earnings trajectory could overshoot these estimates significantly. Conversely, if capacity additions come online faster than expected, or if AI chip architecture shifts reduce sUBStrate content per unit, the cycle could peak earlier than the model implies.

A second unresolved question is the impact of technology sUBStitution. The report notes that some CPUs are adopting T-glass from second-source suppliers, but it does not fully explore what happens if GPU and ASIC designers successfully qualify alternative sUBStrate technologies or materials. The ABF sUBStrate market has historically been protected by the complexity of the manufacturing process and the length of customer qualification cycles. But technology disruption in the semiconductor industry rarely follows linear timelines. A breakthrough in alternative packaging technologies could reshape the competitive landscape in ways that the current model does not capture.

A third question concerns geographic diversification and geopolitical risk. Chinese MLPCB and HDI companies are accelerating overseas capacity in Southeast Asia, while Taiwanese suppliers are also expanding. But the report does not analyze how trade restrictions, export controls, or supply chain decoupling could alter the demand patterns for specific suppliers. If hyperscalers and AI chip designers are forced to diversify away from certain geographic concentrations, the winners and losers could shift dramatically.

These unanswered questions are not weaknesses in the analysis. They are the natural boundaries of any forward-looking research. The value of the report is that it provides a clear framework for tracking the key variables that will determine how the cycle unfolds. Investors who use this framework to monitor pricing trends, capacity announcements, and technology developments will be better positioned to adjust their views as new data emerges.

## A Decision Framework for Investors Navigating the AI PCB Cycle

The report's analysis can be translated into a structured decision framework for investors. The framework has three layers: demand visibility, pricing power, and competitive positioning.

First, demand visibility. The report shows that AI server shipments are the primary driver of PCB and sUBStrate demand, but not all AI exposure is created equal. Companies with direct exposure to AI accelerators and server CPUs have higher revenue visibility than companies exposed to general networking or consumer electronics. The report's exhibit on AI PCB mix shows that Gold Circuit, Ibiden, and Victory Giant have AI PCB mix exceeding 50 percent of their PCB revenue. Unimicron's AI mix is 43 percent, but the report expects this to grow to half of company revenue as AI ABF and HDI expand. Investors should prioritize companies where AI exposure is not just a growth driver but the primary growth driver.

Second, pricing power. The raw material tightness in T-glass, CCL, copper foil, and ABF film creates a favorable pricing environment for the overall PCB market. But pricing power varies significantly by segment. ABF sUBStrate suppliers with advanced capabilities have the strongest pricing power because their customers have limited alternative suppliers in the near term. The report estimates that ABF sUBStrate ASP increased 5 percent quarter-on-quarter in the first quarter and will accelerate to 7 to 10 percent quarter-on-quarter through 2026. Suppliers in more commoditized PCB segments will benefit from the rising tide, but their pricing power is more constrained by competitive dynamics. Investors should look for companies where ASP growth is driven by mix improvement and customer concentration in premium segments, not just general market tightness.

Third, competitive positioning. The capacity expansion cycle favors incumbents with established customer relationships and qualification track records. The report notes that some CPUs are adopting T-glass from second-source suppliers, but GPU and ASIC adoption of tier-2 suppliers is expected to remain limited through end-2026. This qualification moat is a critical advantage for companies like Unimicron and Ibiden. However, the capex wave also creates risk. Companies that over-invest in capacity that becomes underutilized when the cycle turns will destroy shareholder value. The report's conservative margin assumptions for Unimicron reflect this concern. Investors should differentiate between companies that are investing to capture structural demand growth and companies that are investing defensively to avoid being left behind.
