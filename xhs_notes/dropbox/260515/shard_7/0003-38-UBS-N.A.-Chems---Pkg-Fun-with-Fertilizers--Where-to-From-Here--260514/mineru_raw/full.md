# Fun with Fertilizers Highlights

- Where to from here? We walk through our latest views on the sector and our preferences looking forward below. We remain Sell rated on NTR & IPI. Within our Neutral ratings, we rank CF > LXU > MOS.   
- Review of NPK pricing & updated outlook: We review the latest NPK pricing trends below. Within we provide our updated price deck & pricing revision and walk through our updated pricing outlook for both 2026 and in the medium term, see Figures 1-2.   
- Nitrogen: Nitrogen pricing has increased since the start of the Middle East conflict, with a wider premium to the cost curve reflecting globally constrained supply. Ammonia/urea/nitrates benchmarks are up \~25–60% vs. pre-conflict. While pricing remains structurally elevated, we've begun to see a shift lower in spot pricing in recent weeks on urea, and the Tampa ammonia contract came in below expectations. This indicates we're moving past the seasonal peak in pricing, and 2Q is likely to be the peak pricing quarter for the cycle. Physical markets are still pricing in a shorter disruption and upside risks for 2026/2027 remain if trade disruptions persist later into the year.   
- Natural Gas: U.S./Canada gas prices have seen a limited response to the Middle East conflict and remain favorable. Ex-U.S. gas are trending in the \~\$16-17 per MMBtu range, well below 2022 peaks, and we view the gas market as pricing in a shorter-term duration for production interruptions.   
- Phosphates: Phosphate stripping margins are seeing significant compression driven by highly elevated input costs for sulfur/ammonia that have increased well above the move higher in phosphate prices. Compressed margins are driving supply curtailments (OCP, MOS, PhosAgro), that combined with outages are further constraining supply against an already tight S/D setup. This has kept upward pressure on already elevated prices despite weaker demand tied to affordability issues. As stripping margins continue to compress globally, we see risks of further supply cuts which should theoretically flow back into higher pricing, but it's not clear that the market can bear those higher prices. This setup is negatively impacting the earnings for MOS/NTR phosphate businesses.   
- Potash: Pricing has moved up since the onset of the Middle East disruption on the combination of peak northern hemisphere demand, increased demand from Brazil and on higher freight costs. We've yet to see pricing start to move lower, though we expect modest declines seasonally as 2Q progresses. Demand destruction risks have also been more limited (\~5–15%) relative to phosphates. We don't expect producer margins to benefit as much as the headline price increases which have partially been driven by freight impacts. Supply from the Middle East is largely uninterrupted.   
- Crop pricing: Corn futures for 2026/2027 are up \$0.25-\$0.30 since the Middle East conflict on lower production expectations tied to reduced fertilizer applications globally (\$45/acre impact). Soybean futures have increased by \~\$0.40 since the conflict (\$20/acre impact). We expect 2027 crop futures to continue to rise as lower production and tightened stock/use ratios become apparent through the year.

# Where to from here for Fertilizers?

\- Fertilizer fundamentals remain mixed by nutrient exposure. We remain Sell rated on NTR & IPI. Within our Neutral ratings, we rank CF > LXU > MOS. On a longer duration trade disruption we expect CF/LXU to outperform and MOS to underperform. NTR fundamentals benefit only modestly from an elongated disruption scenario but with much less leverage than CF/LXU, but given we see the stock as somewhat overvalued at current levels, we see upside as more limited unless potash fundamentals show further improvement. In a reopening scenario this would reverse, we'd expect CF/LXU to underperform as nitrogen normalizes and MOS to outperform on expectations of normalizing phosphate raw materials costs that would lift stripping margins, although the interaction here with phosphate pricing is complicated.

\- Nitrogen linked exposure remains highly macro linked to the length of expected Middle East trade flow disruption with longer (positive) and shorter (negative) for the stocks (CF, LXU, NTR). Sentiment (per our convos) had also shifted in recent weeks towards the view that 2Q26 would represent peak nitrogen pricing and peak annualized earnings. This would then see a declining annualized earnings outlook moving beyond 2Q and is viewed as a challenging setup for the stocks. Our updated price deck and earnings estimates are consistent with the view that 2Q will be the peak and we expect at least a modest seasonal decline into 3Q with seasonal pressure and soft demand post northern hemisphere season outweighing the impact of supply shortages, but this debated.

\- For CF, our 2026/2027 EBITDA estimates are +17%/+21% above cons. That we believe needs to shift higher, but we see this already reflected in the stock at current levels. We see CF implying a base value at 1Q26 of \~\$105/sh on our \~\$2.05B mid-cycle EBITDA estimate (implies \~\$8.50 TTF) at \~8.5x (med-term multiple). At \$5-\$6B in peak annualized EBITDA, CF is accruing \~\$4-\$5/quarter in excess FCF implying a further 3-4 quarters of excess earnings which we see as about fair as the disruption impacts are likely to persist into 2H26/2027 on a moderating basis. We see downside support around the \~\$105/sh level though CF could correct below this in a normalization scenario, but the base value likely shifts up to the \~\$125-\$130 range on a year out basis. We see the upside as in the \~\$140-\$145 range and the skews become more interesting \~\$105-\$110 (positive) and \~\$130-\$135 (negative).

\- For NTR where we remain Sell rated, our 2026/2027/2028 EBITDA estimates are +1%/-3%/-8% vs. consensus. We continue to see risks to the med-term potash outlook with incremental supply exceeding demand growth through later in the decade and see modest downside risks to pricing. This compares with the bulls expecting potash price increases into 2027 on 1) better crop prices and farmer profitability flowing through to stronger demand and 2) the S/D being tighter than our view near term, which is the key upside risk vs. our thesis. NTR will also see declining EBITDA as nitrogen markets normalize (we see them over earning by \~\$1.5B in 2026) and if our potash outlook plays out, NTR stock will also be fighting against a declining EBITDA outlook going forward. We see mid-cycle EBITDA in the \~\$5.5B range which at a \~7.5x multiple implies \~\$62/sh at 1Q26, increasing to \~\$65/sh on our year out basis. If potash pricing ends up flat on 2025 levels (\~\$20-\$30/MT above our mid-cycle assumptions) this would add \~\$300-\$450M to our mid-cycle EBITDA estimate and get to \$70-\$72/sh on a year out basis, similar to current trading.

\- For MOS (Neutral rated), we see a challenging year ahead. Our 2026/2027/2028 EBITDA estimates are -17%/-23%/-14% vs. consensus. MOS is seeing negative impacts from the Middle East disruption as higher phosphate raw material costs (ammonia/sulfur) compress stripping margins. The impact has been severe enough to drive production curtailments of higher cost phosphates in Brazil and even for MOS' U.S. assets with two plants to cut op. rates to 50% during 2Q. We see this driving a >50% decline in combined Phosphate/Fertilizantes EBITDA in 2026 while potash earnings are close to flattish (-3% Y/Y). Net this depresses 2026 EBITDA to near \$1.6B, below our view of mid-cycle in the \~\$1.85B range. MOS trading near \$23/sh is similar to that implied by our \~\$1.85B mid-cycle EBITDA at a \~6.0x multiple (plus \~\$6/sh SAU holdings). FCF generation is being depressed near term as well on lower earnings and higher W/C, and we see MOS trading on only a 0.2%/2.0% FCF yield over 2026/2027. We see risks on phosphates for MOS in both directions, but are biased more negatively. If prices move higher more in response to supply shortages and higher raws, stripping margins could improve faster than our expectations, but if this is capped by the high degree of demand destruction expected, we could see phosphate prices flat/down and margins compress further. Potash risks are similar to NTR and would see improved earnings if pricing stays flat or moves higher through 2H26. The Bull case from here would be for both of these factors to improve together over the next couple of years - higher potash pricing/earnings while phosphate markets stay tight and raws abate, though we see this as less likely.

# Brazil fertilizer imports up 3% YTD, N & P down LSD%, K up mid-teens%

- We're still in a lower seasonal period in the annual import cycle (April YTD typically a \~25% share) but ramping towards the peak import period in 3Q (ex. nitrogen which peaks in 4Q). Aggregate April 2026 fertilizer imports were down 11% Y/Y. On a YTD basis, Potash is up 17% Y/Y, while phosphates and nitrogen are down -1%/-4%. YTD NPK blends are down 10% Y/Y. YTD through April, phosphate and potash imports were +80% and +60% above avg, respectively, while Nitrogen and NPK blends were slightly below. Cumulative YTD imports through April were up +3% Y/Y and +30% vs. 2019-21 avg.   
- We track Brazil fertilizer imports via UBS Evidence Lab (> Access Dataset). We are tracking import trends to evaluate shifts in demand dynamics for both Phosphates and Potash due to grower unaffordability concerns (N&P pricing) driving demand destruction along with the impacts from nitrogen supply shortages. We believe April imports have only seen a limited impact so far from the Middle East disruption as shipments typically take 6-8 weeks to reach Brazil and would expect this impact to grow for nitrogen and phosphates as we get May/June data. Even if the Strait reopens in the near term, we see a \~3 month minimum gap before plants have restarted and product is again arriving in Brazil net of transit times. This is pushing up against time constraints to position fertilizers in time for Brazil's peak import period. trade flows restart, should trade flows remain interrupted as we approach Brazil's peak important period seasonally in 3Q.

# Nitrogen; Ammonia/Urea/UAN +6%/-11%/flat vs. 2 weeks ago; QTD tracking +\$175/+\$200/+\$175/MT Q/Q

\- Ammonia: Global prices remain elevated on supply disruptions driven by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz (3 vessels still stranded), ongoing Middle East outages, and limited alternative supply availability. Additionally, the latest India tender for \~520kt of ammonia is not expected to be filled and to add further upward pressure to global prices. The Tampa May contract settled at \~\$825/MT (+\$50/MT M/M), though below expectations in the \$825-\$870 range. This was driven by improved supply from the U.S. Gulf, with GCA and Beaumont ramping and the extension of the Jones Waiver Act helping to reduce shipping cost pressures. Global supply remains structurally tight, however, and we expect pricing to remain flat to modestly lower sequentially from 2Q/3Q, vs. typically larger declines. Benchmarks are +\$200–370/MT since late Feb. QTD U.S. pricing is \~\$20/MT below our updated 2Q26e which incorporates the higher contract pricing. Neutral for CF, LXU, MOS, NTR, relative to our updated estimates post 1Q results.

\- Urea: Prices were softer globally this week on seasonal demand declines and bearish sentiment related to expectations for a peace agreement in the Middle East. In the U.S., NOLA pricing reached its lowest level since early March. Despite the near-term demand pullback, underlying supply remains constrained, with some Iranian production still offline and global availability relatively tight with trade flows restricted. Demand has weakened on affordability pressures, and buyers are shifting toward lower-cost alternatives (i.e. AS). Domestic spot pricing is +\$135/MT higher since prior to the conflict. There has been no update on Qatar production and Iran production remains down, while BPIC is undergoing maintenance following a drone strike last month. QTD U.S. pricing is tracking \~\$15/MT above our updated 2Q26e which incorporates further seasonal declines as 2Q progresses more than offsetting pressure from constrained supply Neutral for CF, NTR, relative to our updated estimates.

\- UAN: UAN pricing across regions was largely stable this week. In the U.S., similar to urea, demand has been softening as we move past the peak period and on farmer affordability concerns, though side-dress applications will support continued activity in late May/June. AN markets also continue to be tight, with this expected to worsen as mining demand picks up heading into summer. U.S. UAN prices are +\$210/MT (+58%) post-conflict, and QTD is tracking +\$25/MT vs. our updated 2Q26e where we expect to see prices begin to moderate as the quarter progresses. Modest positive relative to our updated estimates for CF, LXU, NTR.

\- Nat gas input costs: HH/AECO are +1%/+2% vs. two weeks ago. U.S./Canada gas has seen a muted reaction to the Middle East conflict (-9%/+2% since Feb). QTD pricing is tracking similar to our updated input cost estimates, which were revised lower. Neutral for CF, LXU, NTR input costs relative to our updated estimates.

\- Nat gas cost curve impact: TTF/JKM pricing is +4%/+2% vs. two weeks ago (spot \~\$16.05/16.85 per MMBtu), with QTD tracking below our revised 2Q26e that assumes further declines as the quarter progresses. Gas has moved up much less than we expected at the onset of the Middle East disruption (and relative to Russia/Ukraine impacts in 2022) which has driven a widening cost curve premium in nitrogen. On average, ammonia pricing has increased \~\$100 more than what the move in gas pricing would imply due to supply constraints, while urea/UAN pricing has moved up \~\$35/\$140 more than the move in gas, widening the premium to the cost curve. We expect supply constraints to drive above average premiums to the cost curve over 2026/2027 that progressively moderates.

# Phosphates prices flat to up 1% vs. 2 weeks ago; U.S. QTD tracking +\$75-\$120/Offshore +\$140-\$155 Q/Q

\- Prices were generally unchanged across U.S./offshore benchmarks last week. U.S. QTD pricing is tracking +\$75–120/MT, with offshore +\$140–155 Q/Q. Pricing remains elevated on higher ammonia/sulfur inputs and supply curtailments, though demand has also been negatively impacted in the U.S. and Brazil. U.S. applications are complete in some areas, and expectations are for reduced applications in Brazil due to affordability issues. Since pre-conflict, U.S. prices are +\$100–140/MT, export prices are +20% (+\$145/MT), and Brazil +25% (+\$170/MT). Supply remains tight with constraints from OCP (up to \~30% production cuts in 2Q), MOS shutdowns in Brazil, China export limits, Russia supply disruptions due to drone strikes, and Strait of Hormuz disruptions. In addition, MOS announced 50% curtailments at their Louisiana and Bartow facilities (\~1.7mmts) driven by high costs/limited availability for sulfur. Sulfur pricing has reached as high as \$900-1,150/MT between India, China, and Brazil, which is compressing stripping margins. We've seen compression of up to \~\$120/MT in offshore regions. QTD phosphates pricing vs. our updated 2Q26e for the U.S. is tracking in line for NOLA and -\$12/MT for Tampa, with offshore similar to UBSe. While phosphates pricing has been elevated, this has been more than offset by higher input costs which is negative for MOS & NTR.

# Potash prices (as of 4/28) were flat to up 2% vs. 2 weeks ago; Spot tracking +\$15 to +\$30 Q/Q

\- Prices were flat to up modestly vs. two weeks ago, with spot tracking +\$15–30/MT Q/Q. Increasing U.S. and Brazil demand during the quarter coupled with higher freight costs have led to higher pricing. NTR increased potash prices by \~\$10/MT for its North America summer fill, at an offer level of \$385/ST FOB. Canpotex is fully sold through 6/30, indicating offshore volumes are largely on track for the year, and demand destruction risk remains modest (\~5–15%) relative to phosphates. Offshore pricing has also increased on higher freight costs combined with improved demand during the quarter (offshore +\$20-35/MT since pre-conflict). QTD pricing is tracking +\$5/MT vs. our updated 2Q26e for the U.S., and +\$5-10/MT for offshore as we expect modest seasonal declines as the quarter progresses. It's also questionable how much benefit producers will see realized via higher margins, with a portion of the prices increases linked to freight. Supply from Israel/Jordan remains uninterrupted, and potash has been less exposed to Strait of Hormuz disruptions. Upward pricing momentum in potash is positive for IPI, MOS, and NTR.

# Corn/Soy Futures for 2026/2027 have shifted higher since the start of the year; We expect further increases to 2027 futures as the year progresses and lower production becomes apparent

\- Spot prices for corn/soybeans were +2%/+3% vs. two weeks ago at \~\$4.75/\$12.15. Corn futures for 2026/2027 at \$4.90/\$5.00 have moved up \~\$0.25/\$0.30 since the end of Feb/start of the year adding \~\$45/\~\$60 per acre (on 2025 yields). Soybean futures for 2026/2027 of \$11.74/\$11.36 have moved up \~\$0.40 since the end of Feb and \~\$0.75-\$1.10 since the start of the year adding \~\$20/\~\$40-\$55 per acre (on 2025 yields). We estimated farmer break even to cover total production costs was \$4.70/\$11.95 for corn/soy in 2025 and expected this would need to increase in 2026 to \$5.10/\$12.60 on the roll through of higher costs before government support payments (\$4.88/\$12.00 net of govt payments). See Figs 6-7 on YTD trends in corn/soy futures.

- We expect fertilizer costs to be up \~18-25% overall and above this level for those not pre-committed on nitrogen/phosphates prior to the Middle East conflict. The fertilizer basket for corn/soy has increased 29%/17% or \~\$58/\~\$15 per acre on spot pricing relative to pre-Middle East conflict levels. Further government support to growers beyond the \$12B initial farmer assistance package is likely in light of additional cost pressures as a flow on from the Middle East conflict and from past history during midterm election cycles. Channel checks point to expectations of further farmer assistance being available in the fall.   
- We expect 2027 crop futures will need to move higher as 2026 progresses and the impact of lower fertilizer applications globally flow through to reduced yields and or planted acres, ultimately lowering production levels and tightening stocks/use ratios.   
- A significant portion of the U.S. remains under drought conditions (see Figure 19) and April precipitation also remains below average levels (see Figure 20) in the Western and South/Eastern U.S. Lack of sufficient precipitation during the crop growth cycle could further lower yields/production and support higher crop prices into 2027.

# Nitrogen / Energy Input Costs:

# • 2026 Outlook:

- Our European gas costs (TTF) shift sUBStantially lower to \~\$15.15/MMBtu from \~\$22.40/MMBtu shortly after the onset of the Middle East disruption. We haven't seen nearly the increase we expected in gas prices, despite reduced availability with the cost increase being much milder than that seen in 2022 post Russia/Ukraine. We also reduce our U.S. gas input cost outlook for Henry Hub to \$3.15 from \$3.50/MMBtu prior on the benign response to the global disruption.   
- Nitrogen prices appear to be peaking at a sUBStantially lower level than we'd initially assessed post the onset of the Middle East disruption on a combination of 1) lower cost curve support, and 2) premiums to the cost curve that are sUBStantial but not quite enough to compensate overall. We reduce our 2026 ammonia/urea/UAN forecast by 16%/10%/5% from our prior estimates. We'd note however that there's yet to be any improvement in physical trade flows to date and with a sUBStantial share of global nitrogen production impacted by the Middle East disruption (\~20%+ of urea) we see risks that pricing either 1) sees a much more muted seasonal decline post the peak northern hemisphere season than is typical (reflected in our base case), or 2) could potentially continue to increase as supply shortages become more acute.

# • Med-term Outlook:

- Our medium term European gas outlook (TTF) shifts much more modestly, we reduce 2027 to \$12.90/MMBtu while lifting 2028-2030 1%-9%/yr which lifts the med-term average to \~\$8.70/MMBtu from \~\$8.20 previously. We view this as supportive of our mid-cycle view of the cost curve and nitrogen prices. U.S. gas input costs are largely unchanged in the \~\$3.60-\$3.70 range.   
- While we reduce the expected nitrogen pricing peak in 2026, our 2027 estimates see much more modest adjustments. We leave ammonia close to unchanged at \~\$595/MT, we lift urea by 9% to \~\$500/MT and we reduce UAN 2% to \$350/MT. We expect the lagged impacts of the Middle East disruption to continue to drive pricing spreads above cost curve support levels into 2H26 and 2027 as it will take several months to get product moving again to export destinations once a credible reopening of the Strait is proven out. We then expect the pricing premium to the cost curve to moderate progressively over time, however this does raise potential downside risks if the reopening were to proceed faster than we expected, or demand declines significantly as buyers shift to the sideline with no incentive to make purchases in the near term in anticipation of price declines. We make modest changes to our 2028-2029e estimates with ammonia/urea/UAN 3-6% higher per year.

# Potash:

\- 2026 Outlook: We modestly lift our 2026 pricing estimates for U.S. NOLA/Brazil/SE Asia by 2%/7%/3% to reflect the move up in prices during 1Q/2Q26. We still expect to see seasonal pricing declines into 3Q post the peak northern hemisphere season before a modest increase into 4Q. We see both moves being more muted this year than is typical. The move in prices has been highly debated, the potash bulls expect further price increases in the 2H with no seasonal declines and for the producers to see higher realized margins from the increases (vs. being offset from higher freight costs). This remains to be seen. Potash markets remain largely unimpacted by the Middle East disruption. Demand destruction impacts have been mixed, which coupled with general cost pressure on the grower could be a swing factor in whether we see pricing hold in or fall during the 2H.

\- Med-term Outlook: Our 2027-2030 forecast is largely unchanged. We continue to expect potash markets to see excess supply growth relative to demand growth as the decade progresses, and expect this to put downward pressure on pricing of \~\$10-\$15/MT per year, pushing pricing below mid-cycle levels while this is absorbed. We do however see downside support around the \~\$300/MT range as cash costs have moved higher and don't expect pricing to retest the lows seen over 2016-2019.

# Phosphates:

# • 2026 Outlook:

\- Phosphate markets are under a significant degree of pressure from the combination of high input costs (ammonia/sulfur) where supply has been constrained by the Middle East disruptions, combined with already elevated phosphate pricing that is driving significant demand destruction. This has seen phosphate prices increase, but not enough to offset the impact of higher raw material costs, compressing stripping margins. Margins have been compressed to such an extent that we've seen producers partially curtail supply (OCP 2Q turnarounds, MOS Brazil production curtailed, MOS U.S. production \~50% curtailed at two facilities). We lift our 2026 phosphate pricing outlook by \~1%-5% by region. We reduced our ammonia outlook by 16%, but we lift our sulfur price outlook by a further 17% and see risks there still biased to the upside. We see stripping margins compressing from \$620 in 3Q25 to only \$320 by 3Q26 and averaging \~\$360 for the year. The key interaction here is where phosphate prices ultimately settle out with higher input costs and constrained supply putting further upward pressure on pricing, but further price appreciation is limited by demand destruction and lack of affordability.

# • Med-term Outlook:

\- We lift our 2027 phosphate pricing outlook by 6%-10% by region as we see the lagged impacts on input costs/supply flowing through to a more moderate pricing declines into next year. That said, we still have benchmark prices down \~\$100/MT Y/Y, which is assuming a sUBStantial normalization. We expect input costs to decline, both ammonia/sulfur, however the path here will be tied to the timing and extent of an improvement in trade flows out of the Middle East. Our outlook beyond 2027 is only modestly changed on the phosphate pricing side, we continue to see prices moderating progressively but staying well above mid-cycle levels over the next few years with limited supply availability to address the S/D tightness which with declining input costs also sees stripping margins improve back into the \~\$375-\$400 range over our outlook

# Brazil Fertilizer Imports Update

Figures 9 to 18 below walk through the latest Brazil fertilizer import data trends (April 2026 data) in aggregate and across nitrogen, potash, phosphates and NPK blends. Figures on the LHS show monthly imports by product and figures on the RHS show cumulative import trends through the year, both including a 2019-21 average and then for 2022 onwards.

- Aggregate Fertilizers: Cumulative total fertilizer imports were +3% Y/Y and +30% vs. the 2019-21 avg. April was down \~11% Y/Y and \~71% above the 2019-21 average. On an aggregate basis, peak fertilizer imports seasonally are 3Q (\~30%) followed closely by 4Q (\~29%) while 1Q/2Q are \~20% each.   
- Nitrogen: Cumulative nitrogen imports were down 4% Y/Y and -5% below the 2019-2021 avg. April was -15% Y/Y but 25% above the 2019-2021 average. Peak nitrogen imports seasonally are 4Q (\~33%) followed by 1Q & 3Q (\~25%), with 2Q smallest (\~17%).   
- Potash: Cumulative potash imports were +17% Y/Y and +58% vs. the 2019-2021 avg. April imports were up 7% Y/Y and 93% above the 2019-21 average. Peak potash imports seasonally are 3Q (\~33%) followed by 4Q (\~27%). 1Q is the lowest seasonal quarter (\~18%) and 2Q is also below average (\~22%).   
- Phosphates: Cumulative phosphates imports were down 1% Y/Y but +79% vs. the 2019-21 avg. April phosphate imports were -33% Y/Y but +85% vs. the 2019-21 average. Peak phosphate imports seasonally are usually 3Q (\~33%) followed by 4Q (\~27%). 1Q is the lowest seasonal quarter (\~15%) and 2Q is average (\~25%).   
- NPKs: Cumulative NPK imports were -10% Y/Y and -3% vs. the 2019-21 average. April imports were up 40% Y/Y and +99% vs. the 2019-21 average. Peak NPK imports seasonally are usually 3Q (\~32%) followed by 4Q (\~29%). 1Q & 2Q are the lowest seasonal quarters (\~20% each).

# Valuation Method and Risk Statement

Earnings for commodity chemical companies are sensitive to swings in energy costs, especially oil and natural gas. Commodity chemical margins are influenced by market supply and demand dynamics that individual companies cannot control. As technology evolves, existing assets and businesses can become less competitive as newer, low-cost processes are developed. Historically, valuations for commodity chemical companies tend to be volatile. Our price targets are based on a P/E multiple in relation to the local market and on EV/EBITDA compared to the local market.

CF: Our CF price target is based on an EV/EBITDA multiple relative to the local market environment. Key risks to our rating and price target include lower crop prices (which may lead to lower farmer incomes and reduced spending on fertilizers), lower realized nitrogen prices, higher prices for key raw material inputs, operational problems, and adverse regulatory changes.

IPI: Price target is based on EV/EBITDA multiples relative to local markets. Key risks include lower agricultural crop prices (which may lead to lower farmer incomes and reduced spending on fertilizers), lower realized potash prices, higher prices for key raw material inputs, higher than expected mining development and/or operational costs, delays or denials in the granting of mining approvals and permits, and unfavorable regulations affecting fertilizer demand. IPI is also exposed to oil and gas activity in the Delaware basin through its water and associated services sales. Earnings in this segment could be impacted by declines in oil and gas activity in the region.

LXU: Our LXU price target is based on an EV/EBITDA multiple relative to the local market environment. Key risks to our rating and price target include lower crop prices (which may lead to lower farmer incomes and reduced spending on fertilizers), lower industrial production impacting industrial nitrogen consumption, ower realized nitrogen prices, higher prices for key raw material inputs, operational problems, and adverse regulatory changes.

MOS: Our price target is based on EV/EBITDA multiples relative to local markets. Key risks to our recommendation include lower crop prices (which may lead to lower farmer incomes and reduced spending on fertilizers), lower realized phosphate and/or potash prices, higher prices for key raw material inputs, higher-than-expected costs for flooding and/or other miner-related operational problems, and adverse legal judgments.

NTR: Our Price Target is based on an EV/EBITDA multiple relative to the local market. We point out to investors the potential risks inherent in the fertilizer sector including, but not limited to, the volatile nature of commodities prices, which could potentially be significantly different from expectations. Furthermore, Nutrien is also exposed to other risks including political, financial, and operational risks, which all have the potential of significantly impacting company performance.
