# European Luxury

# Expert Call Feedback: From Hype to Heritage

# Polarising trends drive returned focus to quality and relationships

Yesterday, we hosted an expert call with the founder of a global luxury resale and archival fashion platform in order to better understand latest trends in the secondary luxury landscape. Commenting on the broader luxury market, the expert believes a market correction is taking place, with increasing polarisation across brands and consumer segments, following years of democratisation of the luxury category and a growing tilt towards aspirational consumers, away from the core. More recently, the trend-sensitive consumers have slowed, while core high-end clients remain highly engaged and increasingly focused on rarity and craftsmanship. As such, the expert believes the key question for the sector's outlook lies in how brands "scale back" and focus on the core to drive growth, with client relationships and product quality at the heart of momentum. More broadly, in expert's view resale is becoming a core part of the luxury ecosystem, enabling circular consumption and supporting primary market.

# Hard luxury tailwind from consumers' trading-up within the categorial pyramid

At the category level, performance remains robust in core segments such as handbags and ready-to-wear, while there is a notable acceleration in fine jewellery and watches, driven by the trading-up behaviour from the entry-level leather goods among repeat consumers. In general, pricing dynamics reflect a bifurcated market. Pricing for iconic items remains highly resilient, while more trend-driven or limited-edition pieces have normalised materially after reaching peak multiples during the pandemic. Buyers and sellers on the platform were said to have similar profiles, with the "relationship-driven" nature of the sourcing and selling activities as a key differentiator compared to other resellers. Particularly on the supply side, the expert noted that inventory availability has structurally improved, reflective of the reduced stigma around resale and stronger relationships with VIP consignors, although some sellers are temporarily holding back supply in anticipation of a pricing rebound to previous secondary multiples.

# Increasing desirability for archival pieces from distinctive creative eras

The polarised sector backdrop appears consistent with recent brand-specific dynamics. The expert highlighted Chanel (private) as an outperformer, Citing renewed interest in leather goods following Matthieu Blazy's creative debut. Among LVMH's (Buy) key brands, Louis Vuitton continues to show stable momentum, with strong performance of classic vintage monograms driven by the latest campaign, while secondhand Dior demand remains more selective, focused on iconic eras (e.g. Galliano). Elsewhere, Gucci (Kering, Neutral) is seen as more volatile and exposed to fashion cycles after prior overexposure, although archival demand is re-emerging with the Tom Ford-inspired Demna era. Trends at Prada and Miu Miu (Prada, Neutral) remain very strong, supported by broad client relevance, with Prada secondary prices close to retail. Finally, the expert noted growing interest in The Row and Phoebe Philo, reflecting demand for discretion and craftsmanship, alongside a nascent pickup in archival Versace (Prada, Neutral).

# Hermès seeing normalisation post speculative post-pandemic buying

Hermès (Neutral) remains the anchor of the resale market, with demand still structurally strong but increasingly concentrated among VIP clients seeking rare and less recognisable pieces than the "bread and butter" standard Kelly and Birkin bags. While pricing for core models remains resilient, the normalisation of limited-edition bags highlights the fading influence of speculative, status-driven post-pandemic buyers. It was noted that resale premium of some of the pre-loved Hermès bags was once at \~2-2.5x retail value, which has now normalised to \~1-1.5x.
