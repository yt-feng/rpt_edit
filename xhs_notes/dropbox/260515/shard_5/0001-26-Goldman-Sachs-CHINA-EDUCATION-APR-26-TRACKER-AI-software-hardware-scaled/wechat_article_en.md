# AI Is Reshaping China’s Education Market Faster Than Offline Recovery Can Offset Structural Headwinds

The April 2026 data from China’s education sector reveals a market at an inflection point. AI-driven learning tools are scaling at triple-digit rates, while offline tutoring licenses continue their structural decline and overseas study demand softens. The controlling insight is straightforward: the winners in China’s education market will not be those who simply manage offline recovery, but those who can capture the AI-native user base that is growing far faster than the rest of the sector combined. ByteDance’s Doubao Study, with daily active user growth accelerating to 128% year-on-year, and TAL’s Xueersi.com, with MAU growth accelerating to 22%, are the clearest signals that the center of gravity has shifted. Investors who frame the sector through the lens of offline capacity expansion or traditional tutoring enrollment risk missing the more consequential structural shift already underway.

Why now matters. The April data marks the first sustained period where AI education apps have achieved meaningful scale relative to the K-12 student population. Combined MAUs of major AI learning tablet brands reached 16 million, equivalent to 9% of China’s K-9 student population, up from 6% a year ago. This is not a niche anymore. It is a penetration curve that, if sustained, will redefine total addressable market assumptions for the entire sector. Meanwhile, the offline non-academic tutoring license count continued its downward trend, and overseas study visas granted to Chinese students by Australia and Canada declined 6% year-on-year in January-February 2026. The convergence of these trends creates a strategic imperative: the future of China’s education market belongs to companies that can build software and hardware ecosystems around AI-powered personalization, not those that rely on regulatory reopening or geographic expansion of physical centers.

## AI Education Apps Are Outpacing the Broader Market by an Order of Magnitude, and the Gap Is Widening

The most important data point in the April tracker is not the absolute user numbers but the growth trajectory. Doubao Study’s DAU growth accelerated to 128% year-on-year, making it the sixth-largest AI native app in China by daily active users. This is not a one-month anomaly. The acceleration from March to April, combined with sustained time-spent market share gains among major K-12 learning tools, indicates that ByteDance has found a product-market fit that competitors have not yet replicated. Gauth, ByteDance’s overseas education app, achieved monthly billings of approximately $1.0 million, up 35% year-on-year, with last-twelve-month billings reaching $12.9 million. Gauth also introduced two new features in April: Gauth Atlas, an interactive tool that transforms topics into dynamic visual knowledge maps, and an AI Live Tutor feature launched in Southeast Asia. These product innovations are not incremental; they represent a deliberate strategy to embed AI as the core learning interface rather than as an add-on.

The implication for incumbents is stark. Traditional online tutoring platforms, including those operated by TAL and New Oriental, are growing MAUs at rates between 6% and 22% year-on-year. That is respectable, but it is not competitive with the AI-native growth rates. The gap suggests that the AI education apps are not merely taking share from incumbents; they are expanding the total addressable market by attracting users who were not previously engaged with digital learning tools. For investors, the question is whether the incumbents can build their own AI-native products fast enough to retain relevance, or whether they will be relegated to serving a shrinking offline segment.

## AI Learning Tablets Show Divergent Brand Performance, with Premium Positioning Outperforming Discounting

The combined GMV of major AI learning tablet brands dropped 2% year-on-year in April, a sharp deceleration from 29% growth in March. However, this aggregate figure masks a crucial divergence. Yuanfudao and TAL saw GMV growth of 68% and 20% year-on-year respectively, while Xiaodu, BBK, Zuoyebang, and iFlytek all recorded declines. The pattern suggests that the market is bifurcating between brands that can command premium pricing and those that are caught in a race to the bottom.

TAL’s tablet ASP showed a year-on-year decline narrowing to just 6%, while increasing month-on-month for four consecutive months to approximately 4,000 RMB. This is a significant signal. After launching the X5 Ultra model at 5,900 RMB (or 4,900 RMB post national sUBSidy), TAL is demonstrating that consumers are willing to pay for AI-enhanced hardware. The month-on-month ASP recovery suggests that the tablet category is not commoditizing; rather, it is segmenting into premium AI-enabled devices and basic alternatives that are losing share. For investors, the key metric to watch is not just GMV growth but whether ASP trends can sustain or improve, as that will determine margin profiles.

The broader tablet market penetration at 9% of K-9 students implies that the ceiling is still far off. But the April data also raises a cautionary note: the 47% month-on-month GMV decline suggests that the March surge may have been pulled forward by promotional activity or seasonal factors. The May quarter, which is the largest for tablet sales, will be the true test of whether the penetration trend is structural or episodic.

## Offline Tutoring Licenses Continue to Decline, Confirming That Regulatory Headwinds Are Structural, Not Cyclical

The number of offline non-academic tutoring licenses continued its downward trend in April, and the report notes that the leading education companies expanded their learning center capacity only by low single digits quarter-on-quarter. This is consistent with a market that has accepted a permanently smaller offline footprint. The not-for-profit subject tutoring platforms saw combined MAUs decline 21% year-on-year in April, with total time spent declining at a similar rate.

The strategic implication is clear: the offline recovery thesis that some investors have been betting on is not materializing at scale. The regulatory environment has not loosened, and the economics of operating physical learning centers remain challenged by high fixed costs and limited pricing power. Companies that continue to allocate capital to offline expansion are likely to face diminishing returns, while those that redirect resources to AI-enabled products and online platforms are better positioned for the next growth cycle.

The report also notes that TAL and New Oriental are estimated to have expanded learning centers by low single digits quarter-on-quarter. This is a measured approach, but it raises the question of whether even this pace of expansion is optimal. If AI apps are capturing the growth in user engagement, the marginal return on an additional learning center may be lower than the marginal return on an additional AI product feature.

## Overseas Study Demand Is Softening, but the Composition of Demand May Be Shifting in Ways the Report Does Not Fully Capture

Student visas granted to Chinese students by Australia and Canada declined 6% year-on-year in January-February 2026, with Australia down 5% and Canada down 9%. This continues a trend that has been evident since 2024, driven by a combination of geopolitical tensions, tighter visa policies, and the rising attractiveness of domestic higher education options in China.

What the report does not fully address is whether this decline is uniform across all segments or whether it is concentrated in certain types of institutions or programs. It is plausible that demand for elite overseas institutions remains resilient, while demand for mid-tier programs is declining as Chinese universities improve their global rankings and as cost-conscious families reassess the return on investment of overseas education. If that is the case, the overseas study market may be undergoing a compositional shift rather than a simple contraction, with implications for the business models of companies that serve this segment.

Additionally, the report does not break out data for the United States or the United Kingdom, which remain the largest destinations for Chinese students. Without that data, it is difficult to assess whether the Australia and Canada trends are indicative of a broader pattern or specific to those countries. Investors should watch for visa data from the US and UK in the coming months to determine whether the overseas study headwind is global or regional.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: Three Questions to Determine Which Education Companies Will Win in the AI Era

The April tracker provides enough data to construct a simple but powerful decision framework for evaluating education companies. Investors should ask three questions about any company in the sector.

First, what is the company’s AI-native user growth rate relative to the market? Companies whose core products are AI-powered and generating user growth above 50% year-on-year are in the offensive camp. Companies growing at 10-20% are in a defensive position. Companies with declining or flat user engagement are at risk of structural obsolescence. The data shows that ByteDance’s Doubao Study and Gauth are in the first camp, TAL’s Xueersi.com is in the second, and several legacy platforms are in the third.

Second, is the company’s hardware strategy focused on premium AI-enabled devices or on volume-driven discounting? The tablet data shows that premium positioning correlates with positive GMV and ASP trends, while discounting correlates with declining GMV. Companies that can demonstrate that their hardware ASP is stable or improving, even as volumes fluctuate, are likely to have better margin profiles and more durable competitive advantages.

Third, is the company’s capital allocation aligned with the AI opportunity or with legacy offline expansion? The offline license data suggests that continued investment in physical learning centers is a low-return strategy. Companies that are redirecting capital toward AI product development, software features, and digital content are better positioned for the next five years.

These three questions do not yield a single answer for every company, but they provide a consistent framework for evaluating trade-offs. The report’s coverage universe includes only one Buy-rated name (TAL), with Neutral ratings on New Oriental and Gaotu, and Sell ratings on East Buy and Offcn. This rating distribution is consistent with the framework: TAL has the strongest AI tablet momentum and accelerating online MAU growth, while the others face varying degrees of structural headwinds.

## The Report Leaves Several Open Questions That Deserve Further Analysis

The April tracker is rich in data, but it also leaves several important questions unanswered. First, the report does not provide a breakdown of AI education app monetization beyond Gauth’s billings. How are Doubao Study and other domestic AI apps generating revenue? Are they relying on sUBScriptions, advertising, or in-app purchases? Without this data, it is difficult to assess whether the user growth is translating into sustainable business models.

Second, the report does not address the competitive dynamics between ByteDance’s AI education products and TAL’s offerings. Both are growing, but they are targeting different segments of the market. ByteDance’s Doubao Study is a general-purpose AI study companion, while TAL’s Xueersi.com is more closely tied to its curriculum and tutoring ecosystem. Will these products converge or remain differentiated? The answer will determine whether the market becomes a winner-take-most contest or a multi-player ecosystem.

Third, the report does not analyze the impact of national sUBSidies on tablet ASP and demand. The X5 Ultra model’s price after sUBSidy was 4,900 RMB, which is close to the overall ASP of 4,000 RMB. How much of the recent ASP recovery is driven by product mix shifts toward higher-end models, and how much is driven by the temporary effect of sUBSidies? The sustainability of ASP trends will depend on the answer.

These are not gaps in the report’s analysis; they are natural open questions that arise from any high-quality research. For investors who want to explore these questions in greater depth, the full report contains additional exhibits and data that provide a more complete picture.
