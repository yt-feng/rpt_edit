# China Education Is Being Reshaped by AI Scale, Not Regulatory Relief

The narrative that China's education sector is simply waiting for regulatory loosening to return to its former growth trajectory is increasingly untenable. The April 2026 data from a comprehensive industry tracker tells a different story: the sector is being fundamentally restructured by the scaling of AI-native products, and the winners are those who can command user attention and monetize it through software and hardware integration, not those who expand offline footprints. The report's key finding is that ByteDance's Doubao Study has accelerated its daily active user growth to 128% year-on-year, while the combined gross merchandise value of major AI learning tablets has slipped 2% year-on-year, revealing a market at an inflection point where software engagement is decoupling from hardware sales.

This divergence matters because it signals that the traditional playbook of selling hardware devices or expanding tutoring center capacity is no longer sufficient. The offline tutoring license count continues its downward trajectory, and even as learning centers for major players like New Oriental and TAL Education expand modestly quarter-on-quarter, the structural headwind is clear. Meanwhile, the overseas study market faces sustained pressure, with student visas granted to Chinese students by Australia and Canada declining 6% year-on-year in the first two months of 2026. The old growth engines are sputtering, and the new ones require a different strategic mindset.

The strategic question for investors and operators is no longer about when regulation will ease. It is about which companies can build durable AI-driven user engagement, how they will monetize that engagement, and whether the hardware ecosystem can survive the transition to software-first business models. The answers are not uniform across the sector, and the data from April 2026 provides a nuanced but decisive picture.

## ByteDance's Doubao Study and Gauth Are Winning the AI Education Arms Race Through User Engagement and Monetization

The most striking data point in the April tracker is the continued acceleration of Doubao Study, which has sustained its position as the sixth-largest AI native app in China by daily active users. With DAU growth of 128% year-on-year, Doubao Study is not merely riding a wave of general AI interest; it is capturing a specific and valuable segment of the K-12 learning tool market. The time spent share data shows ByteDance's app steadily encroaching on established players like Zuoyebang and Yuanfudao, indicating that the product's utility is translating into genuine user stickiness, not just download numbers.

This success is not confined to China. Gauth, ByteDance's overseas education app, has introduced new features including Gauth Atlas, an interactive tool that transforms topics into dynamic visual knowledge maps, and an AI Live Tutor feature in Southeast Asia. The results are tangible: monthly billings reached approximately $1.0 million in April, representing 35% year-on-year growth, with last-twelve-month billings hitting $12.9 million. More importantly, Gauth's monthly billings per average DAU grew 49% year-on-year to $0.35, suggesting that the platform is not just attracting users but is becoming more effective at converting them into paying customers. This dual success in domestic and international markets demonstrates that AI education products can achieve scale and monetization simultaneously, a combination that has eluded many edtech companies in the post-pandemic era.

The implication for the broader market is that AI-driven education apps are no longer experimental. They are becoming the primary interface for learning, and the companies that build the best user experience will capture disproportionate value. ByteDance's ability to leverage its existing AI infrastructure and user data gives it a structural advantage that traditional education companies will find difficult to replicate.

## TAL Education's AI Learning Tablet Strategy Shows That Hardware-Software Integration Is a High-Wire Act

TAL Education's performance in the AI learning tablet market illustrates the complexity of competing in a hardware ecosystem that is being disrupted by software. On the surface, TAL's tablet gross merchandise value grew 20% year-on-year in April, a solid performance that outperformed the overall market decline of 2% year-on-year. However, the month-on-month decline of 42% in GMV and 47% in shipments highlights the volatility of this business, particularly as the market adjusts after the March promotional period.

The more encouraging signal lies in the average selling price trajectory. TAL's tablet ASP saw its year-on-year decline narrow to 6%, while increasing 6% month-on-month to approximately RMB 4,000 in April. This marks the fourth consecutive month of sequential ASP increases, driven by the launch of the X5 Ultra model priced at RMB 5,900, or RMB 4,900 post national sUBSidy. This suggests that TAL is successfully moving up the value chain, convincing consumers to pay more for a premium product that likely includes more advanced AI tutoring features.

The strategic challenge for TAL is that its tablet business is operating in a market where the combined GMV of major brands dropped 2% year-on-year, with significant divergence among players. Yuanfudao grew 68% year-on-year, while Xiaodu declined 60% and BBK dropped 40%. This dispersion indicates that the tablet market is not a rising tide lifting all boats; it is a zero-sum game where product differentiation and brand loyalty are becoming decisive. TAL's ability to maintain growth while improving ASP is commendable, but the question remains whether the tablet business can achieve the scale necessary to offset the structural headwinds in its offline tutoring operations.

## The Offline Tutoring and Overseas Study Headwinds Are Structural, Not Cyclical

The data on offline learning center capacity and overseas study visas should give pause to any investor betting on a simple reversion to pre-regulation norms. The number of offline non-academic tutoring licenses continued its downward trend in April, and while the tracker estimates that New Oriental and TAL learning centers expanded low single-digit percentages quarter-on-quarter, this expansion is occurring against a backdrop of shrinking license availability. This is not a temporary dip; it is a regulatory reality that will constrain the physical footprint of tutoring companies for the foreseeable future.

The overseas study market faces similar structural pressures. Student visas granted to Chinese students by Australia and Canada declined 6% year-on-year in January-February 2026, with Australia down 5% and Canada down 9%. These declines reflect a combination of geopolitical tensions, more restrictive immigration policies, and the growing attractiveness of domestic higher education options. For companies like New Oriental that have historically relied on overseas test preparation and study advisory services, this trend represents a significant and likely persistent headwind.

The implication is that the growth story for China's education sector will increasingly come from domestic, AI-enabled products rather than from international expansion or offline capacity. Companies that fail to pivot their business models toward software and AI risk being left with declining legacy operations.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Path to Profitability for AI Education Apps

While the tracker provides granular data on user engagement and monetization for AI education apps, it leaves several critical questions unanswered. The most pressing is whether the current monetization models for apps like Doubao Study and Gauth are sustainable at scale. Gauth's $0.35 monthly billings per average DAU is impressive, but it remains unclear whether this metric can be maintained as the user base continues to expand, particularly if ByteDance decides to prioritize user acquisition over revenue in new markets.

Another open question is the competitive dynamics between ByteDance's AI education products and the offerings from established education companies like TAL and New Oriental. The tracker shows that Xueersi.com's MAU growth accelerated to 22% year-on-year in April, while TAL's Peiyou app saw moderation to 19% growth. This suggests that TAL's digital strategy is gaining traction, but the company is still competing against a tech giant with vastly superior AI capabilities and data resources. Can TAL and other education-focused companies build AI products that are genuinely differentiated, or will they be forced to rely on ByteDance's technology stack, ceding control of the user relationship?

Finally, the report does not address the regulatory landscape for AI education products specifically. While offline tutoring licenses are declining, there is no indication of whether AI tutoring apps will face their own regulatory scrutiny. Given the Chinese government's focus on data privacy, content moderation, and the prevention of excessive academic pressure on students, it is plausible that AI education apps could become subject to new regulations that limit their functionality or monetization potential. Investors and operators should monitor this risk closely.

## A Decision Framework for Investors and Operators in the New Education Landscape

Based on the April tracker data, stakeholders should evaluate China education opportunities through a three-lens framework that prioritizes AI engagement, monetization efficiency, and regulatory resilience.

First, assess AI user engagement quality over quantity. The raw DAU and MAU numbers are important, but the more revealing metric is time spent share and the growth rate of engagement per user. Doubao Study's ability to increase its share of time spent among K-12 learning tools is a stronger signal than its DAU growth alone. Companies that can demonstrate deepening engagement, not just expanding reach, are building defensible moats.

Second, evaluate monetization efficiency through the lens of revenue per user and the sustainability of that revenue. Gauth's 49% growth in monthly billings per average DAU is a positive indicator, but investors should examine whether this is driven by price increases, higher conversion rates, or a mix shift toward higher-value users. The key question is whether the monetization model can scale without degrading the user experience or attracting regulatory attention.

Third, stress-test regulatory resilience by examining the degree to which a company's revenue is tied to activities that could be restricted. Companies with heavy exposure to offline tutoring or overseas study advisory should be valued with a discount for regulatory risk, while those whose revenue comes from AI software and hardware with clear educational utility may warrant a premium. The declining license count and visa numbers suggest that regulatory risk is not going away.
