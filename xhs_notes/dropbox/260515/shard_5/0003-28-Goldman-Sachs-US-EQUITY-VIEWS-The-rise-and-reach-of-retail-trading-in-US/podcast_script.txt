EN_A: So they trade way more than their asset base suggests. But how does that actually work? Do they trade directly on exchanges?

EN_B: Not really. Most retail orders never hit the public exchanges. Over 87% of retail order flow goes to specialized market makers called wholesalers, and about 84% of those orders are internalized, meaning the wholesaler fills them from their own inventory.

EN_A: So when do retail traders actually move the market?

EN_B: During periods of high volatility. The report notes that retail trading in index-linked ETFs spiked during the selloffs in 2020, 2022, and 2025. And when individual stocks drop sharply, retail tends to pile in.

EN_A: What about leverage? Are they using borrowed money?

EN_B: Yes, margin debt and leveraged ETFs. Margin balances at major brokers are now at 1.8% of customer assets, similar to the 2021 peak. And retail traders are twice as active in leveraged S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 ETFs compared to their non-leveraged versions.
