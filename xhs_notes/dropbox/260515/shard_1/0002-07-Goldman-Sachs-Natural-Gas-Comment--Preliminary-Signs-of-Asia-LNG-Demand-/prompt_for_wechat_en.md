# Natural Gas Comment: Preliminary Signs of Asia LNG Demand Recovery Keep Risks to 2H26 TTF Skewed to the Upside

We have argued that weak Asia demand for LNG has bought Europe time. The lower the LNG imports in places like China, South Korea and Japan, the higher the LNG volume available to go to Europe, with this dynamic ultimately limiting the risk premium embedded in TTF prices.   
However, because we expect LNG imports to rebound in China and South Korea in particular relative to this spring's lows, driven by summer demand and storage rebuilding, we have argued that risks to TTF (and Asia LNG) prices remain skewed to the upside, should the Hormuz disruption linger.   
- With two weeks of data now available for May, we see Asia LNG demand starting a path to recovery (Exhibit 1), with the current four-week-moving average (4wma) of LNG imports running 4 mtpa above our 225 mtpa May expectations. This reinforces the upside risks we see to our 44/40 EUR/MWh (\$14.90/\$13.65/mmBtu) 3Q26/4Q26 TTF price forecasts (to 65/53 EUR/MWh) in a scenario where a Hormuz flow normalization is delayed to late-Jul (vs our late-Jun base case):

China imports on the rise. While China LNG imports remain down yoy, that gap is narrowing as imports rise sequentially. The four-week-moving average for LNG imports is at 48 mtpa, well above the 36 mtpa March average (Exhibit 2). We expect further upside to imports from here (including 3Q26 at 67 mtpa) to allow China gas inventories to build to levels slightly above last year's ahead of the next winter.

☐ South Korea imports on the rise. We see a similar pattern (though in smaller scale) of rising LNG imports in South Korea, as we move away from shoulder season and closer to summer. The four-week-moving average for LNG imports is at 42 mtpa, above the 39 mtpa April average and our 40 mtpa May expectation.
