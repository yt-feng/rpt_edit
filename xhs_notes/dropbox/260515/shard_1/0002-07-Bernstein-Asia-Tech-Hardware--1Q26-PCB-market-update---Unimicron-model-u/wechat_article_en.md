# The PCB Industry Is No Longer a Cyclical Sideshow—It Is Now the Structural Battleground for AI Infrastructure

For years, the printed circuit board and sUBStrate industry was treated as a lagging indicator of semiconductor demand—a steady, unglamorous enabler that investors could safely ignore until the next upcycle. That assumption has broken. The first quarter of 2026 confirms that the PCB supply chain has crossed a structural inflection point, driven not by broad economic recovery but by the relentless scaling of AI data center infrastructure. The combined revenue of sampled AI PCB companies rose 38% year-on-year and 8% quarter-on-quarter in 1Q26, with ABF sUBStrate revenue growth accelerating to 40% YoY—already surpassing the previous peak set in the fourth quarter of 2022. This is not a bounce; it is a regime change.

What makes this moment different is the simultaneity of demand, pricing, and capacity dynamics. AI server shipments are accelerating. Raw material tightness across T-glass, copper-clad laminate, copper foil, and now ABF film is persisting through 2026. And the industry is entering an aggressive capex cycle that is not merely about adding volume but about building high-end, geographically diversified capacity. The narrative has shifted from "how much can they produce" to "who can secure the supply chain and capture the pricing power."

This report, based on a detailed analysis from a leading Asian technology research team, argues that the PCB and sUBStrate market is undergoing a multi-year re-rating. The winners will be those companies that combine AI exposure, cost pass-through capability, and disciplined capacity investment. But the market may be underestimating the complexity of margin recovery, the risk of supply chain bottlenecks, and the unevenness of adoption across end markets. The following sections unpack the key forces shaping this market and what they mean for strategic decision-making.

## ABF SUBStrate Revenue Has Already Exceeded the Prior Cycle Peak, and the Pricing Trajectory Is Accelerating Beyond What Consensus Models Assume

The most striking data point from the 1Q26 update is the recovery trajectory of ABF sUBStrates. Revenue growth accelerated to 40% year-on-year, surpassing the previous cyclical high from the fourth quarter of 2022. This is significant because the 2022 peak was driven by a broad-based pandemic-era demand surge across consumer electronics, PCs, and networking. The current cycle is narrower and more concentrated: it is being driven almost entirely by AI accelerators, server CPUs, and the associated high-end networking infrastructure.

The pricing environment is strengthening further. The report estimates that ABF sUBStrate ASPs increased 5% quarter-on-quarter in 1Q26 and will accelerate to 7-10% quarter-on-quarter through the remainder of 2026. The driver is a roughly 30% raw material cost hike beginning in the second quarter, combined with a richer mix of high-end ABF products. This is not a temporary supply-demand imbalance. It reflects a structural upgrade in the complexity of sUBStrates required for AI chips, which demand tighter line widths, larger form factors, and higher layer counts.

The implication is that companies with direct exposure to AI ABF sUBStrates—such as Unimicron and Ibiden—are entering a multi-year pricing upcycle that consensus models may not fully capture. The report models Unimicron's AI ABF plus HDI revenue reaching half of total company revenue going forward, supporting a 30% revenue CAGR from 2025 to 2028. But the more important insight is that the pricing power is not uniform. It accrues to those who have secured long-term agreements with leading chip designers and who can demonstrate the technical capability to produce the most complex sUBStrates.

## Raw Material Tightness Is Shifting from a Cost Headwind to a Competitive Moat—Companies That Control the Supply Chain Will Capture the Asymmetric Upside

The report identifies a persistent tightness in raw materials including T-glass, high-end CCL, and copper foil that is likely to remain through the end of 2026. Now, ABF film costs are also reported to rise in the second half of 2026. This is not merely an input cost story; it is a structural constraint that will separate winners from laggards.

The key dynamic is that raw material scarcity is creating a two-tier market. Tier-1 suppliers of T-glass and ABF film are effectively capacity-constrained, and their customers—the sUBStrate and PCB manufacturers—are competing for allocations. The report notes that some CPUs are beginning to adopt T-glass from second-source suppliers, but GPU and ASIC adoption of tier-2 suppliers is expected to remain limited through the end of 2026. This means that the highest-value segments of the market will remain dependent on a narrow set of qualified suppliers.

For PCB and sUBStrate manufacturers, the strategic implication is clear. Those who have long-term supply agreements with upstream material suppliers, or who have invested in backward integration, will have a cost and reliability advantage. Those who rely on spot-market procurement will face margin compression and delivery risk. The report's channel checks confirm that pricing power is cascading downstream: CCL has experienced multiple price adjustments since 2025, and ABF film hikes are now expected. The ability to pass through these cost increases to end customers—without losing share—is the defining competitive test of this cycle.

The second-order implication is that the current capex cycle is not just about adding capacity; it is about securing supply chain sovereignty. Chinese MLPCB and HDI companies are scaling advanced production in China while accelerating overseas capacity in Southeast Asia. Taiwanese suppliers are also increasing investment. Unimicron has guided for NT$34 billion in capex in 2026, up 33% year-on-year. Kinsus has guided for 31% and 25% year-on-year capex growth in 2026 and 2027 respectively. Gold Circuit has guided for NT$17 billion-plus in 2026 capex, more than double the prior year. These are not incremental investments; they are strategic bets on a multi-year demand cycle.

## The Margin Recovery Is Real, but Consensus May Be Overestimating the Speed and Underestimating the Shape

The report's analysis of gross margin trends reveals a clear divergence. Companies with high AI exposure—Victory Giant, Gold Circuit, WUS Group—have already seen significant margin expansion, with consensus expecting further sequential growth through 2026 and 2027. But the recovery for others is more gradual. Unimicron, despite its strong revenue growth and pricing power, is expected to see gross margins reach only approximately 35% by end-2028, according to the report's model. This is a more conservative trajectory than consensus, which may be assuming a "shortage-driven" margin spike.

This distinction matters. The market is pricing in a rapid margin normalization for many PCB players, but the report's detailed modeling suggests that margin improvement will be sequential and lumpy, not linear. The reasons are structural. First, the ramp-up of new capacity involves initial inefficiencies and depreciation charges that depress margins in the near term. Second, the product mix shift toward higher-end sUBStrates is gradual, as qualification cycles for new AI chips take time. Third, raw material cost increases may not be fully passed through in every quarter, creating margin volatility.

The report's estimate for Unimicron's EPS is instructive: EPS is modeled to more than triple to NT$14 in 2026, then grow approximately 80% in 2027 and approximately 45% in 2028 to NT$36.6. That is a compound annual growth rate of over 60% from 2025 to 2028. Yet the report still believes its estimates are below consensus for 2027 and 2028. This suggests that the market may be expecting an even more aggressive margin expansion than the report's conservative assumptions allow. The open question is whether consensus is correct in expecting a faster recovery, or whether the report's gradualist view is more realistic given capacity ramp constraints and input cost dynamics.

## The Report Does Not Fully Answer How the Market Will Price the Geopolitical Risk Embedded in the Capacity Expansion Race

One of the most important strategic developments highlighted in the report is the geographic diversification of PCB and sUBStrate capacity. Chinese companies are expanding both domestically and in Southeast Asia. Taiwanese suppliers are accelerating capacity expansion on ABF sUBStrates and PCBs for AI use. Japanese players like Ibiden and Ajinomoto are investing in new facilities. The report documents this trend but does not fully explore its implications for pricing, supply security, and long-term competitive dynamics.

The critical unanswered question is whether this capacity buildout will lead to oversupply in the 2028-2030 timeframe, or whether demand growth will absorb it. The report's demand projections are robust, but they are based on current AI adoption trajectories and chip roadmaps. If AI infrastructure investment decelerates, or if a new sUBStrate technology emerges that renders current capacity obsolete, the aggressive capex cycle could become a liability.

Moreover, the geographic dispersion of capacity introduces new risks. Supply chains that span multiple jurisdictions are more resilient to localized disruptions but also more exposed to tariff policy, export controls, and regulatory divergence. The report notes that Chinese companies are expanding overseas to mitigate geopolitical risks, but it does not model the potential impact of trade restrictions on cross-border sUBStrate flows. For investors and strategists, this is a first-order uncertainty that requires independent scenario analysis.

## Decision Framework: How to Evaluate PCB and SUBStrate Companies in the AI Era

For readers who need to translate this analysis into actionable decisions, the following framework captures the key variables that will determine relative performance in this cycle.

First, assess AI revenue exposure and mix quality. The report makes clear that not all AI PCB exposure is equal. Companies that serve the highest-value segments—AI accelerators, server CPUs, advanced networking—will enjoy stronger pricing power and more durable demand. The report's exhibit on AI PCB mix shows a wide dispersion: Gold Circuit has 70% AI mix in its PCB business, while Kinwong has only 12%. The quality of that exposure matters as much as the percentage.

Second, evaluate pricing power and cost pass-through capability. In an environment of rising raw material costs, companies that have long-term agreements with customers and suppliers will be able to maintain or expand margins. Those that rely on spot pricing will be squeezed. The report's analysis of ABF sUBStrate ASP trends and raw material tightness suggests that pricing power is concentrated among a few players.

Third, analyze the capex cycle for discipline and timing. The report documents a significant ramp in capital expenditure across the industry. The key question is whether a company's capex is aligned with visible demand or speculative. Unimicron's 33% capex increase is backed by clear AI demand visibility over the next two years. Other players may be investing on less certain assumptions.

Fourth, consider margin trajectory versus consensus. The report's estimates for Unimicron are below consensus for 2027 and 2028, suggesting that the market may be pricing in a faster margin recovery than the fundamentals support. This creates potential for either disappointment if consensus is wrong, or upside if the report is too conservative. Investors should stress-test their own assumptions against both the report's model and consensus.

Fifth, monitor the raw material supply chain for bottlenecks and sUBStitution risks. The report notes limited GPU/ASIC adoption of tier-2 T-glass suppliers by end-2026. If that changes, it could alter the competitive dynamics for sUBStrate manufacturers. Conversely, if tier-1 supply remains tight, pricing power will persist.
