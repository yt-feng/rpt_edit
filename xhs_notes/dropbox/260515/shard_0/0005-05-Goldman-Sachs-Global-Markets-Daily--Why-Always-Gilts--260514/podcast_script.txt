ZH_A: 最近英国国债收益率又飙了，30年期收益率突破了5.8%，这是1998年以来的最高水平。这背后到底发生了什么？

ZH_B: 其实核心问题不是英国国债突然变得多糟糕，而是市场在为英国经济的高不确定性和高通胀买单。简单说，英国过去几年增长很弱，但通胀却是主要经济体里最高的，这种组合让投资者要求更高的风险溢价。

ZH_A: 所以这份报告真正想回答的问题是什么？是英国国债是不是被过度抛售了？

ZH_B: 对，报告的核心问题是：当前这么高的收益率和风险溢价到底合不合理？结论是，大部分是合理的，但也有一些短期因素在放大波动。

ZH_A: 那我们先聊聊宏观因素。英国经济到底哪里出了问题？

ZH_B: 英国的经济表现确实很特殊。增长弱，但通胀高，而且调查显示，英国的经济不确定性在主要经济体中也是最高的。这种低增长、高通胀的组合，让债券作为风险对冲工具的价值下降了，所以投资者要求更高的溢价。

ZH_A: 这意味着什么？是不是说英国国债的避险属性在减弱？

ZH_B: 没错。当债券和股票的相关性变高时，它的避险价值就打了折扣。英国因为能源结构依赖天然气，工资和价格又容易传导，所以通胀更顽固，这让市场对英国资产的定价更谨慎。

ZH_A: 除了宏观，还有什么因素在推高收益率？

ZH_B: 供给和需求的变化也很关键。英国财政赤字很大，再加上英国央行的量化紧缩，导致国债供应量大增。而需求端，英国养老金系统在2022年危机后，对国债的需求大幅下降了。

ZH_A: 所以是供给多了，但大买家少了？那这个趋势会持续吗？

ZH_B: 报告指出，虽然供给压力还在，但一些相对指标其实在改善。比如英国国债的互换利差和收益率曲线形态，最近几个季度相比其他市场其实有所好转。这说明英国国债并不是所有坏消息的源头。

ZH_A: 那短期来看，最大的风险是什么？

ZH_B: 最大的风险来自财政政策。如果英国政府因为国防开支增加等原因放松财政纪律，市场可能会进一步推高收益率。报告估算，中期赤字每增加1个百分点，国债收益率可能上升30到40个基点。

ZH_A: 所以如果财政压力加大，英国国债的风险溢价可能短期内很难降下来？

ZH_B: 对，短期内风险溢价确实会比较顽固。但报告也指出，一些长期因素最终会消退。比如量化紧缩会逐步放缓，财政赤字在现有政策下也会慢慢收窄。

ZH_A: 那投资者现在应该怎么看英国国债？是抄底还是继续观望？

ZH_B: 报告的核心判断是，宏观基本面才是决定国债收益率的长期因素。如果英国经济数据最终支持英国央行在2026年暂停加息、2027年开始降息，那对英国国债会是持续的利好。但当前的不确定性仍然很高，所以完整报告里还有很多细节值得深挖。

ZH_A: 听起来，这份报告既解释了当前的高收益率是合理的，也指出了未来可能的变化方向。如果想了解更多关于英国央行政策路径和供给压力对收益率的量化分析，还是得去读完整报告。

EN_A: So UK gilts are in the spotlight again. The 30-year yield just crossed 5.8 percent, a level we haven't seen since 1998. What's really going on here?

EN_B: The short answer is that the market is pricing in a lot of uncertainty and inflation risk specific to the UK. The economy has been growing slowly but inflation has been the highest among major economies. That combination justifies a higher risk premium.

EN_A: So what question is this report really trying to answer? Is it about whether gilts are being unfairly punished?

EN_B: Exactly. The key question is whether these elevated yields and risk premiums are justified. And the report's conclusion is that most of it is justified, but there are also some short-term factors amplifying the moves.

EN_A: Let's start with the macro picture. What makes the UK special?

EN_B: The UK has this unusual mix of weak growth and high inflation. On top of that, survey measures show the UK has the highest macro uncertainty among developed economies. When growth is weak and inflation is high, bonds become less effective as a hedge for risky assets. So investors demand a higher premium.

EN_A: So you're saying gilts are losing their safe-haven appeal?

EN_B: Partially, yes. When bond returns become more correlated with equities, their diversification benefit drops. The UK's energy mix, which is heavily dependent on gas, and its wage-price indexation make inflation more persistent. That makes the market more cautious on UK assets.

EN_A: What about supply and demand? Are those factors at play too?

EN_B: Definitely. The UK has large fiscal deficits and the Bank of England is doing quantitative tightening, so bond supply is high. On the demand side, UK pension funds have structurally reduced their gilt holdings after the 2022 crisis.

EN_A: So more supply, fewer big buyers. Is that trend going to continue?

EN_B: The report says that while supply pressure is still there, some relative measures are actually improving. For example, gilt swap spreads and the shape of the yield curve have gotten better compared to other bond markets in recent quarters. So gilts aren't always the source of bad news.

EN_A: What's the biggest risk in the near term?

EN_B: Fiscal policy. If the UK government loosens fiscal discipline, say for higher defense spending, the market could push yields even higher. The report estimates that a 1 percentage point increase in the medium-term deficit could add 30 to 40 basis points to gilt yields.

EN_A: So if fiscal pressure builds, the risk premium might stay elevated for a while?

EN_B: Yes, it will be sticky in the near term. But the report also points out that some of these factors will eventually fade. Quantitative tightening will slow as the BoE's balance sheet shrinks, and fiscal deficits are expected to narrow under current policies.

EN_A: So what should investors do? Is this a buying opportunity or a time to stay away?
