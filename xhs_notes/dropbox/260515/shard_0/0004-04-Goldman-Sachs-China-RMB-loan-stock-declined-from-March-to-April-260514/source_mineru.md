# China: RMB loan stock declined from March to April

# Bottom line:

April credit data came in well below market expectations, mainly due to weaker-than-expected bank loans extension. Shadow banking credit also declined from March to April after seasonal adjustment. The decline in RMB loan stock in April was broad-based, except for a surge in bill financing. This is a short-term form of bank lending that usually surges as banks try to fill unused loan quota. Official media attributed the decline in loan extension partly to stronger corporate bond issuance, which has sUBStituted for bank lending amid lower financing costs. But weaker-than-expected TSF flows suggest that underlying credit demand remains soft, in our view. In light of today's weaker-than-expected credit growth, we revise down our 2026 full-year total social financing growth forecast to $8.0\%$ yoy (vs. $8.5\%$ yoy previously).

# Key numbers:

New RMB loans (flow, reported): RMB -10bn in April (RMB loans to the real economy: RMB -400bn) vs. Bloomberg consensus: RMB 300bn, GS forecast: RMB 300bn.

Outstanding RMB loan growth: 5.6% yoy in April (+4.5% mom sa ann, estimated by GS); March: 5.7% yoy (+4.5% mom sa ann).

Total social financing (TSF flow, reported): RMB 620bn in April, vs. Bloomberg consensus: RMB 1250bn, GS forecast: RMB 1300bn.

TSF stock growth: $7.8\%$ yoy in April, vs. $7.9\%$ in March. The implied month-on-month growth of TSF stock: $7.9\%$ in April (seasonally adjusted annualized rate), vs. $6.1\%$ in March.

M2: 8.6% yoy in April (10.7% mom sa ann estimated by GS) vs. Bloomberg consensus: 8.5% yoy, GS forecast: 8.5% yoy. March: 8.5% yoy (3.8% mom sa ann).

# Main points:

1. April total social financing (TSF) flows came in well below consensus expectations, mainly due to weaker-than-expected bank loans extension and a notable decline in outstanding shadow banking credit.
