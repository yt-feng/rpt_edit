# AI Transceiver Demand Is Accelerating Faster Than Most Models Anticipate, Creating a Structural Supply Challenge That Will Define the Next Investment Cycle

The most important signal from the past quarter in optical networking is not a shipment number or a product launch. It is a single statement from the CEO of Lumentum in April 2026: the company expects to be "sold out through 2028" within two quarters. This is not a cyclical upswing. It is a structural inflection in how hyperscalers build AI infrastructure, and the implications extend far beyond one company's order book.

For investors tracking the AI transceiver industry, the central question has shifted from "how fast will demand grow" to "how long will supply constraints persist and who benefits most." The numbers now being discussed are materially larger than what consensus models captured even six months ago. A global investment bank has raised its 2027 AI transceiver shipment forecast by 98 percent and its 2028 forecast by 87 percent, driven largely by a reassessment of 1.6T adoption. The industry total addressable market is projected to expand from USD 18 billion in 2025 to approximately USD 102 billion in 2028, representing a more than fourfold increase in three years.

This article unpacks what has changed, why the current demand wave is different from previous cycles, where the bottlenecks are forming, and what strategic questions remain unanswered for investors and industry participants alike.

## The Industry Leader's "Sold Out Through 2028" Statement Signals That Demand Is Outstripping Even Aggressive Capacity Plans

When the CEO of Lumentum states that hyperscaler demand is accelerating so rapidly that the company could fill its entire order book through 2028 within two quarters, it is worth pausing on what that means. This is not a forecast of demand. It is a statement that demand already exceeds what the company can produce for the next two and a half years, assuming no further acceleration.

The math behind this claim is striking. Lumentum expects approximately 85 percent compound annual growth in InP optical lane volume demand for EML, CW, and UHP lasers through 2030. Their internal capacity model shows a persistent and widening gap between lane volume demand and lane capacity, with demand reaching roughly 110 inches by 2030 against capacity of only 40 inches. This is not a temporary mismatch. It is a structural shortage that will define pricing power, technology adoption, and competitive dynamics for the remainder of the decade.

The implications are straightforward. Companies that control InP sUBStrate capacity, laser manufacturing, and advanced packaging will enjoy pricing leverage that most semiconductor cycles do not sustain. The shortage is not limited to Lumentum. Coherent has spoken about scaling InP capacity by more than 100 percent over the next two years. Sumitomo and Broadcom have also announced capacity expansions. The aggregate response from suppliers is significant, but the demand trajectory from hyperscalers is growing faster than any single supplier's expansion plan.

## The Upward Revision in 1.6T Forecasts Reflects a Structural Shift in Hyperscaler Architecture, Not Just a Volume Increase

The most dramatic forecast revisions are not in 800G, where growth is already well understood. They are in 1.6T, where the bank now expects 79 million units in 2027, up from a prior estimate of 24 million units, representing a 233 percent upward revision. For 2028, the expectation has moved from 27 million to 87 million units, a 226 percent increase.

This is not a marginal adjustment. It represents a fundamental reassessment of how quickly hyperscalers are moving to higher-speed interconnects. The reasoning is grounded in observable behavior. Multiple vendors reported large orders, product launches, and technology demonstrations in March and April that point to accelerating adoption of 1.6T. The technology is not just on roadmap. It is being deployed in volume sooner than previously modeled.

The strategic significance of this shift is that 1.6T carries higher average selling prices and better margins than 800G. For companies like Eoptolink, which demonstrated a next-generation 1.6T DR4 OSFP transceiver at OFC 2026 that reduces required fiber count by 50 percent compared to the previous generation, the revenue and margin trajectory is being pulled forward. The bank has raised its price target on Eoptolink to RMB 710, reflecting this improved visibility.

What this means for the broader ecosystem is that the transition to 1.6T is not a gradual upgrade cycle. It is a forced migration driven by the sheer scale of AI training and inference clusters. As hyperscalers build clusters that require tens of thousands of GPUs, the bandwidth requirements at the rack and cluster level exceed what 800G can economically deliver. 1.6T becomes a necessity, not a luxury.

## Component Shortages Are Reshaping Technology Choices, Accelerating the Shift Toward Silicon Photonics

One of the most consequential second-order effects of the supply crunch is the acceleration of silicon photonics adoption. According to LightCounting, silicon photonics' share of the optical transceiver market rose from 10 percent in 2018 to 33 percent in 2024 and is expected to become the dominant technology in 2026.

The conventional narrative around silicon photonics has focused on performance advantages: lower power consumption, higher integration, and better thermal characteristics. Those advantages are real, but the current acceleration is being driven by something more urgent. EML shortages are pushing end users to adopt alternative solutions. When you cannot get enough EML lasers to meet your deployment schedule, silicon photonics becomes the pragmatic choice, not just the technologically interesting one.

This dynamic creates a self-reinforcing cycle. As more volume shifts to silicon photonics, manufacturing yields improve, costs come down, and the technology becomes more competitive even after EML supply eventually catches up. The companies that are investing in silicon photonics capacity today are positioning themselves for a market structure where the technology may hold a permanent cost and supply advantage.

The open question is whether the silicon photonics ramp can happen fast enough to relieve the near-term bottleneck. The bank's analysis suggests that component shortages remain the key risk to rapid volume growth in 2026 through 2028. Fabrinet's management noted on their earnings call that datacom revenue would have been a new record by a wide margin if not for supply constraints, and that the shortages span lasers, memory, and certain ASICs. This is not a single-point failure. It is a multi-component constraint that will take years to fully resolve.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer Yet: The Timing and Magnitude of the CPO Transition and Its Impact on Pluggable Transceiver Demand

The report provides a framework for CPO adoption but leaves several critical questions unresolved. Under the base case, CPO switch volume could grow from 4,712 units in 2025 to 103,889 units in 2028. The bull and bear cases span a wide range, from 50,000 to 250,000 units by 2028. Views on CPO penetration by 2030 range from 10 percent to 50 percent.

This wide dispersion reflects genuine uncertainty about how quickly CPO will displace pluggable transceivers. The report notes that Corning's recent investment from NVIDIA gives the company ample access to fiber and optics for CPO, but the bulk of the impact starts in 2028. For the next two years, pluggable transceivers remain the dominant architecture.

The strategic question for investors is whether the CPO transition represents a disruptive threat to incumbent transceiver vendors or a complementary growth opportunity. If CPO adoption happens at the high end of estimates, it could compress the total addressable market for high-speed pluggable transceivers in the 2028-2030 timeframe. If adoption is slower, the pluggable market could continue expanding well beyond 2030.

The report keeps its CPO estimates unchanged from February, suggesting that the recent demand acceleration has not yet changed the analysts' view on CPO timing. But the uncertainty around this question is precisely why investors need to track the data points as they emerge. The next 12 to 18 months of hyperscaler architecture decisions will be decisive.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: Three Questions to Assess Positioning in the AI Transceiver Cycle

For investors trying to translate the report's analysis into actionable decisions, three questions provide a useful framework.

First, how exposed is your investment to the supply-constrained portion of the value chain? Companies that control InP sUBStrate capacity, laser manufacturing, or advanced packaging are likely to enjoy pricing power and margin expansion even as volumes grow. Companies that assemble transceivers but depend on external component supply may see revenue growth but margin compression if component costs rise faster than ASPs.

Second, how does your investment benefit from the 1.6T acceleration? The 233 percent upward revision in 2027 1.6T shipments suggests that companies with strong 1.6T product positions will see revenue and earnings pulled forward. The key is to distinguish between companies that have demonstrated production capability versus those that are still in development.

Third, what is your view on CPO timing and its impact on your investment's terminal value? If you believe CPO adoption will be rapid, you may want to favor companies with CPO exposure or technology that remains relevant in a CPO architecture. If you believe pluggable transceivers will remain dominant through 2030, the current cycle has more room to run.

These questions do not have easy answers, which is precisely why the full report and its underlying data are valuable. The bank's analysis provides detailed shipment forecasts, technology roadmaps, and company-specific price targets that can help investors refine their views.
