# Signs of recovery – time to revisit?

# Companies seeing signs of recovery in recent data

We held a series of calls recently with two-wheeler companies. The following are our key takeaways. 1) Electric two-wheeler (e2W) volumes in China stabilised in Q226 with volumes flat after a 20% decline in Q126, pointing to a demand recovery. 2) OEMs are raising prices by 3-5% in April-May to pass on higher input costs, thus gross margins should hold up in Q226 or even exceed Q126 levels; there is no large-scale price war. 3) High oil and gas prices have driven strong export sales; sales of Ninebot's e-scooters in Europe and Yadea's e2Ws in ASEAN beat our estimates. 4) The industry continues to innovate, including Yadea's adoption of sodium batteries in two-wheelers. Overall, we like Ninebot (689009 CH) and Yadea (1585HK) most, both Buy rated, given their improved fundamentals and limited investor holdings. We also like CFMoto (603129.SH) and Loncin (603766.SH), both Buy rated, although there are some near-term headwinds, such as tariffs, FX and a decline in OEM demand.

# Ninebot: confidence in e2W business growth

Ninebot reiterated its confidence in achieving e2W volume sales of 5.5m in 2026, implying 30% YoY growth. It has mainly marketed e-motorcycles so far this year, but will add more e-bike models from mid-May. The company foresees a record-high peak season in Q326. In terms of pricing and margin, the company flagged a lower margin for e2W business (not the whole group) for full-year 2026 versus 2025. Ninebot believes margins bottomed in Q1, stabilised in Q2 and are likely to be higher in H226. Due to cost inflation, the company raised prices by up to Rmb100 in late April. For more details, see page 2.

Yadea reiterated 2026 guidance: volume growth of 4%, ASP up 5-10% and gross margins flat. # Yadea (1585.HK, Buy)

Price hike: Due to higher costs since March, Yadea has raised prices for its three-wheeler products by Rmb100-200, or 3-5%. In late April, the company raised prices on two-wheelers by Rmb50-100, or 3-5%.

# Ninebot (689009.SH, Buy)

Ninebot held a two-wheeler business conference call with investors on 13 May.

Moat around e2Ws business : Ninebot has set the e2W industry benchmark for other brands. While competitors may imitate its design and smart features, Ninebot believes its user experience remains unique. First, by leveraging intelligent electric vehicle integration, Ninebot anticipates a significant leap in product competitiveness in 2027, which should keep it ahead of the industry. Meanwhile, with comprehensive upgrades to after-sales service and a balanced distribution network by the end of 2026, Ninebot's core strengths create sUBStantial barriers for competitors.

Industry demand: China e2W industry demand is showing clear signs of recovery from 26Q2. Ninebot believes industry demand will decline in H126 due to past demand becoming overextended, but expects demand to be flat or even grow slightly in H226.

Ninebot sales volume trend: Ninebot reiterated its e2W sales volume guidance of 5.5m in 2026F, expressing confidence in the target. For 2026 YTD, e-motorcycles have risen to account for the majority of its e2W sales. But from the end of May, with its full covered e-bikes launched in the market, Ninebot believes e-bikes sales will likely rise. Ninebot is confident that e-bikes can achieve good sales volume in Q326.

Overseas e2W: Ninebot conducted a thorough assessment of the Vietnam market in March 2026. Ninebot believes the Vietnam market is turning rapidly to e2W from ICE 2W and it can replicate its success in China's e2W market there. Ninebot also mentioned purchasing a motorcycle company in China, extending the company's edge into motorcycle design and manufacturing.

ASP and gross margin: The company raised prices of e2Ws at the end of April, but it flagged cost inflation in motors, tires and lamps. Product mix: Ninebot is launching more products in the Rmb3,500-4,000 segment, thus competing with incumbent brands to gain further market share. Ninebot said it will not move further into the Rmb3,000-3,500 segment without a cost edge.

# Valuation Method and Risk Statement

Ninebot: We value the company using a PE multiple. Upside risks include: 1) increasing demand for high-end e-bikes; 2) right-of-way relaxations in more Cities, driving e-motorcycle demand; 3) expansion of new businesses such as robot mowers and off-road vehicles exceeding expectations.

CFMoto Power: We value the company using a DCF methodology. Downside risks include: 1) less-than-expected loosening of motorcycle bans/restrictions policies in Chinese Cities, leading to lower-than-expected adoption of large-displacement motorcycles and slow demand growth; and 2) pressure from rising raw material costs. For the company, downside risks include: 1) slower-than-expected launch and market acceptance of its large-displacement motorcycle products in China, affecting its motorcycle sales volume and ASP; 2) failure to obtain export tariff exemptions for its four-wheeler products, impacting its export volume and margins; and 3) frequent product safety incidents that dampen sales volume. We value Loncin Motor using PE. Downside risks for Loncin include: 1) its large-displacement motorcycle products falling short of expectations in launch pace and market recognition terms, impacting the company's motorcycle sales volume and ASP; and 2) frequent occurrence of safety incidents for its products, denting sales volume. Upside risks include: 1) Loncin's large-displacement motorcycle products proving more popular than expected; and 2) expansion of its four wheeler offerings overseas beating expectations.
