# IBM FQ2'26: Nothing broken; Market-perform TP \$230

IBM reported FQ2 2026 earnings yesterday after the market close.

IBM reported Q2 results that fell short of prior expectations following a rare pre-announcement, with total revenue of \$17.2B growing just 1% yoy (vs. cons at \$17.5bn) and EPS of \$2.93, up 5% yoy. In line with the company's pre-announcement the weakness was related to slippage of some deals (mainly in mainframes and transaction processing) due to capex budget constraints in the current rising price environment driven by memory shortages.

FY26 revenue guidance reduced slightly but most slipped deals should come through in rest of this year. FY26 revenue growth revised down to 4-5% (from 5%+) as mainframe and associated software weakness is somewhat offset by strength in Distributed Infra (Power servers and Storage) with the low end reflecting current environment as the base case. FY26 software growth revised down to 6% - 8% (from 10%+). FY26 infrastructure growth revised up to +LSD% (from -LSD%), driven by growth in distributed infrastructure and expectation that most of the slipped deals in mainframes in FQ2 get realized in 2H'26. FY26 consulting revenue growth expectation same at low-to-mid-single digit%. IBM continues to expect FCF +\~\$1B YoY and 100bps of operating pretax margin expansion.

## DETAILS

IBM reported FQ2 2026 earnings yesterday after the market close.

IBM reported Q2 results that fell short of prior expectations following a rare pre-announcement, with total revenue of \$17.2B growing just 1% yoy (vs. cons at \$17.5bn) and EPS of \$2.93, up 5% yoy. In line with the company's pre-announcement the weakness was related to slippage of some deals (mainly in mainframes and transaction processing) due to budget constraints in the current rising price environment driven by memory shortages.

Software: \$7.8B, +5% YoY. The shortfall was concentrated in Transaction Processing (-8% / -9% constant currency), as a number of large deals slipped late in June when clients redirected spending toward servers, storage, and memory ahead of expected price increases. Data grew +19% / +18% cc, boosted by Confluent's first full quarter post-close. Hybrid Cloud (Red Hat) grew +11%, accelerating a point sequentially on stronger subscription mix and stable consumption growth, with OpenShift ARR now at \$2.2B. Automation grew +4% / +3% cc, remaining largely insulated given its subscription-based mix. About 80% of software revenue is recurring (Red Hat, HashiCorp, Confluent) and grew \~8%, while the remaining 20% transactional revenue was down high-single-digits.

Infrastructure: \$3.8B, -7% YoY. IBM Z fell -42% while Distributed Infrastructure (Power and Storage) was the standout at +37%, its best quarterly growth on record, as enterprises reprioritized amid anticipated memory price increases.

Consulting: \$5.3B, flat / +1% cc YoY. Signings grew +6% for a second straight quarter, with generative AI representing about 50% of signings and now over 30% of backlog, as clients moved from AI pilots toward enterprise-wide deployment.

Margins, EPS, and FCF. IBM's operating gross margin was down 70bps yoy at 59.4%, while operating pre-tax income margin expanded 30 bps yoy to 19.2%. IBM reported EPS of \$2.93, up 5% yoy, vs consensus of \$2.97. FCF arrived at \$2.5bn, lower than consensus of \$2.9bn.

## LOWERED GUIDANCE AND OUR THOUGHTS

FY26 revenue guidance reduced slightly but most slipped deals should come through in rest of this year. FY26 revenue growth revised down to 4-5% (from 5%+) as mainframe and associated software weakness is somewhat offset by strength in Distributed Infra (Power servers and Storage) with the low end reflecting current environment as the base case. FY26 software growth revised down to 6% - 8% (from 10%+). FY26 infrastructure growth revised up to +LSD% (from -LSD%), driven by growth in distributed infrastructure and expectation that most of the slipped deals in mainframes in FQ2 get realized in 2H'26. FY26 consulting revenue growth expectation same at low-to-mid-single digit%. IBM continues to expect FCF +\~\$1B YoY and 100bps of operating pretax margin expansion.

Infrastructure meaningfully underperformed (-7% YoY) as guided in the prelim. Within the segment, IBM Z revenue fell 42%, while Distributed Infrastructure (Power and Storage) grew 37%. On the mainframe business, management emphasized that there is no evidence of customers migrating away from the platform and IBM Z continues to underpin more than 70% of the world's transaction value. Management attributed the bifurcation within Infrastructure to a shift in customer spending late in Q2, with clients prioritizing servers, storage, and memory purchases to secure supply-constrained infrastructure ahead of anticipated price increases. During our follow-up call, we asked what drove the upward revision in FY26 Infrastructure growth expectations from low-single-digits negative to low-single-digits positive despite recent mainframe weakness. Management indicated that: (1) mainframe assumptions remain unchanged in this upward revision; (2) demand for Power and Storage has strengthened materially; and (3) the record \$500 million backlog is expected to be largely converted to revenue in Q3.

Management noted that many customers purchase mainframe hardware and the associated software stack through Enterprise License Agreements (ELAs), which typically include a significant concentration of Transaction Processing software. As a result, softer mainframe purchasing activity has had a corresponding impact on this software category. In contrast, subscription- and consumption-based software offerings are generally treated as operating expenses rather than capital expenditures and have therefore remained largely insulated from the recent CapEx spending dynamics. It was also highlighted that approximately 80% of annual software revenue is recurring, driven by subscription and consumption-based products such as Red Hat, HashiCorp, and Confluent.

Reported Data growth of 19% /18% constant currency, was materially supported by the inclusion of Confluent, and organic Data revenue was likely flattish. Confluent generated approximately \$282 million of revenue in Q2 2025; applying a 21% growth rate—reasonable given Confluent's historical growth trajectory and IBM claiming that the business was off to a strong start—implies roughly \$342 million of Q2 2026 revenue. Against estimated total Data revenue of approximately \$1.8 billion, this suggests ex-Confluent Data revenue of roughly \$1.458 billion, or a decline of approximately 3% yoy assuming FQ2'25 Data revenue was roughly \$1.5bn. Management attributed the weakness to the delayed mainframe enterprise license agreements, noting that while roughly 60% of the affected software was Transaction Processing, a significant portion also included Data and Automation software. This indicates that a meaningful portion of IBM's Data and Automation software portfolio remains tied to mainframe, and can be affected by mainframe weakness.

Consulting revenue came in at \$5.3B, flat /1% constant currency, with generative AI now representing about 50% of signings and over 30% of backlog as clients shift from AI pilots toward enterprise-wide deployment. This aligns with our broader industry thesis that enterprises are moving from AI experimentation to large-scale adoption as more organizations recognize the productivity gains and ROI potential that AI can deliver. However, our CIO survey tells a more cautious story on the demand for third-party consultants: a net 25% of CIOs now expect to use consultants to deploy AI, down from a net 35% in our prior survey, suggesting waning appetite for external support even as internal AI adoption accelerates. More notably, a net negative -15% of CIOs expect their consulting spend to increase over the remainder of 2026, a signal that there could be headwinds down the road.

## WE REDUCE ESTIMATES AND TP TO \$230 ON 17X NEW FY27 EPS

We tweak estimates down, reflecting recent results and latest guidance. We don't see anything broken here, rather just some push outs due to severe capex budgetary pressure at customers. Our new FY26 and FY27 EPS is \$12.23 and \$13.40 respectively. We also lower our TP to \$230 on 17x (inline with 5 year average and vs. 20x before) our new FY27 EPS.
