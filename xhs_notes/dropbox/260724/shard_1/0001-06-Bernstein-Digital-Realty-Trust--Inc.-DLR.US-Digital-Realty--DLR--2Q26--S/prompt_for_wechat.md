## WHAT WE LEARNED

Digital Realty's 2Q26 results were broadly consistent with our long-term view of DLR as a global, full-spectrum data center platform that combines a hyperscaler “floor” with an accelerating colo and connectivity engine, while leveraging strategic private capital to scale capacity without over-levering the balance sheet. Core FFO per share excluding net promote reached \$2.13 in 2Q26, up 14% YoY. On the top line, DLR posted double-digit growth across virtually every major operating and financial metric, including same-capital cash NOI, which increased 8.9% YoY (7.2% on a constant-currency basis), while maintaining net debt-to-Adjusted EBITDA at 4.7x despite nearly \$6 billion of net new investment activity in the quarter. The quarter also benefited from a \$0.07 per share net gain from business interruption insurance proceeds related to lost rent from a prior incident in Singapore, providing a modest tailwind to earnings; while this benefit had been contemplated in the full-year outlook, the timing was uncertain, and 2Q now reflects the full value of the settlement. We also note that with the (1) net promote, (2) insurance benefit, and (3) dilution from the 12M-share Blackstone equity issuance, the 2Q results were much closer to consensus expectations—specifically, +1% beat on AFFOPS and Core AFFOPS (Exhibit 1, Exhibit 2), and -3% vs consensus for adj. diluted EPS (Exhibit 3).

On development, DLR accelerated an increasingly partner-capital-supported pipeline: the company invested \$1.1B in development capex in the quarter (net of partner share), commenced 312MW of new development, delivered 76MW of IT capacity (60% pre-leased), and expanded its under-construction pipeline to 1.4GW at a total cost of \$20B—an 100% increase from 1H26. Pro forma for the July hyperscale leases, the development pipeline is now 63% pre-leased with average expected stabilized yields of 11.5%, and more than 80% of the pipeline is located in the Americas, with significant activity in NoVA, Charlotte, Atlanta, Sao Paulo and planned construction in Kansas City starting in 2H26. Coupled with \~\$635M of annualized rent scheduled to commence in 2H26 (45% in 3Q and 55% in 4Q), \$480M slated for 2027, and \$312M for 2028 and beyond, the expanded pipeline and backlog provide a long runway for double-digit Core FFO per share growth, which management now expects to extend into 2027 and beyond.

Strategically, DLR continued to lean into its three core growth pillars of colo and connectivity, hyperscale, and strategic private capital. In hyperscale, DLR agreed to acquire Blackstone's 64% blended equity interest in three fully leased Northern Virginia hyperscale data centers (288MW) for \$1.2B in cash plus 12.3M DLR shares (valued at \~\$2.3B), assuming its partner share of a \$725M loan and remaining capex, and expects these assets to be accretive to Core FFO per share in both 2027 and 2028 while still raising 2026 guidance by 1.5% despite closing pre-stabilization. In private capital, the pending acquisition of Columbia Capital (for \~\$485M) and a 16% interest in Teraco (for roughly \$650M in DLR stock), together with the \$3.25B US hyperscale fund and over \$12B of remaining capacity to support hyperscale development, meaningfully scale DLR's fee-based earnings and fund commitments across fiber, mobility and enterprise technology, reinforcing the platform's ability to grow hyperscale capacity without materially increasing corporate leverage.

We maintain \~27x NTM AFFO, as the 2Q26 print adds conviction around three key elements of our thesis: renewed strength and consistency in 0-1MW plus interconnection bookings (including AI-related deployments and record channel contributions), structural pricing power evidenced by 25%+ cash renewal spreads and accelerating same-capital cash NOI growth, and a de-risked, partner-funded hyperscale buildout that extends DLR's double-digit Core FFO per share growth runway into 2027 and beyond. From here, we are focused on the trajectory of 0-1MW AI and enterprise bookings, utilization and margin trends as large campuses across Northern Virginia, Charlotte, Atlanta, Kansas City, Sao Paulo and key EMEA/APAC hubs ramp, and the pattern of Core FFO guidance revisions.

## THE LONG VIEW

DLR is largely understood to be a hyperscaler landlord. We model HSD hyperscaler revenue growth through 2030 (in line with consensus), providing a nice floor for the stock. What gets less attention is DLR's enterprise trajectory. DLR's interconnection density and global footprint (300+ data centers across 50+ metros on 6 continents) should enable outsized share gains in a growing market. We model 15-20% CAGR for DLR's <1MW segment..

In terms of future builds, we believe DLR's development pipeline has been de-risked with its capital structure strategy. In addition to various JVs, DLR has its U.S. Hyerscale Data Center Fund (inaugural closed-end fund, \$3.2B+ of LP equity commitments). With limited speculative builds and partners to offset investment while DLR retains operating control and collects fees, development risk is asymmetric.

For catalysts, we are watching DLR's enterprise and bookings mix, particularly for continued acceleration in the <1MW segment. We will also maintain a close eye on utilization and margins by geography, JV activity, and core FFO guidance revisions, in addition to the risk of future equity issuance.
