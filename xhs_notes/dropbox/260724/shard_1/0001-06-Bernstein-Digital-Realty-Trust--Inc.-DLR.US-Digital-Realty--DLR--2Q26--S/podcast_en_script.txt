EN_A: So strong results on the surface, but not much to show on a per-share basis? That sounds frustrating.
EN_B: Exactly. They had a 188 million dollar net promote from that acquisition that softened the blow, about 50 cents per share impact. But that is not repeatable every quarter. It does serve as a positive proof point for their longer term strategy though.
EN_A: So what is the real question this report is trying to answer?
EN_B: It is very straightforward: How does Digital Realty accelerate its growth pipeline without destroying per-share value? They have three growth engines—hyperscale, enterprise colo, and private capital—but each comes with a different cost.
EN_A: Let us start with hyperscale. It sounds like that segment looked weak.
EN_B: It does look weak in isolation, but it is mostly a timing issue. The first quarter had an outsized lease in Charlotte, their largest ever. And then two big signings happened in July, which will show up in the third quarter. So it is not a trend problem, it is a timing problem.
EN_A: The pricing power numbers look pretty crazy though.
EN_B: Yes, renewal spreads exceeded 66 percent on a cash basis and 92 percent on a GAAP basis. That is massive, but the report calls it an anomaly. You cannot extrapolate that forward. Still, the backlog jumped to 1.4 billion dollars, so demand is clearly there.
EN_A: What about the enterprise colo business? The report seems really excited about it.
EN_B: That is the favorite part of the story. The zero to one megawatt segment had another record quarter, hitting 108 million dollars. That is the first time they have ever crossed the 100 million mark. Interconnection contributed about 19 percent of that. Pricing is strong across geographies and churn is low.
EN_A: So what does that mean for the investment case?
EN_B: Hyperscale is big but low margin and highly competitive. Enterprise colo and interconnection are higher value, more stable revenue streams. The report argues Digital Realty is underpenetrated in this segment and has significant room to gain market share.
EN_A: And the development pipeline? I see they have almost 1.4 gigawatts under construction.
EN_B: Yes, and 54 percent is already pre-leased. If you count the July signings, that number is closer to 64 percent. But there is one notable detail: the Atlanta facility is not pre-leased yet. The report says it is not speculative given the demand environment, but that is something to keep an eye on.
EN_A: So what is the biggest concern coming out of this quarter?
EN_B: Dilution. The share count increased about 6 percent from the fourth quarter and about 8 percent year over year. At the same time, development capex guidance was raised meaningfully, by 750 million dollars for 2026. So the tension is clear: balancing an accelerating growth pipeline against ongoing per-share dilution.
EN_A: Is there anything that can offset that dilution?
EN_B: Yes, there is about 12 billion dollars in off-balance-sheet dry powder, and room to raise about 0.8 turns of debt. But the report does not fully answer one question: Will the pace of dilution accelerate in coming quarters? If capex keeps going up, the pressure to issue more equity could increase.
EN_A: So what is the bottom line from this report?
EN_B: They maintain an Outperform rating with a 226 dollar price target. They believe Digital Realty is one of the most credible hyperscale data center builders in the world, and the enterprise colo business has a long runway for growth. They also like the capital recycling strategy and the off-balance-sheet development model.

If management keeps raising guidance, the dilution story becomes much less scary. But if guidance comes down, the market might lose patience with the dilution.
