ZH_A: 最近油价波动很大，听说有份报告提到了新的上行风险。这到底是怎么回事？

ZH_B: 这正是报告最值得聊的地方。他们维持2026年第四季度布伦特原油80美元的预测，前提是冲突在第四季度前缓解。但风险明显偏向上行。

ZH_A: 400万桶无法改道？那意味着什么？

ZH_B: 这意味着如果这三个关键点同时受阻，供应中断的风险会急剧放大。报告里还提到了哈萨克斯坦的出口也因俄乌局势升级而下降，这又加了一重风险。

ZH_A: 那既然风险这么大，报告为什么还坚持80美元的预测呢？

ZH_B: 因为下行因素也很实在。比如6月中东产量超预期，中国、韩国和部分中东国家的需求又弱于预期。报告认为这些下行压力会抵消部分风险。

ZH_B: 报告认为，夏季油价会保持强势。他们给出了三个理由：一是7月中东产量可能下降；二是夏季出行旺季会提振需求；三是韩国和日本在补充战略储备，不再像之前那样大量释放了。

ZH_B: 报告预计2027年市场会转为过剩，前提是霍尔木兹海峡保持开放。他们预测的过剩量高达每天320万桶。但有意思的是，他们认为油价不会因此暴跌。

EN_A: So oil prices have been all over the place. I heard there is a new report flagging some serious upside risks. What is the big takeaway?

EN_B: The main takeaway is that the Red Sea tensions are creating new upside risks for oil prices. But here is the twist: the report is not actually changing its base case forecast.

EN_A: Wait, they see risks but they are not adjusting the forecast? That sounds a bit contradictory.

EN_B: That is exactly the interesting part. They are keeping their Brent forecast at 80 dollars for the fourth quarter of 2026. But that is based on an assumption that the conflict de-escalates by then. The risks are clearly tilted to the upside.

EN_A: So where exactly are these risks coming from? Is it just the Red Sea shipping lane?

EN_B: The report highlights that nearly 9 million barrels a day of oil have been moving through the Bab-el-Mandeb strait recently. Of that, about 4 million barrels a day would be very difficult to reroute if the Strait of Hormuz, Bab-el-Mandeb, and the Suez Canal all faced disruptions at the same time.

EN_A: 4 million barrels that cannot be rerouted? That sounds huge. What does that actually mean?

EN_B: It means the risk of a major supply disruption spikes if those three chokepoints are hit simultaneously. The report also mentions that Kazakhstan exports have dropped due to the Russia-Ukraine escalation, adding another layer of risk.

EN_A: So with all these risks, why are they sticking with the 80 dollar forecast?

EN_B: Because the bearish factors are real too. For example, Middle East production was higher than expected in June. And demand from China, South Korea, and parts of the Middle East has been weaker than anticipated. The report sees these downside pressures balancing out some of the risks.

EN_A: So it is a tug-of-war between bulls and bears. What about the near term? Where do they see prices heading this summer?

EN_B: They expect prices to stay strong through July and August. They give three reasons. First, Middle East production is likely to fall in July. Second, summer travel season will boost demand. And third, countries like South Korea and Japan are now refilling their strategic reserves instead of releasing them.

EN_A: Sounds like summer is well-supported. What about 2027?

EN_B: The report sees the market moving into a surplus in 2027, assuming Hormuz stays open. They project a surplus of 3.2 million barrels a day. But interestingly, they do not think prices will crash.

EN_A: Why not? That is a huge surplus.

EN_B: Because there are two floors. One is global strategic stockpiling, which they estimate at 1.2 million barrels a day in 2027. The other is US shale, which is very price sensitive. If oil stays below 60 dollars for too long, shale production will slow down significantly.

EN_A: You keep saying assuming Hormuz stays open. What if it does not?

EN_B: That is where the report gets really interesting. In their upside scenario, if Hormuz remains disrupted through 2027, Brent could exceed 120 dollars a barrel in late 2026 and average 100 dollars in 2027.
