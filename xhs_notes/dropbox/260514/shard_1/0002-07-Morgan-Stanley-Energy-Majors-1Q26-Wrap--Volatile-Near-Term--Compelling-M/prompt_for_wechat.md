# Stronger-than-expected 1Q, but the key debate shifts to durability and timing

The European majors entered 1Q26 with expectations already elevated. Consensus earnings had repriced sharply higher into results as commodity prices strengthened, while Energy equities materially outperformed the broader market.

Against that backdrop, the sector still delivered earnings beats.

Contrary to intuition, however, the dominant driver was not upstream prices - instead, most earnings beats came from downstream and trading. That distinction is relevant for how we think about 2Q26 and beyond.

The 1Q26 earnings season signals three things in our view:

- Integrated models continue to monetise disruption through trading and optimisation   
- The full upstream pricing benefit from the stronger commodity environment has not yet flowed through to earnings   
- Management teams remain cautious on distributions despite stronger cash generation, preferring to preserve balance-sheet flexibility

The implication is that 1Q26 likely marks the start of a broader earnings and distributions uplift rather than the peak.

# Earnings beats on top of elevated expectations – driven by downstream and trading

Across the peer group, results generally exceeded expectations despite a significantly higher pre-results bar. The dominant drivers for the earnings beats were downstream and trading.

Downstream, refineries capitalised on the loss of Middle Eastern supply as well as on regional crude and product dislocations. Assets ran exceptionally hard over the quarter, and margins sustained strong levels despite weaker months in January and February – which suggests a strong exit rate out of March into 2Q.

Shell, BP and Eni were the clearest examples of this dynamic.

- BP reported adjusted earnings 20% ahead of consensus, with the beat overwhelmingly driven by Customers & Products, where operating profit exceeded expectations by \~30% due to refining, midstream and exceptional oil trading performance. Its upstream and LNG divisions missed expectations by \~8%.   
- Shell similarly beat consensus by 8% through a combination of strong trading, refining and marketing prints, with Chemicals & Products and Marketing beating expectations by a combined 35%, while Integrated Gas missed by more than 10%.   
- Eni effectively demonstrated the opposite dynamic, reinforcing where the earnings upside actually came from in 1Q. While upstream performed well – production grew 9% Y/Y – the company missed expectations because outages in two major refineries (Ruwais and Sannazzaro) prevented it from capturing the March refining margin rally, combined with weaker LNG/Power results.

# Production and pricing impacts shift into 2Q – with pricing still dominating the net effect

At first glance, output in 1Q26 appeared surprisingly resilient. Oil and gas production across the majors was broadly flat quarter-on-quarter despite material portfolio exposure to the Middle East Gulf. This resilience reflected two offsetting mechanisms:

• disruption effects were felt for only one-third of the quarter   
- organic portfolio growth across the group offset part of the disruption

The picture should change in 2Q.

On aggregate, we estimate the majors to have $\sim 10\%$ aggregate production exposure to Middle East shut-ins in 2Q; with low- to mid-single-digit underlying annual organic growth, the net effect is likely to be a $\sim 5\%$ production decline compared to the same quarter of last year.

Indeed, companies are now broadly guiding for lower production quarter-on-quarter as disruption impacts become more fully reflected, and maintenance activity increases.

- TotalEnergies indicated that current shut-ins in Qatar, Iraq and UAE offshore represent around 15% of total production exposure.   
- BP guided to lower reported upstream production in 2026 due to Gulf of America maintenance and Middle East disruption effects.   
- Equinor is expected to see lower production in 2Q despite effectively no Middle East exposure, primarily due to turnaround activity

However, we maintain that the more important variable here remains pricing. The commodity backdrop strengthened materially through the quarter, but the realised benefit in 1Q earnings was still relatively muted.

This reflects contractual pricing lags, weaker January-February pricing, and potentially realised discounts versus spot benchmarks.

As a result, realised pricing across the group lagged the move in spot commodities by roughly \$5/bbl across the peer group. The stronger commodity environment should therefore become much more visible in 2Q realised earnings.

At the commodity level, our estimates imply roughly $\sim 40\%$ quarter-on-quarter improvement in realised upstream pricing assumptions entering 2Q26 versus 1Q26.

The key uncertainty is whether realised prices continue to trade at wider-than-normal discounts to benchmarks due to timing lags, regional pricing distortions or transportation/logistics costs, for instance.

Even so, the broader conclusion remains unchanged: production losses are localised and operationally visible. Pricing benefits are global and apply across entire portfolios. On balance, this asymmetry remains structurally positive for sector earnings.

# Conservative distributions despite materially improved cash generation

The strongest message out of this earnings season was, in our view, a remarkably conservative stance on capital allocation.

The majors' existing capital frameworks were set ahead of a significantly weaker commodity outlook, with the sector preparing for a period of large oil and gas surpluses: capex budgets were tightened, distributions were rebased, and divestment and cost savings programmes became the norm.

Now, despite a higher earnings run-rate and a stronger commodity outlook, companies did not alter frameworks beyond what already existed.

Where flexibility was already embedded in frameworks, they moved toward the higher end of existing ranges:

- TotalEnergies maintained buybacks at the top-end of the range (\$1.5bn/qtr) while reiterating payout discipline   
- Eni raised 2026 buybacks guidance to €2.8bn, effectively increasing its planning scenario to \$80/bbl oil prices and €50/MWh TTF.

But, importantly, neither company moved beyond their pre-existing distribution frameworks.

Elsewhere, distributions were unchanged – or even lowered:

- BP reiterated its buyback pause and prioritised accelerated de-leveraging   
- Equinor maintained its buyback and dividend unchanged, flagging that changes ought to be made on "money already earned" as opposed to stronger forecasts   
- Shell notably increased its dividend by a surprise +5% Q/Q, but reduced its buyback to \$3.0bn/qtr, effectively trimming annual distributions paid by \~\$1.5bn

The message from management teams was very consistent: companies remain cautious about over-distributing into a highly volatile commodity environment.

In practice, the sector is taking a deliberate wait-and-see approach, and management teams appear comfortable allowing additional cash to accumulate on balance sheets in the near term.

However, increases in distributions are a question of "when", not "if", in our view. In a higher-for-longer commodity price environment, current dividend and buyback run-rates might be simply insufficient to meet existing policy floors at \~35-40% of CFFO returned to shareholders.

In our commodity strip (Exhibit 6), we expect CFFO payout policies to be cleared at around \$4bn for Shell, \$2bn for TotalEnergies and €775mn for Eni (without accounting for a potential special dividend). This would mark a \~30% increase from current run-rates, likely to come before the end of the year.

While Equinor and BP don't have such explicit mechanisms, an increase in returns in the peer group would likely cause them to follow, as they sit on an improved balance sheet and struggle to remain competitive on distributions.

In summary, despite the conservative message out of 1Q26, our expectation for a step-up in buybacks remains unchanged. As the situation remains volatile, management teams have chosen to build balance sheet dry powder – at some point, however, this is likely to translate into higher distributions capacity.
