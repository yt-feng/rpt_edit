EN_A: Restocking for what? For future exports or for domestic demand?
EN_B: That is the big question. The report lays out two possibilities. One is that manufacturers are stockpiling raw materials to meet future export orders, maybe from global demand for AI data centers or EVs. In that case, exports will catch up and the trade surplus will widen again. The other is that domestic demand is picking up, driven by investment in AI infrastructure or a recovering property market. Then imports will keep outpacing exports, and the surplus will keep shrinking.
EN_A: So which one does the report think is happening?
EN_B: It thinks both are happening at the same time. On the external side, semiconductor exports from neighboring economies are strong, and China's own export order PMI is rising. On the domestic side, fixed-asset investment has rebounded, manufacturing profits are recovering, and the property market is showing signs of stabilization.
EN_A: And either way, the yuan strengthens?
EN_B: Exactly. If external demand is the main driver, the narrowing trade surplus is temporary. Once the export orders are fulfilled, the surplus will widen again, and exporters will repatriate more dollars, pushing the yuan up. If domestic demand is the main driver, then growth and inflation will pick up, forcing interest rates higher. That tightens liquidity and also pushes the yuan up. Two paths, same destination.
