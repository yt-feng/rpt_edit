# The Rolling Recovery Is Not a Replay; It Is a Regime Shift That Rewards Cyclical Exposure and Punishes Defensive Posturing

The dominant market narrative for much of 2024 and early 2025 was one of narrow leadership, macro anxiety, and a defensive crouch. Investors who held their ground through that period were rewarded with a V-bottom reversal that caught many off guard. But the mistake now would be to treat the current rally as a mean-reversion bounce within an otherwise fragile cycle. The evidence from the latest mid-year outlook, built on three structural themes that have been in place since last year, points to something more durable: a rolling recovery that is broadening, accelerating, and shifting the center of gravity away from the megacap names that defined the post-COVID bull market.

This is not a market that is simply going higher because liquidity is loose or because the Fed is dovish. This is a market that is being driven by a powerful earnings cycle, one that is now spreading beyond the narrowest part of the index. The S&P 500 price target of 8,300 over the next twelve months, based on 20.5 times forward earnings of 404 dollars, implies a 12 percent upside from current levels. But the real story is not the index level; it is the composition of the earnings growth that will get us there. The median stock in the Russell 3000 is now posting the strongest earnings growth in four years. The breadth of earnings revisions has turned decisively positive after hitting a trough in April 2025. And the leading earnings model is forecasting double-digit growth that extends into 2027.

The strategic implication is clear: the rolling recovery thesis is not a forecast; it is already happening. Investors who position for continued cyclical leadership, who rotate away from the defensive sectors that have been a drag on relative performance, and who look beyond the Mag 7 for earnings growth will be the ones who capture the next leg of this cycle. Those who remain anchored to the narrow, defensive, or AI-hype-driven trades of the past two years risk being left behind as the regime shifts.

## The Earnings Cycle Is Broader and Stronger Than the Market Appreciates, and It Is Being Driven by the S&P 493, Not the Mag 7

For the past two years, the narrative around US equities has been dominated by the Mag 7. Their earnings growth was extraordinary, their margins were expanding, and their valuation multiples seemed to defy gravity. But a careful reading of the data shows that the earnings cycle is now turning in favor of the rest of the market. In 2024, the Mag 7 delivered net income growth of 37 percent, while the S&P 493 managed only 3 percent. That gap was the defining feature of the market. But the projections for 2026 show a dramatic convergence: the Mag 7 are expected to grow net income by 33 percent, while the S&P 493 are expected to grow by 14 percent. In 2027, the two groups are projected to grow at nearly identical rates: 15 percent for the Mag 7 and 14 percent for the S&P 493.

This convergence matters for two reasons. First, it means that the earnings tailwind is no longer a narrow phenomenon. The median stock is seeing its earnings accelerate, and that acceleration is broad-based across sectors. Second, it means that the valuation premium that the Mag 7 have enjoyed, which was justified by their earnings superiority, is now harder to defend. If the S&P 493 are growing earnings at nearly the same rate as the Mag 7, but at a fraction of the valuation multiple, then the relative value trade is clear. The report explicitly identifies the hyperscalers as an attractive relative value trade, precisely because their forward earnings are strong but their valuations are no longer demanding. This is not a call to sell the Mag 7; it is a call to recognize that the opportunity set is expanding.

The leading earnings model, which uses a composite of regional economic activity, supplier delivery data, wage tracking, and small business confidence indices, is forecasting double-digit EPS growth that extends through 2027. Consensus estimates have been rising steadily, with 2026 EPS now at 334 dollars and 2027 EPS at 380 dollars. The gap between the top-down and bottom-up estimates is closing, which suggests that the earnings cycle is real and that the risk of a downward revision is diminishing.

## Cyclicals Will Outperform Defensives Because the Operating Leverage Is Still Improving and AI Adoption Is Adding a Second Derivative

The sector recommendations in the report are unambiguous: overweight Financials, Industrials, and Consumer Discretionary Goods. Underweight Staples and Real Estate. This is not a tactical call; it is a structural positioning that follows from the rolling recovery thesis. When earnings are accelerating, when operating leverage is positive, and when the economy is running hot, cyclicals tend to outperform defensives. The logic is straightforward: cyclical companies have higher fixed costs and greater sensitivity to revenue growth. When revenue accelerates, a disproportionate share of that growth flows through to the bottom line. Defensive sectors, by contrast, have stable but low growth profiles. They are bond proxies in an equity market, and when the earnings cycle is strong, they become relative laggards.

What makes this cycle different is the role of AI adoption. The report frames AI not as a speculative theme but as a real driver of operating leverage. As companies across the economy adopt AI tools, they are able to reduce costs, improve productivity, and expand margins. This is not just a tech sector story. The report highlights that AI adoption is impacting banks, professional services, and industrial companies. The survey results referenced in the report suggest that the depth of impact is surprising and that the data has not been fully appreciated by the market. For investors, this means that the earnings acceleration in cyclicals may have more staying power than in previous cycles, because it is being augmented by a technology-driven productivity boost.

The preference for Financials is particularly telling. Banks benefit from a steepening yield curve, which improves net interest margins, and from increased lending activity as the economy expands. Industrials benefit from capital spending, reshoring, and infrastructure investment. Consumer Discretionary Goods benefit from a strong labor market and rising real wages. These are not speculative bets; they are bets on the structure of the current expansion.

## The Valuation Pullback Has Created an Entry Point, but the Real Upside Comes from Earnings Growth, Not Multiple Expansion

The S&P 500 forward P/E has pulled back from its peak, but the report projects that the multiple will remain relatively stable at around 20.5 times forward earnings. This is not a market that is being driven by valuation expansion. The 12 percent upside to the 8,300 price target comes almost entirely from earnings growth, not from a higher multiple. In the bull case, the P/E expands to 21.0 times, but even there, the majority of the return is driven by earnings.

This is an important distinction because it changes the way investors should think about risk. If the market were being driven by multiple expansion, then any shift in sentiment, any hawkish Fed surprise, or any geopolitical shock could cause a sharp de-rating. But when the market is being driven by earnings, the risk is different. The risk is that earnings disappoint, not that multiples compress. And the evidence suggests that earnings are not only meeting expectations but exceeding them. The earnings revisions breadth has turned positive, the leading indicators are strong, and the consensus estimates are rising.

The report provides three scenarios: bear, base, and bull. In the bear case, the S&P 500 falls to 5,900, representing a 20 percent decline. That scenario assumes that earnings growth stalls and that the multiple contracts to 19.0 times. But even in that scenario, 2026 EPS is projected at 299 dollars, which is only slightly below the current consensus. In other words, the bear case is not a recession; it is a slowdown. The bull case, with a target of 9,400, assumes that earnings accelerate further and that the multiple expands modestly. The base case, at 8,300, is the most probable outcome, and it is supported by the weight of the data.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: How Much of the Earnings Acceleration Is Cyclical Versus Structural, and What Happens When the AI Adoption Cycle Matures

The report is remarkably thorough, but it leaves several open questions that should trouble any serious investor. The first is the distinction between cyclical and structural earnings growth. The rolling recovery thesis is fundamentally cyclical: it assumes that the economy is in a mid-cycle expansion, that operating leverage is improving, and that earnings will continue to accelerate. But how much of the current earnings strength is being driven by structural factors like AI adoption, and how much is simply the result of a strong economy? If the economy were to slow, would the earnings acceleration reverse, or would the structural tailwinds from AI be strong enough to offset the cyclical headwinds?

The report does not provide a clear answer to this question, and that is not a criticism. It is an honest reflection of the uncertainty that every investor faces. The AI adoption cycle is still in its early stages, and the full impact on productivity and earnings will take years to materialize. But the market is already pricing in some of that impact. If the adoption cycle matures faster than expected, or if the productivity gains turn out to be smaller than anticipated, then the earnings projections for 2027 and 2028 could prove to be too optimistic.

The second open question is about valuation. The report argues that the hyperscalers are an attractive relative value trade, but it does not fully address the absolute valuation of the market. At 20.5 times forward earnings, the S&P 500 is not cheap by historical standards. The report acknowledges this implicitly by projecting a stable multiple rather than an expanding one. But if interest rates remain elevated, or if the risk-free rate rises, then a 20.5 multiple may be harder to sustain. The report's base case assumes that the macro environment remains supportive, but it does not stress-test the valuation under different rate scenarios.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: Three Questions to Determine Your Positioning

Given the strength of the rolling recovery thesis, the broadening of earnings, and the sector recommendations, how should an investor translate this analysis into a portfolio decision? The following framework is designed to help you assess your own positioning and determine whether you are aligned with the regime shift.

First, ask yourself: Is my portfolio overweight or underweight cyclicals relative to defensives? If you are underweight cyclicals, you are implicitly betting against the rolling recovery thesis. The data strongly suggests that cyclicals will outperform defensives over the next twelve months. If you are not comfortable with that bet, you need to understand why. Is it because you believe the economy will slow? Is it because you think the earnings acceleration is unsustainable? Or is it because you are simply anchored to the defensive positioning that worked in 2024? The report provides a clear answer: cyclicals win.

Second, ask yourself: Am I relying on the Mag 7 for earnings growth, or am I looking at the broader market? If your portfolio is dominated by the Mag 7, you are exposed to a concentration risk that is not justified by the earnings projections. The S&P 493 are expected to grow earnings at nearly the same rate as the Mag 7 in 2026 and 2027, but at a fraction of the valuation. This is a relative value opportunity that should not be ignored. The report explicitly highlights the hyperscalers as a relative value trade within the Mag 7, but the broader implication is that the rest of the market offers better risk-reward.

Third, ask yourself: Am I positioned for earnings growth or for multiple expansion? If you are betting on multiple expansion, you are betting on sentiment, liquidity, and macro stability. If you are betting on earnings growth, you are betting on the real economy. The report makes a compelling case that the earnings cycle is real and that it is broad. If you believe the data, then your portfolio should be tilted toward sectors and stocks that have the highest operating leverage and the strongest earnings momentum. That means Financials, Industrials, and Consumer Discretionary Goods. It does not mean defensive sectors, real estate, or staples.

## The Unresolved Tension That Makes This Report Worth Reading in Full

The most interesting tension in this report is between the confidence of the earnings projections and the uncertainty of the macro environment. On one hand, the data is clear: earnings are accelerating, breadth is improving, and the leading indicators are pointing higher. On the other hand, the market has already priced in a significant amount of good news. The S&P 500 is trading at a level that implies a soft landing and a strong earnings cycle. If the macro environment deteriorates, or if the AI adoption cycle disappoints, the downside could be significant.

The report does not resolve this tension, and it should not be expected to. What it does is provide a framework for thinking about it. The rolling recovery thesis is not a prediction; it is a conditional statement. If the economy continues to expand, if earnings continue to accelerate, and if AI adoption continues to drive productivity gains, then cyclicals will outperform, the S&P 493 will catch up to the Mag 7, and the market will move higher. If those conditions are not met, then the bear case becomes more likely. The decision for investors is not whether to be bullish or bearish; it is whether to bet on the conditions that support the bull case or to hedge against the conditions that support the bear case.
