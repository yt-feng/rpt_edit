# The Dollar's Next Leg Depends on Two Unresolved Risks: Iran and NVIDIA

The market is approaching a fork in the road, and the path forward for the US dollar is not simply a matter of momentum. A global investment bank report makes a clear argument: the positive momentum for the US dollar may continue in the near term, but the conviction behind this view is now conditional on two specific, high-stakes events that could either reinforce or reverse the trend. This is not a call for a one-directional dollar bet. It is an argument that the next major move in currency markets will be determined by the interplay of geopolitics and technology earnings, and that the most astute positioning today reflects a careful weighing of these risks rather than a blanket embrace of dollar strength.

For the professional investor, the immediate takeaway is that the tailwinds for the dollar remain intact, but they are no longer automatic. Pre-existing support from high energy prices and the negative implications for most of the world on trade and capital flows continue. However, this support has been supplemented by three specific factors: higher US inflation data pushing yields higher and raising concerns about the outlook for Fed rate cuts; the US economy holding steady as reflected in strong employment and retail sales data; and US equities breaking new highs. This combination has created a powerful narrative of US exceptionalism that has driven the dollar higher. Yet the report is careful to note that the sustainability of this narrative is now being tested.

The central thesis is that while the bias is toward stress building for emerging market and Asian FX, particularly in South and Southeast Asia, the risk of a reversal is real and concentrated. A further de-escalation of the Iran situation could lead to lower global energy prices, more positivity for broad markets, and some softening of the dollar. This is the key tension: the dollar is benefiting from geopolitical risk, but that same risk carries the potential for a sharp unwind if the situation improves. The market is pricing in a continuation of the current environment, but the report suggests that this pricing may not fully account for the possibility of a diplomatic breakthrough.

## The Dollar's Strength Is Now a Function of Two Tail Risks, Not Just Fundamentals

The report identifies two specific risk events that will determine the near-term trajectory of the dollar: the US-Iran-China geopolitical dynamic and NVIDIA's earnings. These are not peripheral concerns. They are the two variables that, in the report's framework, will either validate or undermine the current dollar bid.

On the geopolitical front, the report highlights a critical divergence between the US and Chinese positions. The US has made clear its opposition to the militarization of the Strait and any effort to charge a toll for its use, and has stated agreement that Iran can never have a nuclear weapon. China's position, by contrast, has been more circumspect, calling for a solution that takes into account the concerns of all parties and for shipping routes to be reopened as soon as possible. The question is whether China will exert significant pressure on Iran. The report notes that limited comments from China suggest this may not be the case, but the risk is that if China does play a more significant role to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, the resulting decline in energy prices could undermine a key pillar of dollar support.

The report also introduces a political dimension that is often overlooked by purely economic models. It notes that Time Magazine highlighted that in the last three mid-terms where the price of oil is around USD100 per barrel at this point in the cycle, the party in the White House lost 29 House seats on average. This creates a powerful incentive for the current administration to seek a resolution, but it also creates a counter-incentive for Iran, which may view higher energy prices as an effective way to adversely affect the administration. This is a classic strategic standoff, and the market is not fully pricing the possibility that either side could blink.

On the technology front, the report focuses on NVIDIA's earnings as the second critical variable. With the stock up significantly this month and even more since the end of March, investors may need both a beat of revenue expectations and strong forward guidance to maintain the current level of enthusiasm. The report's logic is that any disappointment could lead to downside risks to US equities, lowering the attractiveness of US assets and the dollar. However, it also warns that if there is a sharp US equity sell-off, emerging market and Asian risk FX will underperform the dollar broadly speaking, as compared with other parts of the G10. This creates a nuanced scenario: a moderate disappointment could weaken the dollar, but a severe sell-off could actually strengthen it as capital flows back to the US.

## The Most Conviction-Rich Trade Reveals a Deep Structural Divergence Within Asia

The report's highest conviction trade is a long position on the Singapore dollar against the Indonesian rupiah, with a maximum conviction level of five out of five. This is not a tactical call. It is a structural bet that reflects a fundamental divergence between two Asian economies that are both exposed to the same global headwinds but are responding in fundamentally different ways.

For Indonesia, the report identifies a cascade of negative factors that are creating a self-reinforcing cycle of pressure on the rupiah. The central bank has been an aggressive seller of foreign exchange reserves, but despite this intervention, the rupiah has still underperformed. The report argues that the central bank may now allow some FX flexibility, which would help reduce the drainage of reserves but would also mean further currency weakness. The market is questioning the central bank's willingness to hike rates aggressively, and the report's discussions with investors suggest that even a rate hike will be insufficient in the current backdrop. The balance of payments pressures are intensifying due to rising geopolitical risks, persistent fiscal concerns, and a worsening current account deficit driven by dividend repatriation and elevated energy prices.

The structural issue goes deeper. The report notes that following the MSCI Index Review, Indonesia could see foreign equity outflows of a significant magnitude. Furthermore, the index provider will soon communicate on the free float assessment of Indonesian securities as part of a scheduled Market Accessibility Review. Investor concerns remain about possible adjustments to the foreign inclusion factor, which could exacerbate foreign equity outflows. The report's discussions with market participants suggest that halving the foreign inclusion factor could result in foreign equity outflows of a sUBStantial amount. Another index provider has also noted that it will delete the affected Indonesian securities in its June index review. This is a structural headwind that no amount of central bank intervention can fully offset.

For Singapore, the report identifies a completely different set of dynamics. Elevated energy prices amid the US-Iran stalemate could increase inflationary pressures in the local economy, and the central bank's latest inflation statement included a more explicit characterization of upside inflation risks. This is important because Singapore uses the exchange rate as its primary monetary policy tool, and higher inflation creates the conditions for further currency appreciation. The report also notes that US financing conditions remain relatively loose, which reduces the risk of a sharp global growth slowdown and lowers the negative tail risk on Singapore's growth. The current relatively strong pace of the Singapore dollar's nominal effective exchange rate appreciation should also provide near-term support.

The contrast is stark. Indonesia is facing a crisis of confidence that is being exacerbated by structural factors such as index rebalancing and potential rating agency downgrades. Singapore is facing a manageable inflation problem that its central bank is well-equipped to handle. This divergence is not temporary. It reflects fundamental differences in economic management, institutional credibility, and exposure to global capital flows. The report's maximum conviction on this trade is a statement that these differences are likely to persist and even widen.

## The Political Risk Premium in the UK and Japan Is Still Being Misunderstood

The report extends its analysis beyond Asia to identify two G10 trades that reflect a similar logic of structural divergence: long euro against the pound, and long Swiss franc against the Japanese yen. Both trades have high conviction levels, and both are based on the idea that political risk is not fully priced into the respective currencies.

For the UK, the report argues that the political risk premium is unlikely to be fully priced in the FX market yet. There are growing risks on the survival of the Prime Minister, and this political uncertainty is likely to weigh on the pound. The report's logic is that the market is still treating the UK's political situation as a manageable risk, but the underlying dynamics suggest that the risk of a significant political event is higher than currently priced. This is not a call for an imminent crisis, but rather a recognition that the political risk premium in the pound is too low given the potential for disruption.

For Japan, the report maintains its conviction on long Swiss franc against the yen, but with an important caveat. The trade is performing well, but the report is becoming more watchful as the dollar-yen exchange rate moves closer towards a key level. The risk of Ministry of Finance intervention is gradually increasing again, and this creates a potential headwind for the trade. The report's logic is that while the fundamental case for yen weakness remains intact, the risk of intervention creates a tactical uncertainty that needs to be managed. This is a classic example of a trade that is right on the fundamentals but faces near-term execution risk.

The common thread in both trades is that political risk is being underestimated. In the UK, the risk is domestic political instability. In Japan, the risk is policy intervention. In both cases, the market is pricing these risks as manageable, but the report suggests that the potential for a significant move is higher than currently discounted. This is a contrarian view that requires conviction, but it is supported by a careful analysis of the underlying political dynamics.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The China Question and the Limits of FX Intervention

For all its analytical depth, the report leaves several meaningful questions unanswered. The most important of these is the role of China in the Iran situation and the broader implications for the dollar and Asian FX.

The report notes that it remains to be seen whether China will exert significant pressure on Iran, but it does not provide a clear framework for assessing the probability of this outcome. The report presents the competing narratives: the US wants China to play a more significant role, but China's public statements have been cautious. The report also notes the risk that Iran may hold back on any agreement on the view that higher energy prices are an effective way to adversely affect the administration. But it does not resolve the tension between these two possibilities.

This is not a criticism of the report. It is a recognition that the situation is inherently uncertain and that any attempt to assign precise probabilities would be misleading. The report's value is in identifying the key variables and the range of possible outcomes, not in making a definitive prediction. For the investor, the implication is that exposure to this risk should be managed carefully, with an eye to the potential for a sharp reversal if the situation changes.

Another unresolved question is the effectiveness of FX intervention. The report documents significant intervention by multiple central banks in Asia, but it also notes that despite this intervention, currencies have continued to weaken. The report does not fully address the question of when intervention becomes counterproductive or when it signals a loss of control. The discussion of Indonesia is particularly instructive: the central bank was an aggressive seller of reserves, but the rupiah still underperformed. The report suggests that the central bank may now allow some FX flexibility, which is a tacit admission that intervention has its limits.

The report also does not fully explore the implications of the index rebalancing for Indonesia. The potential for foreign equity outflows is clearly identified, but the report does not model the second-order effects on the currency or on the broader economy. If the outflows materialize, they could trigger a vicious cycle of currency weakness, capital flight, and further outflows. The report flags this risk but does not quantify it.

## A Decision Framework for Navigating the Next Two Months

For the serious investor, the report provides a clear framework for decision-making over the next two months. The framework is built around three key questions.

First, what is the probability of a US-Iran diplomatic breakthrough? The report suggests that the market is not pricing this possibility highly, but the political incentives for both sides create a non-trivial probability of a deal. If a breakthrough occurs, the dollar is likely to weaken, energy prices will fall, and Asian currencies will benefit. The report's highest conviction trade, long SGD/IDR, would be tested by this scenario because the Singapore dollar's advantage is partly based on energy prices supporting its inflation dynamics. However, the structural divergence between Singapore and Indonesia would still favor the trade, even if the near-term catalyst changes.

Second, what is the probability of a significant NVIDIA earnings disappointment? The report's logic is that the stock's recent run-up has created high expectations, and any disappointment could trigger a sell-off. But the report also notes that the relationship between US equities and the dollar is not linear. A moderate sell-off could weaken the dollar, but a sharp sell-off could strengthen it as capital flows back to the US. The investor needs to assess not just the direction of the earnings outcome but also the magnitude of the market reaction.

Third, what is the probability of policy intervention in Japan? The report flags this risk but does not resolve it. The investor needs to assess the likelihood that the Ministry of Finance will intervene as the dollar-yen approaches a key level. If intervention occurs, the yen could strengthen sharply, which would benefit the CHF/JPY trade but could also create broader market dislocations.

The framework is not a simple checklist. It requires the investor to assess probabilities and to position accordingly. The report's highest conviction trades are designed to perform well in a range of scenarios, but they are not immune to tail risks. The investor should monitor the key variables closely and be prepared to adjust positions if the probabilities change.
