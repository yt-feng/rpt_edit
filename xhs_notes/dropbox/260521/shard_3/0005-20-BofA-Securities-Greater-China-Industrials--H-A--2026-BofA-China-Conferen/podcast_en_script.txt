EN_A: So I just went through a pretty detailed report from a global investment bank on China's industrial sector. What do you think is the big question it's trying to answer?
EN_B: The big question is straightforward: where will the growth come from for Chinese industrial companies in 2026? After talking to 18 companies, the report points to three clear drivers: exports, electrification, and AI-related demand.
EN_A: Exports? But the global trade environment is pretty messy right now, especially with all the USD volatility. How can these companies still rely on exports?
EN_B: That's actually the first thing that surprised me. Major construction machinery players are forecasting around 20 percent year-on-year export growth for 2026, while domestic revenue is only growing in the single digits. Take Sany for example, it expects U.S. revenue to grow over 20 percent, and Africa even 20 to 30 percent.
EN_A: So how are they managing the FX risk? They can't just ignore it.
EN_B: The report says the leading players are doing three things: reducing foreign currency exposure, shortening overseas accounts receivable periods, and using more scientific hedging. Zoomlion, for instance, expects its full-year FX loss to narrow from over 400 million RMB in the first quarter to around 200 million for the whole year.
EN_A: Okay, so exports are still a big theme. What about electrification and AI? How do they show up in the report?
EN_B: For electrification, the report notes that since March, rising energy prices have boosted demand for electric machinery. Sany has already launched over 100-ton electric excavators with positive customer feedback. On the AI side, it's more about factory automation and data center equipment. Megmeet, for example, saw its AI server power supply revenue jump 67 percent year-on-year in the first quarter, and it's already shipping to a U.S. cloud service provider.
EN_A: Wait, Megmeet is supplying to U.S. clients? That sounds like a big shift. What else did the report say about automation?
EN_B: The automation sector is in an upcycle. The factory automation companies surveyed, like Han's Laser and Wuxi Lead, all saw orders picking up month over month in April. Han's Laser expects its battery-related revenue to grow 60 percent year-on-year in 2026, driven by lithium battery and semiconductor demand. And 3D printing equipment is becoming a new growth area.
EN_A: Speaking of new tech, what about humanoid robots? Last year everyone was talking about hardware. What's changed this year?
EN_B: That's one of the key suspense points in the report. A humanoid robot expert says the battlefield in 2026 will shift from hardware to embodied AI and training data. The VLA plus world model approach is becoming mainstream. But the report doesn't fully answer how hardware and AI model compatibility will be resolved, especially for sensors and dexterous hands, which have high entry barriers.
EN_A: So it sounds like a "hardware first, software later" kind of story. What about commercial vehicles and engines? Any new moves there?
EN_B: This is really interesting. Weichai Power plans to start delivering 2 to 5 MW gas generators to U.S. AIDC clients from the fourth quarter of 2026, targeting 200 units in 2026 and over 1,000 by 2027. Yuchai is also expanding capacity and planning to export to the U.S. by late 2027. So Chinese engine makers are tapping into the AI data center backup power market.
EN_A: So from construction machinery to robots to data centers, Chinese companies seem to be diversifying. But does the report mention any risks or uncertainties?
EN_B: Yes, for example, heavy-duty truck exports are seeing short-term delays in Southeast Asia and Africa due to fuel shortages. And some new businesses, like humanoid robot commercialization, still need more validation.
EN_A: So what's the one part of the report you'd recommend people dig into?
EN_B: I'd say the suspense around humanoid robots shifting from hardware to AI, and the changing role of Chinese companies in the global data center supply chain. The report gives initial insights, but the full picture on how these trends will play out requires reading the complete analysis. If you want those details, I'd suggest checking out the original report for each company's specific targets.
