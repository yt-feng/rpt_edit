EN_A: So the April passenger vehicle insurance registrations dropped 19 percent year-on-year. That sounds pretty brutal. Is the Chinese auto market falling apart?
EN_B: Not exactly. If you look closer, the wholesale numbers were only down 4 percent. The big gap between wholesale and retail tells us something important. Dealers are destocking. They are not ordering as many cars from manufacturers, but that is a deliberate move, not a sign of collapsing demand.
EN_A: So the 19 percent drop is more about inventory management than consumers walking away?
EN_B: Exactly. The report estimates that channel inventory decreased by about 23,000 units in April. That means manufacturers are controlling their shipments, and dealers are clearing out old stock. It is actually a healthier sign for the market than a panic sell-off.
EN_A: But if demand is still there, why are retail sales so weak? Are people just not buying cars?
EN_B: People are buying, but they are being more selective. Look at the EV segment. April EV insurance registrations were 827,000 units, down only 6 percent year-on-year and up 3 percent month-on-month. The structural demand for EVs is still there. The pain is concentrated in the internal combustion engine segment.
EN_A: So the old guard, the joint venture brands, are they getting crushed?
EN_B: They are under severe pressure. Japanese joint ventures like GAC Honda dropped 49 percent, Dongfeng Honda dropped 47 percent. German brands are not doing much better. SAIC Volkswagen fell 46 percent, FAW Volkswagen fell 33 percent. Even luxury names like Porsche dropped 45 percent, Lexus dropped 34 percent. It is not just one brand, it is the entire joint venture ecosystem shrinking.
EN_A: What about the Chinese domestic brands? Are they all winning?
EN_B: It is highly polarized. SAIC Motor Passenger Vehicle grew 33 percent year-on-year, thanks to new model deliveries. But Great Wall Motor dropped 16 percent, Changan dropped 11 percent, GAC Trumpchi dropped 34 percent. So domestic brands are not a monolith either. The ones with new energy models and fresh product cycles are surviving, the others are struggling.
EN_A: Speaking of new energy, BYD saw a 30 percent drop in April insurance registrations. That is a big surprise. What happened?
EN_B: This is a key point. BYD's market share fell from nearly 30 percent late last year to 22.2 percent in April. It is still number one, but the gap is narrowing. Two reasons. One, the base effect, BYD had extremely high numbers last year. Two, competitors are catching up fast. Startups like Leapmotor, NIO, and XPeng are growing very quickly.
EN_A: Leapmotor sold 50,000 units, up 53 percent. NIO up 34 percent. XPeng down 17 percent. That is a huge divergence. What is driving Leapmotor's surge?
EN_B: Leapmotor is winning on value for money. Its C-series models are very competitive in the 100,000 to 150,000 RMB price range. NIO is winning on its battery swap network and premium service. XPeng's decline is likely a product cycle issue, they are waiting for new models. The report gives a lot of detail on this, but there is one question it does not fully answer.
EN_A: What is that?
EN_B: Who are these startups taking market share from? Are they taking it from the joint venture brands, or are they taking it from BYD? If it is from joint ventures, the story can continue. If it is from BYD, then the competition is about to get much more intense. The report does not give a clear answer on that. We need to watch the next few months of data.
EN_A: So the real value of this report is not the headline number, but understanding the structural shift happening underneath.
EN_B: Exactly. April data is like a mirror. It shows who is swimming naked and who has a life jacket. The traditional joint ventures are being forced out, domestic brands are splitting into winners and losers, and the new energy startups are rising fast. But the biggest question remains. Once the joint ventures are gone, who will be the ultimate winner? This report gives us a great starting point, but the answer will come in the next few months.
