ZH_B: 没错，这份报告的核心判断是，AI基础设施的需求正在从以GPU为中心的系统，向更广泛的CPU服务器扩展。特别是代理式AI的兴起，会显著增加对CPU服务器的需求。这意味着，像戴尔、慧与和超微电脑这样的服务器OEM厂商，将迎来一个重要的增长机会。

ZH_A: 代理式AI？我们经常听到训练和推理，代理式AI和它们有什么不同吗？为什么它会更需要CPU？

ZH_B: 你可以这样理解。训练大模型是GPU的强项，就像用一台超级计算机处理海量数据。简单的聊天机器人推理，也是单次输入输出。但代理式AI不同，它需要执行多步骤任务，比如调用工具、检索数据库、进行验证循环、管理上下文等等。这些任务需要大量的协调和编排工作，而CPU正是处理这些“杂务”的专家。报告里引用英特尔的话说，在推理中，CPU和GPU的比例已经从1比8压缩到1比4，未来可能趋向1比1。

ZH_A: 所以，这不仅仅是企业服务器更新换代的故事，而是一个全新的增长引擎。报告里有没有给出更具体的增长预测？

ZH_A: 这个预测听起来很乐观。但报告里也特别分析了超微电脑。超微最近的表现似乎有些复杂，这是为什么？

ZH_B: 超微电脑最近的财报看起来不错，利润超预期了，但背后的原因值得深究。利润改善是因为产品组合变了：AI服务器的占比下降了，而传统服务器和企业客户的占比上升了。这听起来是好事，但问题是，超微电脑的核心投资逻辑是AI服务器的强劲增长。现在这个逻辑正在动摇。报告认为，这背后可能反映了两个问题：一是传统服务器需求确实在复苏，二是因为一些声誉问题，超微可能在AI服务器领域丢失市场份额。

ZH_A: 那这对戴尔和慧与意味着什么？谁会是最大的受益者？

ZH_B: 报告认为，戴尔和慧与都能从这两个趋势中受益，但戴尔是更明确的选择。报告认为戴尔是真正的AI赢家。原因有三点：规模优势、最强的融资能力，以及最深厚的客户关系。这使得戴尔最有能力承接超微电脑流失的订单，无论是在云、企业还是主权AI市场。报告将戴尔的目标价从220美元大幅上调到了280美元。

ZH_A: 那慧与呢？报告对它的判断是什么？

ZH_B: 慧与也会受益于更强劲的服务器需求和市场份额增长，特别是在企业和主权客户领域。但报告给它的评级是“市场表现”，目标价从21美元上调到35美元。这意味着慧与的增长确定性可能不如戴尔那么强。

ZH_A: 听起来，这份报告的核心观点是，AI基建正在进入一个新阶段，GPU不再是唯一的主角。但报告里有没有一些没有完全展开，或者仍然存在不确定性的地方？

ZH_B: 当然有。比如，超微电脑的客户流失到底有多大，以及这些流失的订单有多少能真正转化为戴尔和慧与的收入，报告里没有给出具体数字，这需要后续财报来验证。另外，代理式AI对CPU需求的增长，是否真的会像报告中预测的那么快，也取决于整个AI应用生态的成熟度。这些是完整报告里会继续展开的悬念。

ZH_A: 看来，要了解这些更深入的细节，比如具体的估值模型和风险分析，还是得去读完整的报告。感谢你今天的分享，这为我们理解AI硬件投资提供了一个全新的视角。

EN_A: We have a new report from a global investment bank on U.S. IT hardware, specifically server OEMs. It says Agentic AI is going to drive a huge wave of demand for traditional CPU servers. That sounds pretty different from the GPU-centric story we’ve been hearing. What’s the main argument here?

EN_B: The core idea is that AI infrastructure demand is broadening beyond just GPU systems. The rise of Agentic AI, which involves more complex, multi-step tasks, is expected to boost demand for CPU-based servers significantly. This creates a major opportunity for server OEMs like Dell, HPE, and Super Micro.

EN_A: Agentic AI. We hear about training and inference all the time. How is this different, and why does it need more CPUs?

EN_B: Think of it this way. Training a large model is a GPU-heavy job, like using a supercomputer. Simple chatbot inference is also a one-shot thing. But Agentic AI involves a workflow: planning, calling tools, querying databases, validating results, managing memory. All that orchestration and data movement is handled much better by general-purpose CPUs. The report cites Intel saying the ratio of CPUs to GPUs in inference has already gone from 1:8 to 1:4, and could move towards parity.

EN_A: So this isn’t just about enterprise server refreshes. It’s a whole new growth driver. Does the report put any numbers on this opportunity?

EN_B: Yes, and they are pretty striking. AMD raised its server CPU market growth forecast to over 35% annually. The bank’s own back-of-the-envelope math suggests the total traditional server market could be 2.5 to 4.5 times larger by 2030. And it’s not just enterprises driving this; it’s also cloud providers and so-called "tier-2" cloud players, making the demand base much more durable.

EN_A: That’s a very bullish outlook. But the report also spends a lot of time on Super Micro. Their recent results seemed mixed. What’s going on there?

EN_B: Super Micro beat on earnings, but the reason is a warning sign for the AI thesis. The beat came from a mix shift: a lower percentage of AI servers and a higher percentage of traditional servers and enterprise customers. This improved margins, but it also means their core AI server growth story is weakening. The report suggests this reflects both a recovery in traditional servers and potential market share loss for Super Micro due to recent reputational issues.

EN_A: So what does that mean for Dell and HPE? Who benefits the most?

EN_B: Both can benefit, but the report is very clear that Dell is the preferred winner. They call Dell the real AI winner. The reasons are its massive scale, the strongest financing capabilities, and the deepest customer relationships. This positions Dell best to capture any orders that Super Micro loses, whether from cloud, enterprise, or sovereign AI customers. The bank raised its price target for Dell from 220 to 280 dollars.

EN_A: And what about HPE?

EN_B: HPE will also benefit from stronger server demand and some market share gains, especially in enterprise and sovereign accounts. But the bank rates it as Market Perform, with a price target raised from 21 to 35 dollars. The message is that the upside is less certain compared to Dell.
