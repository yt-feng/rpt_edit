EN_A: So bond yields have been climbing. The big question is, when does that start to hurt stocks?

EN_B: It is the lowest since the 2008 financial crisis. And it is 90 basis points below the long-term average of 3.1 percent. So from a relative value perspective, stocks are looking expensive compared to bonds.

EN_B: Not necessarily. The report notes we are still above the extreme levels of the tech bubble in 2000. But the cushion is much thinner now.

EN_A: Cushion meaning the room for yields to rise without causing trouble?

EN_B: Exactly. If bond yields keep going up, stocks have much less buffer. And from a long-term asset allocation view, bonds probably deserve a bigger weight than they have had in the past 15 years.
