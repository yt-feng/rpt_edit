EN_B: I think this is a report worth paying attention to. The main reason is that it’s not just relying on one indicator. It brings together two independent signals — one from households and one from the bond market — and they’re both pointing in the same direction.

EN_B: About two weeks ago, the same team highlighted that a specific measure from the Conference Board’s consumer survey had shifted. It’s a net interest rate expectations indicator. EN_A: So that was the early warning. But at the time, the market might not have fully priced it in?
EN_B: Exactly. And now we have a second signal. The Treasury market is starting to say the same thing.
EN_A: Which specific metric are they looking at?
EN_B: They focus on the spread between the 2-year Treasury yield and the effective fed funds rate. This spread briefly rose above 50 basis points recently and is now around 45 basis points. Historically, that’s been a pretty reliable leading indicator.
EN_A: Leading for what? For how much the Fed might move?
EN_B: Yes. They ran a regression going back three decades, and they found that this spread leads the year-over-year change in the fed funds rate by about eleven months, with a peak correlation of 66%. And that correlation would probably be even higher if the fed funds rate hadn’t been stuck at zero for so long after the financial crisis.
EN_A: So if the spread is at 45 basis points now, what does the historical model suggest?
EN_B: The regression implies an increase in the fed funds rate of roughly 30 basis points over the next year. That’s basically one standard hike. But it’s important to note that this is not a prediction — it’s a risk signal.
EN_A: So both households and the bond market are now aligned. Does that mean a hike is inevitable?
EN_B: Not at all. And the report itself leaves a key question open. It says that if this spread sustains at current levels, that’s when it becomes really significant. But whether it sustains depends on upcoming inflation data, the jobs report, and what Fed officials say next. The report doesn’t fully answer that.
EN_A: So the real value of this report is that it gives us a framework — a set of indicators to watch — rather than a final call.
EN_B: Exactly. And one more thing: the report notes that this spread is now at its highest level since late 2022. And we all remember what happened after that — the most aggressive hiking cycle in decades.
EN_A: So history doesn’t repeat, but it rhymes. What about the practical side? For an investor, does this signal mean they should adjust their portfolio?
EN_B: That’s a great question, and it’s actually something the report doesn’t fully explore. It’s more of a macro risk warning than a trade recommendation.
