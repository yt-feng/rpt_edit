We expect the Bank of Korea Monetary Policy Committee to sound incrementally more hawkish on upside inflation risks at its first meeting under Governor Shin Hyun-song, reflecting ongoing Middle East energy disruptions and a renewed pickup in metropolitan housing prices. That said, we expect guidance to remain measured, given uncertainty around the duration and intensity of the energy shock and still-anchored inflation expectations. Policy uncertainty also remains over how the government will use sizable excess tax revenues—estimated at more than 5% of GDP—generated by major Korean memory producers.

We expect the BOK to raise its 2026 growth forecast from $1.9\%$ toward the $2.5\%$ consensus. We expect the BOK to raise its 2026 CPI forecast from $2.3\%$ , but likely only modestly. 6. Financial flows have been a key driver of recent KRW weakness. Retail outbound flows surged in Q4, foreign equity investors' hedging demand rose in January, and foreign investors sold off Korean equities in March along with spikes in global risk aversion amid the war in the Middle East. More recently, USDKRW moved back above 1,500 together with foreign equity outflows reaching a record high of some US\$30bn in May (Exhibit 10). By contrast, local retail investment abroad slowed sharply, from outflows of US\$10bn in Q1 to marginal inflows over the last two months, likely reflecting tax incentives introduced in December 2025 and fading expectations of Fed rate cuts.

That said, we think the improving current account will become a more important driver of the KRW in coming months. Outbound flows from pension funds and retail investors should ease in 2026 on recent government measures, while KRW demand from memory companies is likely to rise sharply on local capacity expansion (US\$80bn) and interim corporate tax payments as noted above. Our strategists also argue that the recent pickup in foreign equity outflows largely reflects portfolio rebalancing and diversification rules rather than weakening corporate fundamentals (Exhibit 11). More than 90% of year-to-date foreign selling has been concentrated on the two major memory makers, and they estimate that cumulative selling tied to diversification rules has reached roughly US\$69bn since end-October. With foreign ownership in Korea's semiconductor sector now at a 10-year low despite a strong outlook for earnings, much of the foreign-driven selling may already be behind us.
