# Korea Views: May MPC Preview — A Measured Hawkish Turn

We expect the Bank of Korea Monetary Policy Committee to sound incrementally more hawkish on upside inflation risks at its first meeting under Governor Shin Hyun-song, reflecting ongoing Middle East energy disruptions and a renewed pickup in metropolitan housing prices. That said, we expect guidance to remain measured, given uncertainty around the duration and intensity of the energy shock and still-anchored inflation expectations. Policy uncertainty also remains over how the government will use sizable excess tax revenues—estimated at more than 5% of GDP—generated by major Korean memory producers.

We expect the BOK to raise its 2026 growth forecast from $1.9\%$ toward the $2.5\%$ consensus. While Q1 GDP was an upside surprise, we do not expect the Bank to move materially above $2.5\%$ , given the drag from prolonged energy disruptions and a still-narrow, tech-led recovery. With tech accounting for only around $10\%$ of GDP and $1\%$ of employment, the recovery remains too narrow to justify a meaningfully stronger growth upgrade.

We expect the BOK to raise its 2026 CPI forecast from $2.3\%$ , but likely only modestly. Persistently high energy prices are the main driver, but still-anchored inflation expectations and soft non-tech activity and labor market conditions should limit the scale of any upward revision. Headline CPI rose to $2.6\%$ yoy in April, broadly in line with expectations, with momentum led by oil-related goods and services. High-frequency data points to stabilizing inflation momentum in May. Meanwhile, Seoul housing prices have resumed rising since mid-March, reviving concerns around housing market overheating.

We see support for Korean bonds from both WGBI inclusion and the AI-driven super surplus. Strong chip revenues could raise interim excess tax payments by KRW60trn in August, expanding the fiscal buffer and helping reduce FX market imbalances. While financial outflows have been a major source of KRW weakness in recent years, we think the scale and persistence of the improving current account can more than offset those depreciation pressures.

Our BOK terminal rate forecast remains $3.00\%$ by end-2026, with 25bp hikes in both July and October. It would remain below both the $3.5\%$ peak reached in the previous post-pandemic hiking cycle and current market pricing of $4.25\%$ . We see risks to our rate view as broadly balanced, with the energy price path and the fiscal treatment of excess tax revenues the two main swing factors.

5. While global factors are putting upward pressure on Korean yields, we think the AI-driven super surplus should provide an important offset by narrowing the fiscal deficit. Since passage of the supplementary budget, earnings forecasts for semiconductor companies have been revised up sharply. Our tech analysts estimate that 2026 tax provisioning by Korea's two largest chipmakers—most of it payable domestically—could jump to as much as KRW150trn from KRW12trn in 2025. That would materially strengthen Korea's fiscal buffer and help reduce imbalances in the FX market (Exhibit 9). $^{2}$

The sharp increases in tax provisioning implies roughly KRW60trn in interim excess tax payments by end-August from major semiconductor companies, or KRW85trn including the KRW25trn already incorporated in the supplementary budget. That would be more than sufficient to cover the planned consolidated general government budget deficit of KRW52.7trn for 2026. Korea should also continue to benefit from WGBI-related flows, which we estimate could total roughly KRW55trn cumulatively this year.
