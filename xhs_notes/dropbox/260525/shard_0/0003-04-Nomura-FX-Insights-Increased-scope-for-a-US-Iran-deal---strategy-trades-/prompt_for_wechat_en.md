# Asia FX

Short USD/CNH: We raise the conviction level on our short USD/CNH position to 4/5 (from 3), targeting 6.60 (\~3% gains) by end-August. With rising scope for a US-Iran deal (see above), we are more convinced that USD/CNH can move significantly lower along with a weaker broad USD: 1) USD/CNH was much more sensitive to downmoves in DXY than upmoves. From the start of the Iran war (28 February) to date, DXY is 1.4% higher, while USD/CNH is actually 1.2% lower. During this period, the sensitivity of USD/CNH to upward moves in DXY has been low at \~24%, while USD/CNH's sensitivity to DXY moves lower is much stronger at \~54%; 2) the USD/CNY daily fixing continues to drift lower amid some official constraint. Today, the USD/CNY fixing came in another 55pips lower to reach a new low of 6.8318 (since 15 February 2023), with the positive error remaining stable at 448pips; 3) onshore corporates are likely to continue repatriating USD holdings, owing to China's continued strong export growth, as well as some local expectations for RMB appreciation; 4) a constructive backdrop for US-China relations provides a conducive environment for China capital inflows; and 5) RMB appreciation should also be supported by its substantial undervaluation. Based on our four FX valuation models, RMB remains undervalued by an average of 9.5%, with its productivity-adjusted REER undervalued by 19.3%.

Short USD/TWD: We initiate a short USD/TWD position with a modest conviction level of 3/5, targeting 30.5 (\~3% gains) by end-September. Amid a potential US-Iran agreement that could re-open the Strait of Hormuz (see above), we see scope for TWD to appreciate, driven by: 1) a likely softer USD and risk positive environment, which should facilitate TWD appreciation against USD, alongside a broadly lower USD/Asia complex. Our expectation for CNH appreciation (see above) should also provide a tailwind for TWD; 2) despite being highly exposed to the Hormuz energy shock, TWD has outperformed within AeJ since the onset of the Iran war, owing to the strength of its tech sector. Therefore, we would expect TWD to enjoy a tailwind, if energy prices ease from currently elevated levels. Conversely, if the US-Iran stalemate continues, we would also not expect TWD to depreciate much; 3) Taiwan should remain a significant beneficiary of strong global AI demand and capex. Global tech investment shows no signs of slowing, as US hyperscalers have ramped up their FY2026 capex guidance in their Q1 earnings calls by \~USD55bn from their previous forecasts. Locally in Taiwan, TSMC's strong capex (\~USD70bn in 2027) and local sourcing plan will likely be another positive catalyst for Taiwan domestic semi supply chain. Overall, we expect foreign equity inflows to remain supportive for TWD; 4) global AI demand is, in turn, supporting robust local fundamentals. Our economics team recently revised up their 2026 Taiwan GDP growth forecast to 8.4% from 8.0%, reflecting the strong AI-led export and investment cycle, which should more than offset the impact of the Middle East shock; 5) a softer USD environment may offer some Taiwan lifers and exporters an opportunity to consider increasing their FX hedging ratios (tactically) and reducing their USD hoarding, respectively, especially if USD/TWD swap points are in premium; and 6) potential scrutiny from the US Treasury on Taiwan's currency practices and macroeconomic policies. This may somewhat limit CBC's USD buying inclination.

Long SGD/IDR: Following the lowering of our conviction level on 22 May, owing to the positive US-Iran developments, we maintain our conviction level at this same elevated 4/5 today. In Singapore, we continue to see scope for SGD to outperform, as: 1) the economy remains resilient. Long EUR/INR: We maintain the conviction level on our long EUR/INR trade at 3/5 (lowered conviction to 3/5 on 22 May). Long EUR/PHP: We take profit on our long EUR/PHP by lowering the conviction level to 2/5 (from 3; lowered conviction to 3/5 on 22 May). Nonetheless, we believe medium-term depreciation pressures on PHP from higher current account deficits, local FX deposit outflows and political uncertainties are likely to persist over the medium term.

Long EUR/GBP: We remain long EUR/GBP, although we can understand the potential for some GBP outperformance on improved risk sentiment. If the focus on the Middle East starts to fade, local cyclical stories should then become more important again. On that front, there is a risk BoE decides not to hike, in light of the soft local labour market and recent inflation data, while the ECB still seems committed to hiking. This, as well as UK political risks, justify EUR/GBP trading higher, in our view.

Long EUR/SEK and long USD/CAD: We took profit on our long EUR/SEK trade for a small gain, as we think SEK will receive some more near-term positivity than EUR, even though we think the Riksbank can remain more dovish than the ECB. # Rates strategy

Fresh hope of a deal to end the US-Iran conflict led to a relief rally in Asia rates this morning. While details remain to be seen and oil prices are unlikely to fall back to levels seen before the war, a deal should at least result in the rate hike premia in some countries coming off. In Asia, Korea and India are the two markets that are pricing in the most hikes (in Korea, \~100bp are priced over next 12 months, and in India, there is still close to 100bp priced over the next four meetings). We are more positive on Korea than India, as the BOK is less likely to surprise with large one-off hikes and as fiscal/bond supply-demand dynamics are better in Korea (WGBI inflows). We maintain a receive 2y2y Korea NDIRS at a high conviction level of 4/5 and target a move to 3.75%. In Singapore, we maintain a receive Jun-1y SORA position on the view that SGD inflows will continue, with FX reserves still increasing. We expect the SORA fixing to average \~1.1% and receiving in the front end still offers good carry. Separately, we maintain a pay Jun-10y HK IRS position, albeit at a lower conviction level of 3/5, after we reduced from 4/5 last week, as we believe the US will remain above pre-war levels, with recent growth data and NFP on the strong side, while better Hong Kong macro and bond supply sentiment should continue to support a narrower HK-US spread.
