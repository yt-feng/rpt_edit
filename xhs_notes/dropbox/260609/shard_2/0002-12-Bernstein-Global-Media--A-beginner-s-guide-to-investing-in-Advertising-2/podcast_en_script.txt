EN_B: The key insight is that advertising growth is driven by redistribution, not by an increase in total consumption. Global media consumption has plateaued at around 11 hours per day. So inventory growth is limited, and value is shifting between formats and platforms.
EN_A: So it’s not about a bigger pie, but about who gets the bigger slice?
EN_B: Exactly. Digital now accounts for over two-thirds of global ad spend. This fragmentation makes execution more complex and puts a premium on scale, targeting, and measurement. The big digital platforms with superior data ecosystems are the main beneficiaries.
EN_A: What’s the core question this report really tries to answer?
EN_B: It asks: within this fragmented landscape, which sub-sectors have real structural opportunities, and which ones only look good on the surface? The report makes a critical distinction between two very different advertising markets.
EN_A: Two different markets? How so?
EN_B: Market number one includes agencies and traditional media. It’s concentrated, cyclical, and relies on a few thousand big clients. Ad spend here is volatile and often the first to get cut in a downturn. Market number two is the digital platforms, driven by millions of small and medium businesses. It’s structurally more resilient and harder to predict from top-down indicators.
EN_A: So the risk-return profile is completely different between the two?
EN_B: Right. The data shows that SME-driven revenue accounts for roughly two-thirds of major platform ad revenue. This engine is often underappreciated by the market, and it explains why platform-led models have persistently outperformed.
EN_A: Can digital ad growth continue? It’s already such a big share.
EN_B: The report argues that growth has entered a second phase. The first phase was about onboarding SMEs with self-serve tools. The second phase, starting around 2023, is driven by inventory expansion and personalization at scale.
EN_A: What does that mean in practice?
EN_B: Think retail media, marketplaces, and streaming platforms. Retail media, in particular, offers high-margin, near-zero-cost inventory with strong targeting advantages. Platforms like Amazon and delivery ecosystems benefit a lot. AI acts as an accelerant here, improving targeting, creative generation, and conversion.
EN_A: Are traditional players doing anything to adapt?
EN_B: Yes. Broadcasters, out-of-home advertisers, and agencies are successfully replicating digital features. They’ve launched their own digital platforms, and those segments are growing faster. But the problem is, these businesses still represent a small part of their total revenue.
EN_A: So it doesn’t move the needle enough?
EN_B: Exactly. Core linear advertising continues to decline, and digital growth only partially offsets the gap. High client concentration and cyclical exposure still weigh on their investment appeal.
