EN_A: So we just got a fresh report on US luxury credit card spending, and May numbers actually went up again. Are high-end shoppers still spending?
EN_B: They are. Total luxury brand card spending grew 3% year-on-year in May. That is the fifth straight month of growth. It did slow a bit from April's 4%, but the real story is the two-year stacked number.
EN_A: Two-year stacked? Why does that matter more than the regular year-on-year?
EN_B: Because the base from last year was very different. If you only look at year-on-year, you might think the growth is just easy math. But the two-year stacked turned positive in May, from minus 3% in April to plus 2%. That tells you the momentum is real, not just a base effect.
EN_A: So is this broad-based, or just a few brands pulling up the average?
EN_B: It varies a lot by category. Watches and jewelry are the stars. Watches jumped 7% in May, jewelry accelerated to 6%. But leather goods and ready-to-wear were much weaker. Leather goods only grew 2%, and its two-year stack is still negative 7%.
EN_A: So what does that mean? Are people buying more hard luxury instead of handbags?
EN_B: Exactly. And it ties into pricing. Hard luxury brands have been raising prices more aggressively because of higher precious metal costs. And consumers are accepting it. That tells you high-end demand is still strong, especially among wealthy shoppers.
EN_A: What about lower-income consumers? The report mentions consumer sentiment is weakening.
EN_B: Yes, the Michigan survey has dropped for three months in a row, and the drop is sharper for lower-income groups. But luxury spending is mostly driven by the top tier. They are still supported by wealth effects from stocks and home prices.
EN_A: So the big picture is a K-shaped recovery. High end is resilient, mass market is struggling.
EN_B: That is the key takeaway. But the report also warns that if geopolitical tensions and inflation persist, real income growth turning negative and a savings rate that has already fallen to 2.6% could eventually hurt even high-end spending.
