ZH_A: 最近我读到一份某外资投行发布的全球支付行业年度预测报告，感觉很有意思。它讨论了一个核心问题：现金数字化还有增长空间吗？

ZH_B: 没错，我们刚更新了第12版全球支付预测模型。核心判断是：现金数字化仍有空间，但增长节奏在放缓。

ZH_A: 那这份报告真正要回答的问题是什么？是不是在说支付行业已经接近天花板了？

ZH_B: 其实不是。报告要回答的是：在卡支付渗透率已经很高的背景下，未来五年增长动力来自哪里。我们预测2025到2030年，全球卡交易量年增长率约为8%，略低于疫情后的9%。

ZH_A: 8%听起来还不错，但相比历史确实在减速。这意味着什么？

ZH_B: 这意味着增长从“跑着追”变成了“走着追”。过去，现金数字化每年贡献3个百分点的增长；未来可能只有2个百分点。主要原因是美国市场已经高度饱和，渗透率达到72%。

ZH_A: 那美国之外的市场呢？欧洲和亚洲的情况应该不一样吧？

ZH_B: 对，欧洲反而是一个亮点。我们预测欧洲卡交易量年增长可达11%，尤其在德国、意大利、西班牙这些国家。因为欧洲的现金使用比例还很高，比如德国POS交易中现金占比32%。

ZH_A: 32%？这比我想象的高很多。所以欧洲还有很大空间。

ZH_B: 确实。而且全球来看，仍有约11万亿美元的现金或支票支付未被数字化。这相当于一个巨大的“待转化池”。

ZH_A: 那亚洲呢？报告里说亚洲增速在放缓，是因为中国吗？

ZH_B: 部分原因是中国市场的特殊性。报告目前排除了中国大陆的数据，因为本地支付方式如支付宝和微信支付占据主导。而且亚太地区卡渗透率只有40%，但增长受限于本地支付竞争。

ZH_A: 我注意到报告还提到了一个很有趣的点：交易笔数增长比金额增长更快。

ZH_B: 对，这是个关键洞察。我们预测全球交易笔数年增长9%，高于金额的8%。因为Visa和Mastercard超过三分之一的收入来自交易笔数，这意味着即使金额增速放缓，收入仍可能保持两位数增长。

ZH_A: 原来如此。那像稳定币、账户对账户支付这些新方式，会不会颠覆卡支付？

ZH_B: 报告的观点是，这些方式更多是补充而非替代。比如在印度和巴西，本地支付很流行，但信用卡交易仍在以中高个位数增长。至于稳定币，报告认为在零售消费领域，它更像“一个在寻找问题的解决方案”。

ZH_A: 这个说法很形象。那报告有没有提到一些还没完全展开的悬念？

ZH_B: 有两个值得关注的点。第一是“代理商务”，也就是AI驱动的自主交易。报告认为这可能是未来增长的催化剂，但现在下结论为时过早。第二是跨境支付，报告说会单独分析。

EN_A: I just read a global payments forecast report from a major investment bank. It asks a key question: is there still room for cash digitization?

EN_B: Exactly. We just updated our 12th annual global payments model. The core view is: cash digitization still has runway, but the pace is slowing.

EN_A: So what is the real question this report is trying to answer? Is it about whether the payments industry is hitting a ceiling?

EN_B: Not exactly. The real question is: where will growth come from over the next five years, given that card penetration is already high? We forecast global card volume growth at around 8 percent annually from 2025 to 2030, slightly below the 9 percent post-pandemic rate.

EN_A: Eight percent sounds decent, but it is a slowdown. What does that mean for investors?

EN_B: It means growth is shifting from a sprint to a jog. In the past, cash-to-card conversion added about 3 percentage points per year. Going forward, it might be closer to 2 points. The main reason is that the U.S. market is already highly saturated, with card penetration at 72 percent.

EN_A: What about markets outside the U.S.? Europe and Asia must look different.

EN_B: Europe is actually a bright spot. We project card volume growth of around 11 percent annually there, especially in countries like Germany, Italy, and Spain. Cash still accounts for 32 percent of point-of-sale transactions in Germany.

EN_A: Thirty-two percent? That is higher than I expected. So there is plenty of room in Europe.

EN_B: Exactly. Globally, there is still about 11 trillion dollars in cash or check payments waiting to be digitized. That is a huge addressable pool.

EN_A: What about Asia? The report mentions a slowdown there. Is it because of China?

EN_B: Partly. The report excludes mainland China from its forecasts because local payment methods like Alipay and WeChat Pay dominate. In the rest of Asia-Pacific, card penetration is only around 40 percent, but growth is constrained by competition from local payment schemes.

EN_A: I noticed the report also highlights that transaction count growth is outpacing volume growth.

EN_B: That is a key insight. We forecast global transaction count growth at 9 percent, higher than the 8 percent for volume. Since Visa and Mastercard derive over a third of their revenue from transaction counts, this means revenue could still grow at double-digit rates even if volume growth slows.

In markets like India and Brazil, local payments are popular, but credit card volumes are still growing at high single to low double digits. As for stablecoins, the report says they are still "a solution looking for a problem" in retail consumer payments.
