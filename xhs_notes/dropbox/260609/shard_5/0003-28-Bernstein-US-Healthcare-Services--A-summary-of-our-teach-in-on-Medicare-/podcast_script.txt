ZH_A: 最近我们收到了一份关于美国医疗服务的深度报告，听说主要讲的是Medicare Advantage，也就是MA计划。这个领域最近好像波动很大，到底发生了什么？

ZH_B: 没错，这份报告的核心判断其实很简单——MA市场正在经历一个典型的保险周期。前几年竞争过度，导致利润率跌到了谷底，但报告认为，2026年开始利润率会触底回升。

ZH_A: 触底回升？那具体是怎么判断的？之前为什么会这么惨？

ZH_B: 报告解释得很清楚。在2005到2015年那段时间，MA的收入增长率高达15%到20%，利润率在5%左右。这么高的回报自然吸引了大量竞争对手涌入。结果就是市场变得拥挤，利润率被压缩。

ZH_A: 所以就是典型的“钱太好赚，大家一窝蜂进来，然后大家一起赚不到钱”？

ZH_B: 对，就是这个逻辑。而且更糟的是，后来还出现了所谓的“费率冲击”，就是政府给的费率调整跟不上实际的医疗成本上涨。这直接导致了2023、2024、2025年MA板块表现很差。

ZH_A: 那报告里说的“触底回升”具体是什么时候？回升的幅度有多大？

ZH_B: 报告预测，2026年行业利润率会扩张大约1个百分点，然后从2027年到2030年，每年再增长大约0.5个百分点。推动力有三个：一是竞争对手开始退出，二是费率从2024、2025年的低点改善，三是整个行业开始更注重定价对利润的影响。

ZH_A: 听起来很乐观。但长期来看，MA这个市场会变成什么样？报告有没有给一个终局判断？

ZH_B: 有。报告认为，MA最终会变成一个类似“公用事业”的市场。什么意思呢？就是市场会整合到大概4到5家大型玩家手里，利润率变得很薄——Medicaid大概1.5%，MA大概2%到3%。增长还是会有的，但不再是高增长、高利润的黄金时代了。

ZH_A: 那现在哪些公司在里面占主导地位？报告有没有点名？

ZH_B: 报告提到，UnitedHealth和Humana一直是前两名，CVS是稳定的第三名。这三家加起来大概占市场份额的55%。但报告强调，市场现在还是比较分散的，长期才会走向整合。

ZH_A: 那除了利润率，报告还提到了哪些重要的趋势？比如新技术或者政策变化？

ZH_B: 报告指出了三个关键的“颠覆因素”。第一个是GLP-1类药物，就是那些减肥药。它们会增加药费成本，但另一方面，如果它们真的能延长寿命，那Medicare的总市场可能会扩大5%到10%。第二个是人工智能，可以帮助医院提高效率。第三个是价值导向的医疗，就是按效果付费的模式，会变得越来越重要。

ZH_A: 听起来GLP-1的影响很复杂，一边是成本，一边是市场扩大。那政策方面呢？报告怎么看？

ZH_B: 政策方面，报告认为“全民医保”这种激进方案现在不太可能了。因为MA已经覆盖了大约55%的Medicare参保人，而过去只有三分之一。所以政策焦点会转向“可负担性”和“扩大覆盖”。报告甚至预测，如果政策不干预，到2028年无保险率可能会从现在的6%左右上升到9%。

ZH_A: 9%？那民主党肯定会出手干预吧？报告有没有说具体怎么做？

ZH_B: 报告提到，民主党可能会推动恢复无保险率到5%左右。但具体用什么工具、怎么实现，这份报告并没有完全展开。这其实是一个悬念，完整报告里应该会有更深入的分析。

ZH_A: 有意思。那关于MA的利润率回升，报告有没有什么风险提示？比如如果医疗成本继续超预期上涨怎么办？

ZH_B: 这也是一个值得追问的点。报告认为长期来看，报销费率会落后于医疗成本趋势大约100到150个基点。这意味着利润率回升的幅度可能有限。但具体哪些公司能扛住、哪些会掉队，报告里可能还有更细致的分析。

ZH_B: 没错，完整报告里还有详细的股票评级表和财务预测，值得仔细看。

EN_A: So we got this deep dive into U.S. healthcare services, specifically Medicare Advantage. It sounds like there's been a lot of turbulence in this space. What's the big picture?

EN_B: The report's main argument is pretty straightforward. MA is going through a classic insurance cycle. There was too much competition, margins got crushed, but the report thinks margins will start to recover in 2026.

EN_A: Recover from what exactly? What caused the mess in the first place?

EN_B: Between 2005 and 2015, MA was a goldmine. Revenue was growing 15 to 20 percent a year, and margins were around five percent. That attracted a ton of new players. The market got crowded, and then things got worse when the government's rate adjustments didn't keep up with rising medical costs.

EN_A: So it's the classic story of too many people chasing the same dollar, and then the dollar got smaller.

EN_B: Exactly. And that mismatch between rates and costs really hurt in 2023, 2024, and 2025. That's when the industry really underperformed.

EN_A: So when does the recovery start? And how big is it?

EN_B: The report sees margins expanding by about one percentage point in 2026, and then about half a point each year after that through 2030. The drivers are threefold. Competitors are pulling back. Rate improvements are coming off the 2024-2025 lows. And the industry is finally focusing on pricing for profit, not just market share.

EN_A: That sounds pretty optimistic. But what's the long-term picture? Does the report have a final destination in mind?

EN_B: Yes. It sees MA becoming a utility-like market. That means it consolidates to four or five big players, and margins become very thin. Think maybe 1.5 percent for Medicaid and 2 to 3 percent for MA. Growth will still be there, but it won't be the high-growth, high-margin party it used to be.

EN_A: Who are the big players now? Does the report name names?

EN_B: UnitedHealth and Humana have been the top two for a decade. CVS is a consistent number three. Together they have about 55 percent of the market. But the report notes it's still relatively fragmented. The consolidation is a long-term view.

EN_A: Besides margins, what are the other big themes in the report? Any tech or policy shifts?

EN_B: Three key disruptors. First, GLP-1 drugs, the weight loss drugs. They add pharmacy costs, but if they really extend lifespan, they could expand the total Medicare market by 5 to 10 percent. Second, AI, which can improve hospital efficiency. Third, value-based care, which is paying for outcomes, is going to become more central as margin pressure persists.

EN_A: GLP-1s sound like a double-edged sword. Cost up, but market size up too. What about policy? What does the report see there?

EN_B: The report thinks Medicare for All is less likely now because MA already covers about 55 percent of Medicare beneficiaries, compared to just a third in earlier reform debates. So the policy focus is shifting to affordability and coverage expansion. The report even warns that without intervention, the uninsured rate could drift from around 6 percent today to potentially 9 percent by 2028.

EN_A: Nine percent? That's a big jump. Would Democrats try to stop that? Does the report say how?

EN_B: It mentions a Democratic push to restore the uninsured rate to around 5 percent. But the report doesn't fully lay out the specific policy tools. That's one of the things you'd need to read the full report for. It's a bit of a cliffhanger.

EN_A: And on the margin recovery, are there any risks? What if medical costs keep rising faster than expected?

EN_B: That's another good question. The report thinks reimbursement rates will lag medical cost trends by about 100 to 150 basis points in the long run. So the margin recovery might have a ceiling. Which companies can handle that and which can't? That's probably in the full report too.
