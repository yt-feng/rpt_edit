## China Financials | Asia Pacific

# New Consultation Paper on RMB Deposit and Loan Interest Rate Management Rules to Support Fair and Market-oriented Pricing

## Key Takeaways

On June 5, PBOC issued draft RMB Deposit and Loan Interest Rate Management Rules, indicating more market-based pricing and fair competition.  
It clarified responsibilities of financial institutions and PBOC; FIs may independently set deposit/loan rates based on PBOC rules and commercial principles...  
...while PBOC and its branches should strengthen supervision and guide the market interest rate self-regulatory mechanism.  
Banks are explicitly prohibited from high-interest deposit solicitation, as it could lead to disorder in deposit market competition.  
Lenders must clearly display annualized loan rates (where loan cost should include interest and fees directly related to loans) and penalty annualized rates.

An update to the 1999 framework to reflect comprehensive management system for loan and deposit rates: The rules were drafted to adapt market-oriented interest rate reform and consolidate various rules on interest calculation, settlement and institutional responsibilities. The rules standardize interest rate calculation, change penalty-rate add-ons to negotiated penalty rates, and remove management on interest rates other than loans and deposits.

More importantly, this sets up a clear rule book for market-based pricing with a clear message to combat unfair/illegal competition:

- Loan cost should include interest and fees directly related to loans.  
- High-interest deposit solicitation is clearly banned, including illegal manual interest supplements, breaching self-regulatory caps and deposit-loan linkage.

We view this positively for bank NIM trends on both the asset yield and funding cost sides, as it should support continued revenue recovery and stable profit growth at Chinese banks.
