ZH_A: 今天我们要聊的这份报告，来自某外资投行，讲的是美国6月份的汽车销售数据。先问个直接的问题，这个月的数据到底怎么样，是好是坏？
ZH_B: 整体来说，是超预期的。6月美国轻型车年化销售速率，也就是SAAR，落在1650万到1670万辆之间，明显高于市场预期的1630万辆。
ZH_A: 1650万到1670万，这个数字意味着什么？是回暖的信号吗？
ZH_B: 对，这确实是个积极的信号。因为5月份的SAAR只有1610万，去年6月是1580万，所以环比和同比都在改善。这说明汽车需求依然比较坚挺，没有像有些人担心的那样大幅下滑。
ZH_A: 那具体是哪些车型在拉动增长？消费者现在更偏爱什么？
ZH_B: 6月份，轿车、皮卡和SUV的销量同比都是增长的，其中SUV表现最突出，销量同比增长了8%，市场份额也略微提升到了57.4%。皮卡的市场份额反而略有下降，从20%降到了19.4%。
ZH_A: 看来SUV依然是美国消费者的心头好。那几家大车厂，比如通用和福特，表现怎么样？
ZH_B: 表现分化很明显。通用汽车6月销量同比增长了6%，但福特却下滑了2%。在大型皮卡这个关键市场，通用的份额提升到了40.3%，而福特的份额从35.1%降到了33.1%。
ZH_A: 福特皮卡份额下滑，是不是跟去年那个知名工厂火灾有关？
ZH_B: 对，就是那个Novelis工厂火灾，导致福特F系列减产了约10万辆。虽然福特计划在2026年追回5到6万辆的产量，但影响还在。所以福特皮卡的丢失的份额，很大一部分被通用和Stellantis抢走了。
ZH_A: 那新能源车这边呢？电动车和混动车的表现应该也很受关注吧？
ZH_B: 情况很有意思。6月份纯电动车销量同比大跌了30%，市场份额从去年的8%降到了5%。但混动车销量却暴增了43%。这是一个非常鲜明的对比。
ZH_A: 电动车销量跌了30%，这听起来有点吓人。是因为特斯拉的数据不好吗？
ZH_B: 特斯拉确实是主要原因之一。报告估算，特斯拉整体销量同比下降了27%，Model 3和Model S/X的降幅更大。但需要注意，特斯拉不公布美国月度数据，所以电动车这块的数据估算成分比较大，需要谨慎看待。
ZH_A: 那其他品牌的电动车呢？比如通用和福特？
ZH_B: 同样不乐观。通用的凯迪拉克Lyriq销量跌了21%，雪佛兰Equinox EV更是大跌82%。福特的F-150 Lightning电动皮卡销量也暴跌了78%。可以说，除了少数车型，整个纯电市场6月份都面临很大压力。
ZH_A: 混动车增长43%，这个数字很亮眼。这是不是说明消费者在“退而求其次”，从纯电转向了混动？
ZH_B: 这是一个很合理的解读。在消费者对充电设施、续航仍有顾虑，且电动车价格相对较高的情况下，混动车提供了一个更务实的过渡方案。这个趋势值得继续观察。
ZH_A: 那我们再看一下库存和价格。报告里提到，经销商的优惠力度和库存水平怎么样？
ZH_B: 6月份单车激励支出同比微增2%，环比还下降了1%，平均约3464美元。这说明车厂并没有因为销量好就大幅降价促销。库存方面，总库存约272万辆，环比有所增加，但仍然低于历史正常的300万辆水平。库存天数也从5月的47天微增到49天。
ZH_A: 库存低于正常水平，激励支出也没怎么增加，这其实说明市场供需还是比较紧的，对吗？
ZH_B: 没错。虽然整体消费者信心偏软，但汽车需求依然有韧性，车厂在定价上也比较有底气。这跟报告里提到的“领先指标分析”方向一致，比如消费者调查和谷歌搜索流量都显示需求在改善。
ZH_A: 好，那最后问一个宏观的问题。整个二季度的情况怎么样？对于全年，这份报告有什么判断？
ZH_B: 二季度整体销量同比增长了1%，环比增长了15%。但通用和福特的季度销量都是同比下降的，分别降了4%和10%。对于全年，分析师认为他们之前给出的1600万辆的预测依然合理，因为上半年的平均SAAR是1590万辆，基本吻合。
ZH_A: 所以报告的核心判断是，需求比预期的要好，但电动车是个明显的短板。那在报告最后，有没有提到接下来需要重点关注什么？
ZH_B: 有，报告指出了几个关键主题。第一是成本与价格，特别是内存和原材料成本的波动。第二是关税问题，尤其是USMCA的不确定性。第三是新车周期，比如特斯拉的Y L、Rivian的R2，以及通用新一代全尺寸皮卡和福特F系列的复苏。这些具体影响，在完整报告里都有更深入的分析。

EN_A: So, we just got a fresh report from a global investment bank on June U.S. auto sales. Let me start with the obvious question: was it a good month or a bad month?
EN_B: It was actually better than expected. The seasonally adjusted annualized rate, or SAAR, came in between 16.5 and 16.7 million units, well above the StreetAccount consensus of 16.3 million.
EN_A: 16.5 to 16.7 million. What does that tell us? Is the market heating up?
EN_B: It’s a positive sign. To put it in context, May’s SAAR was 16.1 million, and last June it was 15.8 million. So we’re seeing improvement both month-over-month and year-over-year. It suggests auto demand is holding up pretty well, despite some broader economic worries.
EN_A: What’s driving that demand? Are people buying trucks, SUVs, or cars?
EN_B: All three segments saw year-over-year growth, but SUVs were the clear winner. SUV sales jumped 8% year-over-year, and their market share inched up to 57.4%. Pickup trucks, on the other hand, lost a bit of ground, with their share dropping from 20% to 19.4%.
EN_A: So SUVs are still king. How about the big automakers? How did GM and Ford do?
EN_B: It was a tale of two companies. GM’s sales were up 6% year-over-year, while Ford’s were down 2%. And in the critical large pickup segment, GM actually gained share, moving up to 40.3%. Ford’s share, on the other hand, fell from 35.1% to 33.1%.
EN_A: Is that Ford pickup decline still tied to that factory fire from last year?
EN_B: Exactly. The Novelis fire cost Ford about 100,000 units of F-Series production in 2025. They plan to recover about half of that this year, but the impact is still lingering. GM and Stellantis have been the main beneficiaries.
EN_A: Let’s talk about the green side. How did EVs and hybrids perform?
EN_B: This is where it gets really interesting. Pure battery EV sales plummeted 30% year-over-year, and their market share dropped from 8% to just 5%. But hybrids? They surged 43%. It’s a stark contrast.
EN_A: A 30% drop in EV sales sounds brutal. Is that all because of Tesla?
EN_B: Tesla is a big part of it. The report estimates Tesla’s overall sales were down 27%. Model 3 sales fell 40%, and the older Model S and X are basically winding down. But here’s the catch: Tesla doesn’t report monthly U.S. sales, so the EV data involves a lot of estimation. We should take those numbers with a grain of salt.
EN_A: What about other EV makers like GM and Ford?
EN_B: Not great either. GM’s Lyriq was down 21%, and the Equinox EV dropped a staggering 82%. Ford’s F-150 Lightning saw a 78% decline. It was a tough month across the board for pure EVs, with very few exceptions.
EN_A: But hybrid sales up 43%. Is that a signal that consumers are shifting from full electric to hybrids as a more practical choice?
EN_B: That’s a very fair interpretation. Given lingering concerns about charging infrastructure and higher EV prices, hybrids offer a pragmatic middle ground. It’s a trend we’ll definitely want to keep an eye on.
EN_A: Let’s shift to pricing and inventory. Are dealers offering big discounts?
EN_B: Not really. Incentive spending per vehicle was up just 2% year-over-year and actually down 1% from May, averaging around $3,464. So manufacturers aren’t aggressively cutting prices. On inventory, total stock rose slightly to about 2.72 million units, but that’s still below the historical normal of over 3 million. Days of inventory ticked up to 49 from 47.
EN_A: So low inventory and stable incentives. That sounds like a market where supply is still fairly tight?
EN_B: Exactly. Despite soft consumer sentiment in some surveys, auto demand has remained resilient, and OEMs have been able to hold the line on pricing. This aligns with the report’s own leading indicators, like consumer surveys and Google search traffic, which have been pointing to improving demand.
EN_A: How did the whole second quarter shape up? And what’s the outlook for the full year?
EN_B: For the second quarter, total sales were up 1% year-over-year and up 15% from the first quarter. But both GM and Ford saw their quarterly sales decline, by 4% and 10% respectively. For the full year, the report’s analyst thinks their 16 million unit forecast is still on track, since the year-to-date SAAR average is 15.9 million.
