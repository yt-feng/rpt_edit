## Banks: Credit Deceleration while New Policies Enhance Balance Sheet Defensiveness

The PBOC summarized the transformation of China's financing structure and announced a series of new policies at the Lujiazui Forum, including the refinement of short-term interest rate regulation mechanisms, the piloting of offshore RMB FX trading in the Shanghai Free Trade Zone, and the study of establishing a macro-prudential tool for non-bank liquidity support in specific scenarios. Against the backdrop of slowing credit growth, we anticipate these new regulatory tools will enhance the defensiveness of banks' balance sheets.

For stock selection, we reiterate our Buy ratings on 1) CCB and BOC for their larger and stronger balance sheets, and 2) BONB, underpinned by its high-quality growth (see here) and its position as a primary beneficiary of these new policies, particularly given its higher proportion of interbank liabilities and trading account assets.

The specific implications for banks are as follows:

## 1. Increased Direct Financing Proportion and Slower, Higher-Quality Loan Growth as the New Normal

According to the PBOC, China's financing structure is undergoing a significant transformation. Indirect financing, predominantly loans, has seen its share in Total Social Financing (TSF) increments decline from over 80% to 45% by 2025. Similarly, its proportion in outstanding TSF has decreased from nearly 100% to 2/3 by 2025. Furthermore, a notable divergence in loan allocation is evident: new loans to the property and infrastructure sectors have fallen from over 60% to just 10% currently, while new loans to the “Five Key Areas” of finance—technology finance, green finance, inclusive finance, elderly care finance, and digital finance—have surged to over 70%.

The PBOC believes that as the economic structure upgrades from asset-heavy traditional industries reliant on medium-to-long-term loan financing to asset-light emerging industries that depend on more diversified financing methods, maintaining past credit growth rates is both challenging and unnecessary. The central bank emphasizes that enhancing the quality and efficiency of loans is paramount, suggesting that “slower, higher-quality loan growth” is likely to become a new normal for the economy.

This assessment aligns with our recent field trip takeaways and the TSF data trends, further reinforcing our view that credit growth will continue to decelerate, while bond investments will maintain relatively faster growth. In an environment characterized by a slower pace of balance sheet expansion, we believe the intrinsic quality and defensiveness of banks' balance sheets will become increasingly critical.

## 2. Improved Short-Term Interest Rate Regulation Mechanism to Stabilize Interbank Funding Costs

The PBOC has announced an adjustment to its operating rate, setting it at the 7-day reverse repurchase operation rate plus or minus 25bps, a change from the previous plus 50bps/minus 20bps. This effectively narrows the interest rate corridor from 70bps to 50bps, establishing a range of 1.15-1.65% based on the current 1.4% policy rate.

We anticipate that this reduction in the short-term interest rate ceiling and the narrowing of the corridor will help mitigate short-term rate volatility, thereby stabilizing banks' interbank funding costs and thus NIM, particularly during periods of tighter liquidity such as quarter-ends and tax seasons. Given that small and medium-sized banks typically have weaker deposit bases and a higher proportion of interbank funding in their liability structures compared to larger banks, they are expected to benefit more significantly from this policy. For example, among the banks we cover, Industrial Bank, Huaxia Bank, and BONB exhibit the highest interbank liability proportion, recorded at 29%, 26%, and 22% respectively in 2025, in contrast to 15% for the large four SOE banks.

## 3. Offshore RMB FX Trading Pilot to Expand International Business for Participating Banks, but with Limited Earnings Contribution

The PBOC has authorized six banks—ICBC, ABC, BOC, CCB, BoCom, and CITIC Bank—to conduct offshore RMB FX trading in the Shanghai Free Trade Zone, utilizing the China Foreign Exchange Trade System (CFETS) platform. This initiative aims to promote the two-way opening of the foreign exchange market.

This development implies that pilot banks will be able to meet the FX hedging needs of overseas investors onshore (in Shanghai). This capability is expected to facilitate the expansion of their international business, generate new fee income growth points, attract new clients, and enhance client stickiness. However, cross-referencing data from the top international business player like BOC reveals that its entire international settlement business contributes less than 7% to its fee income and less than 1% to its total revenue. Therefore, we believe this new business will not have a significant financial

impact on banks in the short term.

## 4. Macro-Prudential Tool for Non-Bank FIs Liquidity Support to Stabilize Bond Market and Investment Income

The PBOC will study the establishment of a macro-prudential tool designed to provide liquidity support to non-bank financial institutions in specific scenarios. This tool would offer emergency liquidity via swaps when systemic pressure arises in bond or other markets, normal liquidity channels are obstructed, and a collective liquidity crisis among institutions could potentially trigger systemic risks. The PBOC explicitly states that this is not intended as a normalized liquidity provision; moreover, non-bank FIs will be required to furnish high-quality collateral to prevent moral hazard within the financial market.

Under normal circumstances, non-bank FIs primarily source their liquidity from banks. Consequently, when systemic pressure emerges in the bond market (e.g., the wealth management product redemption wave in late 2022) and banks restrict lending, non-bank FIs are often compelled to sell highly liquid assets, exacerbating market volatility and net asset value (NAV) declines. Therefore, the PBOC's provision of emergency liquidity through swaps is expected to help stabilize the bond market, which in turn will contribute to the stability of banks' bond investment income, particularly for their FVTPL and FVTOCI accounts. Among the banks we cover, BONB, BONJ, and BOC exhibit the highest proportions of trading accounts within their investment assets, recorded at 67%, 67%, and 55% respectively.
