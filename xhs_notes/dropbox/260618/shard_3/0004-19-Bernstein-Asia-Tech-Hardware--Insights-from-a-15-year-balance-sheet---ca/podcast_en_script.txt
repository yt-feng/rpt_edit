EN_A: So today we are digging into a report that spent a lot of time looking at the balance sheets and cash flows of Asian tech hardware companies over the past 15 years. What do you think is the single biggest question this report is trying to answer?
EN_B: I think it comes down to one thing: how much is AI demand actually changing the profitability of these hardware companies? The report uses a key metric called ROE, return on equity. And what it finds is that companies with high AI exposure are seeing a very sharp upcycle in ROE, while consumer electronics names are still climbing much more slowly.
EN_A: So that means AI is really redrawing the line between winners and losers in this sector?
EN_B: Exactly. Take Chroma, which makes testing equipment. The report estimates its ROE could hit over 30% by 2026, and maybe even higher. On the other hand, consumer-focused names like Luxshare and Sunny Optical are only expected to see their ROE gradually improve to the mid-teens or low twenties. That gap really reflects how much AI revenue they have on their books.
EN_A: Besides ROE, what other tools did the report use to break down the competitiveness of these companies?
EN_B: It used something called DuPont analysis, which breaks ROE into three parts: profit margin, asset turnover, and leverage. That gives a much clearer picture. For example, Largan has a very high net profit margin, close to 40%, but its asset turnover is very low because it holds a lot of cash and has heavy assets. So its ROE is not as high as you might expect.
EN_A: So high margins do not always mean high returns?
EN_B: Right. And the opposite is true too. Companies like Luxshare and Quanta, which are ODM manufacturers, have very low margins, but they make up for it with high asset turnover and leverage. So their ROE actually looks pretty good. This shows that in the upstream equipment space, you make money through technology and niche markets, but in the ODM space, it is all about operational efficiency and scale.
EN_A: How is the overall financial health of the industry? Are there any red flags?
EN_B: Overall, balance sheets are still pretty strong. Most companies are net cash, and debt-to-equity ratios are stable or declining. But there is one exception: Quanta. Its leverage is rising because it is expanding its AI server business. Also, in terms of capital expenditure, Unimicron and Largan are spending a lot, one because of strong PCB demand, and the other because Apple has very strict requirements for lens manufacturing equipment.
EN_A: The report also updated its models for some specific companies, right? Like Chroma and Sunny Optical?
EN_B: Yes. The report is very bullish on Chroma. For Sunny Optical, the report is optimistic about it gaining more iPhone camera module share, but it also points out that memory cost inflation will be a headwind in 2026.
