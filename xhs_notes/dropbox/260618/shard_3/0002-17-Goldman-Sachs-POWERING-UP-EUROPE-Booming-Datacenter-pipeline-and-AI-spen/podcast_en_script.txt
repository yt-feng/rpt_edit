A year ago, it was only 170. Some of these requests may be speculative, but the sheer scale and speed of growth strongly signal a major construction phase for data centers across the continent.
EN_A: So what is the core question this report is trying to answer? Is it just about how much power data centers will consume?
EN_B: Not exactly. The report’s central thesis is bigger. It argues that the boom in data centers and AI spending will fundamentally reshape Europe’s power system and its utility companies. It’s not just about “we need more power.” It’s about utilities becoming the pivotal players in this trillion-dollar build-out. They will provide the energy, scout for land, secure grid connections, and reinforce the existing power grids.
EN_A: That leads to the first big insight. How large is the data center demand, really? Does the report have a concrete forecast?
EN_B: It does. The European Data Center Association expects 20 gigawatts of new capacity by 2031, tripling the total to about 35 gigawatts. That would represent roughly 10% of Europe’s total power demand by then. The report calculates that starting around 2028 to 2029, data centers alone could drive about 1.5% annual growth in European power consumption. But that is the base case.
EN_A: You said “base case.” Is there a more aggressive scenario?
EN_B: Yes, and that is the second key insight: agentic AI. The report highlights that a single agentic AI query can consume 6 to 13 times more energy than a traditional AI chatbot, and potentially up to 50 times more. If the adoption rate of agentic AI in Europe increases by just 10 percentage points, it could boost data center needs by another 25 to 30%. In an upside scenario, Europe could have 80 gigawatts of data center capacity by 2035, accounting for a quarter of total power demand.
EN_A: A quarter of all power? That is huge. So what does this mean for the earnings of utility companies?
EN_B: That brings us to the third insight: what the report calls a once-in-a-generation earnings super-cycle. In a “hyper-electrification” scenario, European power demand could grow as fast as 5% per year by 2029 to 2030. That would require 3.5 trillion euros in investment, mostly in renewables and grids. The report estimates that its key “electrification compounders” could see earnings per share grow at a compound annual rate of around 9% in the base case, and well above 10% in the hyper-electrification scenario.
EN_A: That sounds very optimistic. But are there any open questions the report does not fully answer?
EN_B: Definitely. For example, how much of that 480-gigawatt pipeline is actually real? The report acknowledges it may be speculative, but it doesn’t provide a conversion model. Another big question is how quickly the energy efficiency of agentic AI will improve. The report assumes a conservative energy use per query, but if efficiency improves much faster, the entire demand model would need to be revised.
