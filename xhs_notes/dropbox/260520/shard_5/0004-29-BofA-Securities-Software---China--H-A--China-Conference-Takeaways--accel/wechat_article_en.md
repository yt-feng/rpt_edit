# AI Monetization in China's Software Sector Is Accelerating, But Growth Visibility Is Diverging Sharply Across Sub-Segments

The narrative around artificial intelligence in China's enterprise software market has shifted from speculative promise to measurable revenue contribution. Based on investor meetings with management teams from over ten listed software companies at a major China conference in Shenzhen during mid-May 2026, a clear pattern emerges: AI monetization is no longer theoretical. Token-based pricing models are lifting average revenue per user, and the transition from Software-as-a-Service to Results-as-a-Service is fundamentally reshaping how value is captured. Yet beneath this accelerating trend lies a critical divergence. Enterprise resource planning platforms and photo editing and design tools are demonstrating superior growth visibility, supported by rising AI revenue contributions and high recurring revenue shares. Meanwhile, demand for IT outsourcing, cybersecurity, and property software remains conspicuously soft. This is not a uniform recovery. It is a selective acceleration that rewards companies with clear AI product roadmaps, sticky customer relationships, and disciplined cost structures.

The timing matters because the market is at an inflection point. Several companies are guiding for improved cash flow and profitability in fiscal year 2026, driven by disciplined headcount expansion and rising operating efficiency from AI adoption. The question for investors is no longer whether AI will generate revenue, but which business models will sustain the momentum and which will face structural headwinds. The answer lies in understanding the shifting architecture of software pricing, the diverging demand profiles across end markets, and the strategic implications of a multi-model AI environment that lowers switching costs and intensifies competition among large language model vendors.

## Token-Based Pricing Is Reshaping Software Economics, But Not Every Company Benefits Equally

The most consequential shift emerging from the conference discussions is the move from traditional seat-based licensing toward usage-based and token-based pricing models. This is not a minor adjustment in billing methodology. It represents a fundamental redefinition of the value proposition. Under the old SaaS model, revenue was predictable but capped by headcount. Under token-based models, revenue scales with actual consumption, creating upside potential that is directly tied to user engagement and AI compute usage. One company reported that its token-based annual recurring revenue grew from RMB 60 million in January 2026 to over RMB 200 million by April 2026, and it now expects token-based revenue to account for 20 percent of total revenue in fiscal year 2026. Another firm guided that AI-native product revenue will exceed RMB 1 billion in the same period.

The so-what here is profound. Token-based pricing decouples revenue growth from headcount growth, which is why profitability is improving even as companies invest heavily in AI capabilities. However, this model also introduces new risks. It requires sustained user engagement and continuous value delivery. If the AI features fail to drive repeat usage, token consumption stalls, and revenue becomes lumpy. Furthermore, companies that rely heavily on third-party large language model APIs face margin compression if they cannot shift high-usage functions to self-developed or fine-tuned vertical models. The winners in this transition are those that control their own model stack and can manage the cost of inference at scale. The losers are those that remain dependent on external API providers without a clear path to internalization.

## ERP and Creative Tools Exhibit Strong Growth Visibility, While IT Outsourcing and Cybersecurity Lag

The divergence in demand trends across software sub-segments is stark and instructive. Enterprise resource planning platforms and photo editing and design tools are showing robust growth, underpinned by rising AI revenue contributions and a higher share of recurring revenue. One ERP provider reported accelerated revenue growth in the first quarter of 2026 compared to the prior year, with strong operating cash flow and profitability improvement. Its net dollar retention rate for large enterprises, which had declined, is expected to recover by the fourth quarter of 2026. The company is also reducing headcount by 6 percent year-to-date, targeting fewer than 10,000 employees by fiscal year 2028, which suggests a deliberate strategy to improve operating leverage. For creative tools, management expressed confidence in maintaining gross profit margins around 70 percent in the long term, despite rising AI credit consumption, because the company is shifting high-usage functions from third-party APIs to its own fine-tuned models.

In contrast, IT outsourcing, cybersecurity, and property software demand remains soft. One IT outsourcing company expects only low-single-digit revenue growth in fiscal year 2026, as rapid growth in AI products and services is partly offset by declines in traditional outsourcing due to weak downstream demand. It is reducing headcount by approximately 1,000 employees per month. A cybersecurity firm targets faster revenue growth and operating cash flow breakeven in fiscal year 2026, but it acknowledges that large enterprises in critical infrastructure sectors have already set their 2026 IT budgets, leaving limited incremental spending for AI-related cybersecurity products until 2027. The property software company continues to face pressure from weak real estate sales, although the pace of decline is narrowing, and its profitability improvement is driven by cost control and AI efficiency gains rather than top-line expansion.

The pattern is clear. Companies serving mission-critical enterprise functions with high switching costs and clear AI use cases are benefiting from accelerated spending. Companies exposed to discretionary IT budgets or cyclical end markets are struggling to translate AI investment into revenue growth.

## The Rise of Agentic AI and Multi-Model Strategies Is Reshaping Competitive Dynamics

An expert meeting with a founder of an AI technology company provided additional strategic context. Agentic AI capabilities, particularly in coding, are improving rapidly, significantly enhancing developer productivity and driving a structural decline in software development costs. This has two major implications. First, it lowers the barrier to entry for new software solutions, enabling small, agile teams to compete with established vendors. Second, it pressures mid-sized software companies that lack the scale to invest in proprietary AI models or the agility to adapt quickly. The industry structure is evolving toward a barbell shape, with large software vendors and small, nimble teams emerging as beneficiaries, while mid-tier firms face increasing pressure for business transformation.

Furthermore, the rising adoption of multi-model strategies is lowering switching costs and intensifying competition across large language model vendors. Companies are no longer locked into a single model provider. They can mix and match models based on cost, performance, and task-specific requirements. This is good for enterprises because it drives down API costs and improves model quality through competition. But it is challenging for software vendors that have built their value proposition on a proprietary model. The ability to integrate multiple models seamlessly and manage inference costs efficiently becomes a competitive differentiator. Companies that can abstract away the complexity of multi-model orchestration while delivering consistent user experiences will capture disproportionate value.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Sustainability of Token-Based Revenue and the Risk of AI Commoditization

Despite the optimism around token-based pricing and AI monetization, several critical questions remain unresolved. First, the sustainability of token-based revenue growth is unproven over a full economic cycle. If user engagement plateaus or if AI features fail to demonstrate incremental business value, token consumption could decelerate, leading to revenue disappointment. The report does not provide data on token churn rates or the elasticity of demand with respect to pricing changes. Second, the risk of AI commoditization is real. As multi-model strategies become standard and as open-source models improve, the differentiation between software vendors may narrow. If every ERP system or creative tool offers similar AI capabilities, pricing power erodes, and margins compress. The report highlights that one company expects AI-related cybersecurity budgets to increase only in 2027, suggesting that enterprise adoption cycles may be longer than the market anticipates. Third, the impact of potential regulatory changes on AI data usage and model deployment is not addressed. China's regulatory environment for AI is evolving, and any tightening could disrupt the monetization trajectories outlined in the report.

These open questions do not invalidate the bullish thesis, but they underscore the importance of selectivity. Investors need to differentiate between companies that are building durable competitive advantages through proprietary data, vertical models, and deep customer relationships, and those that are riding a temporary wave of AI enthusiasm.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: Three Filters to Evaluate AI Software Opportunities

Based on the insights from the report, investors should apply three filters when evaluating Chinese software companies exposed to AI monetization.

First, assess the pricing model transition. Companies that are moving from seat-based to token-based or usage-based pricing should demonstrate not just revenue growth but also improving unit economics. Examine whether gross margins are stable or improving as AI usage scales. If a company relies heavily on third-party API costs, ask whether it has a credible plan to internalize high-usage functions. The ability to control inference costs is a leading indicator of sustainable margin expansion.

Second, evaluate end-market exposure. Companies serving large enterprises in critical infrastructure sectors, such as energy, finance, and telecommunications, are likely to have more predictable demand and higher willingness to pay for AI features. Companies exposed to government-related clients, property development, or discretionary IT budgets face greater uncertainty. The report clearly shows that state-owned enterprises and large private enterprises are driving demand, while government-related clients have yet to recover. Align investment decisions with this demand gradient.

Third, analyze the competitive positioning within the AI value chain. Companies that own proprietary data sets, have built vertical-specific models, or offer integrated multi-model orchestration are better positioned to defend their margins than companies that are essentially resellers of generic AI capabilities. The expert meeting highlighted that large software vendors and small agile teams are the likely winners, while mid-sized firms face transformation pressure. Identify where a company sits on this barbell.
