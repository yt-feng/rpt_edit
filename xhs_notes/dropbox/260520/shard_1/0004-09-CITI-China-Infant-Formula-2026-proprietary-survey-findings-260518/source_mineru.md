# Citi China Infant Formula Survey

Sam Teeger (a2), Tiffany Feng (China Feihe, H&H), Joanne Wuensch (Abbott), Cedric Besnard (Danone, Nestle), conducted a survey of 1,200 Chinese consumers, with the support of Citi's Research Innovation Lab, which helped coordinate the survey and gather the data we leveraged to develop insights into trends in China.

# Potential stock implications from our survey

# a2 Milk (covered by Sam Teeger)

The survey findings provide deeper insight from a consumer perspective into competitor stock levels following well documented recalls/shortages. However, a2 was not able to maximise this potential opportunity due to its own supply issues, and according to our survey, consumers are switching to domestic brands. The survey also identifies an improving consumer perception of domestic infant formula milk quality, particularly those that are manufactured in China. The survey suggests the best way for a2 to mitigate this risk is to ensure it does not under-invest in the science behind its products with nutritional value/scientific benefit being the primary driver influencing consumer choice and HMOs being very important (hence the Genesis shortage is particularly unfortunate). The survey also provides support that a2 is partially decoupling from the birth rate through several factors including premiumization, category expansion, diversifying to products for older consumers and English label relative outperformance. However, the survey does caution against assuming marriages will translate to more births in the near term. The next two zodiacs Horse (2026) and Goat (2027) appear to be incrementally positive from a birth rate perspective.

We recently downgraded a2 Milk to Sell as we see risk that supply chain constraints could persist for longer than expected, and even when they are resolved it may not be easy or cheap for a2 to win back customers who have switched to competitor brands.

# China Feihe (covered by Tiffany Feng)

Feihe has successfully solidified its leadership as the No.1 domestic IMF brand through its "most suitable for Chinese babies" positioning and a strong super-premium product mix (68% of sales in 2025). The company benefits from premiumization, declining breastfeeding rates, HMO innovation (being among the first domestic brands to launch HMO products), online expansion (26% of revenue in 2025, improved market share at 13.2% in 2025), and macro tailwinds such as improved financial health, childcare subsidies, and surprisingly favorable zodiac cycles. Feihe also stands to benefit the most from consumers' preference for freshness, thanks to its newly launched fresh ingredient traceability system in Jan 2026, which allows consumers to verify core ingredient production dates via QR code, effectively building consumer trust.

Industry outlook for 2026E remains tough given potential mid-single digit decline in infant population size (0-3 years old). That said, Feihe may have a mild topline recovery given its inventory clearance in 2H25 has set an easy comp base and new HMO product is likely to be a growth pillar on which management has strong ambitions. We are Neutral on Feihe as we think the weak industry outlook has been priced into the stock's low teens P/E and \~6% dividend yield. See our latest report (2H25 Results Missed But Dividend In Line; Targets Stable 2026) for details.

# Health and Happiness (covered by Tiffany Feng)

H&H is a meaningful beneficiary of consumer switches from recalled brands, as its European-produced (France and Denmark) premium and super-premium IMF products align well with demand for safety and quality. The company benefits strongly from premiumization (22.1% super-premium market share in Jan-Feb 2026), its early-mover advantage in HMO-added products, rapid online channel growth (45% in 2025, accounted for 29% of sales; improved market share at 3.3% in 2025), and robust execution (72% IMF sales growth in 1Q26). However, heightened consumer scrutiny of expiration dates and freshness puts some pressure on H&H's global supply chain model, though this has been well managed historically. H&H also enjoys additional advantages from its diversified portfolio in pediatrics and health supplements.

In our latest report (A Strong Start to the Recovery Year), we highlighted 1Q26 sales growth ahead of mgmt's full year growth target. While weak infant population trend brings challenge to H&H's BNC business, it recorded strong rebound in IMF sales in 1Q26 and mgmt expect IMF sales to grow at mid to high teens in 2026E. Besides, it took market share from underperforming foreign brands and has developed successful measures in marketing, branding, distribution, and execution. More importantly, we expect ANC and PNC growth and finance cost reduction to drive 2026E earnings significantly. We have a Buy on H&H.

# Danone and Nestlé (covered by Cedric Besnard)

The survey confirms that recent recalls did have an impact on both stock levels and consumer behaviors, although it appears that Nestlé recalls seem to have had a wider impact on on-shelf availability/stock levels, which may imply that Danone could have taken some share from Nestlé in Q1, at least within the imported segment. We would think that the key risk for both multinationals, post recalls, is a more lasting willingness from Chinese consumers to switch to domestic, long expiry date Chinese products, as highlighted in the survey, although we also found in some of the answers some comfort around LT buffers, especially around premiumization and the switch towards adjacencies (pediatrics, older demographics) where we think both groups have scale and hospital penetration.

Danone (DANO.PA): Defendable margin, but IMF drag, time needed for US EDP.

Nestlé (NESN.S): A RIG beat more than enough for now to support H2 relative resilience

# Abbott (covered by Joanne Wuensch)

Abbott ceased selling its pediatric nutrition products directly into China in 2023, maintaining adult nutrition sales although not significantly, thus these results are largely neutral to its sales there. With that said, its Nutrition franchise has stepped through a number of hurdles, including volume and pricing headwinds, the loss of a WIC contract (Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and children in the U.S) which has since been replaced, plus the ongoing necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) in premature infant litigation. In 1Q26, Nutrition sales were \$2.017B (down 7.7% y/y), with U.S. sales down 11.6% and OUS down 4.7% as the franchise continues to struggle, albeit an improvement from the 4Q25 and management commentary that it is making early progress noting “volumes are starting to recover.” Yet top-of-mind for investors, particularly in our conversations, is: does it make sense to keep the franchise? Management addressed this, noting that it continuously does a comprehensive assessment of its assets at both a product and geographic level, evaluating gaps versus the

competition. Further, while management likes “the diversity of our business model” and will not make long-term strategic decisions based on near-term challenges, it is focused on returning to growth.

# Key takeaways

# #1. Noticeable shortages of recalled formula brands and switching from recalled brands to Chinese brands

84% of respondents who currently use or used to purchase Danone (Aptamil, Nutrilon) and/or Nestle Wyeth (NAN, Illuma, S-26) have found these labels to be unavailable this year due to recalls (Figure 1). The largest proportion of respondents found less stock of Nestle but Danone stock levels unchanged, which correlates to the fact that Nestle infant formula was recalled in China, but not Danone.

The majority of respondents who used to buy Danone and/or Nestle Wyeth switched to a Chinese brand (63%), whilst 35% switched to other international brands (Figure 2).

# Potential stock implications

■ a2 Milk. Any potential tailwind from competitor recalls/stock issues seem to have been a missed opportunity given its recent supply issues causing stock shortages in China. In addition, any tailwinds to a2 from competitor recalls may be limited due to the majority of switching from recalled brands to Chinese brands, rather than international brands.   
China Feihe/Health and Happiness. We believe H&H is a meaningful beneficiary from such a switch because H&H's major IMF products are produced in Europe (France plus Denmark) and positioned at premium and super-premium segments, which meet the demand of consumers who are switching from the recalled brands. We do not think Feihe gained meaningfully from such incident due to different marketing positioning.   
Danone/ Nestlé. This is an indication that there has been some switch and negative perception headwinds for both Danone and Nestlé, although it is interesting to note that the transition seems to be directed more towards domestic brands than other multinationals, which we think is a reflection of a wider category trend as well as Danone (in particular) dominance in the imported multinationals' space. However, we also note that Nestlé recalls seem to have had a wider impact on on-shelf availability/stock levels, which may imply that Danone could have taken some share from Nestlé, at least within the imported segment. We think the impact is more negative for Nestlé given relative stock levels perception vs Danone.

# #2. Improving perception of domestic brand quality, however country of origin less relevant with nutritional value / scientific benefit more important

Whilst the percentage of respondents that prefer foreign brands has declined to its lowest point since the start of our infant formula surveys in 2020 (Figure 4), the proportion of respondents who prefer domestic brands is unchanged since 2025, with the majority (55%) of respondents having no preference for domestic or foreign brands (Figure 3). Figure 5 also shows more respondents feeling indifferent to foreign and domestic brands compared to previous years.

This could represent a shift in consumer priority with consumers preferencing nutritional benefit over brand and/or country of origin. Figure 7 shows that whilst brand and nutritional value/scientific benefit were of equal influence in 2025, brand is now second to nutritional value in factors influencing choice of formula milk. Country of origin has also dropped in importance since 2025, now no longer in the top 3 factors.

Nevertheless, the perception of Chinese formula milk quality continues to improve more than foreign brands, with almost three quarters of respondents with improving views of the quality of Chinese brands in the past 12 months vs roughly half of respondents for foreign brands (Figure 8). In addition, Figure 9 shows a much larger proportion of respondents (50%) are more likely to buy a foreign brand if it is manufactured in China (24% in 2025), with only 6% less likely (9% in 2025), and 44% indifferent (68% in 2025). China continues to be the top country of origin for quality in the survey (Figure 6), and Chinese brands continue to be considered as offering the highest quality infant milk formula (Figure 5) and preferred over foreign brands (Figure 4).

# Potential stock implications

■ a2 Milk. The improving quality perception of domestically produced infant formula is a headwind for a2 Milk, given all of its formula is produced in New Zealand and we think shifting any production to China may be some time away given the company is occupied with ramping up production at and integrating its newly acquired Pokeno facility.

# Potential stock implications

■ a2 Milk. Due to birth rate associated challenges, a2 has been innovating and diversifying its product range into adjacent categories aimed at older consumers (older kids, seniors) or new categories (vitamins). Our survey results suggests a2 is likely to find success with this strategy. Our on the ground China research has suggested positive early sell through trends for kids fortified milk powder and seniors products, which is supported by company disclosure. a2 indicated the pediatric supplement category is a rapidly growing market with \~NZD 8 billion in retail sales and the company is planning to roll out products over 2H FY26. Near term sales are not expected to be material but there is significant longer term potential.   
China Feihe/Health and Happiness. H&H would benefit more from this trend given its rich offerings in pediatrics and health supplements. Feihe would benefit as well but it mainly focuses on milk formula innovations e.g. adult milk powder.   
Danone. We think Danone has a best-in-class presence in Medical Nutrition, especially via pediatrics (Nutricia) and for older demographics via Nutrison, benefitting from the group's strong access to tier-1 hospitals, and the Fortimel umbrella brand.   
Nestlé. Nestle also has a presence in both pediatrics and Adult Nutrition, addressing roughly the same segments (tube feeding, supplements for elder malnutrition).   
Abbott. With Abbott offering a diverse product line beyond infant nutrition, more consumer interest in other milk products along with Abbott's brand recognition should benefit the company in China.

# Potential stock implications

a2 Milk. a2's infant formula stock in China is typically fresh, as seen from our China trips. Our most recent visit to China revealed that most a2 China Label Stages 1-3 tins had expiration dates between 3 and 18 months which is within the ideal expiration date for all survey respondents. Recent stock shortages are likely to mean that a2's stock is even fresher than normal over the near to medium term.   
China Feihe/ Health and Happiness. Given consumers' preference for freshness, we believe Feihe benefits the most given its newly launched fresh ingredient traceability system in Jan 2026. By scanning the QR code on the bottom of the can, consumers can now directly trace the production dates and inspection reports of core ingredients. We think the system helps build consumer trust in Feihe. For H&H, as its major IMF products are produced in Europe, the heightened consumer scrutiny of expiration dates puts pressure on H&H's global supply chain model, though we think it has been well managed in the past.   
Danone/ Nestlé. We think it is too early to assess whether any stock replenishment would lead to longer expiry date; but we think this is more of a topic for Nestlé than for Danone given the result of the survey to an earlier question.   
Abbott. We view these results as neutral for Abbott.

# Guide to Citi Fundamental Research Investment Ratings:

Citi stock recommendations include an investment rating and an optional risk rating to highlight high risk stocks.

Risk rating takes into account both price volatility and fundamental criteria. Stocks will either have no risk rating or a High risk rating assigned.

Investment Ratings: Citi investment ratings are Buy, Neutral and Sell. Our ratings are a function of analyst expectations of expected total return ("ETR") and risk. ETR is the sum of the forecast price appreciation (or depreciation) plus the dividend yield for a stock within the next 12 months. The target price is based on a 12 month time horizon. The Investment rating definitions are: Buy (1) ETR of 15% or more or 25% or more for High risk stocks; and Sell (3) for negative ETR. Any covered stock not assigned a Buy or a Sell is a Neutral (2). For stocks rated Neutral (2), if an analyst believes that there are insufficient valuation drivers and/or investment catalysts to derive a positive or negative investment view, they may elect with the approval of Citi management not to assign a target price and, thus, not derive an ETR. Citi may suspend its rating and target price and assign "Rating Suspended" status for regulatory and/or internal policy reasons. Citi may also suspend its rating and target price and assign "Under Review" status for other exceptional circumstances (e.g. lack of information critical to the analyst's thesis, trading suspension) affecting the company and/or trading in the company's securities. In both such situations, the rating and target price will show as “—” and “-” respectively in the rating history price chart. Prior to 11 April 2022 Citi assigned "Under Review" status to both situations and prior to 11 Nov 2020 only in exceptional circumstances. As soon as practically possible, the analyst will publish a note re-establishing a rating and investment thesis. Investment ratings are determined by the ranges described above at the time of initiation of coverage, a change in investment and/or risk rating, or a change in target price (subject to limited management discretion). At times, the expected total returns may fall outside of these ranges because of market price movements and/or other short-term volatility or trading patterns. Such interim deviations will be permitted but will become subject to review by Research Management. Your decision to buy or sell a security should be based upon your personal investment objectives and should be made only after evaluating the stock's expected performance and risk.

# Catalyst Watch/Short Term Views ("STV") Ratings Disclosure:

Catalyst Watch and STV Upside/Downside calls: Citi may also include a Catalyst Watch or STV Upside or Downside call to indicate the analyst expects the share price to rise (fall) in absolute terms over a specified period of 30 or 90 days in reaction to one or more specific near-term catalysts or events impacting the company or the market. A Catalyst Watch will be published when Analyst confidence is high that an impact to share price will occur; it will be a STV when confidence level is moderate. A Catalyst Watch or STV Upside/Downside call will automatically expire at the end of the specified 30/90 day period. The Catalyst Watch will also be automatically removed if share price performance (calculated at market close) exceeds 15% against the direction of the call (unless over-ridden by the analyst). The analyst may also remove a Catalyst Watch or STV call prior to the end of the specified period in a published research note. A Catalyst Watch/STV Upside or Downside call may be different from and does not affect a stock's fundamental equity rating, which reflects a longer-term total absolute return expectation. For purposes of FINRA ratings-distribution-disclosure rules, a Catalyst Watch/STV Upside call corresponds to a buy recommendation and a Catalyst Watch/STV Downside call corresponds to a sell recommendation. Any stock not assigned to a Catalyst Watch Upside, Catalyst Watch Downside, STV Upside, or STV Downside call is considered Catalyst Watch/STV No View. For purposes of FINRA ratings distribution-disclosure rules, we correspond Catalyst Watch/STV No View to Hold in our ratings distribution table for our Catalyst Watch/STV Upside/Downside rating system.
