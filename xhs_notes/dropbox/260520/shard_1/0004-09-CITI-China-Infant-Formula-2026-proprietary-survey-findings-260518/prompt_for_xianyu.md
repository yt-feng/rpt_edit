# Citi China Infant Formula Survey

Sam Teeger (a2), Tiffany Feng (China Feihe, H&H), Joanne Wuensch (Abbott), Cedric Besnard (Danone, Nestle), conducted a survey of 1,200 Chinese consumers, with the support of Citi's Research Innovation Lab, which helped coordinate the survey and gather the data we leveraged to develop insights into trends in China.

# Potential stock implications from our survey

# a2 Milk (covered by Sam Teeger)

The survey findings provide deeper insight from a consumer perspective into competitor stock levels following well documented recalls/shortages. However, a2 was not able to maximise this potential opportunity due to its own supply issues, and according to our survey, consumers are switching to domestic brands. The survey also identifies an improving consumer perception of domestic infant formula milk quality, particularly those that are manufactured in China. The survey suggests the best way for a2 to mitigate this risk is to ensure it does not under-invest in the science behind its products with nutritional value/scientific benefit being the primary driver influencing consumer choice and HMOs being very important (hence the Genesis shortage is particularly unfortunate). The survey also provides support that a2 is partially decoupling from the birth rate through several factors including premiumization, category expansion, diversifying to products for older consumers and English label relative outperformance. However, the survey does caution against assuming marriages will translate to more births in the near term. The next two zodiacs Horse (2026) and Goat (2027) appear to be incrementally positive from a birth rate perspective.

We recently downgraded a2 Milk to Sell as we see risk that supply chain constraints could persist for longer than expected, and even when they are resolved it may not be easy or cheap for a2 to win back customers who have switched to competitor brands.

# China Feihe (covered by Tiffany Feng)

Feihe has successfully solidified its leadership as the No.1 domestic IMF brand through its "most suitable for Chinese babies" positioning and a strong super-premium product mix (68% of sales in 2025). The company benefits from premiumization, declining breastfeeding rates, HMO innovation (being among the first domestic brands to launch HMO products), online expansion (26% of revenue in 2025, improved market share at 13.2% in 2025), and macro tailwinds such as improved financial health, childcare subsidies, and surprisingly favorable zodiac cycles. Feihe also stands to benefit the most from consumers' preference for freshness, thanks to its newly launched fresh ingredient traceability system in Jan 2026, which allows consumers to verify core ingredient production dates via QR code, effectively building consumer trust.

Industry outlook for 2026E remains tough given potential mid-single digit decline in infant population size (0-3 years old). That said, Feihe may have a mild topline recovery given its inventory clearance in 2H25 has set an easy comp base and new HMO product is likely to be a growth pillar on which management has strong ambitions. We are Neutral on Feihe as we think the weak industry outlook has been priced into the stock's low teens P/E and \~6% dividend yield. See our latest report (2H25 Results Missed But Dividend In Line; Targets Stable 2026) for details.

# Health and Happiness (covered by Tiffany Feng)

H&H is a meaningful beneficiary of consumer switches from recalled brands, as its European-produced (France and Denmark) premium and super-premium IMF products align well with demand for safety and quality. The company benefits strongly from premiumization (22.1% super-premium market share in Jan-Feb 2026), its early-mover advantage in HMO-added products, rapid online channel growth (45% in 2025, accounted for 29% of sales; improved market share at 3.3% in 2025), and robust execution (72% IMF sales growth in 1Q26). However, heightened consumer scrutiny of expiration dates and freshness puts some pressure on H&H's global supply chain model, though this has been well managed historically. H&H also enjoys additional advantages from its diversified portfolio in pediatrics and health supplements.

In our latest report (A Strong Start to the Recovery Year), we highlighted 1Q26 sales growth ahead of mgmt's full year growth target. While weak infant population trend brings challenge to H&H's BNC business, it recorded strong rebound in IMF sales in 1Q26 and mgmt expect IMF sales to grow at mid to high teens in 2026E. Besides, it took market share from underperforming foreign brands and has developed successful measures in marketing, branding, distribution, and execution. More importantly, we expect ANC and PNC growth and finance cost reduction to drive 2026E earnings significantly. We have a Buy on H&H.

# Danone and Nestlé (covered by Cedric Besnard)

The survey confirms that recent recalls did have an impact on both stock levels and consumer behaviors, although it appears that Nestlé recalls seem to have had a wider impact on on-shelf availability/stock levels, which may imply that Danone could have taken some share from Nestlé in Q1, at least within the imported segment. We would think that the key risk for both multinationals, post recalls, is a more lasting willingness from Chinese consumers to switch to domestic, long expiry date Chinese products, as highlighted in the survey, although we also found in some of the answers some comfort around LT buffers, especially around premiumization and the switch towards adjacencies (pediatrics, older demographics) where we think both groups have scale and hospital penetration.

Abbott ceased selling its pediatric nutrition products directly into China in 2023, maintaining adult nutrition sales although not significantly, thus these results are largely neutral to its sales there. With that said, its Nutrition franchise has stepped through a number of hurdles, including volume and pricing headwinds, the loss of a WIC contract (Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and children in the U.S) which has since been replaced, plus the ongoing necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) in premature infant litigation.
