# Natural Gas Comment: US Production Showing Signs of Price Sensitivity

Henry Hub has moved back above \$3/mmBtu this week for the first time since late March. The price action has been correlated with the evolution of early summer weather forecasts (Exhibit 1), but likely also more recently supported by declining Haynesville production, tighter-than-consensus storage injections and, to some degree, trade sympathy with the recent gains in European natural gas prices $^{1}$ , as the Hormuz disruption lingers. Out of all these potential drivers, we see the ongoing signs of US dry gas production price sensitivity as the most relevant, as it can impact how the market manages what we expect to be a further softening of US gas balances into 2027.

For the balance of summer 2026 we believe there's a Henry Hub price range that balances the market, rather than a single price path, as we do not see a tight market at its current \$3.17/mmBtu Bal Sum26 strip (or at its near-\$3 prompt, for that matter), nor do we see it congested at our \$3.50 forecast. But even if there's limited information to be extracted from summer prices within this range, we can likely still learn useful information by observing how Haynesville production growth reacts to gas prices.

Muted sequential growth in Haynesville production observed this year compared to a year ago suggests that well inventory and price incentives matter, contrary to what our client conversations going into this year suggested, as most clients expected solid Haynesville production growth at virtually any natural gas price. To be clear, a year ago, producers had plenty of inventoried wells ready to be brought to market under prompt prices that averaged \$3.70/mmBtu in Jan-May25, and we saw Haynesville production up 800 mmcf/d sequentially in Dec24-May25. This year, with well inventory arguably at lower levels, given the significant rise in Haynesville production volumes throughout 2025, and lower Jan-May26 Henry Hub prices, at \$3.19/mmBtu (including sub-\$3 in Apr/May), Haynesville production has declined 200 mmcf/d from Dec25 levels (Exhibit 2). While there's ongoing maintenance impacting production, this tends do be seasonal, hence likely impacting last year's early summer volumes as well. In addition, even focusing on Apr, instead of May, the strongest Haynesville production month YTD, production was up only 200 mmcf/d from Dec25, vs up 700 mmcf/d during the same period in 2025.
