# Large-Cap Institutional Ownership 1Q26: Mega-Cap Tech Under-Ownership Narrows

The gap between Mega-cap tech S&P weighting and institutional ownership narrowed 12bps in 1Q, with mega-cap tech now 125bps under-owned vs. -137bps in 4Q. The rest of large-cap tech is modestly over-owned at +32bps. NVDA remains the most under-owned, followed by AAPL, MSFT, AMZN and GOOGL.

# Key Takeaways

Mega-cap Tech under-ownership vs the S&P 500 modestly narrowed to -125bps from -137bps at 4Q25 end.   
Outside of mega-cap Tech stocks, the spread between institutional ownership and S&P 500 weighting was +32bps, in-line with last quarter.   
NVDA remains the most under-owned large-cap tech stock vs. S&P 500 at -2.39%, followed by AAPL (-2.32%), MSFT (-1.86%), and AMZN (-1.24%).   
■ SNDK is still the most "over-owned" large-cap tech stock at +2.16% vs. S&P 500, more than 2x STX, the next most over-owned stock behind SNDK.   
Institutional positioning in large-cap tech favors AI infra bottlenecks, with relatively high memory/storage ownership and low software ownership exiting 1Q26.

Mega-cap Tech stocks are modestly less under-owned vs. the S&P 500 exiting 1Q26 than at 2025 year-end; NVDA remains the most "under-owned" large-cap tech stock, while SNDK is the most "over-owned" (Exhibit1). Leveraging 13Fs filed for the end of 1Q26, most mega-cap technology stocks remain under-owned relative to their weighting in the S&P 500, with NVDA (-2.39%), AAPL (-2.32%), MSFT (-1.86%) and AMZN (-1.24%) the most under-owned of the mega-cap tech stocks we track. Conversely, SNDK (+2.16%), STX (+0.84%) and CRM (+0.63%), are the most over-owned technology stocks we track relative to their weighting in the S&P 500. On average, the spread between active institutional ownership and S&P 500 weighting for the largest technology companies in the US by market cap - NVDA, AAPL, MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, META, and TSLA - was -125bps exiting 1Q26 (Exhibit 2) vs. -137bps at the end of 4Q25, showing a modest narrowing of mega-cap under-ownership over the last 90 days. For the remaining 21 large-cap tech stocks we track, the spread between institutional ownership and S&P 500 weighting exiting 1Q26 was +32bps, in-line with 4Q25.

Why does this data matter? Each quarter, we track ownership data for 28 of the largest cap tech companies across MS's US technology equity research coverage, which is based on each company's average weighting within the top 100 actively managed institutional portfolios relative to that same company's weighting in the S&P 500. A quant analysis of this historical ownership data indicates that, on average, after adjusting for market cap and earnings beats, there is a statistically significant relationship between low active ownership relative to the S&P 500 and future stock performance. This indicates that on average, stocks appear to experience a technical pull higher when active ownership is much lower than the market, and vice versa.
