EN_B: The key difference is binding power. In the past, these agreements were basically soft commitments. A customer could walk away with little penalty. Now, customers are making upfront payments, sometimes as much as 50 to 100 percent of the future supply value. And these payments are likely non-cancellable and non-refundable.
EN_A: So the customers are putting real money on the table. What does that mean for the memory companies?
EN_B: It transforms their business model. Instead of guessing demand and hoping they build the right amount of capacity, they now have confirmed orders. This reduces the risk of overbuilding and inventory pileups. It also provides a buffer for pricing. Even if the market softens, these contracts have price floors or margin protections.
EN_A: That sounds like a great deal for the suppliers. But why would customers agree to such strict terms?
EN_B: Because memory has become the bottleneck for AI infrastructure. The big cloud service providers, the hyperscalers, they need guaranteed supply to build out their AI data centers.
