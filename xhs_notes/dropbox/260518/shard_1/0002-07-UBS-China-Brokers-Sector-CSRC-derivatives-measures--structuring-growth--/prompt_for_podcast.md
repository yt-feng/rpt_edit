# CSRC derivatives measures: structuring growth, favouring leaders

# CSRC rolled out the first departmental rules governing derivatives

CSRC issued 'Measures for Supervision and Administration of Derivatives Trading (Trial)' ('the Measures'), the first comprehensive departmental rules specifically governing derivatives. The Measures signals a shift toward a more standardized and orderly market. While near-term growth may face some pressure, the Measures lay the groundwork for more disciplined and regulated leverage over time. For intermediaries (e.g., securities and futures firms), tighter requirements on capital, classification, segregation, and reporting would raise operating costs, and therefore may potentially accelerate industry consolidation and favouring leading players. The Measures will take effect on 16 November, implying a six-month grace period. From then, non-compliant firms shall not launch new business, and existing positions must be unwound upon maturity. The Measures apply only to CSRC-regulated trading venues and intermediaries, excluding interbank and bank/insurance OTC markets.

# Building toward long-term upside; leading players well positioned

We believe the roll-out of the Measures signals China's derivatives market shifting toward more standardized and orderly development. Near-term growth may soften as non-compliant products shall be phased out and smaller players with weaker capital bases may exit. However, the Measures open up room for more disciplined and regulated usage of leverage. Leading players should benefit disproportionately, backed by strong capital and scale to meet tighter rules while delivering compliant products. CITICS, CICC, HTSC, and GTHT jointly accounted for nearly 60% (UBS-e) of notional principal of equity derivatives of all listed brokers in 2025 (vs. 2024: \~52%). China's derivatives market remains underpenetrated, especially in financial derivatives. Globally, financial derivatives make up the majority of exchange-traded volumes, versus 33%/20% (value/volume, UBS-e) in China in 2025. Growth should be driven by rising institutionalization and increasing foreign participation as markets open up.

# Key highlights in the Measures

Compared with the draft for public consultation (issued on 16 Jan), the finalized trial version (1) set a clear bar for securities and futures firms, requiring net capital of at least Rmb500 mn over the past six months. The CSRC may adjust this minimum under prudent supervision. We believe this favours well-capitalized leading players and could accelerate industry consolidation. (2) broadened trading restrictions, extending the ban on derivatives activity by listed companies, major shareholders, controllers and executives from their own shares to all equity-like securities. The phase-out of non-compliant structured products may weigh on near-term growth, and (3) clarified scope, defining derivatives as swaps, forwards, and non-standardized options (including combinations), excluding futures. In addition, the Measures clarified the role of derivatives: promoting hedging while curbing excessive speculation. This implies likely tighter scrutiny across product design, investor suitability, and leverage controls, with tools such as margin requirements, position limits, and countercyclical measures. Corporates could leverage derivatives to hedge risk exposures. Listed companies are required to follow formal governance procedures and disclose relevant activities.
