# China's Oncology Pipeline Is No Longer Just Following—It Is Redefining Frontline Standards

The American Society of Clinical Oncology 2026 abstract release marks a decisive inflection point for Chinese biopharma. For years, the narrative was about fast-following and cost advantages. That story is now outdated. What the data reveals is a cohort of Chinese developers generating efficacy signals that challenge, and in some cases surpass, the current global standard of care in first-line non-small cell lung cancer and beyond. This is not a marginal improvement story. It is a structural shift in where the most competitive oncology innovation is emerging.

The most consequential readout is not a single data point but a pattern. Across multiple mechanisms—TROP2 ADCs, PD-1/VEGF bispecifics, PD-1/IL-2 biased antibodies, and trispecific T-cell engagers—Chinese companies are producing response rates and progression-free survival figures that place them in direct competition with, and occasionally ahead of, global blockbusters. The implication is strategic: the center of gravity for first-line NSCLC treatment is shifting, and the next generation of standards will likely be set by assets developed in China, whether through local commercialization or global licensing.

This preview analyzes the key abstracts, interprets what the patterns mean for investors and strategists, and identifies the unresolved questions that will define the next phase of competition.

## First-Line NSCLC Is Becoming a Multi-Arm Race Where Chinese Assets Lead on Efficacy

The 1L NSCLC landscape has become a proving ground for novel mechanisms, and the ASCO 2026 abstracts make clear that the bar is being raised. The most striking data comes from Innovent's IBI363, a PD-1/IL-2 alpha-bias bispecific antibody, which achieved a confirmed ORR of 81.8% when combined with chemotherapy using a novel induction-maintenance dosing scheme. This compares favorably to ivonescimab's 75.9% from HARMONi-6 and sac-TMT's 70.2% from OptiTROP-Lung05, and far exceeds pembrolizumab's 48.3% from KEYNOTE-189. The dosing strategy itself is noteworthy: starting at 3mg/kg with chemo in cycle one, then dropping to 1.5mg/kg, the regimen produced both higher efficacy and better tolerability than either fixed dose level alone. The G3+ TEAE rate of 65.2% for the induction-maintenance cohort was significantly lower than the 93.1% seen at 3mg/kg flat dosing.

The question this raises is not whether IBI363 is active—it clearly is—but whether the mechanism of this dosing effect is fully understood. The fact that a lower cumulative dose delivered higher ORR than a higher single dose suggests an immune priming effect that warrants deeper mechanistic investigation. For strategists, the takeaway is that Innovent has not just another PD-1 combination; it has a potentially differentiated platform that could extend beyond NSCLC.

Meanwhile, 3SBio's SSGJ707 (PD-1/VEGF) showed a cORR of 67.6% as monotherapy at 10mg/kg after 15.2 months of follow-up, with a median PFS of 12.4 months. These figures are competitive with ivonescimab's 50% ORR and 11.1-month PFS in the monotherapy setting. However, the safety profile warrants attention: G3+ TRAE rate was 42.2%, with VEGF-related G3+ AEs at 18.1%, higher than ivonescimab's 10.2%. The trade-off between efficacy and tolerability will be a key differentiator as these assets move toward registration.

CStone's CS2009, a PD-1/VEGF/CTLA-4 trispecific antibody, reported a 90% ORR in a small cohort of 10 patients with PD-L1 TPS >=50%. The upcoming update in approximately 50 patients will be a critical proof-of-concept moment. If the efficacy holds with manageable safety, this molecule could represent a next-generation approach that consolidates multiple immune checkpoint mechanisms into a single agent.

The strategic implication is clear: the 1L NSCLC market is fragmenting into a multi-modal competition where response rates above 70% are becoming the entry threshold, not the ceiling. Companies that cannot demonstrate best-in-class efficacy or a clearly differentiated safety profile will face an uphill battle for first-line positioning.

## DLL3-Targeted Therapies in SCLC Show Durable Survival Signals That Redefine Late-Line Expectations

Small cell lung cancer has historically been a difficult indication with limited options beyond first-line chemotherapy. The ASCO 2026 abstracts bring renewed hope, particularly from Zelgen's alveltamig (ZG006), a DLL3/DLL3/CD3 trispecific T-cell engager. In the 3L+ setting, alveltamig achieved a 12-month OS rate of 65.9% at 10mg and 59.2% at 30mg, implying a median OS potentially in the 15-20 month range. This compares favorably to tarlatamab's 13.6-month median OS in the 2L setting.

Even more striking is the durability of response. Alveltamig's median DOR was 12.6 months, nearly double that of ZL-1310 (6.1 months) and tarlatamab (6.9 months). The 12-month PFS rate of 50.5% further underscores the potential for long-term disease control. For a patient population that typically progresses rapidly, these numbers represent a meaningful step change.

The question that remains unanswered is the patient selection and baseline characteristics. The abstract does not provide full detail on prior treatment lines, brain metastasis status, or biomarker stratification. Understanding which patients derive the most benefit will be essential for positioning alveltamig relative to other DLL3-targeted therapies, including ADCs and CAR-T approaches.

Ivonescimab also showed activity in 2L SCLC, with a 62% ORR and 9.8-month median PFS when combined with liposomal irinotecan. While the sample size is modest, the data reinforces the view that PD-1/VEGF bispecifics may have a role beyond NSCLC. The broader implication is that the SCLC treatment algorithm is becoming more complex, with multiple novel mechanisms competing for the same patient segments.

## The Induction-Maintenance Dosing Paradigm Could Reshape How Immunotherapy Combinations Are Designed

One of the most analytically interesting findings from the ASCO 2026 abstracts is not just what the drugs do, but how they are being dosed. Innovent's IBI363 induction-maintenance strategy is a case study in intelligent trial design. The hypothesis is straightforward: start with a higher dose to drive initial immune activation and tumor debulking, then reduce to a lower maintenance dose to sustain response while minimizing cumulative toxicity. The data supports the hypothesis, but the mechanism may be more nuanced.

The 3-1.5mg/kg cohort not only outperformed the 3mg/kg flat dose on ORR (81.8% vs 57.1%) but also showed a lower G3+ TEAE rate (65.2% vs 93.1%). This suggests that the initial high dose may be critical for priming the immune response, while the subsequent lower dose is sufficient to maintain it. If this pattern holds across other indications, it could influence how a wide range of immunotherapy combinations are designed.

For developers, the implication is that dose optimization strategies—particularly those that differentiate induction from maintenance—may be as important as the molecule itself. For investors, the ability to design smart, de-risked clinical programs is a competitive advantage that is often underappreciated in valuation models.

## What the Report Does Not Yet Answer: Patient Selection, Cross-Trial Comparisons, and the Durability of OS Data

While the ASCO 2026 abstracts are rich in efficacy signals, several critical analytical gaps remain. First, cross-trial comparisons are inherently limited by differences in patient populations, baseline characteristics, and follow-up duration. The ORR and PFS figures for IBI363, ivonescimab, and sac-TMT come from trials with different inclusion criteria, different chemotherapy backbones, and different data cutoff dates. Until head-to-head data emerges, the relative ranking of these assets remains provisional.

Second, overall survival data is still immature for many of the most promising assets. Ivonescimab's HARMONi-2 trial showed an interim OS HR of 0.777 at 39% maturity, which is encouraging but not definitive. For IBI363, OS data has not been reported. The durability of response beyond two years will be a critical differentiator, particularly in first-line settings where patients may live for several years.

Third, biomarker-driven patient selection is largely absent from these abstracts. PD-L1 TPS stratification is reported for some trials, but the granularity is limited. As the treatment landscape becomes more crowded, the ability to identify which patients benefit most from which mechanism will become a strategic imperative. Companies that can pair their assets with robust companion diagnostics will have a significant advantage.

Finally, the safety profiles, while generally manageable, show meaningful differences in VEGF-related toxicities, immune-related adverse events, and hematologic effects. The trade-off between efficacy and tolerability will become more important as these assets move into earlier lines of treatment, where patients have better performance status and higher expectations for quality of life.

## A Decision Framework for Evaluating Chinese Oncology Assets at ASCO 2026

For investors and strategists navigating this data-rich environment, a structured framework is essential. The following four questions should guide the evaluation of any asset:

First, what is the magnitude of the efficacy signal relative to the current standard of care? An ORR above 70% in 1L NSCLC or a median OS above 15 months in 3L SCLC represents a clear differentiation. Assets that are merely comparable to existing therapies face a higher bar for adoption, particularly if their safety profile is not superior.

Second, how durable is the response? Duration of response and PFS at 12 and 18 months are more informative than initial ORR, especially in indications where rapid progression is common. Alveltamig's 12-month PFS rate of 50.5% is more compelling than a high ORR with short DOR.

Third, what is the safety and tolerability profile, and how does it compare to the competitive set? VEGF-related toxicities, immune-related AEs, and hematologic effects are the key categories to monitor. A drug with comparable efficacy but significantly better tolerability has a clear commercial advantage.

Fourth, what is the pathway to registration and global commercialization? Assets that are partnered with major global players (e.g., Kelun with Merck, Akeso with Summit) have a clearer path to global markets. For unpartnered assets, the ability to demonstrate best-in-class data in a registrational trial is critical. The presence of a differentiated dosing strategy or a novel mechanism can also create partnering optionality.
