EN_A: So there was a major coal mine accident in Shanxi recently. How big of a deal is this for the market?

EN_B: According to Mysteel data, total suspended capacity for safety inspections has reached 125 million tons of raw ore, involving 113 coal mines in Shanxi. EN_A: So what does that mean for the coking coal market?
EN_B: Almost all of the suspended capacity is met coal. That translates to about 60 million tons of commercial coal, which is more than 10 percent of the domestic met coal market. That is a pretty big chunk.
EN_A: And what about thermal coal? Could that be affected too?
EN_B: That depends on the policy response. The report points out that after several domestic coal mine accidents in the second half of 2023, China launched a three-year production safety action plan. That led to production declines in key regions, and thermal coal prices went up by 27 percent, while met coal rose by 30 percent.
EN_A: So you are saying history could repeat itself?
EN_B: Exactly. That is the core thesis of this report. The situation is still fluid, but the risk of tighter safety controls is very real. And on top of that, the fundamentals of the coal sector have been improving. Demand from coal chemicals is rising, imports are falling, and independent power producers started restocking early in May.
EN_A: Early restocking? That sounds unusual.
EN_B: It is. Normally this is a slack season, but thermal coal spot prices have moved up from 718 yuan per ton after Chinese New Year to 835 yuan. That is counter to normal seasonality. So if supply gets disrupted on top of that, we could see more upside pressure on prices.
