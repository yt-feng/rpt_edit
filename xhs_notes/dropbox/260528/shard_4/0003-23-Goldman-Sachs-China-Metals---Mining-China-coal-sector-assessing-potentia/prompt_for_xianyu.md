CHINA METALS & MINING

# China coal sector – assessing potential impact to industry production and prices post recent accident

On the evening of May 22nd, a major gas explosion incident took place in Liushenyu Coal Mine in Changzhi City, Shanxi Province, resulting in significant casualties and suspension of the impacted mines for safety inspections (link). The Liushenyu Coal Mine, operated by the Shanxi Tongzhou Group, has an annual production capacity of 1.2mnt of hard coking coal.

According to Mysteel data, currently total coal capacity suspended for safety inspections following the accident has reached 125mnt (annual) raw ore in Shanxi or 113 coal mines, while 16 coal mines that were previously suspended related to the incident have been restarted. Among the currently suspended mines, the reported data indicated nearly 80 mnt or $64\%$ of the suspended coal output are expected to remain offline for at least one month or do not yet have a clear restart timeline. Nearly all affected coal production capacity relates to met coal, which we estimate is equivalent to 60mnt of commercial coal annual capacity, representing more than $10\%$ of the domestic met coal market.

While the situation remains fluid and uncertain and we take no view on the supply recovery timelines, we see risk to Chinese coal production in the coming months due to potentially tightened safety control. Following several domestic coal mine accidents in 2H23, China started a three-year production safety act plan (安全生产治本攻坚三年行动方案), which led to production decline across key coal output regions in subsequent months. As a result, domestic thermal coal pricing moved up by $27\%$ and met coal pricing by $30\%$ during that period.

Fundamentals of the coal sector have been improving, with rising demand from coal chemical production, lower imports, and early restocking by IPPs in May (China monthly monitor in May). As a result, thermal coal spot prices have moved up from Rmb718/t to Rmb835/t since CNY, counter to normal seasonality. We see potential upside risk to prices in the coming months depending on the impact of supply disruptions following the accident.

For context, we estimate for each $10\%$ increase in domestic spot prices, earnings would move up by $4.1\%$ for Shenhua, $7.8\%$ for Chinacoal, and $4.0\%$ for Yankuang. We maintain BUY on Chinacoal A/H.

# GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
