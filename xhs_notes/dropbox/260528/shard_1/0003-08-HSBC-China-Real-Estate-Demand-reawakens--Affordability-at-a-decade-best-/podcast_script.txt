ZH_A: 最近很多人都在问，中国房地产是不是真的到底了？今天我们看到一份某外资投行的重磅报告，标题很有意思，叫“需求重新觉醒”。B，你怎么看这个判断？

ZH_B: 对，这份报告的核心观点其实非常直接。它认为，现在中国房地产的“可负担性”已经回到了十年来最好的水平。这不是一个情绪上的反弹，而是有数据支撑的结构性改善。

ZH_A: 可负担性回到十年最好？这个听起来很具体。他们是怎么衡量的？

ZH_B: 他们用了两个关键指标。一个是“房价收入比”，就是一套房的总价除以家庭年收入。另一个是“月供收入比”，就是每个月要还的房贷占家庭收入的比例。这两个数字，都在过去几年里大幅下降。

ZH_A: 大幅下降？下降了多少？

ZH_B: 以一线城市为例，房价收入比已经从2021年的高点，回落到接近2015年的水平。这意味着什么？意味着一个普通家庭，现在买房的压力比过去几年小了很多。而月供的下降更明显，因为房价跌了，房贷利率也降了。

ZH_A: 房贷利率降了多少？这个影响是不是被低估了？

ZH_B: 这正是报告里一个很有意思的洞察。很多人只盯着房价下跌，觉得这是让房子变便宜的唯一原因。但报告指出，房贷利率下降带来的月供节省，其实被市场低估了。他们算了一笔账，以上海一套70平米的房子为例。

ZH_A: 上海？那可是全国房价最高的城市之一。普通家庭能买得起吗？

ZH_B: 这就是关键。报告说，在2021年，这套房的月供要占到家庭收入的82%，基本上只有高收入家庭才敢碰。但到了现在，月供占收入的比例已经降到了50%左右。而50%是一条红线，银行通常不会批月供超过收入一半的贷款。

ZH_A: 所以现在一个中等收入家庭，在上海也能通过银行贷款买一套70平米的房子了？这确实是个很大的变化。

ZH_B: 对。这意味着潜在购房者的池子，一下子扩大了很多。报告里特别强调，上海二手房交易中，总价低于300万的房子占了64%。这些房子恰恰是普通家庭最可能买得起的。

ZH_A: 那二手房市场现在怎么样？是不是已经回暖了？

ZH_B: 数据很明确。报告显示，上海4月份的二手房周度成交量，创下了2016年以来的新高。而且不只是上海，他们跟踪的13个重点城市里，二手房成交占比在持续上升，已经超过了新房。这说明什么？说明二手房流动性在恢复。

ZH_A: 二手房流动性恢复，对开发商有什么影响？毕竟他们主要卖的是新房。

ZH_B: 这就是报告里提到的下一个关键环节。他们认为，二手房市场复苏之后，会逐步传导到新房市场。因为很多人卖旧房，是为了换新房，也就是所谓的“改善型需求”。如果二手房卖得掉，手里有了钱，才会去买开发商的新项目。

ZH_A: 那开发商现在最需要关注什么？

ZH_B: 报告建议，要密切关注开发商手里那些“标杆项目”的销售情况。如果一个开发商能在二手房活跃的城市，把高端改善型项目卖得又快又好，那就证明它有定价权。这会是下一阶段市场情绪的重要风向标。

ZH_A: 听起来，这份报告对需求端的判断是偏乐观的。那有没有什么风险或者还没说清楚的地方？

ZH_B: 当然有。报告自己也承认，它的测算有一些局限性。比如它用的家庭收入是城市平均数据，但实际购房者的收入可能更高。另外，它假设的房屋面积是统一的，但不同城市、不同家庭的居住习惯差异很大。这些细节，在完整报告里会有更深入的讨论。

ZH_A: 所以这些悬念，是留给那些愿意去读完整报告的人？

ZH_B: 没错。比如，房价和利率的下降，到底能在多大程度上持续释放需求？二手房向新房的传导，在哪些城市会最快发生？这些问题的答案，直接关系到投资策略。想了解更多，还是得自己去翻那份完整的研究。

EN_A: So, is the Chinese real estate market finally hitting the bottom? We came across a report from a global investment bank with a pretty bold title: "Demand Reawakens." B, what’s the main takeaway here?

EN_B: The core argument is straightforward. The report believes housing affordability in China has reset to its best level in a decade. This isn’t just about sentiment. It’s a structural improvement backed by hard data.

EN_A: Best level in a decade? That sounds like a big claim. How are they measuring it?

EN_B: They use two key metrics. One is the price-to-income ratio, basically the price of a home divided by annual household income. The other is the monthly mortgage payment-to-income ratio. Both have dropped significantly over the past few years.

EN_A: Dropped how much?

EN_B: Take tier-one cities. The price-to-income ratio has fallen back to levels last seen around 2015. What that means is, an average family now faces much less pressure to buy a home compared to a few years ago. And the drop in monthly payments is even more dramatic, because both home prices and mortgage rates have come down.

EN_A: How much have mortgage rates fallen? Is that impact being overlooked?

EN_B: That’s one of the most interesting points in the report. A lot of people focus only on falling home prices. But the report argues that the savings from lower mortgage rates are being underestimated. They did a calculation for a typical 70-square-meter apartment in Shanghai.

EN_A: Shanghai? That’s one of the most expensive cities. Can an average family really afford that?

EN_B: That’s the key. In 2021, the monthly mortgage payment for that apartment would have eaten up 82% of a household’s income. Basically, only high-income families could even consider it. Now, that ratio has dropped to around 50%. And 50% is a critical threshold, because banks typically won’t approve a loan if the payment exceeds half your income.

EN_A: So now an average-income family in Shanghai can actually get a bank loan to buy a 70-square-meter home? That’s a huge shift.

EN_B: Exactly. It means the pool of potential buyers has expanded dramatically. The report highlights that in Shanghai’s secondary market, 64% of transactions are for units priced below 3 million RMB. Those are exactly the homes average families can afford.

EN_A: So how is the secondary market doing? Is it actually picking up?

EN_B: The data is pretty clear. The report shows that weekly secondary home transactions in Shanghai hit a new high in April, the highest since 2016. And it’s not just Shanghai. Across 13 key cities, the share of secondary market transactions is rising steadily, now surpassing new home sales. That tells us resale liquidity is coming back.

EN_A: If the secondary market is recovering, what does that mean for developers? They mainly sell new homes.

EN_B: This is where the report gets really interesting. They believe that a recovery in the secondary market will eventually spill over into the primary market. Why? Because many people sell their old home to buy a new, bigger one, what we call upgrade demand. If they can sell their existing property, they have the cash to buy from developers.

EN_A: So what should developers be watching for right now?

EN_B: The report suggests keeping a close eye on how developers’ flagship projects are selling. If a developer can sell high-end upgrade projects quickly and at good prices in cities where the secondary market is active, that shows pricing power. That could be a key signal for market sentiment in the next phase.
