# The Hermès Resale Market Is Stabilizing, and That Changes the Investment Thesis

The secondhand market for Hermès handbags has been sending mixed signals for over a year. Resale premiums on the core Birkin and Kelly models declined from their 2022 peaks, and the narrative shifted toward a brand that had temporarily lost momentum to its competitors. But the latest data from May 2026 auctions reveals something different: the decline is not accelerating into a structural breakdown. It is flattening. And for investors who rely on resale prices as a leading indicator of brand health, this stabilization is the most important signal in the current cycle.

After a period of uncertainty, the core Birkin and Kelly resale premium has moved from 1.2x in the first quarter of 2026 to 1.5x in the second quarter. The expanded tracker, which includes the highly sought-after Kelly Pochette and Mini Kelly II, now sits at approximately 1.8x. These numbers are not at the euphoric levels of 2021 and 2022, but they are no longer falling. The sequential improvement matters more than the absolute level because it suggests that the floor has been found.

What makes this finding strategically significant is the context. The broader luxury goods market has been navigating a difficult macro environment, particularly in China, where consumer confidence remains fragile. Hermès has also faced what the report describes as a "soft patch in momentum" as creative renewals at Chanel and Dior captured the attention of the luxury consumer. In this environment, a stabilizing resale premium is a vote of confidence in the underlying durability of the Hermès business model.

This article will argue that the resale price data offers a clearer lens into brand equity than quarterly revenue reports. It will examine why the stabilization matters, what the regional divergence tells us about demand patterns, and where the unanswered questions remain for investors who are trying to position for the next phase of the cycle.

## The Resale Premium Is Not Just a Price Signal; It Is a Brand Health Indicator That Predicts Primary Demand

The instinct of many investors is to dismiss secondhand pricing as a niche data point relevant only to collectors and speculators. That instinct is wrong. The resale premium on Hermès handbags functions as a real-time gauge of supply-demand imbalance, brand scarcity perception, and consumer willingness to pay above retail. When the premium is rising, it signals that primary demand exceeds the brand's ability or willingness to supply. When it is falling, it suggests either demand softening or supply catching up.

The current data shows that the core tracker premium has moved from 1.2x in the first quarter to 1.5x in the second quarter of 2026. That is a 25 percent increase in a single quarter. The expanded tracker, which captures the most popular models, has risen to approximately 1.8x. These numbers are still below the 2.0x-plus levels seen in 2021 and early 2022, but the direction of travel has shifted from downward to sideways-to-upward.

The "so what" here is that Hermès has not lost its pricing power. In a period where many luxury brands have had to increase promotional activity and discounting to move inventory, Hermès is seeing its secondary market buyers willing to pay a substantial premium over retail. That is a structural advantage that cannot be easily replicated by competitors. It means that even if Hermès experiences a temporary slowdown in revenue growth, the underlying brand equity remains intact.

The report also notes that auction volumes in the second quarter of 2026 are running 26 percent above the level observed in the first two months of the second quarter of 2025. Higher volumes combined with stable or improving prices is a constructive combination. It suggests that the market is liquid and that sellers are not forced to accept lower prices to clear inventory.

## Regional Divergence Reveals Where Demand Is Recovering and Where It Is Still Under Pressure

Not all geographies are moving in the same direction. The data shows that resale multiples in Hong Kong remain the weakest among the tracked regions, while Europe and the United States have started to reflate slightly. This regional divergence is not just a curiosity; it is a map of where the luxury consumer is feeling confident and where they are pulling back.

Hong Kong has been a bellwether for Chinese luxury demand, and its persistent weakness in resale pricing reflects the ongoing challenges in the broader China market. The macroeconomic environment there has been difficult, and consumer sentiment has been subdued. The fact that Hong Kong resale prices are lagging suggests that the recovery in Chinese luxury demand is not yet broad-based.

Europe and the United States, by contrast, are showing signs of improvement. The slight reflation in these regions indicates that the Western luxury consumer remains resilient. This is consistent with what we have seen in other luxury categories, where travel retail and domestic spending in the US and Europe have held up better than expected.

For investors, this regional divergence has a clear implication: Hermès is not a monolith. Its performance will vary by geography, and the overall brand health is being supported by strength in the West while waiting for a recovery in Asia. The resale data provides a granular view of this dynamic that is not available in quarterly earnings reports, which aggregate everything into a single number.

## The Soft Patch in Momentum Is Real, but the Data Suggests It Is Cyclical, Not Structural

The report acknowledges that Hermès has experienced a "soft patch in momentum" in the near term. This has been driven by two factors: difficult year-over-year comparisons from a period of exceptional growth, and the creative renewal at Chanel and Dior that has temporarily shifted consumer attention. The question for investors is whether this soft patch is a temporary cyclical phenomenon or the beginning of a structural decline in Hermès' competitive position.

The resale data argues for the cyclical interpretation. If the soft patch were structural, we would expect to see resale premiums continuing to decline as consumer perception of the brand erodes. Instead, the premiums have stabilized and even improved sequentially. This suggests that the underlying demand for Hermès products remains strong, and that the brand's "growth formula" is intact.

The report points to several catalysts that could reignite momentum in 2027, including a new menswear creative director and the launch of haute couture. These are significant product and marketing events that could refocus consumer attention on Hermès. In the meantime, more innovative marketing or product activities could help bridge the gap.

The key takeaway for investors is that the current soft patch is a buying opportunity, not a warning sign. The resale data provides the evidence that the brand's fundamental value proposition has not been compromised.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Relationship Between Auction Volumes and Premium Durability

One of the most interesting findings in the report is the increase in auction volumes. The expanded tracker shows that auction volumes in the second quarter of 2026 are running 26 percent above the level observed in the first two months of the second quarter of 2025. This is a significant increase, and it raises an important question that the report does not fully resolve: is the increase in volume a sign of healthy market liquidity, or is it a sign that more owners are choosing to sell?

If the increase in volume is driven by a growing number of collectors and investors who are rotating out of Hermès and into other assets, that would be a bearish signal. It would suggest that the marginal buyer is less enthusiastic than the marginal seller. If, on the other hand, the increase in volume is driven by a natural expansion of the secondary market as more bags enter circulation, then it is a neutral-to-positive signal.

The report provides the data but does not make a definitive judgment on this question. The fact that premiums have stabilized even as volumes have increased is a positive sign, but it is not conclusive. Investors who want to fully understand the dynamics of the Hermès resale market will need to dig deeper into the composition of the sellers and the motivations behind the increased auction activity.

This is an area where the full report, with its underlying dataset, would provide valuable additional context. The charts and data that accompany the analysis allow for a more nuanced interpretation of the volume-premium relationship.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: How to Use Resale Data in Your Hermès Thesis

For investors who are trying to build a position in Hermès or adjust an existing one, the resale data provides a clear framework for decision-making. The framework has three components: trend, level, and regional composition.

The first component is trend. Is the resale premium rising, falling, or stable? The current data shows a sequential improvement from 1.2x to 1.5x in the core tracker. That is a positive trend. Investors should monitor this trend on a quarterly basis and adjust their conviction accordingly. If the trend turns negative again, it would be a reason to reduce exposure.

The second component is level. Where does the premium sit relative to historical norms? The current level of 1.5x for the core tracker is below the long-term average of approximately 1.6x to 1.7x. That means there is room for further improvement if demand strengthens. If the premium were to rise above 2.0x again, it would signal that supply constraints are becoming extreme, which could lead to faster primary market price increases.

The third component is regional composition. Investors should pay attention to whether the recovery is broad-based or concentrated in specific geographies. The current data shows strength in Europe and the United States and weakness in Hong Kong. A sustained recovery in Hong Kong would be the most powerful signal that the China headwinds are abating.

This framework allows investors to move beyond the noise of quarterly earnings and focus on the underlying drivers of brand value. It is a more strategic approach to analyzing Hermès than simply looking at revenue growth or margin expansion.
