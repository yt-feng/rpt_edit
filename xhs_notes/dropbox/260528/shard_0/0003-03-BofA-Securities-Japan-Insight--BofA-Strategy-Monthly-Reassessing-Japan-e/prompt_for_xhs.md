# Japan Insight: BofA Strategy Monthly

# Reassessing Japan equities in the age of AI, hardware and physical assets

Investment Strategy

# Reversal in merits of industrial structure: Hardware and physical assets now hold an advantage

Although guidance this results season was conservative, in such years guidance tends to rise in a stepwise fashion as 1Q and 2Q results are released. However, the risk is that inflation pushes up interest rates, but in the US, this is in line with stronger-than-expected economic data. Domestic interest rates have risen on concerns about fiscal deterioration, and 10y JGB yields have temporarily risen to 2.8%, but the TOPIX earnings yield is c.6%. On the other hand, a positive scenario is that rising US long-term interest rates force President Trump to soften his stance, leading to the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz.

It is also important to note that the medium-term attractiveness of Japanese equities is increasing. Japanese companies provide semiconductor manufacturing equipment (SPE), components and materials that are essential to the AI supply chain. In addition, supply chains and production facilities are being rebuilt globally to mitigate geopolitical threats, and wealth is concentrated in these areas. Now is the time to reassess Japan's industrial structure, which has strengths in hardware and physical assets that are easy to pass on to prices. Previous catalysts, such as the shift to an inflationary economy and the acceleration of corporate reform, are also alive and well.

# Initial AI rally has passed, but uptrend likely to continue

From a stock-selection perspective, we believe it is time to focus on AI stocks while diversifying across sectors. The initial rally in AI market has passed, but the uptrend is likely to continue. If the Strait of Hormuz is reopened, high-beta AI stocks have upside potential in a phase where only expectations are rising, and stocks that benefit from fundamentals have upside potential after the Strait is actually reopened. As a basic view, we believe sectors that were the main preference of investors before the closure of the Strait of Hormuz should be included in portfolios. If gradual yen appreciation emerges, there may be opportunities to re-evaluate consumer-related sectors that have historically been disadvantaged.

# Strong earnings, but guidance is conservative

Japanese equities have surged since April, even as the Strait of Hormuz effectively remains closed. AI stocks were the main driver of the rally. A pullback from the rapid rise is naturally possible, but the increase in share prices is natural. This rally is the result of the global dominance of AI, hardware and real assets, and the superiority of Japan's industrial structure. We believe Japanese equities are headed upward over the medium term.

Here, we summarize recent developments. First, announcements of full-year results, which have run their course, have set new record highs for profits, and guidance also calls for profit growth (see our 18 May Japan Equity Strategy). Looking at the contribution to guidance by sector, the electrical equipment sector where many AI stocks belong stands out in the manufacturing industry (Exhibit 3). In the non-manufacturing industry, sectors heavily affected by the Strait of Hormuz closure have seen a decline in profits, but profit growth in the banking sector has offset this to a considerable extent (Exhibit 4). Profit growth in AI and banks is supporting overall earnings.
