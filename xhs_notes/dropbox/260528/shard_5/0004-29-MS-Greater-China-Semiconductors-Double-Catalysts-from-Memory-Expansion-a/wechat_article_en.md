# China's Semiconductor Equipment Market Is Not Just Growing—It Is Restructuring Around Memory and 3D Architecture

The conventional narrative about China's semiconductor equipment market is that it is large, growing, and constrained by export controls. That framing is now dangerously incomplete. A structural shift is underway that will reshape not only the size of the market but its composition, its technology drivers, and the identity of its primary beneficiaries. Based on a detailed analysis from a leading global investment bank, China's wafer fabrication equipment (WFE) market is projected to reach USD 48 billion in 2026 and USD 56 billion in 2027, representing year-over-year growth of 15% and 18% respectively. These figures alone are striking, but they tell only part of the story.

The real strategic insight lies in the dual catalysts that are driving this expansion. First, China's domestic memory manufacturers—YMTC and CXMT—are embarking on a capacity expansion cycle of unprecedented scale, fueled by imminent public listings that will provide long-term capital for aggressive fab construction. Second, Huawei's published breakthrough in 3D integrated circuit architecture, termed "t-scaling" or "LogicFolding," opens a pathway for China to achieve advanced-node-equivalent performance without access to extreme ultraviolet lithography. This is not incremental progress. It is a potential redefinition of the semiconductor manufacturing roadmap for a country operating under technology restrictions.

For investors and industry strategists, the implication is clear: the China WFE opportunity is no longer about broad-based localization of mature-node tools. It is about positioning for a concentrated wave of memory capex and a new class of 3D IC tooling demand that did not exist in prior planning cycles. The companies that supply etch, deposition, hybrid bonding, and through-silicon via (TSV) equipment are the ones that will capture disproportionate value. The rest of the market will grow, but it will not transform.

## The Memory IPO Catalyst Is Transforming Capacity Forecasts Beyond Prior Expectations

The most significant near-term driver of China's WFE demand is not foundry logic but memory, and specifically the capacity expansion plans of YMTC and CXMT. What makes this cycle different from previous ones is the capital structure catalyst. CXMT's STAR Market IPO, with a listing committee review scheduled for late May 2026 and a late-June listing widely anticipated, will inject substantial equity capital into the company's expansion plans. YMTC has formally commenced pre-IPO coaching, marking a critical step toward its own A-share listing. These events are not merely financial milestones; they are strategic enablers that allow both companies to commit to fab construction timelines that would have been difficult to finance through operating cash flow alone.

The numbers reflect this step-change. For CXMT, capacity forecasts have been raised dramatically: from a prior estimate of 30,000 wafers per month (kwpm) to 80,000 kwpm for 2026, and from 50,000 to 100,000 kwpm for 2028. The expansion is now multi-site, with a new Shanghai Chuansha fab coming online in 2026 and an additional Fab 5 planned for 2028. For YMTC, the trajectory is equally aggressive. Fab 3 construction is underway and set to begin ramping in 2026, with Fabs 4 and 5 on the roadmap. Capacity forecasts have been lifted to 85,000 kwpm for 2027 and 100,000 kwpm for 2028, up from prior estimates of 40,000 and 50,000 respectively.

The strategic logic here is compelling. Both companies are using the IPO process not just to raise capital but to signal commitment to their supply chains. Equipment vendors can now plan for orders with greater confidence, and the localization rate for YMTC's new fabs has already reached approximately 60%, particularly for etch, deposition, and hybrid bonding tools. This is not a hypothetical scenario. The capital markets are directly enabling a physical capacity expansion that would otherwise face financing constraints.

## Huawei's LogicFolding Breakthrough Creates a New Equipment Demand Vector That Bypasses Lithography Constraints

The second catalyst is more technical but potentially more transformative over a multi-year horizon. Huawei's published paper on t-scaling theory introduces a concept called LogicFolding, which vertically integrates digital circuit layers during wafer manufacturing to reduce interconnect lengths. In plain terms, this is a 3D IC architecture that allows chips fabricated on 5nm or 7nm nodes to deliver performance comparable to more advanced nodes, because the primary bottleneck is no longer transistor size but interconnect delay. By shortening the physical distance signals must travel, Huawei's approach reduces latency and power consumption without requiring the latest lithography equipment.

This matters because it offers a pathway around the most binding constraint on China's semiconductor ambitions: access to extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography tools. If LogicFolding can be production-proven at scale, Chinese foundries can achieve competitive performance using the deep ultraviolet (DUV) tools they already have access to, combined with advanced packaging and wafer-level integration. The paper explicitly identifies hybrid bonding and through-silicon via (TSV) as critical enablers, and it sets demanding technical requirements: hybrid bonding pitch below 2 micrometers, overlay accuracy under 0.5 micrometers, and near-100% yield through smart redundancy.

For the WFE market, the implication is straightforward but profound. The equipment demand that emerges from this architecture is not for lithography scanners. It is for hybrid bonding tools, TSV etchers, and copper electroplating (ECP) systems for metallization. These are tool categories that were previously peripheral to the mainstream WFE market but are now positioned to become growth drivers in their own right. The investment bank's analysis identifies ACM Research as a primary beneficiary, given its existing ECP product line and the expectation that LogicFolding will drive rapid adoption of TSV metallization tools. But the broader point is that the equipment market is being re-segmented, and the tools that enable 3D integration will command a premium.

## The Localization Rate Is Accelerating, But the Distribution of Gains Will Be Highly Uneven

A common assumption about China's WFE market is that localization is a steady, gradual process that benefits all domestic equipment vendors proportionally. The data suggests otherwise. According to the investment bank's estimates, China's overall WFE localization rate will rise from 16% in 2024 to 30% by 2028. But this aggregate figure masks enormous variation across different fabs and product categories.

Consider the contrast: SMIC's Jingcheng fab has a localization rate of only 22%, while YMTC's Fab 3 is at 60%. CXMT's new Shanghai fab is targeting 35%. The difference is not random. Memory fabs, particularly those operated by YMTC, have been more aggressive in qualifying domestic tools because of tighter export control exposure and a strategic imperative to reduce dependency on Japanese and Western suppliers. For foundries serving a broader customer base, the willingness to substitute domestic tools for established foreign alternatives remains lower, particularly for critical process steps where yield is paramount.

The implication for investors is that not all domestic equipment companies will benefit equally. The winners will be those whose products are already qualified in memory fabs at scale, particularly in etch, deposition, and cleaning. Naura Technology and Advanced Micro-Fabrication Equipment (AMEC) are identified as primary beneficiaries, with price targets raised to RMB 818 and RMB 550 respectively, driven by upward revisions to memory capacity forecasts and robust localization demand. But the list of beneficiaries is narrow. Companies focused on mature-node logic or niche packaging tools may see growth, but they will not capture the disproportionate upside that memory and 3D IC tooling will generate.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Viability and Timing of LogicFolding at Scale

For all its analytical rigor, the investment bank's report leaves several critical questions unresolved, and these are the questions that should occupy the attention of serious investors. The first is the production viability of Huawei's LogicFolding architecture. The paper is published, the theory is articulated, and the enabling technologies are identified. But there is a wide gap between a research paper and a high-volume manufacturing process that achieves the near-100% yield the paper demands. Hybrid bonding at sub-2 micrometer pitch is not a mature process. It is being developed at scale by only a handful of companies globally, and the tooling ecosystem is still evolving.

The second unresolved question is timing. The report projects WFE demand growth through 2028, but it does not specify when LogicFolding-driven tool orders will materialize in meaningful volume. If the architecture requires two to three years of process development and qualification before it enters high-volume production, then the equipment demand may be back-end loaded, creating a risk of disappointment in the near term. Conversely, if Huawei and its foundry partners move faster than expected, the upside could be even larger than the current forecasts.

The third question is competitive response. If LogicFolding proves viable, how will global semiconductor leaders such as TSMC and Samsung respond? They have their own advanced packaging roadmaps, including TSMC's 3D Fabric and Samsung's X-Cube. But Huawei's approach is distinct because it integrates circuit layers during wafer manufacturing rather than after dicing. If this approach offers a performance or cost advantage, it could trigger a wave of investment in hybrid bonding and TSV tools globally, not just in China. That would be a positive for the equipment companies identified in the report, but it would also mean that the competitive dynamics of the broader semiconductor industry are shifting in ways that are not yet fully priced in.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: Three Questions to Determine Positioning

Rather than attempting to predict the exact trajectory of China's WFE market, investors should adopt a decision framework based on three diagnostic questions. Each question maps to a different risk-reward scenario and suggests a different portfolio positioning.

First, how quickly will CXMT and YMTC execute their fab construction timelines? The IPO catalyst is powerful, but IPOs can be delayed, and fab construction can face permitting, supply chain, and labor constraints that are not captured in financial models. If execution is on track, the equipment orders for etch, deposition, and cleaning tools will flow through in 2026 and 2027. If execution slips, the near-term revenue visibility for domestic equipment vendors will weaken, even if the long-term thesis remains intact.

Second, what is the qualification status of domestic tools for LogicFolding's critical processes? Hybrid bonding and TSV metallization are not standard processes. They require tool performance specifications that may not yet be met by domestic suppliers. Investors should track qualification announcements and tool shipments for these specific applications, not just general WFE market share data. The companies that can demonstrate successful qualification with memory or foundry customers for 3D IC applications are the ones that will command premium valuations.

Third, is the global WFE cycle supportive or headwind? The investment bank's global WFE forecast calls for 27% growth in 2026 and 28% in 2027, driven by memory and leading-edge logic. If this cycle materializes, China's domestic equipment companies will benefit from a rising tide, even if their market share gains are modest. If the global cycle disappoints, China's growth will still be positive due to the memory and localization catalysts, but the relative outperformance of domestic names versus global peers will narrow.
