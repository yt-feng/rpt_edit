EN_B: It means the market is still growing, and consumers are signing up for more services than ever. The average household now has more than five streaming services, including free ones, and watches about three hours of video content per day. That’s up from last year.
EN_A: So the big players are getting even bigger? Is that the main takeaway?
EN_B: Exactly. Amazon Prime Video, Netflix, and YouTube are the three dominant platforms, and all three saw their user penetration increase year over year. Netflix went from under 52% to 55%. Amazon Prime Video hit 66%, and YouTube is close to 47%. The big are getting bigger.
EN_A: But Netflix’s stock has been under pressure. What does the survey say about its health?
EN_B: Despite the stock drop, Netflix has the strongest net retention score among all streaming services. Users are much more likely to cancel other services first. And the survey shows that Netflix users come to the platform with high intentionality. They know what they want to watch. That’s a sign of quality engagement, not just passive viewing.
EN_A: That’s interesting. What about the proposed merger of Paramount+ and HBO Max into a "super service"? Does the report give any clues?
EN_B: Yes, the survey results are very encouraging. Only 10% of current subscribers said they are unlikely to subscribe to the new combined service. And more surprisingly, 40% to 50% of people who don’t currently subscribe to either service said they would consider signing up. That’s a huge potential upside.
EN_A: And sports streaming is a big topic. What does the report say?
EN_B: Over 60% of people regularly watch live sports on TV. That number is down slightly, but streaming is clearly gaining. ESPN is still the dominant platform, but Amazon Prime Video jumped from 17% to 22% thanks to NFL Thursday Night Football. Netflix also moved up to 12%.

EN_B: It mentions micro-dramas as an emerging category that’s taking time from younger users, but the data is still preliminary.
