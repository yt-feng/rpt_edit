# US Wind Orders Are Accelerating Despite Policy Uncertainty — And That Changes the Profit Outlook for European Manufacturers

The most important signal in the wind energy market right now is not a policy announcement. It is the order book. Over the past four quarters, Vestas has secured 5.2 gigawatts of US onshore wind orders. In the first two months of the second quarter alone, the company announced 1.5 GW of new US business. Nordex, too, has collected three firm orders totaling 0.5 GW, building on a conditional 1.1 GW order from the second half of 2025. These numbers are not incremental. They represent a structural shift in the pace of commercial activity in the US onshore wind market, and they are happening without full clarity on Section 232 tariff policy.

For investors who have been cautious on European wind manufacturers due to US policy risk, the emerging pattern demands a reassessment. The conventional wisdom has been that uncertainty around trade policy and the Inflation Reduction Act would suppress US wind installations, making European manufacturers dependent on a slower, more politically fragile market. The data now suggests something different: US demand is recovering faster than most models anticipated, and the pricing environment has improved so dramatically that even without full policy certainty, manufacturers are booking orders at a pace that creates upside risk to profit estimates.

The implications are not limited to order volumes. The improvement in US power purchase agreement prices — from roughly $30 per megawatt-hour in 2020-2021 to above $70 per megawatt-hour today — means that the projects being signed now are fundamentally more profitable than those signed during the last cycle. Higher PPA prices, combined with less competition, rising equipment costs due to reshoring, and a higher cost of capital that has disciplined new entry, have created a market structure that favors established manufacturers with US presence. Vestas and Nordex are precisely those manufacturers.

This is not a story about a single quarter of good news. It is a story about a market that has restructured itself in a way that rewards incumbents with scale, geographic diversification, and the ability to execute in an environment where policy clarity is not guaranteed. The question for investors is whether current estimates already capture this shift, or whether the order momentum represents a genuine source of upside that the market has not yet fully priced.

## The US Onshore Market Is Recovering Faster Than Policy Uncertainty Would Suggest

The most striking feature of the current order cycle is its persistence in the face of unresolved policy questions. Section 232 tariffs on steel and aluminum imports, which affect wind turbine manufacturing costs, remain a live issue. The full implementation timeline and scope of the Inflation Reduction Act's domestic content provisions are still being clarified. In a normal market environment, these uncertainties would be expected to delay investment decisions and compress order volumes. That is not what is happening.

Vestas has announced 869 MW of new US orders in a single evening, for undisclosed onshore wind projects, without waiting for policy certainty. The trailing four-quarter order total of 5.2 GW from Vestas alone represents a level of commercial activity that, if sustained, would imply US onshore installations well above the consensus trajectory embedded in most analyst models. Nordex, which had seen its US market share decline from historical levels, is now actively rebuilding its position, targeting a return to roughly 20% market share. The three firm orders totaling 0.5 GW, combined with the earlier conditional order, suggest that Nordex's US recovery is not aspirational but transactional.

The pattern is consistent across both manufacturers, which reduces the likelihood that these orders reflect company-specific factors rather than a broader market recovery. When two major European wind OEMs simultaneously report accelerating US order books, the signal is about the market, not about individual corporate strategy.

What makes this signal particularly important for profit forecasting is the lag between order intake and revenue recognition. The orders being signed today will convert to revenue and earnings in 2027 and 2028. If current estimates for those years do not include sizeable US additions — which, according to the analysis, they do not — then the order momentum creates a clear path to upward estimate revisions. The upside risk is not theoretical. It is embedded in contracts that have already been signed.

## PPA Price Improvement Has Fundamentally Changed the Profitability Equation for US Wind Projects

The structural improvement in US PPA prices is perhaps the most underappreciated driver of the current cycle. From 2020 to 2021, US wind PPA prices averaged roughly $30 per megawatt-hour. Today, they are above $70 per megawatt-hour. That is more than a doubling in the price that developers can secure for the output of new wind projects.

This improvement matters for three reasons. First, it directly improves the economics of new wind projects, making them more attractive relative to gas-fired generation and solar, even in a higher interest rate environment. Second, it allows manufacturers to negotiate turbine prices that reflect a larger share of the project's economic value, because the developer's margin buffer has expanded. Third, it creates a self-reinforcing cycle: better project economics attract more capital, which drives more orders, which improves manufacturer utilization and margin.

The drivers of this PPA price increase are structural, not cyclical. Rising power demand from data centers, electrification, and industrial reshoring has increased the baseline demand for electricity. At the same time, the cost of capital has risen, which increases the required return for any infrastructure investment. Equipment costs have risen due to reshoring and supply chain reconfiguration. And competition among renewable developers has become more rational, as the era of zero-margin bidding to secure market share has given way to a focus on returns.

The combination of these factors means that the PPA price improvement is unlikely to reverse quickly, even if interest rates moderate. The demand side is secular. The supply side is constrained by manufacturing capacity, permitting timelines, and the physical realities of turbine production. The result is a pricing environment that supports higher margins for manufacturers who can deliver.

## The Upgrade Cycle for Vestas and Nordex Is Underway, But the Full Upside Is Not Yet Priced

Both Vestas and Nordex carry Buy ratings with price targets that reflect significant upside from current levels. Vestas has a 12-month price target of Dkr236, with a valuation methodology that combines a discounted cash flow analysis (85% weighting, implying roughly Dkr213 per share) and an M&A valuation (15% weighting, implying roughly Dkr364 per share). Nordex has a 12-month price target of €53.2, based on a DCF valuation with a post-tax WACC of 7.9%.

The critical detail in both valuations is that they explicitly exclude IRA-linked US installations. The analysis assumes that US volumes will be constrained by policy uncertainty, and therefore builds in a conservative view of the US market's contribution to future earnings. The order data now challenges that assumption directly.

For Vestas, the investment thesis rests on margin expansion through 2026, de-risking of the offshore ramp-up, and volume growth driven by higher order intake. The company is expected to deliver 90% of its medium-term target by 2027, largely driven by offshore reaching break-even. The combination of peak offshore wind capital expenditure and improving onshore margins is expected to generate significant free cash flow expansion, supporting a cumulative €5 billion in buybacks between 2026 and 2030. The US order acceleration adds an incremental driver to this thesis — one that is not yet reflected in the numbers.

For Nordex, the thesis is similar but earlier in its development. Improved pricing and order growth have supported a margin recovery, and the company is now positioned to generate higher profits and a stronger balance sheet. The US order recovery is central to Nordex's ability to reach its medium-term outlook, because the US market represents a high-volume, high-margin opportunity that the company has underperformed in recent years. The three firm orders and the conditional order suggest that Nordex is on track to regain its historical US market share, which would add a meaningful growth vector to a business that has been primarily dependent on European demand.

The key question for investors is whether the order momentum will continue, and at what point the analysts will incorporate US upside into their formal estimates. If the current pace of order intake is sustained for another quarter or two, the conservative assumption of ex-IRA US installations may become untenable, triggering a wave of estimate upgrades that could drive the stocks higher even without multiple expansion.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Policy Timing, the Pricing Sustainability, and the Offshore Risk

No analysis is complete, and the most valuable insights often come from identifying what is not yet known. Three open questions stand out.

First, the timing and scope of Section 232 policy resolution remains uncertain. The orders are being signed without full policy clarity, which is encouraging, but it also means that a negative policy outcome could still disrupt the recovery. If tariffs are imposed at a level that significantly increases manufacturing costs, or if they are structured in a way that penalizes imported components, the economics of the projects being signed today could deteriorate. The report does not provide a scenario analysis for adverse policy outcomes, and investors will need to assess their own risk tolerance on this dimension.

Second, the sustainability of PPA prices at current levels is an open question. The structural drivers are strong, but PPA markets can be volatile. If power demand growth moderates, or if a wave of new renewable capacity comes online faster than expected, PPA prices could compress. The report notes the improvement from $30 to $70 per MWh, but it does not model the probability of a reversal or the magnitude of impact on manufacturer margins if prices were to decline by 20% or 30%.

Third, the offshore wind risk is not fully addressed in the context of the US order cycle. Vestas, in particular, has significant offshore exposure, and the offshore business is expected to be a key driver of margin expansion and valuation. If offshore execution falters, or if offshore orders in Europe and Asia do not materialize as expected, the positive impact of US onshore orders could be partially offset. The report treats offshore as a separate driver, but it does not integrate the two narratives into a single risk-adjusted view.

These open questions do not undermine the thesis. They define the boundaries of what is known and what must be monitored. For investors who want to position for the upside while managing the uncertainty, the key will be to track order momentum, PPA pricing, and policy developments on a quarterly basis, and to adjust positions as new information emerges.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: How to Evaluate the US Wind Opportunity

To translate the analytical insights into actionable decisions, a simple framework can help investors assess whether the current opportunity fits their portfolio objectives.

First, assess the direction of order momentum. If Vestas and Nordex continue to announce US orders at or above the current pace for the next two quarters, the probability of estimate upgrades increases significantly. Investors should track quarterly order announcements as a leading indicator, rather than waiting for earnings reports that will reflect orders that were already announced.

Second, evaluate the PPA price trajectory. If US PPA prices remain above $70 per MWh, the structural improvement in project economics is intact. If prices decline toward $50 per MWh, the margin story weakens. Investors should monitor LevelTen or similar PPA price indices as a real-time check on the sustainability of the current environment.

Third, consider the policy risk horizon. The longer the US election cycle and the Section 232 process remain unresolved, the more valuable the current order momentum becomes, because it demonstrates that demand exists even without policy clarity. A policy resolution that is favorable to wind would be a catalyst. An unfavorable resolution would introduce downside risk, but the order data suggests that the market is already operating in a relatively policy-agnostic manner.

Fourth, compare the risk-reward profile of Vestas versus Nordex. Vestas offers scale, diversification, and a clearer path to free cash flow generation and shareholder returns. Nordex offers higher potential upside from US market share recovery, but with less diversification and a smaller balance sheet. The choice between the two depends on whether an investor prefers the lower-risk, higher-certainty profile of Vestas or the higher-risk, higher-upside profile of Nordex.

Finally, recognize that the current opportunity is asymmetric. The downside scenario — policy disruption, PPA price decline, or order slowdown — would likely result in a modest correction from current levels. The upside scenario — continued order acceleration, estimate upgrades, and multiple expansion — could result in significant returns. The asymmetry favors positioning for the upside, provided that the investor is willing to monitor the key variables and adjust if conditions change.
