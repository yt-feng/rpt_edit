# China's UHV Equipment Bidding Cycle Is Not a Spike—It Is a Structural Shift

The second batch of State Grid's equipment procurement for ultra-high voltage line construction in 2026 has reached RMB 9.4 billion, more than double the first batch's RMB 4 billion and already equal to 42.6% of the total value across all five batches in 2025. This is not merely a quarterly uptick in tendering activity. It signals that China's power grid investment has entered a new phase where the pace, scale, and concentration of orders are fundamentally different from prior years. For investors in Chinese electrical equipment, the question is no longer whether UHV spending will grow, but which companies have the structural advantages to capture disproportionate share as the cycle deepens.

The data is unambiguous. State Grid's total equipment procurement for UHV lines in the first two batches of 2026 has already reached RMB 18.4 billion, approaching the RMB 22.1 billion total for all of 2025. With at least two more AC projects expected to be approved this year—on top of the three approved in 2025 that have yet to undergo full equipment tendering—the cumulative value for 2026 could easily exceed RMB 30 billion. This is not a one-year phenomenon. It reflects a deliberate policy choice to use grid infrastructure as a lever for fixed asset investment recovery, as evidenced by the 40% year-on-year surge in power grid capex to RMB 167.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

The strategic implication is clear: the window for building positions in UHV-focused equipment makers is narrowing. The bidding results reveal a winner-take-most dynamic, with Pinggao Electric capturing 22.3% of the second batch's total orders and China XD Electric taking another 20.2%. These two companies alone absorbed over 42% of the RMB 9.4 billion pool, while 79 other entities split the remainder. The pattern suggests that as UHV tendering scales up, the competitive moat around the top suppliers only widens.

## The Concentration of UHV Orders Is Accelerating, Not Reversing

The second batch bidding results confirm a trend that began in late 2025: the top three winners—Pinggao, China XD Electric, and TBEA—collectively captured nearly half of all new orders. But the real story lies in the product mix. Over 57% of the total RMB 9.4 billion went to 1,000kV gas insulated switchgears, the highest-value and most technically demanding component in UHV transmission lines. This is not a commodity market. The barriers to entry for 1,000kV GIS manufacturing are extraordinarily high, requiring years of certification, proven safety records, and the capacity to handle massive single-unit deliveries.

Pinggao's dominance in this segment is particularly instructive. The company won RMB 2.09 billion in this batch alone, including a single package worth RMB 900 million. Its delivery schedule for 2026 calls for 15 knots of 1,100kV GIS units, a 36% year-on-year increase, and over 100 knots of 750kV units, up 7.5%. More importantly, Pinggao's market share in 750kV GIS has already reached 45% based on 2025 bidding data, with total market bidding volume growing 77% year-on-year. When a company holds nearly half of a rapidly expanding market and its order backlog stands at RMB 12.6 billion, the compounding effect on revenue visibility is substantial.

The so-what for investors is that the concentration dynamic is self-reinforcing. As State Grid scales up UHV tendering, it naturally favors suppliers with proven delivery records and the capacity to handle large, complex orders. Smaller players may win occasional packages, but they lack the scale to invest in the R&D and production capacity needed to challenge the leaders in the highest-value segments. This is not a market where latecomers can easily catch up.

## The Macro Catalyst Is Not Just Grid Demand—It Is Fiscal Policy Disguised as Infrastructure

A superficial reading of the bidding data might attribute the surge to China's growing need for long-distance power transmission from renewable energy bases in the west to load centers in the east. That narrative is correct but incomplete. The more powerful driver, as revealed in the report's discussion of power grid capex composition, is the Chinese government's explicit use of grid investment as a tool to stabilize fixed asset investment.

In the first quarter of 2026, power grid capex surged 40% year-on-year to RMB 167.5 billion. Critically, the largest increment came from civil works, not equipment procurement. NARI explained that this was deliberate: civil works spending boosts FAI faster because it occurs at earlier stages of project development. The strategy worked—China's FAI improved from -3.9% year-on-year in 2025 to +1.7% in the first quarter of 2026. NARI expects this fast growth trend to persist into the second quarter.

This insight has profound implications for investors. It means that UHV equipment procurement is not solely dependent on the underlying economics of power transmission. It is also a function of the government's willingness to use grid spending as a counter-cyclical fiscal tool. When the macro environment weakens, UHV tendering may accelerate even if the marginal utility of new transmission lines is debatable. This creates a policy backstop for equipment demand that is more reliable than pure infrastructure need.

The corollary is that investors should monitor China's FAI data and fiscal policy stance as closely as they track State Grid's bidding calendars. A slowdown in broader economic growth could paradoxically be bullish for UHV equipment makers, as the government may front-load grid spending to support employment and industrial output.

## The Report's Framework Has One Critical Gap: The International Revenue Story

The report provides a detailed breakdown of domestic UHV orders and a valuation comp table that includes Asian and global power grid equipment makers. What it does not fully address is the extent to which Chinese UHV equipment suppliers can replicate their domestic success in international markets. The report notes that Pinggao's international order backlog was only RMB 100 million at the end of 2025, adjusted downward after the cancellation of previous high-risk contracts. This is a negligible figure compared to its RMB 12.6 billion total backlog.

Yet the valuation comp table tells a different story. Sieyuan Electric, which the report highlights for fast export growth exceeding 80% year-on-year, trades at 29.6 times 2026 estimated earnings, with a 52% upside to its target price. Its price-to-book ratio of 7.2 times is the highest among Chinese private companies in the sector. The market is clearly pricing in a premium for export exposure. But the report does not explore whether Pinggao, China XD Electric, or TBEA can meaningfully penetrate markets such as Southeast Asia, the Middle East, or Africa, where UHV and high-voltage transmission infrastructure is still underdeveloped.

This is the most important unanswered question for investors. If Chinese UHV equipment makers can replicate their cost advantage and delivery reliability in international tenders, the addressable market expands dramatically. But if they remain overwhelmingly dependent on State Grid procurement, their growth trajectories will be capped by the pace of domestic fiscal policy. The report provides the data to ask this question but not the answer. Investors seeking the full picture will need to dig deeper into each company's export strategy, certification progress, and competitive positioning relative to global players like ABB and Siemens Energy.

## A Decision Framework for Positioning in Chinese UHV Equipment

For investors looking to act on the insights from this bidding cycle, a three-dimensional decision framework can help differentiate among the key players.

The first dimension is UHV order capture. Pinggao is the clear leader here, with 22.3% market share in the latest batch and a 36% year-on-year increase in 1,100kV GIS deliveries planned for 2026. Its order backlog of RMB 12.6 billion provides multi-year revenue visibility. China XD Electric is the second-largest winner but is not covered by the report, meaning less analyst scrutiny and potentially greater information asymmetry. TBEA, while winning only 5% of the latest batch, has the advantage of inexpensive valuations at 13.3 times 2026 estimated earnings.

The second dimension is export growth. Sieyuan Electric stands out with over 80% year-on-year export growth, and its valuation reflects this premium. However, its share of the latest UHV batch was only 2.1%, suggesting that its growth story is more about distribution and medium-voltage equipment than ultra-high voltage. Investors must decide whether the export premium justifies the multiple.

The third dimension is valuation and risk. TBEA trades at the lowest P/E among the covered names at 13.3 times, with 50.4% upside to its target price. Pinggao trades at 19.8 times with 29.9% upside. The spread reflects different growth expectations. Pinggao's higher multiple is justified by its dominant UHV position, but TBEA offers a margin of safety if the UHV cycle disappoints.

The framework suggests that there is no single "best" pick. The right position depends on an investor's conviction about the durability of the UHV cycle and the importance of export diversification. For those who believe the cycle is structural and policy-backed, Pinggao offers the most direct exposure. For those who want a hedge against domestic slowdown, Sieyuan's export story is compelling but expensive. For value-oriented investors, TBEA provides the cheapest entry point into the theme.

## The Unresolved Analytical Questions That Demand Deeper Study

This report provides a snapshot of a market in motion, but it leaves several critical questions open. First, how will rising copper prices affect the profitability of UHV equipment contracts? Pinggao has guided that tendering prices may increase from the second batch onward to reflect higher copper costs, but the magnitude of margin compression is not quantified. Second, will the approval pace of new AC projects in 2026 meet the expectation of "at least two" additional projects? The difference between two and four approvals would significantly alter the total addressable market. Third, can China XD Electric, as an unlisted parent of the winning subsidiary, create shareholder value for its listed entity in a way that is transparent and predictable?

These questions cannot be answered from a single bidding result. They require ongoing monitoring of State Grid announcements, commodity price trends, and company-specific margin disclosures.
