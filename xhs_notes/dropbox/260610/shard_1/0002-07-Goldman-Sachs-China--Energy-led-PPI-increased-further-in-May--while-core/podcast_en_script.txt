EN_B: Exactly. Non-food CPI edged up from 1.8% to 1.9%, and energy prices accounted for all of that increase. Without energy, non-food inflation would actually be lower.

EN_B: Yes, and the report shows the rebound is very concentrated. Upstream sectors contributed 82% of the year-over-year increase, mainly chemicals, oil and gas, and coal.
EN_A: So the pricing pressure is stuck upstream and not passing through to consumer goods?
EN_B: That is the key insight. Consumer goods PPI is basically flat or negative. That means downstream companies have no pricing power and their margins are being squeezed.
EN_A: What does this structure mean for investors?
EN_B: One thing the report hints at but does not fully answer is how long this upstream price pressure will last. It depends on global energy prices and domestic policy.
EN_A: So if we want to figure out where inflation is heading, what should we watch?
EN_B: Two big questions. First, will core CPI keep weakening? That tells us about the real strength of the consumer recovery. Second, will upstream price increases eventually pass through to downstream?
