Our monthly report summarizes key data/charts from China to give investors quick insights into China's macroenvironment and its demand for mineral resources.

China view – Growth softens on weak domestic demand: Our China economics team notes that May activity reinforces a more entrenched two-speed economy, with export-led production holding up while domestic consumption and investment weaken. Retail sales turned negative and the property sector remains fragile. Weaker govt. bond issuance and infrastructure FAI slump reflect a project-pipeline lull post-1Q front-loading. They see 2Q GDP tracking at 4.4% YoY, and expect policy to step up capex-focused fiscal rollout from 3Q, particularly support for AI computing networks, data centers, and smart grids under the "Six Networks" initiative as geopolitical tensions reinforce the imperative of energy security and tech self-sufficiency (see here).

Macro – Property outlook weakens: China’s IP was 4.5% YoY in May (April 4.1%); May PMI was 50 (April 50.3). May CPI was 1.2% YoY (April 1.2%). New property starts fell 24.7% YoY in May (April -27.1% YoY) and GFA sold fell 14.1% YoY in May (April -10.3% YoY). Our China property team expects a weaker 3Q. Secondary real-time home sales in high-tier cities started weakening in mid-May, and have decelerated even faster in recent weeks. Considering still-fragile resident sentiment, diminishing policy effects/pent-up demand, and reduced new saleable resources, the team estimates secondary home sales will turn negative y-y and primary home sales will drop further y-y in 3Q, despite the low base. ## Major Theme of the Month

## Metals in the Eye of El Niño

NOAA has confirmed an El Niño for this year and raised the probability of a very strong event to 63%. Implications for metals include risks to copper production in Chile and Zambia, and potential impacts on some aluminium, zinc and lithium supply. Thermal coal would likely see higher demand.

For further details, see metal&ROCK: Metals in the Eye of El Niño (17 Jun 2026)

El Niño raises copper supply risks: El Niño has officially emerged and is expected to strengthen, with NOAA confirming that Pacific Ocean surface temperatures have warmed sufficiently to trigger an atmospheric response. It has assigned a 63% probability of a very strong event, potentially the strongest on record. This poses notable supply risks for metals, particularly copper, as El Niño could disrupt Chilean production through floods and infrastructure damage, while drought conditions in Zambia may strain its hydropower-dependent grid and impact output.

Other metals at risk too: El Niño poses broader risks across metals markets, with drought threatening hydropower supply for aluminium and zinc smelters in China's Yunnan, while heavier rainfall in Peru and Chile could disrupt zinc and lithium production. Iron ore impacts are likely mixed as favourable conditions in Australia and northern Brazil may support output, but excess rain could hinder logistics in southern Brazil. Meanwhile, thermal coal demand is expected to rise, as hotter Asian summers increase power consumption and drought reduces hydropower reliability, driving greater reliance on coal, albeit unevenly across regions.

MS outlook: With a more mixed macro backdrop as interest rate uncertainty builds, metals have become more linked to idiosyncratic drivers, making disruptions more important. All else equal, the El Niño event should bring upside risks to prices, particularly if more severe weather is observed. We watch copper in particular where supply has already faced significant disruptions YTD (see note), and remain constructive on thermal coal into the summer, with El Niño adding upside risk to demand.
