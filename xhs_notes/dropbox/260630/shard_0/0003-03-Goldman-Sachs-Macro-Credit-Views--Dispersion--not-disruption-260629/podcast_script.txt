ZH_A: 最近有一份外资投行的研报，标题叫“Macro Credit Views: Dispersion, not disruption”，翻译过来就是“分化，而非颠覆”。这听起来很有意思，到底在说什么？

ZH_B: 简单来说，这份报告的核心判断是：现在的信用债市场，虽然看起来整体估值很紧、利差很窄，但水面之下其实暗流涌动。它不认为市场会发生系统性的危机，但强调不同板块、不同评级、不同区域之间的分化会非常明显。

ZH_A: 那这份报告真正想回答的核心问题是什么？它试图帮我们投资人看清什么？

ZH_B: 它想回答一个很实际的问题：在这样一个利差很窄、但无风险利率又很高的环境下，投资人到底该往哪儿走？传统的“买整个指数”的思路可能不太够用了，你需要更精细地选券、选板块、选区域。

ZH_A: 所以分化是关键词。那第一个关键洞察是什么？

ZH_B: 第一个洞察是利率正在重新成为信用债市场的“总指挥”。报告里有个非常直观的数据：在2010年到2021年，美国投资级债券的利差占其总收益的比例平均是43%，但现在这个比例降到了只有15%。也就是说，现在你买一个投资级债券，赚的钱更多是来自无风险利率，而不是信用风险溢价。

ZH_A: 这意味着什么？对普通投资者来说，这个变化的意义在哪里？

ZH_B: 这意味着，过去你判断信用债市场，主要看企业基本面、看违约率。但现在，你得先看利率。如果利率保持稳定，那高票息就能托住市场，利差即使紧也不会出大问题。但如果利率波动突然加大，那信用债的波动也会跟着放大。报告里特别提到，现在的利率波动率和信用利差的相关性比以前高了很多。

ZH_A: 那第二个关键洞察呢？报告里提到区域之间的界限在模糊，这怎么理解？

ZH_B: 对，这是第二个重要的点。今年到目前为止，美国信用债的表现稍微好于欧洲，但差距在缩小。更重要的是，跨区域的债券发行在增加。比如，今年美国公司在欧洲市场发行的欧元投资级债券占比超过了17%，是2017年以来的最高水平。这意味着，你不能再简单地按“美国”或“欧洲”来划分风险敞口。

ZH_A: 所以，一个美国科技公司可能在欧洲发债，这就让两个市场的风险变得交织在一起了。

ZH_B: 完全正确。报告认为，这种跨区域的流动性会继续增强，尤其是在科技行业。这就给投资人提出了新挑战：你的区域配置可能需要更动态、更灵活。

ZH_A: 那第三个洞察，我猜跟评级有关。报告里提到一个很有意思的观点，说BBB级的表现反而比AA级要好？

ZH_B: 这正是第三个洞察。在投资级债券内部，报告建议“向下走一点”，也就是偏好BBB级债券，而不是更安全的AA级。原因很反直觉：很多AA级公司的资产负债表其实是“杠杆不足”的，它们有能力、也有意愿多发债，比如用于回购或投资。这种供给压力反而会压制AA级债券的表现。

ZH_A: 所以BBB级因为供给相对少，反而获得了更好的回报。

ZH_B: 没错。报告数据显示，今年美国投资级市场里，AA级及以上的发行量占比是22%，是2017年以来最高的，而BBB级的占比则低于往年平均水平。供给少，需求又强，表现自然就好。

ZH_A: 那高收益债那边呢？报告说分化更明显。

ZH_B: 对，高收益债市场的分化更剧烈。报告用了一个词叫“a tale of two markets”，两个市场的故事。即使只看BB级，也就是高收益债里质量最高的那一档，内部的差距也非常大。这说明分化不是由最差的CCC级驱动的，而是由基本面驱动的。

ZH_A: 所以选券就变得至关重要了。

ZH_B: 绝对正确。报告的建议是在美国高收益债里超配BB级，低配B级。在欧洲则更谨慎，同样是超配BB级，但低配CCC级。

ZH_A: 说到供给，报告提到今年的发行量非常大，尤其是科技行业。这会不会成为市场的风险？

ZH_B: 这确实是报告重点讨论的第四个点。今年上半年美国投资级债券的发行量已经超过了1.2万亿美元，几乎相当于2022年和2023年全年的总和。其中科技行业占了20%，创了历史新高。报告认为，虽然短期内不担心市场饱和，因为科技公司的杠杆率起点很低，但长期来看，这么大的供给，尤其是在夏季这种传统淡季，可能会造成技术性的压力。

ZH_A: 那报告有没有提到一个大家最近很关心的话题——私人信贷？它会不会成为下一个风险点？

ZH_B: 报告对私人信贷的看法很明确：分化，而非颠覆。它的核心逻辑是，之前市场担心的零售端赎回压力，不能简单外推到占市场绝大部分的机构端。机构基金没有流动性赎回的压力。而且，今年一季度的数据显示，私人信贷的表现依然跑赢了公开市场的高收益债和杠杆贷款。

ZH_A: 但报告也提到了一些需要关注的先行指标，对吧？

ZH_B: 是的，报告非常坦诚地指出，一季度数据是滞后的。它特别提到了两个需要密切跟踪的指标：一个是新增的“非应计状态”贷款规模，另一个是“修改为实物支付”的情况。这两个指标在一季度有所上升，虽然规模还不算大，但值得警惕。

ZH_A: 所以，整个报告看下来，好像留下了一些没有完全展开的问题。比如，AI带来的颠覆性风险到底有多大？

ZH_B: 这正是我们最后要说的悬念。报告提到，软件行业在私人信贷和杠杆贷款市场里都是最大的权重行业。如果AI真的对软件行业造成颠覆，那这两个市场都会受到冲击。但报告也指出，这种颠覆“尚未被证实”。所以，这其实就是一份完整报告里会继续深入分析的关键问题。

ZH_A: 那另一个悬念，是不是跟利率政策有关？

ZH_B: 没错，报告最后也提到了，虽然美联储加息不是基准情景，但如果通胀意外反弹，导致利率再次上升，那对于浮动利率的借款人来说，就是雪上加霜。尤其是在私人信贷和杠杆贷款市场已经面临软件行业潜在风险的时候，央行的反应函数就变得至关重要。这些都是在完整报告里会进一步展开的深度分析。

EN_A: A global investment bank just published a report with a pretty telling title: "Macro Credit Views: Dispersion, not Disruption." So what's the big idea here?

EN_B: The core message is that while credit spreads look historically tight and the market seems calm on the surface, there's a lot of action happening underneath. The report doesn't see a systemic crisis coming. Instead, it argues that the real story is about divergence—between sectors, ratings, and regions.

EN_A: So what's the single biggest question this report is trying to answer for investors?

EN_B: It's trying to answer a very practical one: in a world where spreads are tight but risk-free rates are high, where do you put your money? The old playbook of just buying the index might not work. You need to be much more selective.

EN_A: Let's start with the first big insight. The report says rates are now in the driver's seat for credit.

EN_B: Exactly. The report shows that from 2010 to 2021, credit spreads made up about 43% of the total yield for US investment-grade bonds. Today, that number has dropped to just 15%. So most of your return is now coming from the risk-free rate, not the credit risk premium.

EN_A: What does that mean for an investor in plain English?

EN_B: It means you have to watch the bond market more than the companies. If rates stay stable, the high coupon income will support the market, even if spreads are tight. But if rate volatility spikes, credit spreads will follow. The report notes that the correlation between rate volatility and credit spreads has become much higher recently.

EN_A: The second insight is about the blurring of regional lines. What's happening there?

EN_B: So far this year, US credit has slightly outperformed European credit, but the gap is narrowing. More importantly, the report points out that cross-border issuance is on the rise. For example, US companies have issued more than 17% of all euro-denominated investment-grade bonds this year, the highest since 2017.

EN_A: So a US tech company issuing bonds in Europe means the risks are getting mixed up.

EN_B: Exactly. The report expects this trend to continue, especially in the technology sector. This means you can't just think in terms of "US vs. Europe" anymore. Your regional allocation needs to be more dynamic.

EN_A: The third insight is a surprising one. The report actually prefers BBB-rated bonds over the safer AA-rated ones.

EN_B: That's right. In the US investment-grade market, the report recommends moving down in quality to BBBs. The reason is counterintuitive. Many AA-rated companies actually have under-leveraged balance sheets. They have the capacity and the incentive to issue more debt, for things like buybacks or investments. That supply pressure hurts AA bond performance.

EN_A: So BBBs are benefiting from lower supply?

EN_B: Precisely. The data shows that issuance from AA-rated and above firms represents 22% of total US IG supply, the highest since 2017. Meanwhile, BBB issuance is below its historical average. Less supply plus strong demand equals better performance.

EN_A: What about high-yield? The report says dispersion is much more pronounced there.

EN_B: Yes, it's a tale of two markets. Even within the BB cohort, the highest quality part of high-yield, the gap between the strongest and weakest credits is very wide. This suggests the divergence is driven by fundamentals, not just technicals.

EN_A: So stock-picking is critical.

EN_B: Absolutely. The report recommends being overweight BBs and underweight Bs in US high-yield. In Europe, they are more cautious, also overweight BBs but underweight CCCs.

EN_A: The report also highlights a massive wave of supply, especially from the tech sector. Is that a risk?

EN_B: It's a key theme. Over $1.2 trillion of gross debt has been issued in the US IG market in the first half of this year alone, almost matching the full-year totals for 2022 and 2023. The tech sector accounts for 20% of that, a new record. The report doesn't see an immediate risk of saturation, but it warns that such heavy supply, especially during the typically slower summer months, could create technical headwinds.

EN_A: And what about private credit? Everyone's been worried about that.

EN_B: The report's view is clear: dispersion, not disruption. It argues that the redemption fears from retail funds shouldn't be extrapolated to the institutional side, which makes up over 85% of assets. Institutional funds don't have the same liquidity features. And the Q1 2026 data shows private credit still outperformed public high-yield and leveraged loans.

EN_A: But the report does flag some leading indicators to watch.

EN_B: Yes, it's very honest that the Q1 data is backward-looking. It highlights two key metrics: new loans going into non-accrual status and loans being amended to payment-in-kind, or PIK. Both ticked up in Q1. It's not alarming yet, but it's worth monitoring closely.

EN_A: So there are some big questions the report leaves open. Like, what if AI really disrupts the software sector?

EN_B: That's exactly one of the big cliffhangers. The report notes that the software sector is the largest weight in both the private credit and the broadly syndicated loan markets. If AI disruption materializes, both markets would feel it. But the report also says that disruption "has yet to be confirmed." So that's a key area the full report would explore further.

EN_A: And the other big question, I assume, is about interest rates?

EN_B: Exactly. The report closes by noting that while a Fed rate hike isn't the base case, if inflation surprises to the upside, higher debt service costs would be a real problem for floating-rate borrowers, especially in the software sector. So the central bank's reaction function is another critical variable that the full report would dive into.
