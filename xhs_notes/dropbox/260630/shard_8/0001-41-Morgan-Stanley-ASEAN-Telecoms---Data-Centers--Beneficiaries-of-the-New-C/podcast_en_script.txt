EN_A: So it’s not just about building more buildings?
EN_B: Exactly. The report has a section called “Data Centers 101” that breaks it down into three layers: scarce inputs, facility layer, and IT layer. The real moat isn’t square footage — it’s access to power, cooling capability, and fiber connectivity.
EN_A: And power and cooling are becoming the real bottlenecks?
EN_B: Yes. One key data point: traditional enterprise racks run at 5 to 15 kilowatts. Today’s AI GPU racks are at 40 to 200 kilowatts. Next-gen platforms could go to hundreds of kilowatts. That forces a shift from air cooling to direct-to-chip or even immersion cooling.
EN_A: So the investment thesis changes too?
EN_B: Absolutely. The report says you should not treat every megawatt as equal. An AI-ready megawatt has a very different capex and cooling spec than a traditional colo megawatt. So investors should ask not just “how much capacity,” but “what kind of capacity.”
EN_A: And where does Southeast Asia fit into this?
EN_B: The report argues ASEAN is uniquely positioned — abundant land, competitive power prices, strong subsea cable connectivity, and growing sovereign AI mandates. Singapore is already a subsea cable hub, and Malaysia, Thailand, and Indonesia are rapidly building out capacity.

EN_B: The report spends a lot of time on Singtel. Their strategy is interesting — they’re not just doing wholesale colocation. They’re moving up the stack into GPU-as-a-service, AI-as-a-service, and even their own orchestration platform called Paragon.
