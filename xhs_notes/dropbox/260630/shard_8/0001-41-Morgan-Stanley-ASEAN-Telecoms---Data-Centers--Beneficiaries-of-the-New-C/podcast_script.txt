ZH_A: 最近我看到一份某外资投行的研报，讲的是东南亚电信和数据中心，它提出了一个很有意思的判断——说我们正处在一个AI基础设施建设的“历史性拐点”。你觉得这个判断靠得住吗？

ZH_B: 靠得住。这份报告的核心逻辑是，AI硬件在变，AI框架在变，比如像OpenClaw这样的智能体架构在崛起，这直接改变了数据中心的底层需求——从传统的空间需求，转向了高功率密度、液冷和高速网络。

ZH_A: 所以这不是一个简单的“多建几栋楼”的故事？

ZH_B: 完全不是。报告里专门有一页叫“Data Centers 101”，它把数据中心拆成三层——稀缺资源输入层、设施层、IT层。真正的核心能力，不是你有多少平方米，而是你能不能拿到电、能不能散热、能不能连上光纤。

ZH_A: 电和散热现在成了最大的瓶颈？

ZH_B: 对。报告提到一个关键变化：传统企业机柜功率密度只有5到15千瓦，但AI GPU机柜现在已经是40到200千瓦，下一代平台甚至到几百千瓦。这意味着冷却方式必须从空气冷却转向直接芯片冷却甚至浸没式冷却。

ZH_A: 那这意味着投资逻辑也要跟着变？

ZH_B: 没错。报告强调，不要把每个兆瓦都当成一样的。一个AI就绪的兆瓦，它的资本开支和冷却规格，和传统托管业务的兆瓦完全不同。所以投资者要问的，不是“你建了多少”，而是“你建的是什么”。

ZH_A: 那东南亚在这个背景下，有什么特别之处？

ZH_B: 报告认为东南亚处在一个独特的位置——土地充足、电价有竞争力、海缆连接好，再加上很多国家开始推动“主权AI”战略。比如新加坡已经是海底光缆的枢纽，马来西亚、泰国、印尼也在快速扩张数据中心容量。

ZH_A: 那具体哪家公司被重点分析了？

ZH_B: 报告花了很大篇幅讲新加坡电信，也就是Singtel。它的策略很有意思——不只是做传统的机房托管，而是向上游走，提供GPU即服务、AI即服务，甚至有自己的编排平台叫Paragon。

ZH_A: 所以它想做的，是“AI工厂”的整合商？

ZH_B: 对。报告把它拆成四层——连接层、AI数据中心平台层、GPU服务层、编排层。而且它通过收购STT GDC，一下子进入了12个市场，设计容量接近2.8吉瓦。

ZH_A: 2.8吉瓦，这个数字听起来很大。但报告有没有说，这个规模意味着什么？

ZH_B: 报告认为，Singtel的数字基础设施板块，未来可能占到其SOTP估值的10%以上，而且目前市场还没有完全定价这个部分。这是它提出的一个悬念。

ZH_A: 悬念？还有哪些问题报告没有完全展开？

EN_A: So there’s this research report from a global investment bank that says we’re at a historic inflection point in AI infrastructure build-out. Do you actually buy that?

EN_B: I do. The core argument is that both AI hardware and AI frameworks are shifting — things like agentic AI platforms are changing what a data center really needs. It’s no longer about floor space. It’s about power density, liquid cooling, and high-speed networking.

EN_A: So it’s not just about building more buildings?

EN_B: Exactly. The report has a section called “Data Centers 101” that breaks it down into three layers: scarce inputs, facility layer, and IT layer. The real moat isn’t square footage — it’s access to power, cooling capability, and fiber connectivity.

EN_A: And power and cooling are becoming the real bottlenecks?

EN_B: Yes. One key data point: traditional enterprise racks run at 5 to 15 kilowatts. Today’s AI GPU racks are at 40 to 200 kilowatts. Next-gen platforms could go to hundreds of kilowatts. That forces a shift from air cooling to direct-to-chip or even immersion cooling.

EN_A: So the investment thesis changes too?

EN_B: Absolutely. The report says you should not treat every megawatt as equal. An AI-ready megawatt has a very different capex and cooling spec than a traditional colo megawatt. So investors should ask not just “how much capacity,” but “what kind of capacity.”

EN_A: And where does Southeast Asia fit into this?

EN_B: The report argues ASEAN is uniquely positioned — abundant land, competitive power prices, strong subsea cable connectivity, and growing sovereign AI mandates. Singapore is already a subsea cable hub, and Malaysia, Thailand, and Indonesia are rapidly building out capacity.

EN_A: Is there a specific company that stands out?

EN_B: The report spends a lot of time on Singtel. Their strategy is interesting — they’re not just doing wholesale colocation. They’re moving up the stack into GPU-as-a-service, AI-as-a-service, and even their own orchestration platform called Paragon.

EN_A: So they’re trying to be an integrated AI factory operator?

EN_B: Exactly. The report breaks it into four layers: connectivity, AI data center platform, GPU services, and orchestration. And through the acquisition of STT GDC, they now have a footprint across 12 markets with a design capacity of nearly 2.8 gigawatts.

EN_A: 2.8 gigawatts — that’s huge. But does the report say what that means for valuation?

EN_B: It estimates that Singtel’s digital infrastructure segment could account for more than 10% of its sum-of-the-parts valuation, and that the market hasn’t fully priced that in yet. That’s one of the open questions the report leaves on the table.
