# China’s Mobile Internet Has Reached a Tipping Point: Time Spent Is Shifting, Not Growing

China’s mobile internet ecosystem is no longer a story of aggregate expansion. In May 2026, the country’s monthly active smartphone users remained essentially flat at 1.28 billion, growing just 1.2% year over year. Total monthly time spent, however, grew 10.1% year over year, a pace that has held steady for two consecutive months. This divergence is the first signal that the market has entered a new phase: the battle for user attention has become a zero-sum game.

The data from a major app tracking service reveals a landscape where winners are pulling further ahead and established players are quietly losing ground. ByteDance’s ecosystem now commands 39.2% of all mobile time spent among the top 50 apps, up from 32.6% a year ago. Tencent’s share, by contrast, has slipped from 31.9% to 29.8%. The aggregate numbers obscure a deeper structural shift: the applications that defined the last decade of China’s internet—search, long-form video, traditional social platforms—are being displaced by a new generation of AI-native and short-form content experiences.

This is not a cyclical slowdown. It is a reallocation of user attention driven by three forces: the rise of AI chatbots as primary discovery tools, the relentless expansion of short-form drama and video, and the maturation of e-commerce into a low-growth utility. For investors and operators, the implications are stark. The old playbook of user acquisition through promotions and cross-subsidies is yielding diminishing returns. The new winners are those who own the interface between users and their next decision—whether that decision is what to watch, what to buy, or what to know.

## The First Decline in Xiaohongshu’s Time Spent Marks a Structural Shift in How Users Discover Information

Xiaohongshu, often described as China’s Instagram, registered its first-ever year-over-year decline in monthly time spent in May 2026, dropping 1% despite a 3% increase in daily active users. This is a watershed moment. Since tracking began in 2020, Xiaohongshu had been a consistent growth story, building a loyal user base around lifestyle content, travel recommendations, and product reviews. The decline in average daily time spent per user—down 4% year over year—suggests that its core value proposition is being eroded.

The most plausible driver is the rise of AI chatbots. Xiaohongshu’s fundamental appeal has been its role as a discovery engine: users open the app to search for restaurant recommendations, travel itineraries, or skincare routines. That use case is increasingly being served by conversational AI tools that provide direct, synthesized answers without requiring users to scroll through multiple posts. The pattern mirrors what search engines like Baidu are experiencing—a 21% year-over-year decline in monthly time spent for the Baidu app, driven by a 21% drop in DAU. When users can ask a question and receive a curated response in seconds, the need to browse through user-generated content diminishes.

Xiaohongshu’s response—securing exclusive online streaming rights for the FIFA World Cup 2026 in China—is a defensive move aimed at broadening its demographic reach. But the strategic question is whether live sports rights can reverse a usage pattern that is being reshaped by AI. The data suggests that the platform is now fighting a structural headwind, not a seasonal one. For investors, this raises a critical question: can any content-driven platform maintain user engagement in an era where AI becomes the default interface for information discovery?

## E-Commerce Promotions Are Masking a Deeper Weakness in User Engagement

The 618 shopping festival, which began in mid-May, provided a temporary boost to the major e-commerce platforms. Taobao’s monthly time spent rebounded 17% sequentially, and JD’s rose 19%. But the year-over-year comparisons tell a more troubling story. Taobao’s time spent grew only 7% year over year, while JD’s declined 10% from a high base set by its food delivery launch and aggressive user acquisition a year earlier. Daily active user growth was modest at best: Taobao at 1%, JD at negative 3%.

The sequential lift from promotions is becoming less effective at generating sustained engagement. Pinduoduo, which does not rely on promotional events to the same degree, recorded 13% year-over-year growth in time spent and 8% growth in DAU, with a notable 27% increase in casual users. This suggests that the low-price, everyday-value model is more resilient to the structural shifts in user attention than the promotional cycle model.

The weakness in engagement is expected to translate into soft financial results. A research projection estimates an 8% decline in customer management revenue for Alibaba and a 7% decline for JD in the June quarter. The implication is that e-commerce platforms can no longer rely on promotions to drive meaningful user growth or deeper engagement. The market is maturing, and the next phase of competition will be about efficiency, not acquisition.

## Short-Form Drama Has Become the Dominant Content Format, Displacing Long-Form Video

The most striking divergence in the May data is between long-form and short-form video. Tencent Video recorded a 31% year-over-year decline in monthly time spent, driven by a 22% drop in DAU and an 11% decline in time per user. Youku, Mango TV, and iQIYI all posted double-digit declines in time spent, with DAU drops ranging from 10% to 28%. The long-form video model, which relies on subscription revenue and ad-supported premium content, is under severe pressure.

Meanwhile, the dominant short-form drama app—Red Fruit Free Short Drama—continued its explosive growth. Monthly time spent rose 134% year over year, backed by 110% growth in DAU and 11% growth in time per user. This app, along with others in the ByteDance ecosystem, is capturing the attention that long-form platforms are losing. The format is free, episodic, and designed for mobile-first consumption. Each episode is typically one to two minutes long, creating a frictionless loop that maximizes time spent.

This shift has implications beyond entertainment. Short-form drama is becoming a distribution channel for advertising and product placement, much like short video did before it. For platforms that own both the content and the distribution—ByteDance being the prime example—the economics are attractive. For standalone long-form platforms, the path to recovery is unclear. The data suggests that user behavior has permanently shifted toward shorter, more frequent content consumption, and the long-form model has not yet found a compelling counter-strategy.

## AI Chatbots Are Reshaping the Competitive Landscape Faster Than Expected

The AI chatbot category is no longer an experimental space. In May 2026, Doubao, owned by ByteDance, reached 158 million daily active users, representing 3.6 times year-over-year growth and a 5% sequential increase. It has clearly established itself as the market leader. DeepSeek, following the release of its V4 models in late April, climbed to second place with 30.5 million DAU, surpassing Alibaba’s Qwen app at 27.9 million.

The growth rates are staggering, but the more important story is the shift in user engagement. DeepSeek’s average daily time spent per user rose 94% year over year to 17.2 minutes, while Kimi saw a 61% increase to 11.6 minutes. Doubao’s time per user, by contrast, grew only 7% to 11.1 minutes. This suggests that while Doubao has the largest user base, its users are not spending significantly more time with the app over time. The implication is that the market is still in a phase of user acquisition, and the battle for deeper engagement is just beginning.

The rise of AI chatbots is not happening in isolation. It is directly cannibalizing time spent on search engines, social platforms, and information portals. Baidu’s DAU decline of 21% year over year is the clearest signal of this substitution effect. For platforms like Xiaohongshu, the threat is existential. The question for investors is whether any of the current chatbot leaders can develop a sustainable monetization model, or whether the category will follow the pattern of other utility apps—high engagement, low revenue per user.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Monetization Path for AI and Short-Form Content

The May data provides a clear picture of where user attention is flowing, but it leaves a critical question unanswered: how will the winners monetize this attention? Doubao’s 158 million DAU is an impressive number, but its average time per user is only 11.1 minutes, and the app currently generates minimal direct revenue. The same is true for DeepSeek and Qwen. These are consumer-facing tools, not advertising platforms or subscription services.

Short-form drama apps, while generating high engagement, also face monetization challenges. The free-to-watch model relies on advertising and in-app purchases for early access or exclusive content. It is not yet clear whether the unit economics of this model can support the content production costs at scale. The long-form video platforms, despite their declining engagement, have established subscription and advertising revenue streams. The new entrants have engagement but not yet revenue.

The report also does not address the potential regulatory implications of AI chatbots becoming primary information gateways. In a market where search engines and social platforms have historically been subject to content moderation requirements, the rise of AI-generated responses introduces new complexities. The absence of this analysis leaves a meaningful gap for investors who need to assess political risk alongside competitive dynamics.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: Three Questions to Ask About Any Platform

The May data supports a simple but powerful framework for evaluating any Chinese internet platform. First, is the platform a destination or a utility? Destinations, like Douyin and WeChat, command high time spent and deep user engagement. Utilities, like search engines and AI chatbots, solve specific problems but struggle to retain users for extended periods. The best investments are destinations that are becoming utilities, or utilities that are evolving into destinations.

Second, is the platform’s growth driven by user acquisition or user deepening? The data shows that many platforms are growing DAU but seeing flat or declining time per user. This is a warning sign. Sustainable growth requires both. Platforms like Pinduoduo, which grew both casual and hardcore users, are in a stronger position than JD, which grew casual users but lost hardcore ones.

Third, does the platform have a clear path to monetization that does not rely on promotions or subsidies? The e-commerce data shows that promotional events are becoming less effective at driving engagement. Platforms that can generate revenue through everyday transactions, advertising, or subscriptions are better positioned than those that depend on periodic spikes in user activity.

Applying this framework to the May data, ByteDance emerges as the strongest player across multiple categories: short video, short-form drama, AI chatbots, and office productivity. Tencent retains a dominant position through WeChat, but its other properties are losing ground. Alibaba and Baidu are facing structural challenges that will require significant strategic shifts to address.
