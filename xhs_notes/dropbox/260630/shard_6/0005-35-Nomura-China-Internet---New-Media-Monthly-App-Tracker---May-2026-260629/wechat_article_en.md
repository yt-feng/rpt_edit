# China's Mobile Attention Economy Is Shifting Faster Than Most Investors Realize

The mobile internet in China is not slowing down. Monthly active smartphone users reached 1.28 billion in May 2026, growing a modest 1.2% year on year. Total monthly time spent, however, grew 10.1% year on year. This divergence tells a clear strategic story: the battle for user attention has decisively shifted from acquiring new users to extracting more time from existing ones. The winners and losers in this environment are not those with the largest installed bases, but those whose products command deeper daily engagement.

What makes the May data especially significant is not the aggregate growth, but the structural shifts beneath it. ByteDance's camp now commands 39.2% of total mobile time spent among the top 50 apps, up from 32.6% a year ago, surpassing Tencent's 29.8%. This is not an incremental gain. It represents a reordering of the competitive landscape that has implications for every investor with exposure to Chinese internet equities.

Yet the most provocative signal in the report is not about the giants. It is about Xiaohongshu, the Chinese social commerce platform often described as China's Instagram, which registered its first-ever decline in time spent since tracking began in 2020. A 1% year-on-year decline may seem trivial, but in a market where total time spent is growing 10%, a single percentage point decline in relative terms masks a far more serious underlying problem.

The question every investor should be asking is not whether ByteDance will continue to win, but what happens when the engines that powered the last decade of Chinese internet growth begin to show signs of exhaustion.

## ByteDance's Ecosystem Is Now the Dominant Attention Sink, but Its Lead Creates New Vulnerabilities

ByteDance's rise to the top of the time-spent rankings is not a surprise to anyone who has followed Chinese internet trends. The camp now holds five of the top 20 apps by both monthly active users and time spent share. Douyin and Douyin Lite alone account for 25% of all mobile time spent in China, trailing only WeChat's 19%. What is less understood is the strategic significance of this concentration.

ByteDance's dominance is not built on a single hit app. It is an ecosystem. Douyin captures broad entertainment time. Douyin Lite extends that reach into lighter, more casual usage. Doubao has become the leading AI chatbot with 158 million daily active users. Red Fruit Free Short Drama has exploded with 134% year-on-year growth in time spent. Soda Music and Tomato Music are growing at 79% and 57% year on year in daily active users. Feishu, the enterprise communication tool, grew daily active users 45% year on year.

This breadth means ByteDance is not dependent on any single product cycle. If one app matures, another is scaling. The strategic implication is that competitors cannot simply build a better short-video app to challenge ByteDance. They must compete across a portfolio of attention formats, from short video to AI to music to enterprise tools. Few companies have the capital, talent, or organizational capability to do this.

However, dominance creates its own risks. ByteDance's time-spent share is now so large that it is approaching the ceiling of what one ecosystem can capture. The company's next phase of growth will increasingly depend on monetizing existing attention more effectively rather than capturing more of it. This is a fundamentally different business challenge than the one ByteDance has faced for the past five years. Investors should watch whether the company can sustain revenue growth per unit of attention as its share of total time spent plateaus.

## The AI Headwind Is Reshaping Information Discovery, and Xiaohongshu's Decline Is the Canary in the Coal Mine

Xiaohongshu's first-ever decline in time spent is not a company-specific problem. It is a structural signal that AI chatbots are beginning to cannibalize traditional information-discovery platforms. Xiaohongshu's core value proposition is user-generated content that helps people make decisions about travel, beauty, shopping, and lifestyle. Users open the app to discover what to buy, where to go, and how to do things.

That value proposition overlaps directly with what AI chatbots now offer. Instead of scrolling through search results or social feeds, users can ask an AI assistant a direct question and receive a synthesized answer. The data supports this substitution effect. Xiaohongshu's daily active users grew 3% year on year, but average daily time spent per user declined 4%. More people are opening the app, but they are spending less time in it. This is the classic signature of a platform that is being used as a utility rather than a destination.

The same pattern is visible in the search vertical. Baidu's app saw a 21% year-on-year decline in time spent, driven by a 21% decline in daily active users. Baidu has long been China's dominant search engine, but its relevance is eroding as users migrate to AI-native interfaces. The Baidu keyboard and Baidu Maps also showed declining penetration. The entire Baidu camp lost time-spent share from 6.3% to 4.9% year on year.

The strategic question is whether Xiaohongshu's acquisition of exclusive FIFA World Cup 2026 streaming rights is a sufficient response. Live sports can drive user acquisition and engagement, but it is a temporary fix, not a structural solution to the AI threat. The deeper challenge is that Xiaohongshu must redefine its product experience to incorporate AI discovery without undermining the social and community elements that make it distinctive. That is a difficult product problem that few social platforms have solved successfully.

## The 618 Promotions Revealed a Structural Weakness in Ecommerce That Promotions Alone Cannot Fix

The May data captures the early impact of 618 promotions, China's mid-year shopping festival. Taobao's time spent rebounded 17% sequentially, and JD's rebounded 19%. On the surface, this looks like a healthy recovery. But the year-on-year comparisons tell a different story.

Taobao's time spent grew only 7% year on year. JD's declined 10% year on year from a high base created by its food delivery launch and user acquisition spending in the prior year. Pinduoduo, which does not rely as heavily on promotional events, grew time spent 13% year on year with only 3% sequential growth. The pattern is clear: promotional events are generating less incremental attention than they used to.

The user quality data is even more concerning. JD's daily active users declined 3% year on year, driven by a 12% decline in hardcore users spending more than one minute per day. The growth came entirely from casual users, who increased 30% year on year. Taobao's daily active users grew only 1% year on year, with all growth coming from casual users. Pinduoduo, by contrast, grew hardcore users 4% year on year.

The implication is that promotional events are attracting bargain hunters rather than building loyal user bases. This is not a new problem, but the May data suggests it is worsening. The research report projects an 8% decline in Alibaba's customer management revenue and a 7% decline in JD Retail's revenue for the June quarter. These projections imply that promotional spending is becoming less efficient at converting attention into revenue.

Investors should ask whether the ecommerce platforms are caught in a prisoners' dilemma where they must participate in promotional events to maintain market share, even as the returns on that participation diminish. The alternative strategy, pursued by Pinduoduo, is to compete on everyday low prices rather than event-driven discounts. The data suggests this approach is generating more sustainable engagement.

## Short-Form Drama Is Not a Niche; It Is a Structural Shift in How Chinese Consumers Spend Their Time

Red Fruit Free Short Drama grew time spent 134% year on year, driven by 110% growth in daily active users and 11% growth in time per user. This is not a small base effect. The app now accounts for 4.0% of total mobile time spent in China, up from 1.9% a year ago. It has surpassed Kuaishou, Toutiao, and Pinduoduo in time-spent share.

The contrast with long-form video is stark. Tencent Video's time spent declined 31% year on year. Youku declined 36%. Mango TV declined 5%. iQIYI declined 6%. The declines are driven by both user loss and reduced engagement. Tencent Video's daily active users fell 22% and time per user fell 11%.

This is not a cyclical shift. It is a structural change in content consumption preferences. Short-form dramas are designed for mobile-first, snackable viewing. They are vertically formatted, fast-paced, and optimized for the same behavioral patterns that drive short-video consumption. Long-form video platforms, by contrast, are still organized around episodic, appointment-based viewing that is increasingly incompatible with how users interact with their phones.

The strategic implication for Tencent, Alibaba, and iQIYI is uncomfortable. They have invested billions of dollars in content libraries and production capabilities for long-form video. Those investments are generating declining returns in user attention. The short-form drama model, pioneered by ByteDance, requires a fundamentally different approach to content production, distribution, and monetization. It is not clear that the legacy players can adapt quickly enough.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: How AI Chatbots Will Monetize Their Explosive User Growth

The AI chatbot vertical is growing at extraordinary rates. Doubao reached 158 million daily active users, up 3.6 times year on year. DeepSeek climbed to second position with 30.5 million daily active users after its V4 model release. Qwen grew 77 times year on year from a low base. Ant's Afu grew 11% month on month to 5.1 million daily active users.

These are impressive user numbers, but the report provides no data on monetization. The time-spent metrics are revealing. Quark Browser, which combines AI search with a traditional browser interface, has the highest time per user at 33.5 minutes. DeepSeek has 17.2 minutes. Doubao has only 11.1 minutes, down 3% month on month.

The pattern suggests that users are treating AI chatbots as utility tools rather than destinations. They open the app, ask a question, get an answer, and leave. This is excellent for user acquisition but problematic for monetization. Advertising-based business models depend on sustained engagement. Subscription models depend on perceived value that justifies recurring payment. Neither model is proven for AI chatbots at scale.

The question that the report does not answer is whether AI chatbots will follow the search engine monetization path or the social media monetization path. Search engines monetize through intent-driven advertising when users are actively looking for products or services. Social media monetizes through feed-based advertising that captures attention during idle browsing. AI chatbots currently sit awkwardly between these models. They have user intent but not sustained attention.

Investors should watch for signs that AI chatbot companies are introducing advertising formats, premium features, or transaction capabilities. The absence of clear monetization signals in the May data suggests that the industry is still in the user acquisition phase, and the monetization thesis remains unproven.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: Three Questions to Ask About Every Chinese Internet Investment

The May data provides a rich set of signals, but the challenge is translating those signals into investment decisions. A structured framework can help.

First, ask whether the company's user engagement is growing in depth or only in breadth. The data shows a clear divergence between platforms that are deepening engagement with existing users and platforms that are adding casual users while losing hardcore ones. JD's 12% decline in hardcore users is a red flag. Pinduoduo's 4% growth in hardcore users is a positive signal. Xiaohongshu's 4% decline in time per user combined with user growth suggests a platform that is becoming less sticky.

Second, ask whether the company's product is exposed to AI substitution risk. The data makes clear that information-discovery platforms are vulnerable. Baidu's 21% time-spent decline and Xiaohongshu's first-ever decline are early warnings. Social platforms, entertainment platforms, and transaction platforms appear less exposed, at least for now. But the AI substitution effect will likely expand to other verticals as chatbot capabilities improve.

Third, ask whether the company's monetization model is aligned with how users actually spend time. ByteDance's ecosystem is well-aligned because advertising is native to short-video and short-drama consumption. Long-form video platforms are misaligned because their content requires sustained attention that users are increasingly unwilling to give. AI chatbots are unaligned because their engagement model does not naturally support advertising or subscriptions.

This framework does not provide easy answers, but it focuses attention on the variables that matter most in an environment where total user attention is growing but the distribution of that attention is shifting rapidly.
