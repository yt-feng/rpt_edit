# Precious Comment: Central Bank Gold Purchases: Less Slow Than We Thought, Still Likely to Reaccelerate

We are updating our GS nowcast of central bank gold purchases because, since August 2025, it has begun to systematically undershoot flows implied by physical balances in London. Our updated 12-month moving average (12MMA) nowcast stands at 50 tonnes/month in March compared with 29 tonnes/month under the previous specification (Exhibit 1). $^{1}$

We expect central bank purchases to pick up and average 60 tonnes/month through 2026. Our GS central bank survey points to strong underlying interest in gold, and recent geopolitical developments are likely to reinforce diversification over time — both for central banks and private investors. As a result, we remain constructive on gold and reiterate our \$5,400/toz target for end-2026. That said, we are more cautious in the near term. Gold’s high liquidity makes it a natural source of cash if private investors face liquidity needs — for example, if equity markets sell off amid higher rates and weaker growth expectations linked to geopolitical risks.

We refer the reader to our Gold Market Primer for a detailed description of our GS nowcast of central bank purchases. In short, our GS nowcast infers reported and unreported buying from UK customs data because London OTC is the primary venue where sovereign buyers transact. The UK produces no deliverable gold — there are no significant gold mines or accredited refineries producing the London-standard bars — so all gold traded in London must be imported. Once imported, gold has two main paths. If it remains in the UK, it is held in London vaults — for ETFs, the Bank of England, or other institutions. If it leaves, it is either shipped to Switzerland to be recast into smaller bars for retail demand or transferred to sovereign vaults abroad. Consistent with this, our nowcast is constructed as the sum of (1) UK exports of gold bars to the world excluding Switzerland and (2) the portion of UK exports to Switzerland that we identify as central bank demand.

■ Since we infer central bank demand directly from UK trade data, the key assumption is that central bank gold flows are recorded in UK export data despite international guidelines recommending excluding sovereign gold from trade statistics.
