# The Commodity MLCC Pricing Upcycle Is Not a Repeat of 2018 — It Is a Structural Shift Driven by AI Capacity Absorption

The global multilayer ceramic capacitor industry is about to enter a pricing upcycle that will look nothing like the cyclical booms of the past. The dominant narrative has long been that MLCC pricing follows a predictable pattern of supply glut followed by temporary tightness, driven by smartphone and automotive demand. That framework is no longer sufficient. A structural change is underway: the world's largest MLCC suppliers are systematically transferring commodity-grade capacity into AI-grade production lines, and this reallocation is set to accelerate dramatically over the next three years. The result is a tightening of non-AI MLCC supply that will push industry utilization rates to levels not seen since the 2017-2018 peak, and potentially beyond.

Yageo Corporation, which controls roughly one-quarter of global commodity MLCC capacity by 2026-2028, stands to be a primary beneficiary of this shift. A global investment bank report now projects that Yageo's commodity MLCC pricing will rise by 93% in 2027 and 84% in 2028, driving gross margins to 46% and 51% respectively — levels that would generate record upside from the current NT$1,045.

This is not a forecast built on demand euphoria. It is a forecast built on the arithmetic of capacity reallocation. And it raises a critical strategic question for observers: how do you value a company whose core product line is being structurally constrained by the very same megatrend — artificial intelligence — that is driving its end-market growth?

## The AI Capacity Drain Will Remove Nearly One-Third of Global MLCC Supply from the Commodity Market by 2028

The central insight of the report is deceptively simple: AI demand for MLCCs is growing at a compound annual rate of 93% from 2025 to 2028, and this demand must be met by the same global production base that serves the rest of the electronics industry. The bank estimates that 9% of global MLCC capacity was dedicated to AI applications in 2025. By 2028, that figure will reach 32%. In absolute terms, nearly one in every three MLCCs produced globally will be destined for AI servers, AI accelerators, and the associated power and networking infrastructure.

This matters because AI-grade MLCCs are not simply the same components sold into a different end market. They require higher voltage ratings, tighter tolerances, and more rigorous qualification processes. More importantly, major Japanese and Korean suppliers are prioritizing AI orders because they command better margins and involve long-term contracts with hyperscale customers. The consequence is a deliberate, strategic shift of production lines away from commodity MLCCs — the standard capacitors used in smartphones, PCs, automotive systems, and industrial applications.

The report projects that total non-AI MLCC capacity will actually decline in absolute terms for Yageo over the forecast period, from 80 billion units per month in 2025 to 70 billion in 2028. For the industry as a whole, non-AI capacity growth will be minimal. When combined with a stable 9% CAGR in non-AI MLCC volume demand, the math becomes compelling: the non-AI MLCC industry utilization rate will reach 97% in 2027 and 100% in 2028. The last time utilization approached these levels was in 2017 and 2018, when the industry experienced its most aggressive pricing upcycle in a decade.

## Commodity MLCC Pricing Will Rise Faster Than High-End Pricing Because the Customer Base Is More Fragmented

A counterintuitive finding in the report deserves particular attention: commodity MLCC pricing is expected to rise at a faster rate than high-end MLCC pricing. This runs against the conventional wisdom that premium products command greater pricing power. The logic, however, is grounded in market structure.

High-end MLCCs — those designed for AI servers, automotive safety systems, and industrial applications — are typically sold to a concentrated base of large customers under negotiated contracts. These customers have the scale and bargaining power to resist aggressive price increases. Commodity MLCCs, by contrast, serve a highly diverse customer base spanning thousands of small and medium-sized electronics manufacturers, contract assemblers, and distributors. When supply tightens, these customers have less ability to negotiate. They also operate with thinner inventories relative to their consumption.

The report notes that inventory levels in 2025 were 36% below the peak of the past ten years. This means the industry enters the upcycle with already-depleted stockpiles. As utilization reaches full capacity, the combination of low inventory and fragmented demand creates conditions for a pricing multiplier effect. Every incremental unit of demand must be met by a price increase, not by additional supply. The bank's forecast of 93% and 84% year-over-year price increases for commodity MLCCs in 2027 and 2028 is aggressive, but it is not implausible given the structural constraints.

## The Earnings Trajectory Implies a Valuation Framework That Must Account for Peak-Cycle Profitability

The report's earnings revisions are striking. For 2026, the EPS estimate was raised by 11%. For 2027, the revision is 49%. For 2028, it is 106%. The implied EPS trajectory — from NT$11.51 in 2025 to NT$57.32 in 2028 — represents a fivefold increase over three years. The gross margin expands from roughly 36% in 2025 to over 50% by 2028. The return on equity climbs from 14.3% to 45.8%. The net debt position turns into net cash by 2028.

These numbers describe a company entering a period of extraordinary profitability. But they also raise the classic valuation challenge for cyclical industrials: how much of this peak-cycle earnings power should be capitalized into the current stock price? The report notes that the current valuation multiple is near its historical peak multiple. This implies that the market is willing to value Yageo at its historical peak valuation multiple despite the fact that the earnings driving that valuation are themselves at cyclical highs.

Observers must ask themselves whether this cycle is different. If the AI-driven capacity reallocation is indeed structural — meaning that commodity MLCC capacity will not return to previous levels even after the AI buildout matures — then the peak earnings may prove more sustainable than past cycles. If, however, the capacity shift is reversible, or if new entrants build commodity capacity in response to high prices, then the current valuation may be pricing in a peak that will not last.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Risk of Capacity Response and Demand Elasticity

No analyst report can resolve every uncertainty, and the most valuable contribution of this analysis is to highlight the questions that remain open. Three stand out.

First, the report assumes that major Japanese and Korean suppliers will continue to prioritize AI-grade capacity even as commodity prices rise. This is a reasonable assumption in the near term, but it is not guaranteed. If commodity MLCC pricing increases by 80-90% per year, the margin differential between AI and commodity products will narrow. At some point, the economic incentive to shift capacity back toward commodity production may become compelling. The report does not model this scenario.

Second, the demand elasticity of commodity MLCCs at higher price levels is unknown. If prices double, will end customers reduce their MLCC content per device, or shift to lower-cost alternatives? The report assumes volume demand grows at a steady 9% CAGR regardless of price. This is a simplifying assumption that may prove optimistic if price increases are passed through to consumer electronics and automotive OEMs.

Third, the report does not address the potential for new capacity additions outside the major suppliers. Chinese MLCC manufacturers have been expanding capacity in recent years. If commodity prices rise dramatically, these players may accelerate their investment plans, potentially capping the upside for incumbents like Yageo.

## A Decision Framework for Observers: Three Questions to Resolve Before Committing Capital

For institutional observers evaluating this opportunity, the report provides a clear analytical structure that can be translated into a decision framework. The key is to identify which variables will determine whether the thesis plays out as forecast, and to monitor them systematically.

The first question is capacity allocation. Observers should track quarterly disclosures from major Japanese and Korean MLCC suppliers regarding the share of production dedicated to AI applications. If this share rises faster than the report's projection of 32% by 2028, the commodity pricing outlook becomes even more favorable. If it stalls or reverses, the thesis weakens.

The second question is inventory behavior. The report identifies low inventory as a tailwind, but inventory is also a source of risk. If distributors and OEMs begin to build strategic stockpiles in anticipation of price increases, the reported utilization rates may overstate true demand. Conversely, if inventory remains lean despite rising prices, it confirms that supply is genuinely tight.

The third question is pricing realization. The report forecasts 93% and 84% year-over-year price increases for commodity MLCCs. These are extraordinary numbers. Observers should track monthly or quarterly average selling prices reported by Yageo and its peers to confirm that pricing momentum is accelerating as projected. Any deviation from the forecast trajectory would warrant a reassessment of the earnings model.
