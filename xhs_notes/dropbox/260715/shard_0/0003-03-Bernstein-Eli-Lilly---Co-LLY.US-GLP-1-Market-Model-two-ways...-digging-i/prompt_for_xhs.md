# LLY: GLP-1 Market Model two ways... digging into TRx channel dynamics & longer-term TAM penetration

This note updates our GLP-1 model - which contains both TRx and patient level estimates from 2026 through 2035, noting that the margin of error meaningfully increases as assumptions are layered in the outer years. We estimate the branded US market will reach \$102B in 2031, with LLY capturing \~70% of the share. See here: GLP-1 Market Model

To contextualize this 2031 TAM, these estimates assume that one in two T2D patients will be taking an incretin (up from around one in four last year), while approximately one in four overweight or obese patients will be using one of these medicines.

While there's still a lot to learn about the way this market will segment, we are already seeing channels (insurance vs cash-pay) driving differences in behavior, especially price-elasticity, and as new therapies enter the market with differentiation (mechanism, efficacy, tolerability & convenience), we anticipate further fragmentation of the market. In this model, orals reach a peak of 22% (vs injectables), while high efficacy agents such as Retatrutide are expected to generate \~11% of Lilly's incretin revenue by 2031.

This year also brings specific uncertainties associated with the launch of the first oral products and the Medicare-expansion via the BRIDGE (& subsequently BALANCE) model. While we have tempered our Foundayo expectations in the US, we emphasize that \~47% of the forecast US revenue for this product is tied to diabetes indications in 2031, an area that we believe is under-modeled (despite being submitted for CNPV approval imminently). For the BRIDGE deployment (began Jul. 1), while we have a fair confidence in the potential eligible population (18-20m o/obese) and the prices to be paid (\$245 from gov to manufacturer, +\$50 copay per patient), the greatest uncertainty is the pace of uptake and ultimate penetration of this older, more highly comorbid patient population. We remain cautious on rate of uptake in 2026, and believe that 2027 will be the major year of growth from this channel.

## US GLP-1 MARKET MODEL UPDATE: LILLY CAPTURES THE MAJORITY OF A \~\$100B US OPPORTUNITY

We update our GLP-1 market model, which now incorporates both TRx-based revenue estimates and patient-level adoption assumptions across Type 2 diabetes and obesity/overweight. The revised framework captures epidemiology, class penetration, persistence/adherence, oral vs injectable mix, payer/access dynamics, product-level share, and net pricing assumptions.

## UPDATE SUMMARY

\- We model the US GLP-1 market reaching \~\$102B in 2031, with Lilly capturing the majority of value at approximately \~70% share. While we expect Mounjaro and Zepbound to remain the primary revenue drivers, accounting for approximately 63% of Lilly's GLP-1 revenue in 2031, newer products (including Foundayo, retatrutide, and eloralintide) are projected to become increasingly meaningful contributors, collectively representing the remaining 37% of revenue.

\- Class penetration continues to deepen through 2030s. We estimate Type 2 diabetes GLP-1 penetration reaches \~44% by 2031, while obesity/overweight penetration rises materially to \~23% but remains lower than T2D, reflecting access, reimbursement, persistence, and affordability constraints.

\- Injectables remain the majority of market value, but oral GLP-1s become increasingly relevant post-2026/2027 as Foundayo and Wegovy pill scale. The model still assumes injectables represent the majority of value through the late 2030s (\~79%+ in 2030+), with oral share rising and peaking to around 22% in 2029.

## WHAT CHANGED VS OUR PRIOR MODEL?

The updated model reinforces our view that Lilly captures the majority of US GLP-1 value over time, with Lilly's modeled US share reaching approximately \~70% by 2031. We updated asset-level estimates to reflect recent market dynamics:

\- Mounjaro - US: Near-term reset to reflect latest commentary on pricing & script trends - longer term changes within the margins of error.

\- Mounjaro - ex-US: Higher across all periods, reflecting stronger ex-US adoption assumptions.

\- Zepbound: Modestly lower near term to reflect latest commentary on pricing - esp. CVS formulary for Oct 1 onwards; higher by 2030 as obesity penetration deepens & differentiation of future competitors weaker (largest uncertainty associated with role of Amylins vs Zepbound).

\- Foundayo: Lower across all periods based on more tempered US launch and greater acceptance of Wegovy food & water restrictions/brand name value; remain bullish on ex-US (especially given volume opportunity).

\- Retatrutide: already had a higher expectation vs consensus of Retatrutide role in long-term market - tweaks reflect adjustments to overall market model.

## EX-US ESTIMATES

The opportunity for incretins outside the US remains under-penetrated. Injectable drugs are now launched in most major markets, and for tirzepatide 75% of its revenue in international comes from chronic weight management and is paid for out of pocket (as opposed to reimbursed by state healthcare systems or insurance). Data on the market outside the US is much harder to come by, and is therefore a much more opaque and difficult modeling exercise (hence the considerable beats in the Mounjaro ex-US revenue numbers in recent quarters). We extracted data from a graph presented on a recent LLY earnings call; the underlying data for which came from IQVIA MIDAS. From this we forecast various scenarios for the next 2 years. This gave us rough bookends for growth, and assuming more marginal share gains for Lilly going forward (incremental share gain is harder once you have majority share), we estimate well over double-digit growth for LLY in its international incretin business, over the next 3 years. We estimate Mounjaro ex-US revenues of \$19.7B, \$23.7B and \$25.6B in 2026E-28E, respectively.
