We tend to think many of these concerns are well understood and in some cases overblown, while we see margins as a managed outcome and the market not focused enough on the potential cost saves from AI not priced (real cost savings in the \~20-40% range possible), with new efforts around shorter-form content and more mobile formats (podcasts) and interactive gaming as un-priced call options. We do think a more aggressive buyback cadence beyond the \~\$2bn per quarter that we model this year could help alleviate some fears on capital allocation.

The Engagement Elephant: We would not be shocked if Netflix did decide at some point to stop providing the details of this report given they provide more detail than competitors and they may look to give more selective disclosure as they did with members. We would prefer that they gave us incremental information around quality scoring (aggregating things like completion rates, re-watching, thumbs up/down could go a long way with engagement quality at all-time highs), although we do understand that there could be some competitive intelligence sharing they may not want to do. After having shown strong double-digit engagement growth over the last few years, Netflix has seen y/y hours growth decelerate to the low-single digits and we have a hard time envisioning an acceleration in hours growth in the near-term. We believe this is driven by 3 predominant factors: 1) law of large numbers as they approach 200bn global hours streamed annually, 2) an international mix shift headwind (Japan watches \~60% less TV than the US, as an example) and 3) increased viewing competition, including more short-form. While we continue to think that as a premium subscription service, members vote every month with their wallet and don't value their time spent there in number of minutes, which also means it is less existential than a primarily ad-supported business model would be if engagement growth deteriorated. We do continue to think investors are overly focused on the headline hours number and that it does not correlate nearly as much to revenue growth as many fear. That said, it is an indicator of pricing power long-term and understandably investors are on-edge as we no longer have quarterly sub / net add disclosures. Ultimately, we think it all comes back to pricing power, and our survey work suggests they still have the best perceived original content and the strongest breadth & depth, along with viewer intention.

2022 Analog, Shares Bottomed \~14-15x P/E: With many asking where shares could bottom, we would look to 2022 as the last major period of growing pains for Netflix that saw subs go negative for the first time in 10 years (1Q22 -200K, 2Q22 -970K), which was impacted by the payback from the Covid hangover, along with increased competition and fears of saturation in the S-curve. Shares had bottomed at \~14-15x fwd EPS in Summer 2022 with a turnaround driven by several levers which included the password/paid-sharing crackdown (where they estimated \~100mn HHs watching without paying) and entrance into an ad-supported tier in Oct/Nov '22. We see the potential for new ad-tier markets (15 new coming in 2027 on top of the 12 existing), along with some new formats/partnerships as underappreciated revenue drivers.

Survey Silver Lining (see here): Despite investor concerns around engagement and pricing power, Netflix showed the strongest net retention score of all video streaming services (+18 = 36% to cancel last – 18% to cancel first), followed by YouTube Premium and Amazon Prime Video. While Netflix recently increased prices, the value perception remains strong with most thinking that the current standard ad-free plan pricing at \~\$20 per month is a 'great buy for the money' while they have room to >\$30 per month before it is perceived to be 'getting expensive' and would give some thought to subscribing. However, we note recent third party data suggests a larger than usual spike in churn in the US post price hikes this cycle. We use this data as a directional guide rather than to capture an exact data point. Taking the results with a grain of salt, we conservatively increase churn estimates in 2H26 to reflect this trend (see Exhibit 2).

Mixing It Up: According to the press, Netflix does appear to be testing new avenues of improving engagement and better competing with platforms such as YouTube. They inked deals with publishers that will give them access to shorter-form content (2-3 mins to 20+ mins) to better compete with platforms such as Instagram and TikTok. While the knee-jerk is that this appears somewhat defensive, we do think it is better that they test & iterate new formats and ultimately do all they can to stay as relevant as they can in a highly splintering media backdrop with more and more options vying for attention at an increasing rate. Meanwhile, the WSJ reported last week that following giving subs in France access to broadcaster TF1 recently, they could also look to do similar deals across Europe & LatAm. At the same time, they were said to be exploring adding other live channels that would continuously stream certain programs, or shows and films from a certain genre. Meanwhile, it was reported that they have also explored bundling other subscription-based streaming services, including NBCUniversal's Peacock, into its offering. It would sell those subscriptions through its main app as rivals such as Amazon, Apple & Roku have long done. Assuming Netflix can conservatively capture 20% of non-Netflix US streaming spend through this offering – we estimate \~\$1-1.2bn of incremental revenues, \$400-550mm of incremental OI or \~10 cents of EPS (FY27/28).

The Slate: In terms of notable upcoming releases, we would flag the MLB Home Run Derby (July 13), The Hawk (July 16), 72 Hours (July 24), MLB Field of Dreams Game (Aug 13), Outer Banks season 5 (Aug 20), The Gentleman season 2 (Sept 3), NFL week 1 Rams & 49ers (Sept 10), Lupin s4 (Oct 23), Six Kings Slam (Oct), the Tyson Fury vs. Anthony Joshua fight (Nov), The Adventures of Cliff Booth (Dec 23), NFL on Christmas Day (Dec 25).

Changes to our estimates and PT – We update estimates for 1) FX (stronger dollar, \~40bps headwind to revenue), 2) recent price increases (Nordics, Spain & Hong Kong), and 3) increase 2H26 US churn 50-100bps to account for 1H26 price hikes. Overall, our FY27/28 EPS comes down \~4% (see Exhibit 3). As a result of lower estimates and negative sentiment overhang (discussed above), we lower our mid-27

PT to \$90 (\$115 prior) or 22x NTM EPS (from 30x prior, de-rate given sentiment overhang/comps de-rate), largely in-line with the peer average (vs a premium prior).

Limitations of the Data and Methodology

1. Panel Representativeness: The dataset is based on a panel of approximately 9 million U.S. credit and debit cards. It captures card transactions only and does not include spending through other payment methods, such as cash, ACH transfers, or checks. In addition, because the panel represents a subset of U.S. consumers, it may not fully reflect the broader consumer population. As a result, estimates should be interpreted in the context of the underlying panel.

2. Brand Mapping: Transactions are mapped to brands using available information such as transaction descriptions and merchant category codes (MCCs). While this process is designed to be systematic, some transactions may be subject to misclassification or mapping uncertainty.

3. Data Revisions: Historical card spending data may be revised over time. Revisions can occur due to updates in the underlying panel (e.g., changes associated with the rolling panel construction methodology) as well as improvements to the brand mapping methodology.

4. The dataset tracks card transactions, as a result card replacements or switches may be recorded as churn.
