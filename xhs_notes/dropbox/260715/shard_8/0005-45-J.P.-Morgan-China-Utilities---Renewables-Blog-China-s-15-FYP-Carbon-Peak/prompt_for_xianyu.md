## China announced the 15-FYP Carbon Peaking Action Plan

China announced the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) Carbon Peaking Action Plan on July 9, 2026. The plan views the 15-FYP as decisive for reaching peak emissions on schedule. It targets by 2030 a 17% drop in $CO_{2}$ emissions per unit of GDP versus 2025 and a 25% share of non-fossil energy, while steadily peaking coal and oil consumption and embedding green, low-carbon requirements across the economy. Key measures include rapid expansion of wind/solar/hydro/nuclear and stronger grid flexibility (UHV transmission, storage, pumped hydro, demand response), cleaner substitution away from coal and improved oil/gas use with more green fuels (hydrogen/ammonia/methanol, biofuels/SAF). It also mandates industrial upgrading and efficiency retrofits, builds zero-carbon parks/factories and greener data centers, promotes circular economy actions, and accelerates low-carbon buildings and transport (including higher EV adoption), backed by tighter laws/standards, better emissions accounting, finance and pricing reforms, and expanded carbon and green-certificate markets with clearer local accountability.

Shortly after this announcement, the National Energy Administration (NEA) announced the Energy Sector Energy-Saving and Carbon-Reduction Action Plan (2026–2028) on July 10, 2026. The plan sets a three-year push to cut energy use and emissions across the entire energy chain—production through end-use—while accelerating the shift from fossil fuels to non-fossil energy. By 2028, it aims to raise the non-fossil share by about 1 percentage point per year, increase by roughly 15 percentage points the share of coal power capacity meeting benchmark efficiency standards, and build pilot low/zero-carbon mines, oilfields, and industrial parks. Key actions include retiring or upgrading coal plants, piloting co-firing and CCUS, expanding transmission and storage to absorb more renewables, and reducing emissions in coal and oil & gas operations through electrification, efficiency upgrades, and circular resource use. On the demand side, it promotes renewable-powered industrial parks and microgrids, greener transport and charging infrastructure, cleaner buildings/rural energy, and low-carbon heating—backed by stronger R&D, digital carbon management, improved emissions accounting, and expanded green power/certificate market mechanisms.

JPM view: We view most of the policy targets as largely a continuation of previous policy objectives, and most of them align with our expectations. Within the renewables space, we have OW ratings on Goldwind-H (2208 HK), Orient Cables (603606 CH), Sungrow (300274 CH), Deye (605117 CH), Arctech (688408 CH), GCL Tech (3800 HK), Daqo (DQ US), CGN Power (1816 HK), and Yangtze Power (600900 CH).
