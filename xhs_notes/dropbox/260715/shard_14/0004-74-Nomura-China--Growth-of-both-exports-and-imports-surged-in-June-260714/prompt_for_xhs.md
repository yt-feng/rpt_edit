Economics - Asia ex-Japan

# China: Growth of both exports and imports surged in June

Export growth in USD terms increased to 27.0% y-o-y in June (Consensus: 19.0%; NOM: 16.2%), far above expectations and up sharply from 19.4% in May. Import growth also rose to 36.0% in June from 27.4% in May, notably above market expectations (Consensus: 26.1%; NOM: 26.2%). As a result, the trade surplus widened further to USD125.6bn in June from USD105.4bn in May, marking the highest monthly surplus on record.

As we have flagged in recent months, robust semiconductor trade remained the dominant driver of highly elevated export and import growth in June, with the divergence between value and volume growth still wide, as chip price increases continued to overwhelm quantity growth. We estimate integrated circuits (ICs) contributed 6.5pp to headline export growth in June. Together with automatic data processing (ADP) equipment, which is also closely linked to the current global AI boom and contributed an additional 2.9pp, AI-related exports accounted for 9.4pp of total export growth. Although the proportional contribution to total export growth has moderated slightly from the previous two months, chips remain the absolute primary driver, underscoring the outsized role of the global AI supercycle. Moreover, price effects continued to play a major role in the growth of both exports and imports in June. Automobiles also remained a key driver of export growth in June, with its contribution second only to AI-related products.

Export growth to the US slowed sharply to 15.0% y-o-y in June from 37.3% in May, largely due to base effects, although it remained in double-digit territory. Export growth to the EU and ASEAN surged, with EU-bound export growth rising to 18.6% y-o-y in June from 7.3% in May, reflecting the ongoing trade imbalance, as we recently highlighted that trade tensions between Beijing and Brussels are likely to intensify in H2.

On the import side, crude oil imports continued to contract sharply, with volumes down 41.3% y-o-y in June (May: -29.0%), dragging down import growth by 0.6pp, which reflects Beijing's deliberate reductions in purchases amid elevated prices. The far larger driver was still ICs, which alone contributed 11.9pp to June's import growth. Growth of imports from South Korea rose further to 84.8% y-o-y in June from 84.3% in May, also signalling strong tech import momentum.

## The strong value growth of semiconductor trade remained fully intact

The ongoing AI-driven global technology supercycle continues to support semiconductor trade, with the price-volume divergence remaining wide in June.

\- Growth of integrated circuit exports in value terms increased further to 122.3% y-o-y in June from 111.0% in May, while it turned negative in volume terms to -0.4% (May: 2.1%), implying a price contribution of 122.7pp, which represents more than a 100% price effect and another widening from May's 108.8pp.

\- ICs contributed 6.5pp to headline export growth in June (or 24.1% of the total) with the price effect alone accounting for all the growth, underscoring the outsized impact surging chip prices are having on nominal export growth.

\- On the import side, ICs account for a larger share of imports than exports. Growth of IC imports in value terms increased to 72.3% y-o-y in June from 68.0% in May, while it rose to 6.6% in volume terms (May: -1.0%), with price effects contributing 61.7pp to June growth, narrowing from May's 69.8pp.

\- By our calculations, ICs alone accounted for 11.9pp of headline import growth in June (or 32.9%), with price effects contributing the overwhelming majority.

## China's trade growth with the US remained elevated

As favourable base effects subsided, export growth to the US dropped to 15.0% y-o-y in June from 37.3% in May. As part of the agreement reached during Trump's visit to Beijing, China has resumed purchases of US aeroplanes, which should support China's imports from the US. On 26 June, China Southern Airlines, the largest airline company in China, announced it will purchase seven Boeing jets for about USD3.62bn, with options to buy three additional jets for a total potential value of USD5.24bn.

## Surging export growth in electrical products

Export growth of electronic products in value terms continued to surge amid the global AI upcycle, primarily driven by price effects rather than real volumes. Growth of integrated circuit exports surged further to 121.9% y-o-y in June from 111.0% in May, as global chip prices remained elevated. Export growth of automatic data processing (ADP) equipment and components (mainly computers and parts thereof) remained elevated at 53.2% y-o-y in June (May: 66.1%). Growth of mobile phone exports moderated to 27.8% y-o-y in June from 43.9% in May, which was also driven by price effects due to surging input costs.

On transport equipment, growth of auto exports (including chassis) surged to 69.5% y-o-y in June from 39.3% May, amid rising global demand for EV cars. Growth of auto spare part exports also increased notably to 19.0% y-o-y in June from 5.1% in May. Growth of ship exports surged further to 42.4% y-o-y in June from 29.7% in May.

Export growth of labor-intensive products broadly improved in June. In particular, export growth of clothing, shoes, bags and toys came in at 3.0% y-o-y, -1.1%, 1.7% and -6.4%, respectively, in June, up from -4.1%, -10.3%, -4.9% and -7.0%, in May.

## Import growth remained accelerated amid surging energy and chip prices

Import growth also increased to 36.0% in June from 27.4% in May, beating market expectations (Consensus: 26.1%; NOM: 26.2%), largely driven by a pickup in major commodity imports excluding oil, as well as price effects from AI-related tech products.

Non-oil ordinary imports value growth increased to 33.5% y-o-y in June from 12.1% in May, driven by both AI-related products as well as major commodities excluding oil. Value growth of processing and assembly imports, which are more AI-related, surged further to 51.2% y-o-y in June from 44.9% in May, amid rising prices of AI-related products including memory chips. Growth of integrated circuits in value terms surged further to 72.3% y-o-y in June from 68.0% in May, while in volume terms, it picked up to 6.6% y-o-y from -1.1%.

On crude oil imports, value growth dropped notably to -7.4% y-o-y in June from 15.3% in May, driven by both price and volume effects. In volume terms, it dipped further to -41.3% y-o-y in June from -29.0% in May, despite falling oil prices.

Import volume growth for other key commodities broadly increased in June. Volume growth of coal and soybeans rebounded notably to 29.5% y-o-y and 7.8%, respectively, in June, from -7.7% and -15.3% in May. Volume growth of iron ore also picked up to 6.4% y-o-y in June from -0.4% in May. Volume growth of copper edged down to 4.0% y-o-y in June from 4.7% in May.
