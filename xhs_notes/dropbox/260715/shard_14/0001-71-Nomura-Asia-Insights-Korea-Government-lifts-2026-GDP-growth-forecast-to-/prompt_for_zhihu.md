## Upbeat growth outlook; cautious inflation view

The government has raised its 2026 GDP growth forecast to 3.0% from 2.0% seen early this year (Figure 1), reflecting the strong chip cycle and the government's active fiscal spending, including three mega investment projects. The government expects GDP growth to moderate to 2.2% in 2027.

At the same time, the government expects inflation to rise to 2.6% in 2026, before slowing to 2.1% in 2027, and it emphasized that inflation remains elevated and pledged to step up efforts to ease price pressures.

## Long-term policy objectives

The government set a long-term target to lift potential growth to $3\%$ from current estimates of less than $2\%$ (BOK, OECD), led by its mega projects including the large-scale chip cluster in the southwest region. In addition, the government's long-term vision includes Korea becoming one of the world's four largest exporters, and per capital income reaching USD50,000.

The government will maintain an active fiscal stance via a new Future Preparedness Fund, which will channel excess tax revenue towards youth, next-generation industries, regional development and education (Figure 2). Its size and detailed operating rules have not yet been fixed. Managing inflation, interest rates, FX and housing risks are key policy objectives for maintaining macro-financial stability.

## Main policy package

Supply-chain resilience will be strengthened through domestic production incentives, strategic stockpiles, overseas investment and import diversification. The semiconductor, AI data-centre and physical AI mega projects will anchor growth strategy, alongside region-led development.

The package also supports youth employment, housing and SMEs, while pursuing structural reforms including financial markets, the FX market, pension funds and the public sector..

## NOM's view: A second supplementary budget is likely, but without further KTB issuance

The government is likely to use extra tax revenue for a second supplementary budget to achieve the 3% growth target

We believe the government is likely to strengthen its efforts to achieve the 3% growth target by drawing on more fiscal resources. As is widely known, extra tax revenue is likely to amount to \~1% of GDP. The question is how will the government allocate the expected extra tax revenue if it wants to circumvent the National Finance Act, which stipulates that any surplus tax revenue shall be allocated to debt repayment.

As the government will unveil its long-term fiscal strategy in late-August or early-September following interim corporates' tax payments in August (mid-year tax filing and prepayment) and the government will consequently have more details on tax revenue available for further spending, we will have more details later on whether the government will use all extra tax revenue for further spending or will allocate some to repay debt.

Crucially, this additional revenue should fund a second supplementary budget without the need for further KTB issuance. If fiscal spending accelerates in H2 with a supplementary budget rolling out, we expect it to potentially add around 0.2pp to the growth outlook

assuming a fiscal multiplier of 0.2-0.3, posing upside risk to our below-consensus 2026 GDP growth forecast of 2.5%.

## Active fiscal spending supports the BOK's hawkish stance

On the BOK, following the government's upbeat projections, we expect the BOK to revise up its 2026 GDP growth forecast to close to the government's projection of $3\%$ at its 27 August meeting. Meanwhile, in the near term, the BOK will also likely highlight that growth momentum should remain solid through this year and reiterate its growth optimism.

That said, we believe this optimism is unlikely to lead to back-to-back hikes, as the BOK's inflation outlook has remained largely unchanged since the May meeting. We maintain our forecast for a quarterly 25bp hike at each of the July 2026, October 2026 and January 2027 meetings (taking the terminal rate to 3.25%).
