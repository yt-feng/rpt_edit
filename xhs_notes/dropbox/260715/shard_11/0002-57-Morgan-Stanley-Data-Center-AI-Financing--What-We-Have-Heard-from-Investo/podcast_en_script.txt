EN_A: AI and data center financing is one of the hottest topics in credit markets right now. We got our hands on a new report from a global investment bank that summarizes what they've been hearing directly from investors. B, what's the one big takeaway?
EN_B: The big takeaway is that investors are still adding AI exposure to their portfolios, but their sentiment has shifted from blind enthusiasm to selective caution. They're no longer just buying everything that's issued. They're now carefully weighing three specific risks: construction risk, tenant risk, and asset risk.
EN_A: Break those down for us. What do they actually mean in practice?
EN_B: Sure. Construction risk is about whether a data center can be built on time and on budget. This is relatively new to the public bond market because it used to be handled through project finance. Tenant risk is about whether the company leasing the space can actually pay rent on time. And asset risk is the long-term question: will this data center still be valuable in the future, given its location, power density, and technology?
EN_A: So investors are now asking more structural questions. What kind of bonds are they favoring?
EN_B: They clearly prefer shorter duration and amortizing structures. Amortization means the principal is paid down over time, so even if something goes wrong with the lease later, the loss is more contained. This preference directly reflects their anxiety about long-term uncertainty.
EN_A: Has this shift shown up in market prices? Are AI bonds actually underperforming?
EN_B: Yes, significantly. The report notes that since June, AI-related bonds, especially from the big hyperscalers, have been underperforming. For example, spreads on high-quality hyperscaler bonds have widened by about 24 basis points year-to-date, while the broader investment-grade index has actually tightened. That's a clear sign of sentiment turning.
EN_A: What about the high-yield market? Is construction debt there more risky?
EN_B: In high yield, the debate is less about spread widening and more about project execution. Investors are discussing potential construction delays, complex deal structures, and refinancing paths.
