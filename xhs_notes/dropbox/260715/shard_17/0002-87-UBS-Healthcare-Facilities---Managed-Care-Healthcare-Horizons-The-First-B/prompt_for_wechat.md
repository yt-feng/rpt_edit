## Seniors Healthcare Utilization Changes as They Age

Furthermore, a study published in the American Journal of Managed Care found that patients who received home health care had a 60% lower risk of readmission.

It is unsurprising, therefore, that McKinsey estimates up to \$265 bln worth of care currently delivered in traditional facilities for FFS and MA beneficiaries could shift to the home.

Not only is the US population growing older, but seniors increasingly show a preference to stay home and receive care in what is coined "aging in place". AARP's latest Home and Community Preferences Survey showed that older adults (60+) overwhelmingly want to stay in their homes (75%) and communities (73%). Reasons for this renewed desire to age in place include, but are not limited to, cost of living, maintaining existing social and familial connections, confidence in new assistive technology (doorbells, cameras, smart home devices etc.). While many (44%) feel an institutional move away from home is inevitable (mostly due to health concerns), the growing desire for at home health and aging is yet another tailwind for home health settings.

Given the expectations for increasing volumes moving towards home health settings, it is no surprise that CMS's NHE projections show healthcare expenditures related to home health care growing at a faster rate than total healthcare expenditures, with NHE forecasting home care growing \~7.8% CAGR (from 2025-2034). This is above the \~5.4% CAGR for total healthcare expenditures and the \~4.8% CAGR for hospitals, and \~4% for nursing care facilities and continuing care retirement facilities.

Personal Care Services: While many seniors remain active at 65, the demand for help with activities of daily living (ADLs) increases significantly by age 80. According to Addus, approximately 90% of adults aged 65 and older prefer to "age in place" for as long as possible. Personal care services are able to meet this demand by providing non-medical assistance with tasks such as bathing, dressing, meal preparation, and housekeeping, which are essential for seniors who are at increased risk of hospitalization or institutionalization. The need for assistance with ADLs increases with age, and with the increasing volume of older seniors, the demand for services to assist with ADLs will only increase. According to a 2018 study, roughly 20% of adults aged 85 or older needed help with ADLs, which is more than double the 8.7% of adults aged 75-84 that needed help with ADLs and compares to the 3.7% for the 65-74 age group.

## Skilled Nursing Facilities Another Low Cost Site of Care Option

Similar to home health, we see Skilled Nursing Facilities (SNFs) as one of the main beneficiaries of the aging population trends. Industry operators, such as ENSG, are looking for this to be a decade long tailwind in which it is just in the early innings. While not as cheap as home health, SNFs do offer a low cost site of care setting for payers at just \$550 per day. In addition, top operators who exhibit high quality scores are also able to produce quality outcomes for their patients, and would continue to be a discharge site of care of choice for referral managers.
