## NVS/IONS: HORIZON - pelacarsen (Lp(a)) in cardiovascular disease

Background/Market Opportunity. We see Lp(a)-targeting drugs as a potentially transformative, representing perhaps the last known significant cardiovascular target that has not yet been drugged despite being a risk factor for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) that impacts \~20% of the general population (\~1.4 billion people globally, 60mn+ people in the US) - see our deep dive here. Emerging Lp(a) directed therapies (see below) have reduced Lp(a) by up to \~80-90%+ in prior trials, albeit the potential causal relationship of lowering Lp(a) to improve CV outcomes is awaiting validation per topline data from NVS/IONS' HORIZON trial evaluating pelacarsen likely in 3Q (per NVS' guidance of an early 2H release). For pelacarsen, we model for \$2bn peak sales at a 60% PoS in our NVS model. We note key debates into HORIZON'S data have included:

☐ The translation of Lp(a) lowering to CV risk reduction;

☐ Whether greater Lp(a) reduction drives superior CV outcomes, which would benefit AMGN;

☐ The commercial outlook and readthrough to other studies if the primary endpoint is achieved only in the high-risk subgroup OF horizon and not the overall;

☐ The lack of benefit on high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, an inflammatory marker; and

□ AMGN's and NVS' trial design and baseline characteristics, particularly the isolation of Lp(a) via the optimal treatment of other risk factors (e.g., LDL-C), which is logical from a regulatory/payor perspective but likely impacted event rates.

Catalyst outlook. The Ph3 HORIZON trial of monthly subcutaneous antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) pelacarsen vs. placebo is the first large-scale (n=8,323) pivotal study evaluating the effect of lowering Lp(a) on cardiovascular risk reduction (on expanded MACE; cardiovascular death, non-fatal MI, non-fatal stroke and urgent coronary re-vascularization requiring hospitalization) - thus representing a key catalyst for the field. NVS is testing the primary endpoint in the overall population (Lp(a) ≥70mg/dL) and the high-risk subgroup (Lp(a)≥90mg/dL), where the events-driven study has >90% power to detect a hazard ratio (HR) of approximately 0.80 and 0.75, respectively.

Our analysis of the trial statistics suggests that the $\geq70mg/dL$ could achieve significance with a 12% benefit and the $\geq90mg/dL$ significance could be hit with a 14% benefit, noting management anticipates achieving statistical significance in the 13-15% range and believes that while such a range might be considered modest for LDL lowering, it could be meaningful for Lp(a). That said, uptake is a key area of debate, including Lp(a) testing rates and emerging competition with longer-acting and oral options – NVS is also investigating its next-generation, long acting DII235, which could move to Ph3 if proof-of-concept is achieved with pelacarsen in HORIZON.

■ Options commentary. Assuming that the HARBOR trial for del-desiran and the REMODEL trials for remibrutinib in MS are expected to come in late September/ early October (base don clinicaltrials.gov primary completions), the options market (Sept expiry) is pricing in a $+/-9.2\%$ move for Novartis into HORIZON.
