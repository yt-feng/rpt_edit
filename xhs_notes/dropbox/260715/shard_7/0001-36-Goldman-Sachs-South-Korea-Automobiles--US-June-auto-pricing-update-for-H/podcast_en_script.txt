EN_A: So we have a new report on Korean automakers in the US market. What is the main takeaway here?

EN_B: Look at the June pricing data. The industry average net price was about 46,294 dollars, up only 0.5 percent year over year. But Hyundai and Kia's net price was 34,717 dollars, up 2.6 percent year over year. So their cars are actually getting more expensive relative to the market.
EN_A: And yet they are selling more? That is impressive. What is driving that?
EN_B: It is their hybrid lineup. They have a very strong hybrid product mix, and US demand for hybrids is booming right now. The report forecasts Hyundai's US market share to go from 6.0 percent in 2025 to 6.4 percent in 2026. For Kia, from 5.2 percent to 5.6 percent.
EN_A: Are they on track to hit those numbers?
EN_B: Pretty much. In June, Hyundai was already at 6.2 percent and Kia at 5.1 percent. So the trajectory looks good. But here is the interesting part: while prices are up year over year, they actually fell 0.8 percent month over month.
EN_A: So that is a warning sign? Competition is heating up?
EN_B: Exactly. The report does not fully explore that yet, but it is worth watching. If monthly prices keep declining, then maybe the market share gains are coming at the cost of margins. We need more data to confirm.
EN_A: What about the stocks themselves? What is the report saying?
