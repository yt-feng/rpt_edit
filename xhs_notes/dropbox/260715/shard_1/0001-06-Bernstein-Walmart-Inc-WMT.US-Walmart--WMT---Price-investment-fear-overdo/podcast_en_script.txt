EN_B: We think it's largely overdone. Walmart has always been the price leader. Price investments are nothing new. And this time, they actually have the firepower to do it without damaging profits.

EN_B: Tariff refunds. The report estimates these refunds could be close to half a percent of Walmart's US net sales, roughly 3 billion dollars. That gives them plenty of room to reinvest in rollbacks this summer and keep both customers and the administration happy.
EN_A: So the market is overreacting to the price cut news?
EN_B: Exactly. The core argument is that the fear around margin impact from price investments is overblown. Walmart can maneuver on pricing while still gaining market share.
EN_A: But if price cuts aren't the real story, what is? What's the long-term driver for Walmart's profit growth?
EN_B: That's the real question this report tries to answer. The long-term debate is all about omnichannel profitability improvement, specifically e-commerce and retail media.
EN_A: E-commerce? I thought Walmart's e-commerce was still losing money.
EN_B: It is. The report estimates Walmart US core e-commerce had an EBIT margin of negative 6% in fiscal 2026. But they see a clear path to profitability.
EN_A: What does that path look like?
EN_B: Two main levers. First, automated fulfillment and denser delivery routes. By automating warehouses and optimizing last-mile delivery, they can significantly cut costs. The report believes e-commerce can become profitable on an unsubsidized basis by fiscal 2030.
EN_A: And how much does that move the needle?
EN_B: It could add roughly 100 basis points to Walmart US's overall margin.
EN_A: What about the second lever?
EN_B: Retail media, which is their advertising business. Right now, Walmart US generates about 4 billion dollars from retail media, which is 4% of GMV. The report conservatively estimates that share could grow to 5% by fiscal 2030, while GMV nearly doubles to 225 billion.
EN_A: So advertising revenue could hit 11 billion?
EN_B: Right. And that could contribute about 75 basis points to Walmart US's EBIT margin. Combined, these two drivers could push Walmart US's EBIT margin to 7%.
EN_A: 7% is a big jump from where they are now. But can they really scale e-commerce that fast?
EN_B: The report assumes e-commerce penetration could reach 30% by fiscal 2030, driven mostly by the third-party marketplace. 3P is expected to grow much faster than first-party sales.
EN_A: And on retail media, how do they compete with Amazon?
EN_B: The report doesn't overpromise. They don't expect Walmart to catch up to Amazon's 7 to 8 percent ad take rate anytime soon. But Walmart is making progress. They've acquired Vizio and recently bought Vibe.co, a connected TV ad platform.
EN_A: What do those acquisitions do?
EN_B: They help build a more complete ad ecosystem, especially for small and mid-sized sellers on the marketplace. It makes Walmart a more attractive advertising partner, which could drive higher ad attachment rates.
EN_A: Are there any risks or unknowns the report doesn't fully address?
EN_B: Definitely. For example, the exact cost savings from automation aren't quantified in detail. And whether retail media's take rate can actually move from 4% to 5% is still something that needs to be validated. The report flags these as open questions.
EN_A: So there's still uncertainty?
EN_B: Exactly. And that's why reading the full report is valuable. It includes detailed unit economics and sensitivity analysis that really help you understand the assumptions behind these numbers.
