# eVTOL use cases in expansion with solid policy support; EHang down to Neutral on fair valuation

eVTOL development in China continues to progress with growing commercial applications (e.g. sightseeing, logistics, and short-term transportation), evolving ecosystem, strengthened policy support and more detailed regulation. The use case initially is in Aerial Tourism (point A to B), and has gradually expanded to short-haul logistics, with long-term upside from Air Taxi/ Airport Shuttle services.

Policy support on the Low Altitude Economy: We review recent government standard, policies and regulations for the eVTOL industry. We note that the Civil Aviation Law, announced in Dec 2025, aims to optimize the allocation of low-altitude airspace resources, while the 15th Five-Year Plan positioned the low-altitude economy as an emerging pillar industry in Mar 2026 (Link). We remain positive on the favorable policies and regulation supporting UAM and the Low-Altitude economy.

Expanding eVTOL scenarios for commercialization: We continue to see multiple eVTOL scenarios across (1) air transportation, (2) aerial logistics, and (3) emergency responses. For air transportation, we expect eVTOL companies to start from Aerial Tourism solutions covering Point A to A flights in isolated airspace, before migrating to airport shuttles for Point A to B schedule flights. We also expect to see Air Taxi solutions in the long-term to provide on-demand flights from A to B. For logistics and emergence scenarios, eVTOL could provide either short-haul or long-haul logistics transportation, covering mountains and remote areas, and offer high-rise firefighting services for emergency.

In this note, we downgrade EHang to Neutral from Buy on fair valuation, reflecting our lower estimates driven by potentially longer time needed for commercial services of EH216-S, which could suggest our previous AAV shipment assumptions may have been too aggressive, and VT-35 and overseas expansion remaining in an early stage. Our new TP at US\$7.3 (vs. US\$16.9 prior). More details within.

We remain positive on the long-term opportunities for the eVTOL industry and for EHang as an industry leader. We continue to expect strong revenue growth for EHang at $+77\%$ CAGR in 2026-28E, driven by the ramp up in AAV shipments, along with improving profitability. That said, we believe longer timeline is needed for commercial ticketed services. The company has obtained Type Certificate (TC) and supported its partners in obtaining Operator Certificates (OCs), while it still takes time to start commercial services of EH216-S, pending CAAC approval under the new operational and safety requirements. We continue to view VT-35 for long-distance travel and overseas revenue opportunities as long-term growth drivers, though both remain at an early stage.

We observe the company's valuation de-rating, and reset the multiple to 3.7x 2027E P/S, which is close to its recent trading average vs. our previous TP implied 2027E P/S at 7.5x, which is close to its 2-year avg. P/S. With less upside compared to our Greater China Technology coverage average at $48\%$ , we downgrade EHang from Buy to Neutral. Potential catalysts to drive re-rating include: (1) stronger-than-expected IT macro environment, which could be supported by stronger economic growth or government support, encouraging enterprise procurement on new technology, (2) faster-than-expected commercialization, which could be supported by more new

models to be introduced for various use cases, or new business models accelerating commercialization, or (3) faster-than-expected technology migration or ecosystem development, which could further improve passengers' flying experience.

Our revenue growth estimate of $+77\%$ CAGR in 2026-28E is driven by: (1) the company's partner and subsidiary have obtained OC (Operator Certificate) of EH216S after receiving TC (Type Certificate) and AC (Airworthiness Certificate), supporting the AAV shipment ramp up, (2) EHang's capacity expansion, supporting them to capture the rising demand on AAV, and (3) Ehang is currently working closely with CAAC to fulfill the new operational and safety requirements for commercial ticketed flight services by EH216-S, based on the new regulatory framework from CAAC.

Earnings revision: We keep 2026E estimates unchanged, lower 2027E earnings to net loss of Rmb23m (vs. previously NI at Rmb143m), and lower 2028 NI by $31\%$ , mainly on lower revenues and higher Opex spending. The $12\%$ / $8\%$ revenues cut in 2027 / 28E reflects the longer-than-expected time to start commercial services of EH216-S, which suggests our previous AAV shipment assumptions may have been too aggressive, while the higher opex ratio mainly reflects higher R&D investments to support product development and enhance passengers' flying experience, which should support the company's long term growth.

## Downside/ Upside risks:

(1) Slower/ Faster than expected commercialization: The company is working with partners on eVTOL commercialization across air transportation, logistics, and emergency. If commercialization is slower or faster than we expect, it could create downside or upside to our estimates.

(2) Lower/ Higher shipment of eVTOL: eVTOL sales is the major driver of the company's revenues. Lower or higher shipment of eVTOL, driven by client demand and industry environment, could lead to downside or upside to our earnings estimates.

(3) Worse/ Better than expected eVTOL policy/ regulation: Policy/ regulation supporting eVTOL operation and capacity expansion have an impact on eVTOL company's development. If policy/ regulation is worse or better than expected, this could create downside or upside risk to our estimates.

## GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

## M&A Rank

Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
