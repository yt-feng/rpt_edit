# China: China's 15th FYP for Expanding Consumption — Supply-Side Push, Demand-Side Constraints

Bottom line: China's State Council approved the “15th Five-Year Plan for Expanding Consumption” on July 13, following a joint submission by the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) and the Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM), reinforcing consumption as a medium-term policy priority amid softer retail sales momentum. The plan consolidates existing measures into a broader framework focused on services upgrading, goods replacement demand, new consumption formats, household income and social security support, and the removal of purchase restrictions for autos. In our view, these measures are medium-term in nature and skew more supply-side; the near-term boost is likely to be limited given weak labor market conditions, subdued income expectations, and still-negative property-related wealth effects.

## Main points:

1. Retail sales have been weak this year and underscore the need for a stronger policy framework to support consumption over the medium term. During January-May, retail sales grew only 1.4% yoy, much lower than the 5% yoy over the same period last year. Against this softer cyclical backdrop, the 15 $^{th}$ FYP targets for total retail sales of consumer goods to reach around RMB60tn by 2030, up from RMB50.1tn in 2025, implying a relatively modest 3.7% annualized nominal growth rate during the 15th FYP period, below the 5.0% pace during the 14th FYP. The plan also aims to raise the household consumption rate and increase the services share of household consumption by 2030, two areas where China remains well below major DM economies.

2. To achieve this, the plan lays out several policy priorities, including a broader push to upgrade services consumption, sustain goods demand through replacement and higher-quality supply, cultivate new consumption formats, strengthen household consumption capacity, improve market access and infrastructure, and ease administrative and financing constraints. Taken together, these priorities suggest a more comprehensive, but still largely medium-term, framework for consumption rebalancing.

☐ Services consumption: The plan seeks to expand higher-quality supply across daily services and demographic/lifestyle categories, including catering, lodging, domestic services, elderly care, childcare, culture and tourism, healthcare, sports, and education/training. It also emphasizes standards, workforce training, brand development, digitalization, and local infrastructure, pointing to a more supply-side and institutional push to unlock services consumption during the 15th FYP period.

☐ Goods consumption: The plan continues to support big-ticket durable goods, including housing upgrades, auto consumption and aftermarket sectors, and smart home appliances. It also highlights product innovation, flexible manufacturing, daily consumer goods upgrades, and stronger domestic brand building, suggesting policymakers aim to sustain goods consumption through higher-quality supply and upgrading demand rather than one-off subsidies.

☐ New consumption formats: The plan prioritizes digital, green, “first-launch” (“首发经济”), experience-based and inbound tourism, supported by AI-enabled products and services, low-carbon goods and recycling systems, “first-store” and “first-show” platforms (“首店、首秀”), immersive retail and cultural experiences, and tourism-related inbound spending. In our view, this reflects an effort to generate incremental demand through new scenarios and better supply, while linking consumption policy more closely with industrial upgrading themes such as AI, smart mobility, green supply chains, domestic brands, cultural IP and international tourism.

□ Household consumption capacity: Beyond supply expansion, the plan highlights employment support, income growth, stronger social security, and higher public consumption as key conditions for more durable household spending. Targeted areas include job creation and skills training, wage and redistribution reforms, higher pension and medical insurance support, broader coverage for flexible and migrant workers, long-term care insurance, and increased public spending on education, healthcare, elderly care, and childcare. These measures are consistent with the State Council’s “Special Action Plan to Boost Consumption” announced in March 2025 and the 15 $^{th}$ Five-Year Plan (FYP) full report released in March 2026. Similar to previous high-level policy documents, specifics are lacking in terms of funding sources and magnitudes, timelines, and implementation details.

☐ Market opening and infrastructure upgrades: The plan calls for easing service-sector entry restrictions, improving access for new business models, strengthening platform responsibilities and fair competition, expanding openness in telecom, internet, education, culture and healthcare, and increasing imports of higher-quality consumer goods. It also emphasizes effective investment in consumption infrastructure, including commercial-district upgrades, community services, logistics and cold-chain networks, parking and charging facilities, tourism assets, and digital infrastructure such as 5G, gigabit broadband, Internet of Things (IoT) and computing networks.

□ Removing various purchase restrictions: The plan calls for removing unreasonable consumption restrictions and strengthening fiscal financial support. Key measures include easing auto purchase restrictions, optimizing city-specific housing policies and housing provident fund use, increasing funding for consumption infrastructure, providing interest subsidies for household consumer loans and targeted service-sector business loans, and improving paid-leave enforcement.

3. In our view, the plan largely consolidates existing consumption-support measures into a medium-term framework rather than introducing fundamentally new policy tools. We remain cautious on the pace of consumption recovery: while the plan should improve supply quality and reduce institutional frictions, the near-term impulse is likely to be limited absent stronger demand-side stimulus and given weak labor-market conditions, subdued income expectations, and negative property-related wealth effects. A more durable recovery would likely require firmer job and wage growth, stabilization in household balance sheets, and more direct demand-side support to households.
