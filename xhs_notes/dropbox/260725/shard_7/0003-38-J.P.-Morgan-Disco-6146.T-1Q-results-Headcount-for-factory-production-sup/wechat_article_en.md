The company has not indicated any structural weakness in OSAT demand outside of China, nor has it revised its medium-term outlook for this customer segment. The pullback is geographic and temporal, not secular.

For those assessing Disco’s revenue durability, the key question is whether the OSAT decline will be offset by continued strength in memory and logic. The second-quarter guidance of ¥141.0 billion in shipment value, up 4% sequentially, suggests that management expects exactly that offset to occur. The OSAT pullback is a headwind, but it is a manageable one within a portfolio that now has multiple growth engines.

The production support program extension is directly connected to this capacity constraint. When a company keeps 100 headquarters staff at the factory to support production, it is operating at or near its current capacity ceiling. The risk is that further shipment slippages could occur if demand continues to accelerate faster than Disco can add production capacity. The opportunity is that capacity expansion, when it materializes, will unlock additional revenue that is currently being deferred.

Observers should monitor Disco’s capital expenditure plans and any announcements regarding factory expansion.
