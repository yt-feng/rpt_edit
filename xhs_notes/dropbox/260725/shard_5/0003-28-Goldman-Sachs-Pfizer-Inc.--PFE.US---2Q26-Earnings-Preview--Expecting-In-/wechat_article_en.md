# Pfizer's Second-Quarter Earnings Will Be Routine, But the Stock's Fate Hinges on Two Pipeline Readouts in the Second Half

Pfizer shares trade at 8.75 times 2027 earnings, a roughly 40% discount to the pharmaceutical peer median of 14.5 times. It reflects a market that has lost confidence in the company's growth narrative and is now waiting for proof that the pipeline can replace what COVID revenues once provided. The CFO's departure in June and a recent pipeline setback in non-small cell lung cancer have only deepened the skepticism.

The upcoming second-quarter 2026 earnings report, scheduled for August 4, is expected to show results broadly in line with consensus. Total revenue is forecast at $14.56 billion, a modest 1.3% above the Visible Alpha consensus of $14.38 billion. Adjusted EPS of $0.69 sits 2.2% above the consensus estimate of $0.68. These are not numbers that will alter the trajectory of the stock. The real question is whether management uses the call to signal anything about the two clinical readouts scheduled for the second half of 2026: the Phase 3 MEVPRO-1 trial for mevrometostat in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer and the Phase 1/2 data for the GLP-1/amylin combination.

Investor patience is thin. The market has priced in a low-probability view of Pfizer's pipeline success. That creates asymmetry, but only if the data delivers. For now, the earnings call is a stage, not the main event.

## The COVID Franchise Drag Is Predictable, But the Real Revenue Pressure Is Broader Than Most Investors Assume

The COVID product lines continue to weigh on the top line. Paxlovid revenue is estimated at $145 million for the quarter, 10% below the consensus of $161 million. This is not a surprise. COVID incidence and wastewater viral activity remain low, and prescription trends for Comirnaty are down 77% year-over-year and 72% quarter-over-quarter. The company's full-year 2026 COVID franchise guidance stands at $5 billion, a figure that looks aggressive against the GS estimate of $4.1 billion and the consensus of $4.5 billion. A downward revision at some point this year appears probable.

But the COVID drag is well understood. What is less appreciated is the breadth of product-level headwinds that will compress year-over-year comparisons. Several Seagen-acquired products -- Padcev, Adcetris, Tukysa, and Tivdak -- benefited from a one-time favorable impact of roughly two to three weeks of sales in the second quarter of 2025 due to a wholesaler transition. Paxlovid had favorable rebate accruals in the year-ago period. Xeljanz recorded a one-time favorable adjustment in the United States in the second quarter of 2025, and Abrysvo benefited from a favorable net sales adjustment. These are not structural issues, but they create a baseline effect that will make organic growth look weaker than it actually is.

Xeljanz faces a more fundamental challenge. Its U.S. patent expired in early June 2026, and the loss of exclusivity dynamic was already visible throughout the month. Second-quarter prescriptions for Xeljanz are down 16% year-over-year and 5% sequentially. This is a gradual erosion, not a cliff, but it adds to the sense that Pfizer's base business is under pressure from multiple directions simultaneously.

## Eliquis and Padcev Provide the Bright Spots, But They Cannot Carry the Full Weight of the Portfolio Alone

Not all products are struggling. Eliquis continues to perform. More importantly, a net pricing benefit will begin in the third quarter of 2026 that does not appear to be fully reflected in consensus estimates. This is a genuine tailwind, and it is one of the few near-term catalysts that is not dependent on pipeline success.

Padcev is another standout. The estimate of $649 million is 3.2% above consensus, and the full-year estimate of $2.7 billion exceeds the consensus of $2.5 billion. IQVIA data suggests that the inflection in growth that began in the first quarter of 2026 has continued into the second quarter, supported by the ramp of the EV-303 population and the recent approval of the EV-304 population. Padcev is becoming a meaningful contributor to the oncology franchise, and its trajectory is one of the more encouraging signals in the portfolio.

The challenge is that Eliquis and Padcev, while important, are not large enough to offset the combined pressure from COVID declines, patent expirations, and competitive dynamics in other areas. Vyndaqel and Vyndamax are seeing a deceleration in volume growth, with second-quarter prescriptions up just 6% year-over-year compared to 15% in the first quarter, alongside rising gross-to-net deductions from competitive pressure. Xtandi faces commentary from Astellas that sales are expected to decline and growth is not expected to return. These are not catastrophic trends, but they cumulatively create a headwind that requires either a large acquisition or a pipeline success to overcome.

## The CFO Transition Creates Uncertainty Around Guidance Philosophy, But the Status Quo Signal Is Reassuring

Management had framed the first-quarter earnings call with language about upside convexity to 2026 revenue guidance, citing commercial progress across parts of the portfolio. Some investors have been anticipating a guidance raise at the second-quarter print.

That expectation should be tempered. In the pre-quarter call with the company, it was noted that the new CFO's approach to guidance is similar to that of the departing CFO, which implies a continuation of the status quo. A guidance raise is not impossible, but the leadership transition provides a natural reason for management to remain steady. The more important signal will be the tone around the second-half outlook and whether management reinforces the view that the commercial momentum from the first quarter is continuing.

Capital allocation remains a live debate. Pfizer has roughly $7 billion in remaining business development capacity after accounting for the Innovent deal, which will bring an expected $650 million in acquired IPR&D from milestone payments in the third quarter. ## The MEVPRO-1 Readout Is the Single Most Important Catalyst for the Stock, and the Statistical Bar Is Achievable

The Phase 3 MEVPRO-1 trial, evaluating mevrometostat plus enzalutamide against physician's choice of a second-line androgen receptor therapy plus enzalutamide or docetaxel in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer, has a primary completion date of July 2026. A readout is expected in the second half of the year. This is one of only two major clinical catalysts for Pfizer in 2026, alongside the GLP-1/amylin combination data. After the Sig Vedotin setback in second-line non-small cell lung cancer, investor tolerance for pipeline disappointments is near zero.

The market for metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer exceeds $11 billion across darolutamide, apalutamide, and enzalutamide. A successful mevrometostat launch would also extend the life of Pfizer's Xtandi franchise, which generated $2.2 billion in U.S. revenue in 2025 but faces U.S. substance patent expiration in August 2027. The strategic logic is clear: mevrometostat is not just a new product, it is a franchise extension.

The clinical data supports cautious optimism. In the dose-expansion portion of the Phase 1 study, the 1,250 mg BID dose on an empty stomach combined with enzalutamide showed improved outcomes versus enzalutamide alone, but gastrointestinal tolerability was a concern. Updated data presented at ASCO GU 2026 from two patient cohorts using a lower dose of 875 mg BID with food demonstrated improved tolerability while maintaining equivalent drug exposure. In cohort 2A, which included patients previously treated with abiraterone and enzalutamide, the regimen showed a median radiographic progression-free survival of 14.3 months and a PSA50 rate of 40%. In cohort 2C, which included patients previously treated with abiraterone who were enzalutamide-naive, median progression-free survival was not estimable and the PSA50 rate was 42.9%. For context, Pluvicto demonstrated a median progression-free survival of 11.6 months in its Phase 3 PSMAfore study in the same population.

The statistical bar for MEVPRO-1 appears achievable. The GS powering assumption for mevrometostat is a median progression-free survival of 10 months. The Phase 1 data showed 14.3 months in a more heavily pretreated population. The swing factors are the performance of the control arm and crossover dynamics, but the baseline probability of success is reasonable. On potential stock impact, every 0.25x move in the price-to-earnings multiple represents approximately 3% upside or downside from the current 9.0x multiple used in the GS valuation. A successful readout could prompt a sentiment change that justifies a multiple expansion of that magnitude or more.

## A Decision Framework for Positioning Ahead of the Second-Half Catalysts

Investors evaluating Pfizer at current levels should consider three scenarios, each with distinct implications for positioning.

The base case is that second-quarter earnings come in as expected, management maintains full-year guidance, and the stock trades in a narrow range until the MEVPRO-1 readout. In this scenario, the 7% dividend yield provides a floor, but the absence of a near-term catalyst limits upside. The appropriate positioning is to remain steady with a focus on collecting the dividend while waiting for clarity.

The bull case requires a successful MEVPRO-1 readout. If the data shows a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in progression-free survival with manageable tolerability, the stock could re-rate toward the pharma peer median multiple of 14.5 times. That would imply a stock price in the low $30s, representing roughly 25% upside from current levels. The key leading indicators to watch are whether management provides an update on the readout timeline during the earnings call and whether the tone around the prostate cancer franchise becomes more confident.

The bear case involves a negative readout, a significant guidance cut, or both. In that scenario, the current 8.75 times multiple could compress further toward 7 times, implying a stock price in the low $20s. The dividend would come under renewed scrutiny, and the capital allocation debate would intensify. The risk is asymmetric to the downside if the pipeline fails to deliver, but the current valuation already embeds a significant discount that partially protects against further compression.

The most actionable signal in the near term is not the earnings number itself but the qualitative framing around the second-half pipeline catalysts and capital allocation priorities. Investors should listen for three specific things on the call: whether management provides any update on the MEVPRO-1 readout timeline, whether the interim CFO signals any change in capital allocation philosophy, and whether the tone around full-year guidance suggests confidence or caution.
