【金字塔原理写作原则】
1. 结论先行：文章开头先回答“这份报告最值得看的判断是什么”，而不是介绍背景。
2. 统领思想：全文只能服务一个主判断，避免变成摘要合集。
3. 纵向回答：每一层都要回答上一层提出的“为什么”或“所以呢”。
4. 横向 MECE：每个一级小节必须彼此独立、共同支撑主判断，避免重叠。
5. Synthesis over summary：不要复述报告段落，要提炼“这些事实合在一起意味着什么”。
6. So what：每个小节末尾必须落到对行业、公司、竞争格局、市场定价或读者观察框架的含义。
7. 信息型小标题：所有 `##` 小标题必须是完整、中性的事实句，不能是目录标签，也不能评价好坏或制造冲突。

However, we expect him to stop short of indicating his threshold for rate hikes or potential actions the Fed should take if inflation remains elevated. We expect Warsh to revisit several dovish talking points, including his belief in the disinflationary impact of AI, skepticism on the quality of official inflation data, and possibly the role of money growth in the inflation outlook.

There will likely be two hawkish dissents in favor of a rate hike, from Dallas president Logan and Cleveland president Hammack. A third dissent from Minneapolis president Kashkari is a risk.

Centrist and dovish officials signaled some possibility of future hikes in the run-up to the July blackout period, but most indicated that incremental disinflation would support the case to remain on hold. Given the slowdown in June inflation data, this suggests a solid majority of voters are comfortable with a “wait-and-see” approach.

Although markets are pricing in a decent probability of a rate hike next week, Warsh's inherent dovish bias as well as a potential risk of excessively tightening financial conditions will likely prevent him from advocating for a rate hike.

Jobless claims (Thursday): Jobless claims declined this week, continuing to highlight resilient labor markets. Note, continuing claims data scheduled for release next week refer to the July NFP survey reference week.

PCE deflator (Thursday): Based on June CPI and PPI data, we expect core PCE slowed sharply in June to 0.175% m-o-m, consistent with an annualized pace of roughly 2%. On a y-o-y basis, core PCE inflation likely decelerated to 3.3% in June from 3.4% in the previous month.

Core CPI inflation surprised to the downside, falling by $0.017\%$ m-o-m in June, much lower than $+0.208\%$ in May, the first monthly decline since May 2020. Details of CPI and PPI data suggest supercore PCE inflation decelerated while PCE core goods inflation rebounded slightly.

Looking ahead, we expect easing tariff effects and residual seasonality to keep core PCE inflation on a gradual disinflationary path. We continue to expect y-o-y core PCE inflation to moderate to $3.2\%$ by year-end under the current methodology (planned

methodological changes could lower it further to around $3.0\%$ ). A $0.2\%$ monthly core PCE print would represent further progress toward the Fed's inflation objective and should reduce the likelihood of rate hikes in the near term.

Personal income and spending (Thursday): We expect personal income growth slowed to $0.3\%$ in June following a $0.7\%$ m-o-m increase in May. Compensation of employees remained strong as private payroll income rose in the month; however, this was more than offset by other components as the one-off increase in farm proprietors' income from USDA disaster relief payments faded in the month.

We expect personal spending growth decelerated to 0.3% m-o-m in June from 0.7% in May. Retail sales slowed in the month as energy-related spending slowed. Services spending growth also likely moderated. Based on our PCE inflation estimates, we expect real spending grew 0.4% m-o-m.

GDP Q2 first estimate (Thursday): We expect real GDP growth accelerated to 2.5% q-o-q ar in Q2 from 2.1% in Q1. We expect real final sales to private domestic purchasers accelerated sharply to 3.4% q-o-q ar from 2.2% previously, primarily driven by a rebound in personal consumption growth and robust equipment investment growth. Residential investment likely grew in Q2 for the first time since Q4 2024. Volatile components (e.g., government spending, net exports and inventory investment) were weaker than Q1, and weighed on real GDP growth.

Employment cost index (Friday): We expect the Employment Cost Index, the Fed's preferred wage metric, slowed to 0.7% q-o-q (0.74%) in Q2, following a 0.9% gain in Q1. Wages growth for private employees likely edged up, but remained sideways on a rounded basis at 0.7%, while wages for state and local government employees likely slowed. For the private sector, we expect wage growth for goods producing sector picked up, led by construction industry, while wage growth for service sector industries slowed. Growth in the benefits subcomponent (which tends to increase in Q1) likely ticked down to 0.8% q-o-q following a 1.2% increase in Q1. Overall, our ECI forecast suggests wage growth is moderating gradually, in line with other measures.

University of Michigan consumer sentiment (Friday): The final print of the University of Michigan consumer sentiment likely edged lower to 54.0 from its preliminary print of 54.4. Tensions between the US and Iran escalated during the survey interview period, and energy prices rose. In addition, high frequency data from Morning Consult and SF Fed's Daily News Sentiment Index also point to worsening sentiment.
