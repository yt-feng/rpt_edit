Perhaps the brightest spot in Estee Lauder's turnaround story, Mainland China has gone from declining to double-digit reported growth in just one year. The resurgence has been so pronounced that the key debate now is whether the recovery can be sustained. Given that Mainland China is now the company's fastest-growing segment, the answer has significant implications for the broader turnaround story.

In this series, we will examine whether the drivers behind the recent improvement are durable or a temporary inflection point following several years of challenges. We also explore P&G's SK-II trajectory and its recent rebound.

Today's note, the first in the series, uses Euromonitor data to assess high-level market and competitive dynamics. Through this lens, a durable turnaround would ideally be supported by three factors: a) a steadily growing market, b) consistent market share gains, and c) favorable sub-category trends.

The data paints a mixed picture, though there are reasons for optimism. Ultimately, the sustainability of EL's turnaround will depend on continued market growth (possible, but far from guaranteed) and on whether the Estee Lauder brand can continue to gain share in Skincare and Makeup, as it did in 2025. In our view, this will largely depend on whether the brand's recent success was driven by reinvestment in media and innovation, or by more transitory factors such as channel shifts and other one-off events.

One conclusion is clear: contrary to a commonly discussed narrative, the strong performance of EL in 2025 was not driven by Chinese brands broadly “imploding” or ceding share to multinational competitors (at least based on Euromonitor data). Instead, the data points to other drivers, some of which may prove more durable. This is an encouraging sign for the turnaround.

P&G's SK-II finds itself in a similar position. After years of share losses following 2021, the brand stabilized and gained share last year. The key question is whether this marks the start of a new trend, or simply a one-time bump.

To better understand the durability of these trends, the next note in this series will explore channel dynamics in China and the role they may have played in shaping these recent inflection points.

## OBSERVATIONS

We rate Estee Lauder Market-Perform, with a PT of \$82. After years of decline, EL has returned to growth, but structural challenges will prevent further acceleration. Their channel mix in the US will continue to weigh on growth prospects, as Department Stores continue their decline.

We forecast 36% and 13% YoY EPS growth NTM and NTM+1, respectively, making us bearish vs. consensus on NTM+1 EPS by \~7%. We apply a 24.0x target multiple to derive our target price.

We rate Procter & Gamble Market-Perform, with a PT of \$156. P&G has a diversified portfolio (categories and regions) that is, by design, hard to accelerate or disrupt. Bright spots in Beauty (relative to the rest of the portfolio) will help sustain LSD organic sales growth, with potential upside from upcoming innovation in Laundry Detergents, but challenges from Private Label in more commoditized categories like Bathroom Tissue and from smaller competitors in Disposable Diapers are unlikely to go away.

We forecast 1% and 5% YoY EPS growth NTM and NTM+1, respectively, making us bearish vs. consensus on NTM+1 EPS by \~1%. We have a target multiple of 21.6x.

The Prestige Beauty market in China rebounded in 2025, growing $6\%$ after a decline in 2024. Both EL and PG's SK-II outpaced the market in 2025.

Prestige Beauty sales in China reached RMB186bn in 2025 (\~US\$27bn), up from RMB138bn in 2019. While the double-digit growth rates seen before Covid have yet to fully return, Prestige Beauty has demonstrated greater resilience and a faster recovery than Mass Beauty, continuing to outpace the broader market, with its share of Total Beauty sales rising from 37% in 2019 to 44% in 2025. From 2019 to 2025, Prestige Beauty grew at a 5.1% CAGR, compared with flat growth for Mass Beauty and 2.0% growth for the overall beauty market.

That said, growth has been lumpy and hard to predict. While the market is still below its high of 2021, pointing to some further room for recovery, the on-off nature of growth suggests that another soft year is just as likely as a strong one.

## Skincare

EL share gains in Skincare have not been consistent either. That said, La Mer has been steadily gaining throughout the observed period, with gains every year. The main challenge has been the Estee Lauder brand, which just recently reversed its declining trend in 2025. Clinique and Origins remain stagnant.

The data shows the importance of the namesake brand and the role it has to play to drive recovery for the portfolio. The key question is, was the rebound enabled by media and innovation investments, or from a shift from Travel Retail? Hard to tell, especially given the brand's exposure to Duty Free stores. We will further explore this question in our next note in the series.
