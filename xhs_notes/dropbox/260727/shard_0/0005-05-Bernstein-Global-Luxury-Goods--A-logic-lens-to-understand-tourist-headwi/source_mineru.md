Luxury companies have reported contrasted tourist trends in Europe over 1H26E. Richemont and Zegna delivered relatively robust Europe / EMEA performance 2Q26A, with Zegna highlighting a strong performance from international visitors (see Zegna: Highlights from the Preliminary 1H26 conference call). Burberry too saw strong demand from American tourists in Europe, but noted that this could not offset weaker traffic from Asia (see Burberry: Reflections on 1Q27 (= calendar 2Q26)). Moncler, on the other hand, flagged thin Asian tourist flows into Europe as a core reason for its negative performance in EMEA (see Moncler: Highlights from the 1H26 conference call).

Each company's exposure to a trident of factors has generated diverging results. These factors determine the degree and sensitivity of revenues in Europe to travel flows from Asia, which have been disrupted by the war in the Middle East: (1) exposure to Chinese nationals, with lower exposure the better; (2) the size of regional price gaps between China and France, with smaller gaps the better; and (3) any offset from improving American spending in Europe, with a higher offset the better.

Richemont sits on one extreme... we estimate that Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels carry relatively low exposure to Chinese nationals (\~20-25%), as compared to soft luxury brands (\~30-40%). Regional price gaps are also smaller given hard luxury's global pricing strategy, while booming demand for jewellery in the USA has likely supported American tourist spending in Europe.

... while Moncler finds itself on the other. We estimate that Chinese nationals represent \~35-40% of the main Moncler brand's revenues. Moncler also maintains some of the widest China vs. France price gaps, with prices in France -30% below that of the same product in China (vs. a soft luxury average of -20%). Moncler faces a relative disadvantage when it comes to any potential offset from pond-hopping Americans: brand awareness in the Americas remains a work-in-progress while seasonality generated an additional hurdle.

Weakness at Hermès in 1Q26A should also be seen in this context, offering scope for sequential improvement in 2Q26E. Hermès carries significant exposure to Chinese nationals (\~40%) and continues to implement wide regional price gaps (>30%, albeit on a limited sample). It is unsurprising, therefore, that Hermès revealed that tourists represent up to 50% of revenues in France in order to explain 1Q26A's performance. Looking to 2Q26E, however, the company has indicated that slightly better tourist trends may provide sequential support in France - possibly reflecting significant dynamism in the Americas.

## INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS

Investors keen to identify some readacross from Moncler's 2Q26A performance to luxury names yet to report should bear the brand's relative positioning in mind. Higher than average price gaps between Europe and overseas leaves Moncler exposed to adverse price elasticity effects, when tourist flows to Europe weaken and consumers switch at home to "buy now, wear now". This is making Moncler more seasonal, despite the brand's Spring/Summer product success, and has dragged on Moncler's performance in Europe.

Soft luxury names should face milder tourist headwinds than Moncler. Particularly those with lower exposure to Chinese nationals shopping abroad (e.g. Kering and LVMH). Brands like Hermès, with high exposure to Chinese nationals and high price gaps may find an alternate attenuating factor that Moncler lacks - namely sequentially improving spending from American tourists.
