EN_A: Let’s talk about a new report on STMicroelectronics, a key player in European semiconductors. What is the big takeaway here?
EN_B: The big takeaway is that demand visibility is improving, and AI data centers are the main growth driver. But margin recovery is still a work in progress.
EN_A: So there is a bit of a contradiction here. Growth is strong, but profits are under pressure?
EN_B: Exactly. And that is the core question this report tries to answer. It wants to separate the structural AI growth story from the short-term cost challenges.
EN_A: Let us start with growth. How is AI data centers helping STM?
EN_B: The report says STM raised its 2026 data center revenue outlook to over 1 billion dollars, and for 2027, well above 2 billion.
EN_A: That is a big jump. What is driving it?
EN_B: Optical interconnects, mainly 800G and 1.6T pluggable optics. STM has a leading position in microcontrollers and is scaling silicon photonics for manufacturing.
EN_A: So what does this mean in plain terms?
EN_B: It means AI is not just about training chips. It is pulling the entire optical communication supply chain. And STM already has 2026 data center revenue fully covered by backlog.
EN_A: Then why is the margin story so different?
EN_B: Because STM is in the middle of a manufacturing transformation. It is moving silicon production from 200mm to 300mm wafers, and silicon carbide from 150mm to 200mm.
EN_A: That sounds expensive.
EN_B: It is. The transition will not finish until the end of 2027. Gross margin is taking about 60 basis points hit per quarter from non-recurring costs.
EN_A: But the report says margins will improve sequentially, right?
EN_B: Yes. Q3 gross margin guidance is 37 percent, and Q4 should improve further. But the pace is limited by startup costs at new fabs and ongoing transition expenses.
EN_A: What about the demand side? Any surprises?
EN_B: Demand is broadening. Communications equipment and computer peripherals are the fastest growing segments, with revenue up about 60 percent year on year in Q3, accelerating to 90 percent in Q4.
EN_A: And automotive and consumer?
EN_B: Automotive is tracking as expected. Consumer electronics is the outlier. It might see a negative second half year on year, but full year should still be slightly positive.
EN_A: What about inventory and supply?
EN_B: Distribution inventories are now below normal levels. Some product categories show supply tightness. Book-to-bill is close to 2 times overall, and even above 2 times in optical interconnects.
EN_A: So what does that tell us?
EN_B: It tells us the restocking cycle is underway, and AI demand is accelerating. But there is one thing the report does not fully answer.
EN_A: What is that?
EN_B: The exact ramp up timeline for 300mm capacity. The Agrate fab will not be fully built until 2028. Crolles is expanding too. But how fast those wafers come online and match customer demand is not fully quantified.
EN_A: Anything else investors should watch?
EN_B: The competitive landscape in silicon carbide. The report mentions SiC is improving, but it does not fully address how competitors are moving or whether pricing can hold.
EN_A: So what is the overall rating on STM?
EN_B: Neutral, with a 12-month price target of 53 euros. That is based on 11 times forward EV/EBITDA, reflecting caution on near-term earnings pressure.
