# TCOM required to rectify improper business practices Quick Note

What happened?

On 25 July, China's State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) announced its conclusions following a six-month antitrust investigation into Trip.com (TCOM US, Neutral).

In its announcement, SAMR stated that TCOM held a dominant position in China's online hotel-booking market, with a share of nearly $60\%$ in 2025. The regulator determined that TCOM had abused this position since 2020 by requiring select high-quality hotels to enter into exclusive arrangements, while compelling other properties to offer TCOM their lowest available online prices. Furthermore, SAMR found that TCOM enforced these requirements through preferential or punitive traffic allocation, automated and manual price adjustments, removal of platform privileges, delisting of hotels, and deductions from hotel reserve accounts. SAMR concluded that these practices restricted competing platforms' access to hotel inventory and competitive pricing, interfered with hotels' channel and pricing autonomy, raised entry barriers, and ultimately harmed consumer choice and industry development.

## Regulatory action and financial impact

SAMR has ordered TCOM to cease these practices and refund CNY122.8mn (USD18mn) of hotel reserves. Additionally, SAMR requires the confiscation of CNY1.66bn (USD245mn) in unlawful income and the payment of an additional CNY3.5bn (USD516mn) fine. This fine is calculated based on 7.5% of TCOM's entire China market revenue for FY25. Based on our calculations, the total cash obligation of roughly CNY5.3bn accounts for 11% of TCOM's net cash balance as of 2Q26.

On the same date, TCOM announced that it will fully implement all business practice rectifications required by SAMR and pledged to strengthen its platform governance.

## NOM's take: Long-term competitive headwinds

The total penalty of CNY5.2bn exceeds our initial estimate of CNY3.0bn (or \~5% of FY25 consolidated revenue). While we expect the market to look past this one-off financial penalty, the ensuing adjustments TCOM is required to make to its domestic hotel-booking business may have a long-lasting structural impact on this high-margin segment.

Following the cessation of exclusive arrangements with popular hotels and the relaxation of lowest-price requirements for other properties, competitors such as Meituan (3690 HK, Buy) and Douyin (unlisted) may capitalize on the opportunity to narrow the hotel supply gap and strengthen their product offerings. We expect TCOM to remain the largest hotel-booking channel despite these setbacks, as its distinct one-stop-shop model and superior service quality should continue to differentiate it from major competitors. However, competition in the hotel-booking segment will likely intensify, given that hotel product availability across major online travel agency (OTA) platforms is becoming technically uniform.

The 2021 antitrust investigation into Alibaba (BABA US, Buy) offers a relevant read-across, in our view. After SAMR required Alibaba to abandon exclusive arrangements with large merchants, the company's market share declined steadily from $59\%$ in 2020 to $35\%$ in 2025, based on our estimates. Concurrently, major competitors Pinduoduo (PDD US, Neutral) and Douyin saw their combined market share rise from $17\%$ to $40\%$ over the same period. We believe Alibaba's weakened grip on key sellers played a primary role in this market share loss.
