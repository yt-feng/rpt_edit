EN_A: CATL just reported its second quarter numbers. Net profit up 36 percent year on year, but gross margin is shrinking. What is going on?
EN_B: The numbers were broadly in line with expectations. But the gross margin did compress — down 2.4 percentage points year on year. The report attributes this to higher raw material costs and a shift in revenue mix.
EN_A: So what is the real question this report is trying to answer? Can CATL keep growing?
EN_B: Exactly. There are two core questions. First, can demand hold up? Second, will cost pressure eat into profits? The report says demand is resilient, and cost inflation is manageable.
EN_A: How do we see that demand resilience? Which is stronger — EV batteries or energy storage?
EN_B: In the first half, EV battery revenue grew 46 percent year on year. Energy storage grew even faster, at 88 percent. Total battery shipments were around 430 to 440 gigawatt hours, up about 60 percent. The takeaway is that storage is becoming a second growth engine, and it is accelerating faster than EVs.
EN_A: But if gross margin is falling, does that mean competition is heating up?
EN_B: The margin decline is more structural than a pure price war. Storage has lower margins than EV batteries, and its share is growing. Raw material costs are also higher. But the report believes CATL can offset this through scale and cost controls.
EN_A: There is also a big share buyback announcement — 20 to 40 billion yuan. What does that signal?
EN_B: That is about one to two percent of market cap. Not huge, but the message matters. CATL is starting to return more capital to shareholders. It used to be seen as a pure growth story with little payout. Now you have buybacks plus dividends. That is a clear shift.
EN_A: And valuation? Does the stock look cheap at current levels?
EN_B: The report has a target price of 632 yuan, implying about 65 percent upside from 383. That is based on 25 times 2027 earnings per share of 25.26 yuan. Current multiples are 18 times for 2026 and 15 times for 2027. Not expensive.
EN_A: Sounds pretty bullish. But what risks does the report not fully explore?
EN_B: A couple. If raw material prices keep rising faster than expected, margins could come under more pressure. Also, the competitive landscape overseas — especially local production in Europe and the US — is mentioned as a risk, but the report does not provide a detailed quantification.
