# Affordability

With 10yr Treasuries stuck above 4.15, our affordability assumptions estimates assume a 30yr mortgage rate that finishes 2026 at $\sim 6\frac{1}{4}$ before ending 2027 at $\sim 6\frac{1}{8}$ . Affordability improves over the forecast horizon as income growth exceeds HPA, but it remains near the most challenged it has been in decades.

# Housing Activity

The combination of challenged affordability and geopolitical uncertainty/volatility should keep home sales range-bound. Meanwhile, accelerating weakness in new home pricing and the stubbornly elevated inventories there should keep building volumes muted.

# Home Prices

As inventory growth has outpaced demand, the pace of YoY HPA has decelerated. As we approach 0% nationally, we expect home prices to find a floor. There simply is not enough for-sale inventory to drive meaningfully negative home prices. As mortgage rates languish above 6%, we expect the pace of existing inventory growth to slow, which will allow for a slight acceleration of HPA back to +2% by the end of 2026, where we expect it to stay for 2027.

# Supply

Through the first four months of 2026, non-agency RMBS issuance is up 45% vs. the same window in 2025. That growth has largely been fueled by two products: Non-QM and 2nd liens/HELOCs. We expect full-year 2026 new issuance volumes to finish at a 2.0 record of \$235bn, up 13% from 2025.

# Dialing In on Delinquencies

While non-agency lending in general, and Non-QM lending in particular, have been making up a growing slice of the total origination pie, we do not think it has been easing lending standards that have driven that share gain. In fact, Non-QM delinquencies in recent vintages have been climbing at a slower pace than they did in 2023. We do not expect materially higher delinquencies over the next 12 months.

# Find Value in Non-QM

Non-agency RMBS more broadly, and Non-QM in particular, is well aligned with our positive demand narrative. The bid from insurance companies should remain robust in a stubbornly high rate environment, and we expect banks to play a larger role than they have historically given specific benefits to Non-QM from the de-regulatory backdrop. We like Non-QM across the capital structure at today's levels.

# Redefining the Narrative

# CRE Market Finding Its Footing

# CRE Fundamentals Continue to Demonstrate Resilience

CRE recovery momentum is building, with transaction volumes up 27% YoY in 1Q26 (\$135bn), CRE prices tracking toward a \~4.9% annualized increase, origination volumes ending FY 2025 up +40% YoY, and distressed flows turning negative for the first time in 15 quarters.

# SASB Emerging as Dominant Deal Type

We revise our 2026 non-agency CMBS supply forecast lower to \$175bn, reflecting an upward revision to CRE CLO issuance (\$40–50bn) and downward revisions to both conduit (\$25-35bn) and SASB (\$95-105bn) volumes. We think SASBs could overtake conduit as the dominant CMBS deal type in 2027, with the current rate backdrop acting as a headwind for long-duration, fixed-rate conduit issuance.

# Inflection in Distress Indicators taking Shape

The rate of deterioration in conduit 2.0 distress metrics has slowed with issuance, paydowns, and extensions largely offsetting the inflow of newly delinquent loans. SASB delinquencies have remained choppy, while the reading for CRE CLOs has declined sharply, falling 225bp over the past six months. However, persistently higher rates could pressure borrower cashflows and hamper refi prospects.

# CMBS to Outperform Corporate Credit

We expect US CMBS to outperform corporate credit over the forecast horizon, helped by more favorable supply/demand technicals. We particularly like SASB new issue/CRE CLO top of structures for carry and see relval opportunities in data center exposures where the pricing differential vs other property types reflects investor caution rather than weaker fundamentals.

# Demand (Pull)

The conclusion of Basel III Endgame and favorable NAIC treatment should reinforce demand for senior IG tranches. Robust fixed annuity sales and continued CLO ETF inflows amid elevated rate expectations should also support demand for senior and floating-rate CLO exposure.

# AI Concerns Remain a Key Theme (Push)

We see AI disruption creating meaningful refinancing challenges for B- rated software names with maturities through 2028. We expect a significant portion of them will face impending downgrade pressure with constrained market access and may have to pursue LMEs to extend maturities.

# Supply

We revise down our 2026 NI forecast to \$180bn (\$140bn BSL, \$40bn PC). The revision reflects the slower pace of issuance YTD and a still-challenging arb. We also expect a modest slowdown in refi/reset activity. As a steeper CLO liability curve, wider mezz, and the need for managers to clean up lower-quality collateral pools increase the cost of resets, we expect activity to skew toward partial refis.

# BSL Over PC at the Aggregate Level, But Selective Buying Opportunities Have Emerged

PC CLO spread premia over BSL have widened recently amid growing AI concerns (\~30bp now vs. \~20bp YE25). We expect the basis to widen further from here, reflecting a more balanced supply-demand backdrop compared with BSL and incrementally weaker fundamental dynamics within private credit. However, we view a 40bp spread pickup versus BSL as an interesting entry point for PC AAAs.

# Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO)

Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds. Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "original issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the MBS/CMO's original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even though interest was not received. Investors are urged to consult their tax advisors for more information. Government agency backing applies only to the face value of the CMO and not to any premium paid.
