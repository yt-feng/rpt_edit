ZH_A: 最近铁矿石价格涨了不少，但中国钢铁产量明明在下降，这背后到底是怎么回事？

ZH_B: 这是个好问题。我们刚看过一份外资投行的深度报告，核心判断是：这轮涨价不是需求拉动的，而是成本推动的。

ZH_A: 成本推动？具体是哪些成本在涨？

ZH_B: 主要是柴油、炸药这些采矿投入品涨价，还有海运运费飙升。报告估算，边际矿山的离岸成本每吨涨了7美元，从西澳到中国的运费涨了5美元，从巴西到中国的运费更是涨了13美元。

ZH_A: 所以铁矿石价格能站稳在105美元以上，是因为成本把价格托住了？

ZH_B: 对。报告把2026和2027年的短期价格预测上调了5美元，分别到105和99美元。更关键的是，他们把长期实际价格从80美元直接提到了90美元，这是2023年以来首次调整。

ZH_A: 长期价格上调12.5%，这个动作不小。他们为什么觉得长期价格中枢也要上移？

ZH_B: 主要有三个原因。第一，很多矿山降低了产品品位，相当于每单位铁含量的成本变高了。第二，全球矿业的人工、原材料和资本开支都在通胀。第三，中国钢铁产量比预期的更坚挺，西芒杜投产对价格的冲击可能没那么大。

ZH_A: 说到中国钢铁产量，报告里提到4月年化产量接近10亿吨，这比去年下半年任何一个月都高。

ZH_B: 没错。虽然1到4月累计产量同比下降4.1%，但4月的运行率其实很强。报告还特别提到，中国钢铁出口在1、2月疲软后，3、4月恢复到了1.1亿吨的年化水平。

ZH_A: 那铁矿石进口呢？我看到进口量其实在增加。

ZH_B: 是的，前4个月进口增加了近3000万吨。大部分都堆到了港口库存，创了历史新高，但最近开始回落了。有意思的是，非传统供应商是库存增加的主要来源。

ZH_A: 非传统供应商？这指的是哪些国家？

ZH_B: 报告没有完全展开这一点，但我们可以推测是一些中小型供应国。这部分内容在完整报告里会有更详细的拆解，包括印度出口的长期趋势。

ZH_A: 那中东冲突对不同地区的矿商影响一样吗？

ZH_B: 完全不一样。报告揭示了一个很有意思的差异：澳大利亚的矿商受影响很小，因为运费涨幅相对温和，离岸价格甚至还涨了1%。但巴西的矿商就惨了，因为到中国的运距更长，运费涨了14美元，离岸价格反而跌了10%。

ZH_A: 这意味着澳大利亚矿商的利润率基本没受影响？

ZH_B: 对，报告明确说一级生产商的利润率几乎没有影响。但巴西和非洲的矿商就面临压力。这个地区差异在完整报告里有更详细的成本曲线分析。

ZH_A: 最后问一个关键问题：报告认为铁矿石价格的主要风险是什么？

ZH_B: 两个核心风险。一个是西芒杜的投产节奏，另一个是中东冲突对全球经济增长和钢铁需求的潜在影响。这两个问题在完整报告里都有更深入的探讨。

ZH_B: 没错，我们只是聊了最核心的几个洞察，报告里还有很多值得深挖的内容。

EN_A: Iron ore prices have been climbing, but Chinese steel output is actually falling. What is going on here?

EN_B: That is exactly the question a recent global investment bank report tries to answer. Their main takeaway is that this rally is not demand-driven. It is cost-push inflation.

EN_A: Cost-push? Which costs are rising?

EN_B: Diesel, explosives, and freight rates. The report estimates that marginal miners have seen their spot FOB costs go up by 7 dollars per tonne. Freight from Western Australia to China is up 5 dollars, and from Brazil to China it is up 13 dollars.

EN_A: So the price is being held up by higher costs, not stronger demand.

EN_B: Exactly. The bank has raised its near-term price forecasts by about 5 dollars, to 105 for 2026 and 99 for 2027. More importantly, they lifted their long-term real price from 80 dollars to 90 dollars. That is the first revision since 2023.

EN_A: A 12.5 percent increase in the long-term price. That is a big move. What justifies it?

EN_B: Three main reasons. First, many miners have reduced product quality, which effectively raises the cost per iron unit. Second, there is clear inflation in mining opex and capex. Third, Chinese steel output has been more resilient than expected, which means the Simandou project may not be as disruptive to pricing as previously thought.

EN_A: Speaking of Chinese steel, the report notes that the April run rate was close to one billion tonnes annually. That is stronger than any month in the second half of 2025.

EN_B: Right. The year-to-date numbers look weak, down 4.1 percent, but the April pace is actually quite healthy. And steel exports recovered in March and April after a slow start to the year.

EN_A: What about iron ore imports? I saw they are up significantly.

EN_B: Yes, imports are up almost 30 million tonnes year-to-date through April. Most of that went into port inventories, which hit record levels but have started to draw down. Interestingly, non-traditional suppliers are the main driver of the stock build.

EN_A: Non-traditional suppliers? Which countries are those?

EN_B: The report does not fully unpack that here, but it likely refers to smaller, emerging suppliers. There is a deeper discussion in the full report on how Indian exports might decline over the long term.

EN_A: How has the Middle East conflict affected different miners?

EN_B: Unevenly. Australian miners have been barely impacted. Their FOB prices are actually up 1 percent since the conflict began. But Brazilian miners have seen their FOB prices fall 10 percent because freight costs to China have surged much more.

EN_A: So the margin impact is very different depending on where you are located.

EN_B: Exactly. The report says Tier 1 Australian producers have seen almost no margin compression. But Vale and other long-haul producers are feeling the squeeze. The full report has a detailed cost curve analysis that shows this divergence.

EN_A: One last question. What are the key risks to these price forecasts?

EN_B: Two main ones. The pace of Simandou's ramp-up, and any potential impact on global growth from the Middle East conflict. The full report goes into much more detail on both of these.
