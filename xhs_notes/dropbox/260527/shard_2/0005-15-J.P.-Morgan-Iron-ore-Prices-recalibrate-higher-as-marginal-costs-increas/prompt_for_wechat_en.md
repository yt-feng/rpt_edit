# Long-term price raised to \$90/t

There has been significant pressure on marginal iron ore costs in recent years. This likely explains iron ore's resilience over \$100/t, with any dip below \$90/t met with supply curtailments (eg India has been very elastic). We now see the need for a higher long-term real price of \$90/t vs our previous \$80/t, +12.5% (we last updated in 2023). Our LT price upgrade is largely unrelated to the current cost pressures associated with the Middle East conflict. Key drivers are as follows:

- Reduced product quality: Recently, many iron ore miners have reduced product quality, which is essentially producing the same tonnage but with a lower Fe content. This has essentially increased the cost per Fe unit (some miners will benefit from lower strip ratios associated with this move, while for other miners it's just a function of ore body sequencing).   
- Opex & capex inflation: Inflation pressures (raw materials/labour) have been very evident across global mining opex. Recent capex blowouts (e.g., Jansen/Hermosa etc.) also show capital intensity for mining has risen.   
- Less impact from Simandou: Persistent run rates in China steel output around 1Bntpa suggest more marginal tonnes will remain in the market post Simandou than our 2023 expectations when we set our LT price.   
- Ore body depletion remains a challenge: A lack of iron ore project investment outside major hubs could also present as upside risk to prices medium term. Bearish iron ore price sentiment due to peak steel in China and Simandou fears have deterred project investment. This hit non-traditional iron ore supply over the next 5-10yrs as reserves are depleted.   
- Indian iron ore exports likely to drop longer term: As India's domestic steel industry grows, we believe net exports from the nation will drop.
