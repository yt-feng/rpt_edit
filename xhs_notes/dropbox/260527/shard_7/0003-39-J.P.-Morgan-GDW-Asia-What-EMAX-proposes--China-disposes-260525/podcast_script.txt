ZH_A: 最近一份外资投行的报告很有意思，标题叫“EMAX提议，中国处置”，你怎么看这个标题？

ZH_B: 这个标题其实点出了当前亚洲经济的核心分化。一方面，中国4月数据全面走弱，工业产出环比下跌1%，是三年最大跌幅。另一方面，以科技出口为主的EMAX经济体，比如新加坡、马来西亚，反而因为AI需求爆发，出口势头非常强劲。

ZH_A: 所以这份报告真正想回答的问题是什么？

ZH_B: 它想回答的是：中东冲突对亚洲的影响为什么这么不对称？中国和EMAX，为什么一个在承压，一个在受益？

ZH_A: 那中国这边，4月数据到底有多差？

ZH_B: 非常全面。工业产出、零售、固定资产投资都在下滑。制造业投资同比跌了4.3%，基建投资跌了4.5%，房地产投资更是暴跌20%。报告认为，这背后有两个原因：一是霍尔木兹海峡关闭导致的能源供应中断，二是政策端的限制，比如成品油出口禁令和国内油价上限，降低了国企炼厂的开工意愿。

ZH_A: 这意味着什么？会影响二季度GDP吗？

ZH_B: 报告警告说，二季度GDP增速可能只有4.9%的同比，环比年化增速4.0%。要实现这个预测，5月和6月的工业产出必须每月环比增长超过1%。如果4月的疲软持续，下半年财政刺激加码的可能性会上升。但报告也指出，今年的增长目标区间给了政策更多灵活性，所以市场对加码的预期其实比以前更不确定。

ZH_A: 那EMAX这边呢？为什么它们反而有上行风险？

ZH_B: 关键是科技出口。新加坡和马来西亚4月的科技出口增速还在加速，特别是新加坡，科技出口年化增速已经超过了200%。而且，AI需求从半导体扩散到了硬盘和服务器相关产品。报告认为，二季度这两个经济体的增长风险是偏上行的。

ZH_A: 韩国呢？它好像也在EMAX里面？

ZH_B: 韩国情况比较复杂。一季度GDP环比年化增长高达7%，但二季度预计会大幅放缓，因为要消化一季度的基数效应，还要面对中东冲突的潜在冲击。报告预计4月工业产出会环比下跌2%。不过，原油进口正在恢复，5月出口可能继续增长，加上财政支持，所以二季度的下行风险可能没有看起来那么大。

ZH_A: 那科技出口这么强，价格方面有什么变化吗？

ZH_B: 这是个非常关键的点。报告发现，科技需求太强，供给跟不上，所以量增开始传导到价格上涨。亚洲科技出口的价格年化涨幅已经达到70%，这解释了名义出口增长的一半以上。这意味着，很多看似强劲的出口数据，其实是由涨价撑起来的，实际量的增长并没有那么夸张。

ZH_A: 所以这意味着什么？对投资者来说，这个信号重要吗？

ZH_B: 非常重要。如果价格上涨不可持续，或者需求放缓，那么名义出口就会很快回落。报告里有一个图专门对比了名义和实际科技出口，两者的差距在最近几个月急剧拉大。这是需要持续关注的风险点。

ZH_A: 那央行层面呢？报告提到EMAX的央行在转鹰。

ZH_B: 对，因为中东冲突的影响比预想的要小，而通胀压力在上升。印尼央行已经加息50个基点，主要是为了稳定汇率。韩国央行下周的会议也很关键，可能会上调增长和通胀预测。报告甚至暗示，韩国的加息周期可能比市场预期的更早开始。

ZH_A: 这听起来像是报告里埋的悬念。还有哪些问题没有完全展开？

ZH_B: 有两个。第一，中国财政刺激到底会不会加码？报告说“信心比以前低”，因为政策目标区间给了灵活性。第二，科技价格上涨能持续多久？报告没有给出明确答案，只是指出这是一个需要继续验证的变量。这两个问题，完整报告里应该会有更详细的讨论。

EN_A: So this global investment bank just put out a report with a pretty punchy title: “What EMAX proposes, China disposes.” What’s that about?

EN_B: It’s really about the divergence in Asia right now. On one side, China’s April data was a big miss. Industrial production fell 1% month-on-month, the sharpest drop in three years. On the other side, EMAX economies like Singapore and Malaysia are seeing a tech export boom, driven by AI demand.

EN_A: So the core question the report is trying to answer is: why is the Middle East conflict hitting Asia so unevenly?

EN_B: Exactly. China is getting hit by energy supply disruptions and policy constraints, while EMAX is actually benefiting from the tech cycle. The report calls it an “interesting dichotomy.”

EN_A: Let’s talk about China first. How bad was April?

EN_B: Pretty broad-based. Retail sales softened, fixed asset investment fell 8% year-on-year. Manufacturing investment was down 4.3%, infrastructure down 4.5%, and real estate plunged 20%. The report points to two main factors: the Strait of Hormuz closure disrupting crude oil supply, and domestic policy like the refined oil export ban that reduced refiners’ incentives to run at high capacity.

EN_A: So what does that mean for Q2 GDP?

EN_B: The report sees downside risks. It forecasts Q2 GDP at 4.9% year-on-year, with quarterly annualized growth at 4.0%. To hit that, industrial production needs to rebound by more than 1% month-on-month in both May and June. If April weakness continues, fiscal support could accelerate in the second half. But the report also says conviction is lower than before, because the growth target range gives policymakers more flexibility.

EN_A: And what about EMAX? Why are they seeing upside?

EN_B: Tech exports are the key. Singapore and Malaysia saw further acceleration in April. In Singapore, tech export momentum tore past 200% annualized. And the strength is broadening from semiconductors to disk media and server-related products. The report says risks to Q2 growth forecasts are tilted to the upside.

EN_A: What about Korea? It’s part of EMAX too, right?

EN_B: Korea is more complicated. Q1 GDP surged at a 7% annualized rate, but Q2 is expected to slow sharply, partly due to payback and partly due to potential disruptions from the Middle East conflict. The report expects April industrial production to contract 2% month-on-month. But crude oil imports are recovering, and May exports look strong, so there are upside risks to that sharp slowdown.

EN_A: The report also talks about tech prices. What’s happening there?

EN_B: This is a really important point. Tech demand is so strong that it’s spilling over into prices. Tech export prices in Asia are rising at a 70% annualized rate, which explains more than half of the nominal export growth. So a lot of the strong export data is actually price-driven, not volume-driven.

EN_A: So what does that mean for investors? Is that a warning sign?

EN_B: It could be. If price increases are not sustainable, or if demand slows, nominal exports could drop quickly. The report has a chart showing the gap between nominal and real tech exports widening sharply in recent months. That’s something to watch closely.

EN_A: And on the central bank side, the report says EMAX central banks are tilting hawkish.

EN_B: Yes, because the hit from the Middle East conflict has been less visible than feared, while price pressures are building. Indonesia’s central bank already hiked 50 basis points, mainly to support the currency. The Bank of Korea meeting next week is also key. The report expects them to revise up growth and inflation forecasts, and there’s a risk that a modest hike cycle could start earlier than expected.

EN_A: So what are the big open questions that the report doesn’t fully answer?

EN_B: Two things. First, will China actually deliver more fiscal stimulus? The report says conviction is lower than before. Second, how long will tech price inflation last? The report flags it as something that needs further verification. Those are the two key uncertainties that the full report probably dives deeper into.
