ZH_A: 最近有一份关于日本药企的深度调研报告，走访了九家公司，里面有不少值得关注的线索。B，你觉得这份报告最核心的价值在哪里？

ZH_B: 这份报告有意思的地方在于，它不只是讲日本药企在做什么，而是通过日本这个窗口，看透整个全球生物制药的三角关系：美国负责创新，日本负责商业化和规模化，而中国正在快速崛起成为上游创新的新来源。

ZH_A: 三角关系？这听起来像是一个结构性的变化。那这份报告真正要回答的问题是什么？

ZH_B: 核心问题就是：在专利悬崖和全球竞争加剧的背景下，日本药企如何通过外部合作来“补充管线”，而美国中小型生物技术公司又如何利用这种合作来获得资金和商业化能力。报告通过具体的案例，比如Takeda、Otsuka、Shionogi这些公司，给出了非常具体的判断。

ZH_A: 那我们先从Otsuka说起。报告里提到他们的IgA肾病药物Voyxact，说上市进展超预期。这意味着什么？

ZH_B: 这意味着对于关注这个赛道的投资者来说，Voyxact的早期数据是一个积极信号。报告提到，2026年第一季度的销售额大概对应1000剂左右，如果算上桥接项目可能更多。管理层对即将在欧洲肾病大会上公布的中间期肾功能数据有信心，但关键还是要等两年后的完整数据，才能看到按ADA状态分层的效果。

ZH_A: 所以现在还不能下结论。那对于VRTX这样的公司来说，这个信息有什么影响？

ZH_B: 作为同类药物，Voyxact的进展实际上为整个APRIL抑制剂赛道提供了参考基准。如果Voyxact最终成功，那会提振市场对整个机制的信心，包括VRTX的相关项目。但报告也留了个悬念：Voyxact的长期数据到底怎么样，尤其是在真实世界中的持久性和耐受性，这还需要继续验证。

ZH_A: 那Takeda这边呢？他们有好几个重点项目。比如那个口服TYK2抑制剂Zaso，报告里是怎么看的？

ZH_B: 报告提到Zaso在银屑病的三期数据比管理层预期还好，疗效和口服同类药物比如艾伯维的Envu和强生的Icotyde在一个范围内。他们强调每日一次服药和不受食物影响是优势，但这对医生处方行为到底有多大影响，现在还不好说。另外，他们预计口服药在银屑病市场中的份额会从现在的15%增长到30%到40%，这意味着患者数量可能翻三倍。

ZH_A: 那Zaso的长期安全性数据什么时候出来？这应该是上市的关键吧？

ZH_B: 没错。报告说Zaso的长期安全性数据即将公布，这是提交NDA的先决条件。另外，和百时美施贵宝的Sotyktu进行头对头试验的数据也快出来了，但那个数据更多是作为商业工具，而不是审批的必要条件。这里有个悬念：这些数据会不会被纳入申报文件，取决于和FDA的沟通。

ZH_A: 还有一个药物Oveporexton，它是针对发作性睡病的口服orexin受体激动剂。报告里提到这是个潜在的“重大改变”。

ZH_B: 是的。管理层对这个药很有信心，认为它在疗效和生活质量指标上相比传统药物羟丁酸盐有显著提升。他们预计美国大约有10万NT1患者可能接受治疗，但目前只有1.5万到1.7万人在用羟丁酸盐。所以关键问题不仅是多少人会从羟丁酸盐转换过来，而是如何把整体治疗率提高到40%到50%。

ZH_A: 那和其他竞争药物相比呢？比如Centessa的orexin项目。

ZH_B: 报告指出，Takeda认为在NT1领域很难做得比Oveporexton更好，但他们没有NT2适应症，所以竞争对手可能在NT2上找到机会，从而获得更广的标签。但这样一来，剂量和定价就成了关键变量。这也是为什么Takeda在开发一整个orexin激动剂组合，而不是只靠一个药。

ZH_A: 那Shionogi这边呢？他们的长效HIV项目有什么看点？

ZH_B: 报告重申了他们的时间表：Cabo每四个月一次的长效剂型预计在2028年初上市，而长效整合酶抑制剂VH184大约在2030年。他们相信VH184会改变治疗市场。对于市场格局，他们认为每两个月一次、每四个月一次和每六个月一次的方案最终能分别占据15%、30%和50%的治疗市场份额。

ZH_A: 那在预防方面呢？吉利德的Yeztugo每六个月一次，对现有药物Apretude有影响吗？

ZH_B: 报告说目前没有明显影响。Shionogi认为每四个月一次的Apretude剂型应该会在2028年之前推出。但他们对于美国以外的PrEP商业机会并不乐观。

ZH_A: 最后，我们聊聊这个三角关系。报告提到中国资产正在快速崛起，这对美国和日本公司意味着什么？

ZH_B: 这可能是报告里最宏观的一个洞察。中国在肿瘤和心血管代谢领域正在成为新的创新来源，这迫使美国和日本公司都必须拓宽采购策略。对于美国中小型生物技术公司来说，他们面临的不再只是和日本公司竞争，还要和中国公司抢项目。这加速了整个跨境交易的节奏和竞争烈度。

ZH_A: 那美国公司在这一轮中还有优势吗？

ZH_B: 有。报告认为美国在早期创新和平台技术方面仍然领先，比如双抗、T细胞衔接器和基因疗法。日本公司的优势在于复杂生物制剂的制造和全球商业化能力。而中国公司则在临床执行速度和成本效率上占优。这三者形成了一个相互依赖但又竞争激烈的生态系统。

ZH_A: 听起来，未来的赢家可能是那些能同时利用这三个区域优势的公司。这份报告里还有很多细节，比如PV药物的全球开发模式、眼科领域的竞争等等。如果你想了解更多，我们建议你直接阅读完整报告。

EN_A: So we just went through a pretty dense report from a global investment bank about Japanese pharma. They actually went on a bus tour in Tokyo and met with nine companies. What is the single biggest takeaway here?

EN_B: The biggest takeaway is that this report is not really about Japan. It is about a triangular relationship in global biopharma. The US drives early-stage innovation. Japan provides the capital and the commercial scaling. And China is quickly becoming a new source of upstream innovation, especially in oncology and cardiometabolic.

EN_A: A triangle. That is a big structural shift. So what is the core question this report is trying to answer?

EN_B: The core question is how Japanese pharma companies are going to refill their pipelines as they face a major patent cliff. And the answer is they are increasingly looking outside. They are licensing, co-developing, and partnering with US small and mid-cap biotech companies. But they are also competing with Chinese biotech for the same assets. So the dynamic is becoming more complex.

EN_A: Let us start with Otsuka. The report says their IgAN drug Voyxact is tracking ahead of expectations. What does that mean for investors?

EN_B: It means the early launch is going well. The first quarter 2026 sales suggest about 1,000 doses, maybe more if you include the bridge program. Management is confident about the upcoming interim Phase 3 kidney function data at a major conference. But the real proof will be the two-year data, which should show the effect by ADA status. That will be a key catalyst.

EN_A: So we need to wait for that. What about the read-through for a company like Vertex? They have a similar program.

EN_B: Exactly. Voyxact is a benchmark for the entire APRIL inhibitor class. If the data holds up, it validates the mechanism and lifts the whole sector. But the report does not fully answer the durability question yet. We need to see real-world adherence and long-term tolerability. That is a key suspense point.

EN_A: Now Takeda. They have a lot going on. Let us talk about Zaso, their oral TYK2 inhibitor. What did the report say?

EN_B: The Phase 3 psoriasis data came in better than management expected. Efficacy is in line with other oral drugs like AbbVie's Envu and Johnson & Johnson's Icotyde. They think once-daily dosing and no food effect are advantages. But it is still unclear how much that will change prescribing behavior. They also expect the oral psoriasis market to grow from 15% to 30 to 40% of the advanced mix. That is a threefold increase in patient numbers.

EN_A: And the long-term safety data? That is the gating item for approval, right?

EN_B: Right. The report says the long-term safety data from the 3003 trial is expected soon. That is the key to filing the NDA. There is also a head-to-head trial against Bristol-Myers' Sotyktu coming up. But that data is more for commercial differentiation. Whether it goes into the filing package depends on the FDA. So there is a suspense there.

EN_A: Then there is Oveporexton, the oral orexin agonist for narcolepsy. The report seems very bullish on this.

EN_B: Management is confident. They think it is a step change in efficacy and quality of life compared to oxybates. They estimate about 100,000 NT1 patients in the US could potentially be treated. Only 15,000 to 17,000 are on oxybates now. So the question is not just how many switch, but how do you get the overall treatment rate to 40 or 50 percent.

EN_A: How does it compare to competitors like Centessa's orexin program?

EN_B: Takeda thinks it will be hard to beat Oveporexton in NT1. But they do not have an NT2 indication. So competitors could find an opening there with a broader label. However, dosing and pricing will matter. That is why Takeda is building a whole portfolio of orexin agonists, not just one drug.

EN_A: What about Shionogi? Their long-acting HIV program has some interesting timelines.

EN_B: Yes. They reiterated that the every-four-month version of Cabo will launch in early 2028. Their long-acting integrase inhibitor VH184 is targeting around 2030. They believe every-two-month, every-four-month, and every-six-month regimens will eventually capture 15%, 30%, and 50% of the treatment market, respectively.

EN_A: And on the prevention side? Is Gilead's every-six-month drug Yeztugo having an impact?

EN_B: Not yet, according to the report. Shionogi thinks an every-four-month version of Apretude could launch before 2028. But they are not optimistic about the PrEP commercial opportunity outside the US.

EN_A: Let us close on the triangle. The report says Chinese assets are becoming more competitive. What does that mean for US and Japanese companies?

EN_B: This is the biggest macro takeaway. China is now a serious source of innovation, especially in oncology. That forces both US and Japanese companies to broaden their sourcing strategies. For US SMID cap biotech, it means they are no longer just competing with each other. They are competing with Chinese biotech for partnerships and capital. That accelerates the pace of deal-making and raises the bar for what counts as best-in-class.

EN_A: So who wins in this new system?

EN_B: The winners will be companies that can leverage all three regions. US biotech still leads in early platform innovation like bispecifics and gene therapy. Japan excels in manufacturing complex biologics and global commercialization. China is fast and cost-efficient in clinical execution. The report suggests the most successful assets will be those that combine all three.
