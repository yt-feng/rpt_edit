# Metals & Mining: Met Coal: China supply outage may present upward pressure on Seaborne prices

On 22 May 2026, a fatal gas explosion at the Liushenyu Coal Mine in Qinyuan county, Shanxi, triggered local voluntary mine suspensions and raised the potential for broader regulatory safety measures. We take no view on supply recovery timelines, but attempt to quantify the potential annualised impact of the outages, refresh our import arb charts for Australia PLV HCC into China, and assess NTM EBITDA sensitivities to coking coal prices for GLEN and BHP.

Initial estimates suggest up to \~40Mt (\~80Mt ROM) annualised production could be impacted (\~8% domestic output): The impacted mine has capacity of \~1.2Mtpa, but reports suggest a wider shutdown in Qinyuan county could impact \~25Mt of annualised ROM met coal. Wider implications (mandatory temporary safety shutdowns/inspections) could bring the total impact closer to \~80Mtpa. Based on China's 2025 domestic coking coal output of \~480Mt and assuming a yield factor of 50%, the annualised impact of the incident could be \~8% of clean domestic coking coal output; however, we believe it is unlikely this capacity will be completely offline for an extended period due to the voluntary nature of the shutdowns. A potential offsetting factor for the temporary supply outage is the lowering seasonal demand from India (monsoon season) for spot cargoes, which may temper near-term expectations of a deficit emerging in the seaborne market.

Depending on length and scope, Shanxi suspensions could pose a risk to China's coal blend: Since 2020, China has reduced its dependence on Australian met coal imports; it has increasingly turned to Mongolia (Exhibit 2) and Russia (Exhibit 3), removing dependency from Australia (Exhibit 4). The emerging supply of coal from Shanxi has been critical to coal blends, as it complements the coal from Russia/Mongolia to produce a suitable met coke without the need for imports from Australia, displacing it as a key seaborne supplier. While the timeline and scope of the safety-related disruptions are unclear, the loss of supply from Shanxi could disrupt the ability for steel mills to blend coal to meet specifications, forcing mills to turn to the seaborne market to secure premium tonnes, which could benefit Australian and Canadian producers. We also note that Mongolia relies heavily on trucking for mine to border transportation, and diesel prices and availability could pose further supply risks in 2H'26. For now, however, import data shows that Mongolian exports are \~70% higher YoY for Jan-Apr'26.

# GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
