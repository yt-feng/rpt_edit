# Korea Financials & Fintech: SG marketing feedback: Finding value as defensives amid market volatility

# We see KR financials as defensives given solid dividend yields

Amid increased market volatility, our investor discussions focused around defensives, where we see KR financials well-placed with earnings becoming more resilient and as Value Up efforts (i.e., higher payouts incl. buybacks and cancellation, high-dividend company qualification, tax-exempt dividends) have pushed up the underlying yields. While the sectoral preference have tilted towards KR insurers more recently, we see value in both KR banks and insurance amid the increased market volatility.

# KR Banks taking a pause but we see further upside

With KR banks lifting TSR levels to c.50% in 2025A, investors seem to agree that large commercial banks can reach 1x P/B on 10-11% ROE. While Shinhan/Woori recently renewed Value Up targets, we think the bar is now higher post 2024-25 re-rating and well-anchored expectations. While KR bank shares were sidelined in the recent rally due to lack of near-term catalysts and growing social responsibilities, we still see further upside as they are in the late-stage valuation re-rating phase.

# KR insurers gaining traction with improving fundamentals and Value Up potential

We saw rising interest on KR insurers as select non-life insurers began to show a positive trend reversal in underwriting quality, after years of deteriorating loss ratio trends and heightened competition. Coupled with positive regulatory measures in 2H26E (i.e., GA commission cap, stricter claims review), we expect to see more visible operational improvements. With our expectation for earnings expected to become more stable and growing external pressure, investors are looking for faster Value Up enhancements.

# Limited interest in fintech until the next round of updates on stablecoin

Both Kakaopay and KakaoBank shares displayed stronger share price reaction to stablecoin-related developments, but we sense that investor perception on the underlying fundamentals are different — Kakaopay began delivering revenue growth acceleration and sequentially driving up its OPM while KakaoBank’s deposit growth has softened, potentially due to the ongoing retail money move and its lending growth being hindered by regulations. All-in-all, we believe the market is waiting for tangible stablecoin related developments that can level up the growth trajectories.

# GS Factor Profile

The GS Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:

Growth is based on a stock's forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock's forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock's forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).

Financial Returns and Multiple use the GS analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
