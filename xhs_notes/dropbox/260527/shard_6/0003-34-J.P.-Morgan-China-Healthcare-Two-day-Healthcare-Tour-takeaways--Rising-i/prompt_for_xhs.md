# China Healthcare

Two-day Healthcare Tour takeaways: Rising innovation bar, smarter BD, and verifiable AIDD revenue

On 18-19 May, we visited a number of Chinese healthcare companies spanning the AI drug discovery (AIDD), Biotech, and Medtech spaces. During our tour of these Beijing and Shanghai-based companies, we came away more positive on the sector driven by four key trends: 1) AI platforms are moving from concept to tangible commercialization, with biotechs stepping up in AI drug discovery adoptions; 2) biotech pipelines are advancing beyond simple fast-following toward deeper innovation and meeting differentiated medical needs; 3) companies continue to conduct business development deals to get funding support and global expansion; and 4) hospital end-markets are accelerating the adoption of advanced technologies spanning surgical robotics and biomarker-driven oncology. We believe these shifts are shaping treatment paradigms and improving the sector's global appeal.

\- AI platforms are delivering tangible commercial monetization, and biotech companies are increasingly integrating AIDD workflows to support internal pipelines. XtalPi (2228.HK, Not Covered) has expanded its discovery engine to multiple key drug modalities such as multi-specific antibodies, peptide and siRNAs, deploying a few hundred AI models, 10,000+ AI agents and securing a major US\$345 million collaboration with Eli Lilly in Nov. 2025. We think work by Xtalpi and other AIDD companies clearly demonstrates that AI is scaling from pilot projects to a core revenue driver. Concurrently, traditional biotechs are actively adopting these tools; Harbour BioMed (2142.HK, NC) noted it is integrating an “AI-native discovery engine” under Nona Biosciences to accelerate pre-clinical filtering and support its proprietary pipeline generation. The newly listed METiS (7666.HK, NC) leverages its AiLNP, AiRNA, and AiTEM platforms to both advance internal mRNA drug assets, develop better drug delivery approaches, and secure out-licensing deals. It appears that AI is becoming part of workflow integration, which could support clearer earnings visibility. As for Deep Intelligent Pharma (private), it is using LLM and MAS (multi-agent system) to drive clinical CRO business with 1Q26 net profit exceeding 2025 profit.

\- Biotech pipelines are moving beyond simple fast-following toward deeper innovation. The siRNA biotech Sirius Therapeutics (private) believes it has a differentiated pipeline: it has demonstrated best-in-class knockdown profiles with 90-95% Factor XI reduction for SRSD107 and plans to conduct Ph3 trials for SRSD107, which not many siRNA companies are working on. Furthermore, GluBio (private) is advancing a robust pipeline of novel molecular glues (including GLB-005 and GLB-007) targeting sickle cell disease and beta-thalassemia, highlighting a strong pivot toward differentiated biology and novel MoA (mechanism of action). We also visited InnoCare (9969.HK, NC), which posted strong 1Q26 revenue growth of 38.65%. Management considers the company well-positioned in both blood cancer and autoimmune disease.
