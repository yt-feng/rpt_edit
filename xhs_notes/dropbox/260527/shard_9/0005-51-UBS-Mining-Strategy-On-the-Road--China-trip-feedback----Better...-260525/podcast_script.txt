ZH_A: 最近我看到一份外资投行的中国实地调研报告，标题直接说“Better”，你觉得这个判断靠谱吗？

ZH_B: 我觉得挺靠谱的。他们团队在5月中旬去了北京和上海，见了超过25个行业、政府和专家联系人，整体感觉比去年11月那次调研要更积极。

ZH_A: 所以不仅仅是情绪好转，而是有实质性的改善？

ZH_B: 对。报告提到几个关键信号：一线城市部分区域的房价有所回暖，二手房和租赁收入预期也在改善。股市接近历史高点，通缩在缓解，企业利润一季度增长了15%到20%。

ZH_A: 那出口这块呢？我记得之前大家很担心出口放缓。

ZH_B: 这次调研反而觉得出口是个亮点。联系人反馈出口订单在增加，部分原因是中国的能源成本优势，再加上持续的质量提升。但报告也留了一个悬念：如果出口真的放缓，政府会不会推出消费刺激政策？

ZH_A: 有意思。那具体到商品，他们最看好什么？

ZH_B: 铝和铜是未来6到12个月最看好的。锂价也有支撑，但钢铁和铁矿石的预期相对温和一些。

ZH_A: 铝的供需情况听起来很紧张？

ZH_B: 是的。报告提到中东有大约250万吨的冶炼产能停产，恢复可能需要到2027年下半年。同时中国45万吨的产能上限非常严格，所以供应增长有限。一些联系人认为，这可能导致几十年来最大的铝市场缺口。

ZH_A: 那铜呢？除了需求，还有什么风险？

ZH_B: 铜这边有个很有意思的风险——硫酸。因为中东局势，硫酸供应可能中断，而刚果金80%到90%的铜产量需要进口硫酸。如果供应链出问题，实物铜的生产风险会上升。

ZH_A: 锂呢？最近价格波动很大。

ZH_B: 这次调研对锂比较乐观。关键转变是电池储能系统需求正在爆发。报告预计储能对锂的需求占比会从2025年的25%上升到2027年的40%，对应从30万吨到90万吨碳酸锂当量。

ZH_A: 那钢铁和铁矿石呢？为什么说“中国钢铁的熊市结束了”？

ZH_B: 主要逻辑是钢厂利润在改善。现在吨钢利润在30到90元人民币，比三个月前好了300元。原因包括焦煤价格正常化、供应纪律加强，以及出口套利窗口关闭。但报告没有完全解答的是，如果反倾销加剧，出口还能撑多久。

ZH_A: 最后一个问题，这个报告有没有提到什么政策上的悬念？

ZH_B: 有的。报告说“反内卷”目前不是优先事项，但行业产能利用率会是未来政策走向的关键指标。另外，十五五规划对电网投资大幅增加，比十四五高出40%到60%，这对铜和铝都是长期利好。

EN_A: So I just read a global investment bank report from their latest China trip, and the headline is just one word: “Better.” What does that actually mean?

EN_B: It means the team came back more optimistic than their last visit in November. They met over 25 contacts across industry and government in Beijing and Shanghai, and the overall sentiment was clearly improved.

EN_A: So it’s not just a vibe shift? There’s real evidence behind it?

EN_B: Absolutely. They saw house prices stabilizing in some tier-one cities, the stock market near record highs, deflation easing, and corporate profits up 15 to 20 percent in the first quarter. Exports were also a bright spot, partly because of China’s lower energy costs.

EN_A: But what about the risks? If exports slow down, what happens?

EN_B: That’s actually one of the key questions the report leaves open. The balance of risks is that if exports weaken, the government could step in with consumer-oriented stimulus. But they haven’t done that yet, so it’s still a wait-and-see situation.

EN_A: Let’s talk commodities. What stood out the most?

EN_B: Aluminum and copper are the biggest calls for the next 6 to 12 months. Lithium also looks more positive, while steel and iron ore are seen as more neutral.

EN_A: Why aluminum? That seems like a strong statement.

EN_B: The report says the Middle East has about 2.5 million tons of smelting capacity offline, and it might take until the second half of 2027 to come back. At the same time, China’s 45 million ton capacity cap is very strict. Some contacts think this could lead to the largest aluminum deficit in decades.

EN_A: And copper? What’s the main story there?

EN_B: There’s a hidden risk around sulfuric acid. Most copper production in the DRC relies on imported acid from the Middle East. If that supply gets disrupted, physical copper production could be at risk. Meanwhile, demand from grid spending and electrification remains strong.

EN_A: Lithium has been volatile. What did the report find?

EN_B: The big shift is battery energy storage. The report estimates that storage could go from 25 percent of lithium demand to 40 percent by 2027. That’s a huge jump. One contact even said lithium demand could grow 20 to 29 percent next year alone.

EN_A: So what about steel? Why is the report saying the bear market is over?

EN_B: Steel margins have improved a lot, from near zero to about 30 to 90 yuan per ton. That’s due to lower coke prices and better supply discipline. But the report doesn’t fully answer whether those margins can hold if anti-dumping measures cut into exports.

EN_A: Any big policy takeaways?

EN_B: The 15th Five-Year Plan is a big deal for grid spending, up 40 to 60 percent from the previous plan. That’s bullish for copper and aluminum long term. But the report also notes that “anti-involution” is not a priority right now, which leaves some uncertainty.
