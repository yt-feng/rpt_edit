EN_A: So I came across this report that makes a really bold claim — it says we might be entering an MLCC super cycle, almost like what happened with DDR4 memory two years ago. Do you buy that?
EN_B: Actually, I think the comparison is quite fitting. The core argument is that AI is shifting MLCCs from a commodity to a strategic resource. A single AI server uses 10 to 15 times more MLCCs than a traditional server. But the bigger issue is that high-end MLCC capacity is already being locked in by AI demand, much like how HBM took away capacity from DDR4.
EN_A: So it is not just about more units, but the supply structure itself is being reshaped?
EN_B: Exactly. The report points out that existing MLCC capacity, like the lines that were built for electric vehicles, cannot simply be repurposed for AI servers. The specs are completely different — voltage, packaging, capacitance requirements. That creates a supply-demand mismatch that is not easy to fix.
EN_A: How bad is that mismatch? Did the report try to quantify it?
EN_B: Yes. The report estimates that AI-driven MLCC demand will more than quadruple between 2025 and 2030, while total industry capacity grows only 10 to 15 percent annually. High-end AI-grade production lines are already booked out for years. What that means is the tightness is structural, not cyclical.
EN_A: This sounds a lot like the 2017 MLCC super cycle, right? That was driven by EVs and smartphones.
EN_B: The report actually makes that comparison. In 2017, the cycle was mostly demand-driven. This time, there are additional barriers — technology complexity, long lead times for new capacity, and high entry barriers. Building a new MLCC fab takes about two years, and the equipment is custom-built. So this cycle feels more structural.
EN_A: Who are the dominant players in this market?
EN_B: The high-end AI MLCC market is basically a duopoly between Japanese and Korean companies, with a combined 85 percent share. Murata leads with 45 percent, and its AI server orders are running at about double its capacity. Samsung Electro-Mechanics has 40 percent, and its Tianjin plant is running at full utilization. Chinese players are mostly in the consumer segment.
EN_A: The report also mentioned something about embedded MLCCs. That sounds like a new trend.
EN_B: It is. Embedded MLCCs are becoming a key design feature in AI servers. They are integrated into the ABF substrate, which shortens the current path and reduces power loss. It also frees up board space. This is not just a technical upgrade — it turns MLCCs from a commodity into a strategic component.
EN_A: What about pricing? What is happening in the channel right now?
EN_B: The report says distributor prices have gone up 200 to 300 percent since the first quarter. In Shenzhen's Huaqiangbei spot market, some specifications have risen 6 times year to date. On the contract side, leading suppliers raised distributor pricing by 20 to 30 percent in the second quarter and removed volume discounts.
EN_A: Is there any hype in those numbers?
EN_B: The report acknowledges that some extreme price increases might be driven by inventory hoarding by traders. But overall, inventory levels are very lean. Downstream customers hold less than one month of stock, and distributors have about 1.5 months. So the underlying trend is real.
