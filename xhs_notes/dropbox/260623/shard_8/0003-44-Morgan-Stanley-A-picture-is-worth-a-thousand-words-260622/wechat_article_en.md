# Prescription Data Reveals Which Drug Launches Are on Track—And Which Are Not

The US pharmaceutical market is entering a period where the gap between commercial promise and actual prescription volume has never been wider. In the week ending June 12, 2026, total weekly prescriptions grew just 0.6% year-over-year, down from 2.9% growth in the same period a year ago. This deceleration matters not because the market is shrinking, but because it raises the bar for individual product launches to generate meaningful revenue. When the tide of overall prescription growth recedes, the difference between a well-executed launch and a struggling one becomes starkly visible.

Four major drug launches are now under way simultaneously: Bristol Myers Squibb's Cobenfy for schizophrenia, Gilead's Yeztugo for HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis, Vertex's Journavx for acute pain, and Johnson & Johnson's Icotyde for plaque psoriasis. Each faces a different set of commercial challenges, but they share one common analytical question: can prescription volumes reach consensus revenue estimates? The prescription data from the latest week provides the first serious reality check.

What makes this moment particularly instructive is that the market is not asking whether these drugs will be approved or whether they have clinical value. Those questions have been answered. The market is now asking whether the commercial infrastructure—payer coverage, physician adoption, patient adherence, and sampling dynamics—can convert clinical potential into revenue. Prescription data is the only forward-looking signal that captures all of these forces simultaneously.

The analysis that follows is not a summary of the latest IQVIA data. It is an argument about how to interpret that data in the context of consensus expectations, and a framework for identifying which launches are building genuine momentum and which are masking structural headwinds behind early-adopter enthusiasm.

## The Cobenfy Launch Faces a Volume Gap That Consensus Estimates May Not Fully Capture

Bristol Myers Squibb's Cobenfy received FDA approval for schizophrenia on September 26, 2024, and has now been on the market for over twenty months. In the week ending June 12, 2026, Cobenfy prescriptions totaled approximately 3,200, down from approximately 3,430 the prior week. This is not a trajectory that inspires confidence when measured against consensus expectations.

To understand the magnitude of the gap, consider the math. Consensus estimates for 2026 Cobenfy revenue stand at $296 million. Assuming a net price of approximately $1,450 per prescription, this implies roughly 197,000 total prescriptions are needed for the year. Current weekly volumes of approximately 3,200 would need to more than double and sustain at that level to reach that target. More concerning is the comparison to schizophrenia launch analogs such as Rexulti, Caplyta, and Lybalvi. Achieving consensus would require Cobenfy to track at roughly two to five times the volume those launches achieved at comparable points in their life cycles.

The question this raises is structural, not tactical. Cobenfy operates in a therapeutic category where physician prescribing behavior changes slowly. Schizophrenia patients are often stabilized on existing regimens, and switching carries clinical risk. The launch trajectory to date suggests that Cobenfy is being adopted by a narrow set of early adopters, but has not yet demonstrated the broad-based penetration that would signal a blockbuster product. The data does not tell us whether this is a function of marketing execution, payer restrictions, or simply the inherent conservatism of the psychiatric prescribing community. But it does tell us that the current volume is insufficient, and the gap is large enough that incremental improvements will not close it.

## Journavx Is Caught Between Pricing Pressure and Hospital Channel Blind Spots

Vertex's Journavx, approved for acute pain on January 30, 2025, presents a different analytical challenge. Weekly prescriptions reached approximately 22,430 in the week ending June 12, up modestly from 22,100 the prior week. The product is clearly generating volume, but the revenue implications depend entirely on two variables that prescription data alone cannot resolve: gross-to-net pricing and hospital channel capture.

Vertex has disclosed that the average script duration is now 10 to 11 days, down from 14 days previously, though the company expects duration to increase in 2026. The hospital channel represents approximately 45% of total scripts, and these prescriptions are not captured in the IQVIA data used for this analysis. This means the true prescription volume is meaningfully higher than the reported 22,430, but the exact magnitude is unknown.

The consensus revenue estimate for Journavx in 2026 is $239 million. To reach this target, Vertex needs between 1.2 million and 2.1 million total scripts, depending on whether the gross-to-net discount settles at 50% or 70%. Based on recent quarterly data, the actual GTN appears to be in the 65% to 75% range, which pushes the required script volume toward the higher end of that range. This creates a tension: higher net pricing would reduce the number of scripts needed, but the current GTN data suggests pricing pressure is real and may intensify as payers gain more experience with the product.

The unresolved question is whether the hospital channel will continue to grow as a proportion of total scripts. If hospital adoption accelerates, the reported IQVIA data will understate true volume by an increasing margin, potentially creating a positive surprise for investors who rely solely on retail prescription tracking. But if hospital adoption plateaus, the burden falls entirely on retail scripts, and the current weekly run rate of approximately 22,000 would need to roughly double to meet consensus.

## Yeztugo Benefits From Favorable Payer Dynamics but Faces an Unusual Competitive Landscape

Gilead's Yeztugo, approved on June 18, 2025, for HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis, presents the most encouraging early-stage signal among the four launches. Total weekly prescriptions (oral plus injectable) were approximately 1,720, with injectable prescriptions holding steady at approximately 980. The product has achieved 95% payer coverage, and most payers do not require copays or impose prior authorization or step-edit requirements. This is a remarkable commercial achievement for a product that has been on the market for barely twelve months.

The PrEP market itself is growing at 14% year-over-year, and Descovy maintains a share above 45%. This creates a favorable tailwind for Yeztugo, as the overall market expansion provides room for multiple products to grow without direct cannibalization. The consensus estimate for second-quarter 2026 US Yeztugo revenue is $225 million. A linear projection based on first-quarter script volume and pricing suggests only modest incremental script growth is required to reach this target.

The analytical challenge here is that Yeztugo combines an oral and an injectable formulation, and the revenue per script differs meaningfully between the two. The injectable formulation is higher value and is the primary driver of the revenue estimate, but the data shows injectable scripts have been flat at approximately 980 per week for multiple weeks. If the injectable volume does not accelerate, the revenue trajectory could fall short of consensus even if total script volume increases, because the mix shift toward lower-value oral prescriptions would dilute revenue per script.

The larger strategic question is whether Yeztugo can capture share from Descovy and Apretude in a market that is growing but increasingly competitive. Gilead's own guidance of approximately $1.0 billion in US PrEP revenue for 2026 is above the Visible Alpha consensus of $956 million, suggesting the company itself believes the product will outperform current expectations. The prescription data does not yet confirm this optimism, but it does not refute it either. The next twelve weeks of data will be critical.

## Icotyde's Early Signal Is Promising but Sampling Friction Could Mask True Demand

Johnson & Johnson's Icotyde, approved on March 18, 2026, for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis, is the newest of the four launches and therefore the hardest to evaluate. Weekly prescriptions reached approximately 480 in the week ending June 12, up from approximately 380 the prior week. On the first-quarter earnings call, J&J reported that approximately 1,500 patients had received prescriptions from more than 1,000 unique prescribers within roughly three weeks of launch. This suggests strong initial demand.

However, early feedback from a dermatologist who has prescribed Icotyde to approximately 21 patients indicates that near-term uptake may be tempered by sampling friction. Physicians are still learning how to integrate Icotyde into their treatment algorithms, and sampling programs may not yet be fully operational. This means the IQVIA prescription data may understate true prescribing intent, as physicians may be writing prescriptions that patients are not yet filling due to sampling availability or insurance verification delays.

The consensus revenue estimate for Icotyde in 2026 is $288 million. To achieve this, weekly prescriptions would need to ramp broadly in line with the trajectories of Otezla and Rinvoq at comparable points in their launches. This implies approximately 35,000 to 46,000 total prescriptions for the year, based on a list price of $8,285 per month and a gross-to-net discount ranging from 0% to 25%. The current weekly run rate of approximately 480 is early-stage and not yet predictive, but the trajectory over the next eight to twelve weeks will determine whether Icotyde is tracking toward the Otezla/Rinvoq benchmark or falling short.

The unresolved question is whether Icotyde's oral formulation gives it a fundamental advantage over injectable biologics in the psoriasis market, or whether the convenience of an oral pill is offset by lower efficacy perceptions among dermatologists. If Icotyde is viewed as a compelling oral IL-23 option, it could expand the treated patient pool by attracting patients who were unwilling to use injectables. If it is viewed as a second-line option, its addressable market will be narrower, and the revenue estimates may prove optimistic.

## The Report Raises Three Analytical Questions That Prescription Data Alone Cannot Answer

The prescription data provides a rich foundation for evaluating these launches, but it also exposes the limits of what can be known from IQVIA data alone. Three questions emerge that require additional information beyond what is presented in this report.

First, what is the true gross-to-net trajectory for these products? The analysis uses estimates ranging from 50% to 75% GTN for Journavx and 0% to 25% for Icotyde, but these are wide ranges that produce dramatically different revenue outcomes for the same prescription volume. The report provides the framework for sensitivity analysis but does not resolve the uncertainty. Investors who want to make informed decisions need access to channel checks, payer surveys, and management commentary that can narrow these ranges.

Second, how do hospital and institutional scripts affect the total addressable market for products like Journavx and Icotyde? The IQVIA data captures retail prescriptions, but for products with significant hospital channel exposure, this represents an incomplete picture. The report notes that hospital scripts are not captured for Journavx and that sampling friction may affect Icotyde data, but it does not quantify these effects. The gap between reported IQVIA data and true prescription volume is a source of both risk and opportunity, and it cannot be resolved without additional data sources.

Third, what is the competitive response to these launches? The report tracks biosimilar adoption for Stelara, Humira, and other products, and it monitors the momentum of competing products like Skyrizi, Tremfya, and Rinvoq. But it does not model how competitors will adjust their pricing, marketing, or contracting strategies in response to these new entrants. The competitive dynamics in immunology, HIV, and pain are fluid, and the prescription data captures outcomes, not intentions.

## A Decision Framework for Evaluating Launch Trajectories Against Consensus

For investors and analysts who need to make judgments about these launches, the prescription data provides the foundation for a structured decision framework. The framework has three components: volume trajectory, pricing sensitivity, and channel completeness.

Volume trajectory is the most straightforward. Compare the current weekly prescription run rate to the implied weekly run rate needed to achieve consensus revenue. For Cobenfy, the current run rate of approximately 3,200 per week implies an annualized volume of roughly 166,000, which is below the 197,000 needed for consensus. For Journavx, the current run rate of approximately 22,400 implies an annualized volume of roughly 1.16 million, which is below the 1.2 to 2.1 million needed. For Yeztugo, the current run rate of approximately 1,720 implies an annualized volume of roughly 89,000, which appears sufficient based on the first-quarter revenue per script. For Icotyde, the current run rate of approximately 480 is too early to annualize meaningfully, but the trajectory over the next three months will be decisive.

Pricing sensitivity requires a range of scenarios. For each product, estimate revenue at the high and low ends of the plausible GTN range. If the low-end GTN scenario produces revenue below consensus, the product must rely on volume growth to compensate. If the high-end GTN scenario produces revenue above consensus, the product has a cushion. Journavx is the clearest example of a product where GTN uncertainty dominates the analysis. Yeztugo benefits from relatively stable pricing because of its payer coverage. Cobenfy and Icotyde face GTN uncertainty that is less extreme but still material.

Channel completeness requires an assessment of whether the IQVIA data captures the full picture. For Journavx, the hospital channel gap is significant and unresolved. For Icotyde, sampling friction may be suppressing reported volumes. For Cobenfy and Yeztugo, the channel dynamics are more transparent, but the data still requires careful interpretation.

Applying this framework does not produce a single answer for any of these products. It produces a set of conditions that must be met for consensus to be achieved, and it identifies which conditions are most uncertain. The value of the framework is not certainty, but clarity about what needs to be true for the bull case to hold.
