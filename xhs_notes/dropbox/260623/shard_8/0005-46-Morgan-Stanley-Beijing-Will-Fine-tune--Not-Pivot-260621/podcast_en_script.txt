May’s retail sales were down year-on-year, partly because of a high base from last year’s trade-in subsidy program. But the report stresses it’s not just a base effect. There’s a real slowdown in the underlying demand, driven by a soft labor market.

EN_B: Yes. It means the recovery in domestic demand is slower than many hoped. The second insight is about the oil shock. While the overall drag from higher oil prices is manageable, it’s reinforcing this K-shaped economy. Consumers are cutting back on non-essential oil consumption, like driving gasoline cars and taking flights. That’s a clear signal for the auto and airline industries.
EN_A: So the gap between higher-income and lower-income consumers is widening?
EN_B: Exactly. That’s the K-shape. The high-end export sector is doing well, but mass-market consumption and domestic-demand-driven industries are under pressure.
