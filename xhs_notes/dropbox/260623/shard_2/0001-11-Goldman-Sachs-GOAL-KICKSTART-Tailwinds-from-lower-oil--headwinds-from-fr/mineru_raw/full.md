# Tailwinds from lower oil, headwinds from front-end rates uncertainty

Last week markets were caught between central banks meetings and the ongoing US-Iran negotiations, with a MoU signed last Wednesday. In response to our commodity strategists' lower forecast for Brent crude oil prices our economists have lowered their US 12m recession probability to 15% (from 25%). In his first meeting, the Fed Chair Kevin Warsh held the benchmark rate steady at 3.50–3.75% but delivered a hawkish surprise driving the broad Dollar Index to a one-year high (100.7). Outside the Fed, the ECB hiked policy rates by 25bp as widely expected, while the BoE held at 3.75%. Looking ahead, the US Core PCE release on Thursday will be the key data this week (GS: +0.31% m/m vs. 0.3% cons.). In the UK there will be more focus on political changes with Andy Burnham's victory in Makerfield and the resignation from PM Keir Starmer.

The hawkish FOMC meeting has increased uncertainty around the policy path – the ratio of 2y vs. 5y rates swaption volatility has risen to its highest level since early 2023 (Exhibit 1), suggesting increased uncertainty on the near-term policy path relative to the medium-term. This reflects Chair Warsh’s preference for less forward guidance and fewer press conferences, which could result in a wider distribution of policy outcomes. Despite the sharp decline in breakevens on the back of lower energy prices, front-end nominal yields remain elevated with 2-year real yields near their highest level since October 2024 (Exhibit 2). Equity markets historically digest higher yields when they reflect a friendly growth/inflation/policy mix, but struggle when higher rates are fast and associated with a more restrictive policy path. The Nasdaq/S&P 500 ratio has moved higher despite rising real yields (Exhibit 3), suggesting that AI-related earnings optimism and capex spending have offset the traditional valuation headwinds. However, with higher real yields and a stronger Dollar Gold has sold off - Gold volatility now screens expensive relative to both equity and rates vol (Exhibit 4). Uncertainty around future FOMC communication could keep front-end rates volatility elevated. However, the probability of US 2y yields moving more than ±50bp away from current levels over the next six months has risen to c.41% post FOMC (Exhibit 5), above the January 2026 lows but still mostly below the 2022-2025 levels, indicating that investors increasingly expect a sticky front-end rather than a large repricing in either direction.
