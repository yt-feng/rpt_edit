EN_B: The report is really asking: despite decent tax revenue growth, why is overall fiscal support weakening? It wants to figure out if fiscal policy is becoming less growth-friendly right now, and what the government might do about it in the coming months.

EN_B: On-budget revenue grew 6.6% year-on-year in May, almost flat from April’s 6.7%. But the mix changed. Tax revenue growth slowed to 6.8%, while non-tax revenue rebounded to 5.6% due to a low base. On the spending side, the contraction narrowed from 3.2% to 1.6%, but infrastructure-related spending remained very weak, falling 12% year-on-year.

EN_B: Exactly. It means the improvement came from areas like science and technology, not traditional infrastructure. That weak infrastructure spending directly weighed on investment, which dropped 11.2% in May. This suggests the government is focusing on structural spending rather than a broad stimulus.
EN_A: Second insight: land and property revenue. The report says it got worse.
EN_B: Yes, off-budget land sales revenue plunged 35.8% year-on-year in May, even worse than April’s 34.9% drop. Property-related tax revenue also slipped from negative 0.7% to negative 2.6%. The report estimates total government revenue from the property sector fell 23.9% year-on-year.
EN_A: That sounds serious. So what does this mean?
EN_B: It means the property downturn is directly squeezing local government finances. Even though home transactions in some big cities are showing green shoots, the report expects land sales to keep falling this year and beyond, because developers are still strapped for cash and construction activity remains weak.
EN_A: Third insight: the augmented fiscal deficit metric. What did it show?
EN_B: The report’s own augmented fiscal deficit measure tightened further in May. The three-month moving average improved from negative 9.5% to negative 8.5%, and the twelve-month average from negative 10.8% to negative 10.6%. While fiscal deposit data hints at faster spending of previously raised funds, this tightening suggests overall support is diminishing.
EN_A: So what does that mean for the economy? Is second quarter growth at risk?
EN_B: Yes, the report explicitly says fiscal policy became less supportive in Q2 compared to Q1. This matches their recent downgrade of Q2 real GDP growth to 3.5% annualized quarter-on-quarter. But they also expect support to pick up in the second half of the year.
