EN_A: So the real difference is at the front end. What does that mean for investors?
EN_B: It means if the next Chair steps back from guidance, the front end will start driving the whole curve again. The report has a clever way to show this. It looks at the correlation between 2-year yield moves and the 2s10s slope. Under Greenspan, they moved in opposite directions — the front end was leading. Under Yellen, they moved together — the long end was in charge.
EN_A: So if we go back to a Greenspan-style toolkit, what should investors watch most closely?
EN_B: The report says most people focus on front-end volatility, but they underestimate curve shape volatility — the movement in slope and curvature. Under Greenspan, curvature volatility was 80 basis points. Under Yellen, it was 44. If guidance is reduced, both will rise together.
