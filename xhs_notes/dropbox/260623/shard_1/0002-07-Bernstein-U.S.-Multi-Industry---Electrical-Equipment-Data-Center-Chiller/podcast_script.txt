ZH_A: 最近有个话题特别热，就是数据中心冷却。但大家聊得多的可能是液冷、风冷，很少有人专门讲“冷水机组”，也就是chiller。这份报告说，它才是整个冷却系统里最核心的设备之一。B，你觉得这个结论靠谱吗？

ZH_B: 非常靠谱。报告把chiller定位成数据中心冷却的“心脏”，无论是液冷还是风冷，都离不开它。它负责制造低温水，然后通过管道送到CDU或者CRAH，去带走热量。报告估算，2026年这个市场的总规模大概是80亿美元，到2030年可能翻倍到165亿，年复合增长率在20%左右。

ZH_A: 80亿到165亿，这个增长确实很猛。但报告里提到一个关键问题：到底是选风冷冷水机组，还是水冷冷水机组？这好像不是简单的二选一。

ZH_A: 那听起来水冷不是更好吗？为什么市场还在讨论风冷？

ZH_B: 这就是报告最精彩的地方。它引入了“free cooling”这个概念。当外部温度够低时，数据中心可以关掉压缩机，或者降低它的运行负荷，只用自然冷源来降温。这时候，风冷和水冷的耗电都大幅下降，但水冷机组如果用了冷却塔，水的消耗几乎不变。所以，在free cooling比例很高的环境里，水费可能超过电费，风冷反而更划算。

ZH_A: 所以这个选择不是绝对的，取决于地理位置和气候？报告里是不是还做了区域分析？

ZH_B: 没错。报告以美国四大数据中心市场为例，比如亚特兰大，因为水价高，只要free cooling比例超过50%，风冷就比水冷更经济。而在其他区域，这个临界点更高。这解释了为什么市场上开始有声音说，风冷机组份额在回升。

ZH_A: 听起来水冷机组在free cooling模式下有个“水成本陷阱”。那有没有办法绕过这个问题？

ZH_B: 有，报告提到了一个例外：如果水冷机组用的是干式冷却器，而不是冷却塔，那就能避免水消耗。比如江森自控新推出的York HT机型，就宣称可以在高温环境下使用干冷器，彻底消除用水。但报告也谨慎地指出，这可能会带来更高的电费，因为压缩机需要做更多功。

ZH_A: 所以这是一个“电”和“水”的权衡游戏。那报告对市场上的主要玩家有什么结论？特灵、开利、江森自控，还有维谛，它们的产品差距大吗？

EN_A: So, everyone talks about data center cooling, but most of the conversation is about liquid cooling versus air cooling. There’s one piece of equipment that’s absolutely critical but often overlooked: the chiller. A recent report from a global investment bank says it’s the heart of the cooling system. B, do you agree?

EN_B: Completely. The report positions the chiller as the core component. It creates chilled water that then flows to CDUs or CRAHs to remove heat. The report sizes this market at about 8 billion dollars in 2026, and projects it could double to 16.5 billion by 2030. That’s a compound annual growth rate of roughly 20 percent.

EN_A: That’s a huge growth story. But the report seems to focus on a key debate: air-cooled chillers versus water-cooled chillers. Is it really that simple?

EN_B: Not at all. That’s the central question. Air-cooled units are cheaper upfront but about 30 percent less energy efficient. Water-cooled units are more expensive initially but use much less power. The report does a specific calculation: for an 800-ton chiller running 24/7, an air-cooled unit’s annual operating cost is about 25 percent higher. That implies a roughly three-year payback on the higher upfront cost.

EN_A: So water-cooled is always the winner, right?

EN_B: That’s where it gets interesting. The report introduces the concept of “free cooling.” When the outside temperature drops low enough, you can turn down or even switch off the chiller’s compressor. Both air-cooled and water-cooled units save a lot of power in this mode. But here’s the catch: if the water-cooled chiller uses a cooling tower, its water consumption stays almost constant. So, in a climate with a lot of free cooling hours, the water bill can actually outweigh the power savings. Suddenly, the air-cooled unit looks more attractive.

EN_A: So it’s not a technology choice, it’s a geography and climate choice. Did the report look at specific regions?

EN_B: Yes. They modeled four major U.S. data center markets. In Atlanta, where water is expensive, the air-cooled unit becomes cheaper once free cooling exceeds 50 percent of the time. In other regions with cheaper water, that tipping point is much higher. This explains why some industry players are saying air-cooled chillers are gaining share again.

EN_A: So water-cooled chillers have a “water cost trap” in free cooling mode. Is there a way to avoid that?

EN_B: There is an exception. If the water-cooled chiller uses a dry cooler instead of a cooling tower, it eliminates water usage. Johnson Controls’ new York HT chiller, for example, claims to do this even in hot climates. But the report cautions that this likely comes with higher power costs because the compressor has to work harder.

EN_A: So it’s a trade-off between electricity and water. What about the competitive landscape? Are Trane, Carrier, Johnson Controls, and Vertiv very different?
