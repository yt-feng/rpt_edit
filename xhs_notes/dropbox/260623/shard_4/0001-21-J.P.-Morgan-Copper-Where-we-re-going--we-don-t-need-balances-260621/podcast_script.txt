ZH_A: 今天我们要聊一份关于铜市场的重磅研报。这份报告来自某外资投行，核心观点很有意思——他们说，铜价的基本面分析，传统的供需平衡表，现在可能已经不重要了。B，这到底是什么意思？

ZH_B: 简单来说，报告认为，当前铜市场的定价权，已经从传统的供需平衡，转移到了美国和中国之间的“拔河比赛”。美国通过关税预期，正在疯狂从全球市场吸走铜，而中国作为最大消费国，被迫接受更高的价格。所以，全球是过剩的，但非美市场是紧张的，这导致铜价在去年已经涨了超过40%，达到每吨13600美元左右。

ZH_A: 那这意味着，我们不能再盯着库存数据或者矿山产量来预测铜价了？

ZH_B: 对。报告明确指出，全球精炼铜市场在2025年其实是过剩的，过剩量超过50万吨。但LME铜价却涨了42%。这就是一个脱钩的例证。所以，未来6个月，最大的催化剂不是矿山罢工，也不是中国需求，而是美国对铜的232关税审查结果。

ZH_A: 这个关税审查，具体是什么？为什么这么关键？

ZH_B: 报告说，美国商务部必须在6月30号前向总统提交更新报告。之前他们曾建议从2027年开始，对精炼铜征收阶梯式关税，第一年15%，第二年30%。这个建议没有实施，现在就是要重新评估。报告认为，特朗普政府最终会实施这种阶梯式关税，目的是确保已经运进美国的铜，不会很快再流出去，而是要作为战略储备。

ZH_A: 所以，如果关税是阶梯式上升的，那意味着什么？

ZH_B: 这意味着，在关税生效之前，还会有大量铜涌入美国。这会继续拉紧非美市场，迫使中国继续高价买入。报告认为，在这种情况下，铜价会继续推高，向每吨15000美元迈进。

ZH_A: 那如果关税是一次性到位，比如立刻加到50%，会怎样？

ZH_B: 那就会是利空。因为美国进口窗口会立刻关闭，美国自己已经囤了大约120万吨的铜，这些库存需要很长时间去消化。美国退出进口市场，中国就会重新夺回定价权。到时候，中国可能会等到铜价跌到10000到11000美元才会重新大规模采购。

ZH_A: 所以，整个逻辑的关键，就是“关税的预期”，而不是关税本身。

ZH_B: 完全正确。报告里有一句话很重要：“Escalation is everything”。市场交易的不是关税这个事实，而是对未来关税是否会继续上升的预期。只要市场预期关税还会涨，就会继续抢跑进口，这对铜价就是利好。

ZH_A: 那中国在这个拔河比赛中，就完全没有还手之力吗？

ZH_B: 也不是。报告提到，中国会通过“买家罢工”和出口来制造波动。比如当铜价涨到14000美元时，中国买家就会停止采购，甚至把库存抛到LME去。但问题是，中国自身精矿供应很紧张，它最终还是要进口阴极铜来满足需求。所以，中国的“购买地板价”已经被迫大幅抬高了。以前可能10500美元就买了，现在要12500甚至13500美元才买。

ZH_A: 这听起来像是一个僵局。那报告里有没有提到，什么情况下这个僵局会被打破？

ZH_B: 有。报告提到一个非常看涨的“夹点”风险。如果关税是阶梯式且未来生效，那么在关税生效前，LME库存可能会被急剧抽干。因为全球的铜都会想方设法运到美国去。这会导致LME出现巨大的现货升水，价格可能会在短期内严重超调，甚至超过报告本身已经看涨的预测。

ZH_A: 那这个问题，报告里有没有完全展开？比如，如果中国也采取反制措施，比如限制铜出口，会怎样？

ZH_B: 报告确实提到了这个风险，但并没有给出详细的推演。它只是说，如果中国开始担心供应安全，可能会出台更严格的出口限制。但具体会是什么政策，影响有多大，报告没有完全展开。这可能是完整报告里会继续探讨的内容。

ZH_A: 所以对于普通投资者来说，现在最需要关注的是什么？

ZH_B: 就是6月30号之后，美国到底会怎么宣布。是宣布一个阶梯式的、未来生效的关税，还是立刻实施高额关税，或者什么都不做。这三个结果，对应着完全不同的铜价路径。想要获得更详细的推演和情景分析，我建议你去阅读这份报告的完整版。

EN_A: Today we are diving into a fascinating research report on the copper market. It comes from a major global investment bank, and the headline is a bit provocative. They say that traditional supply and demand balances may no longer be the main driver of copper prices. B, what does that actually mean?

EN_B: It means the copper market is currently being driven by a tug of war between the US and China, not by global surplus or deficit. The US is pulling in massive amounts of copper through the threat of tariffs, while China, the biggest consumer, is forced to pay higher prices to secure its own supply. This is why copper prices are up more than 40% year over year, even though the global refined market is in surplus.

EN_A: So we should stop looking at warehouse inventories to predict where copper is headed?

EN_B: Exactly. The report points out that in 2025, the global refined market was actually in a surplus of over 500,000 metric tons. Yet LME copper prices surged by 42%. That is a clear decoupling. So for the next six months, the biggest catalyst is not a mine shutdown or Chinese demand, but the outcome of the US Section 232 tariff review on copper.

EN_A: Can you explain what this tariff review is and why it matters so much?

EN_B: The US Commerce Secretary has to submit an update to the President by June 30. Previously, they recommended a phased tariff on refined copper: 15% starting in 2027, then 30% in 2028. That was not enacted. Now it is up for review. The report believes the Trump administration will eventually implement a phased, escalating tariff. The goal is to make sure all the copper already imported into the US stays there as a strategic reserve, rather than being quickly destocked.

EN_A: So if the tariff is phased and escalating, what does that mean for prices?

EN_B: It is very bullish. It means there will be another rush to get copper into the US before the tariffs kick in. That will continue to tighten the ex-US market and force China to keep buying at high prices. The report sees copper pushing towards USD 15,000 per metric ton over the coming quarters under this scenario.

EN_A: And what if the tariff is immediate and high, say 50% right away?

EN_B: That would be bearish. The US import arb window would slam shut. The US has already built a stockpile of about 1.2 million metric tons, enough to cover nearly 20 months of imports. With the US out of the market, China regains pricing power. They would likely wait for prices to fall back to USD 10,000 to 11,000 before re-engaging.

EN_A: So the key is really the expectation of escalation, not the tariff itself.

EN_B: Exactly. The report has a great line: escalation is everything. The market is trading on whether the tariff rate will increase in the future. If the expectation is that it will, people will keep importing ahead of it. That is bullish. If the expectation is that it won't, even a high tariff announced today could be bearish.

EN_A: Is China completely powerless in this tug of war?

EN_B: Not entirely. The report mentions that China can cause volatility through buyer strikes and exports. For example, when prices hit USD 14,000, Chinese buyers went cold and even exported some copper to the LME. But because China is structurally short on concentrate, it still needs to import cathode. So its buying floor has shifted much higher. It used to buy at USD 10,500. Now it has to buy at USD 12,500 or even USD 13,500.

EN_A: Sounds like a stalemate. Does the report mention any scenario where this stalemate breaks?

EN_B: Yes, there is a very bullish pinch point risk. If the US announces a forward-starting, escalating tariff, there could be a massive rush to ship copper to the US before the start date. LME inventories could be drained dramatically, leading to a sharp backwardation. Prices could overshoot even the bank's already bullish forecasts.

EN_A: Are there any questions the report leaves unanswered?

EN_B: Yes. For example, it mentions the risk that China might impose stricter export restrictions if it becomes concerned about supply security. But it does not fully explore what those restrictions would look like or how they would affect the market. That is probably covered in the full report.
