# China Solar Demand Will Not Recover in 2027: The Curtailment-Triggered Inflection Point Arrives Only in 2028

The dominant narrative in solar investing has been that China's installation downturn is cyclical and short-lived. That assumption is wrong. According to a detailed expert call hosted by a global investment bank, China's solar installations will decline by 26% year-on-year in 2026, continue falling at a slower pace in 2027, and only begin to recover meaningfully in 2028. The core driver of this extended trough is not policy uncertainty or subsidy phase-outs—it is grid curtailment, which has become so severe that it is now the binding constraint on utility-scale solar deployment. The implication for investors is uncomfortable but clear: the profitability recovery for solar main value chain players, which many models place in 2027, is likely deferred to 2028 at the earliest.

This is not merely a timing shift. The structural nature of the curtailment problem means that the 2028 inflection point is conditional on three specific drivers—policy mandates, grid investment, and load growth—none of which are guaranteed to materialize on schedule. The market has not fully priced the risk that the trough could deepen or extend. The report's expert forecast, which is notably more conservative than the bank's own model for 2027, suggests that the consensus view on the speed of China's solar recovery is optimistic. For investors positioned for a 2027 rebound, the strategic question is whether they are prepared for an additional year of margin compression, capacity exits, and valuation reset.

The following analysis unpacks the expert's demand framework, explains why the curtailment problem is more entrenched than commonly understood, and translates the report's findings into a decision framework for navigating the solar value chain through 2028.

## The 2026-2027 Demand Decline Is Not a Blip; It Reflects a Structural Pipeline Problem

The expert's forecast for 2026 installations—210 to 245 gigawatts, with a midpoint of 238GW—is broadly aligned with the bank's own estimate of 235GW. But the composition reveals a more troubling picture. Utility-scale solar is expected to fall 18% year-on-year, driven by a collapse in new project starts. In the first four months of 2026, utility-scale project starts fell 34% year-on-year, following two years of declining approvals. Given the typical six- to twelve-month lag between construction start and grid connection, this signals that 2027 utility-scale installations will be weak regardless of any policy intervention in the near term.

What makes this structural rather than cyclical is the reason behind the project start decline. The expert attributes it to low investment sentiment among developers, who are unwilling to commit capital to projects where grid curtailment erodes revenue. This is not a financing problem or a technology problem. It is a grid integration problem that has been building for years and has now reached a tipping point. In 2025, 23 provinces reported a decline in solar utilization hours, with an average drop of 12% year-on-year. Western regions like Tibet and Xinjiang have been hit hardest, but the problem is spreading east: Shandong province now sees solar output occasionally exceed 60% of total supply during peak hours, forcing curtailment.

The distributed segments tell a more nuanced story but do not offset the utility-scale decline. Residential solar shows unexpected resilience, with the expert forecasting only a 3% year-on-year decline in 2026, sharply higher than the bank's estimate of a 14GW market. The expert explains this through lower internal rate of return thresholds accepted by players in the POE (power purchase agreement) market, supported by declining financing costs and favorable tariff structures in southeastern China. Commercial and industrial solar, by contrast, is weaker than expected, falling 48% year-on-year. The market is transitioning from grid-connected models to self-consumption driven by solar-plus-storage, but larger "direct supply" projects face longer construction cycles due to complex grid-backup requirements.

The critical insight is that distributed solar cannot compensate for the utility-scale weakness. Even if residential and C&I segments perform at the high end of expectations, the sheer scale of the utility market—which accounted for the majority of China's 300GW-plus annual installations in recent years—means that a multi-year downturn is unavoidable.

## Curtailment Is the Binding Constraint, and It Will Not Be Solved Quickly

The expert's most important analytical contribution is the identification of grid curtailment as the single most important variable determining the timing and magnitude of the demand recovery. This is not a new problem, but its severity has escalated to the point where it now dictates investment decisions at the project level.

The mechanism is straightforward. Utility-scale solar projects in China typically sell power at a fixed feed-in tariff or through market-based mechanisms. When the grid cannot absorb the power, developers are forced to curtail output, reducing revenue and extending payback periods. The expert notes that in many western provinces, utilization hours have fallen so low that new projects cannot achieve the minimum returns required by developers and their financiers. This is why project starts have collapsed even as module prices have fallen to historically low levels.

The report identifies three structural levers that could improve curtailment and trigger a recovery from 2028 onward. First, strategic policy shifts: the National Development and Reform Commission has included clean energy consumption as a mandatory provincial key performance indicator, which creates a direct incentive for local governments to prioritize grid integration. Second, significant grid infrastructure spending: projected to rise to approximately 5 trillion renminbi for 2026-2030, compared to 2.77 trillion renminbi in 2020-2025. Third, sustained load growth: electricity consumption increased 5% year-on-year in the first four months of 2026, providing a growing base of demand that can absorb additional renewable generation.

These levers are plausible but not certain. The 5 trillion renminbi grid investment figure, while large, must be deployed effectively. Much of it will go toward ultra-high-voltage transmission lines that connect western renewable bases to eastern load centers, but these projects take years to complete. The policy mandates will help, but provincial implementation is uneven. Load growth, while positive, may slow if China's economy decelerates further.

The expert's conclusion that the inflection point arrives in 2028, not 2027, is therefore a judgment about the time required for these levers to have a material impact on curtailment rates. It is a conservative view, and one that the report acknowledges is notably more pessimistic than the bank's own model, which forecasts a 14% recovery in 2027.

## The Demand Trough Creates a Darwinian Filter for the Solar Value Chain

If the expert's forecast is correct, the solar industry faces an extended period of demand weakness that will test the financial resilience of every player in the value chain. The bank's analyst team translates this into clear positioning: preference for high-efficiency cell and module players over glass and polysilicon producers.

The logic is consistent with the structural dynamics of the downturn. In a period of demand compression, the companies that survive and emerge stronger are those with technology advantages that allow them to maintain pricing power or cost leadership. High-efficiency cell and module technologies—such as back-contact and heterojunction—command premium pricing and are less exposed to commoditization. The bank maintains a Buy rating on Longi Green Energy, a leading module manufacturer with a strong technology position.

Conversely, glass and polysilicon producers face a more difficult environment. Glass capacity has expanded rapidly, and demand weakness will exacerbate oversupply. Polysilicon prices have already fallen below cash costs for many producers, and a sustained downturn through 2027 could force the exit of marginal capacity. The bank maintains Sell ratings on Flat Glass Group (both A-share and H-share), Tongwei, and Daqo New Energy.

The extended trough has a potential long-term benefit: it accelerates the exit of high-cost, inefficient capacity. The solar industry has suffered from chronic overcapacity, driven by aggressive expansion during the boom years of 2022-2024. A multi-year downturn could force consolidation, leaving the remaining players with a healthier supply-demand balance. But this is a cold comfort for investors holding positions that may face two more years of margin compression.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Risk of a Deeper or Longer Trough

The expert's framework is rigorous, but it leaves several important questions unanswered. First, the forecast assumes that curtailment will improve based on policy, grid investment, and load growth. But what if these levers prove insufficient? Grid investment of 5 trillion renminbi sounds large, but China's grid is already strained, and the integration of intermittent renewable energy at scale requires not just transmission capacity but also advanced grid management, storage, and demand-side response. The report does not model a scenario where curtailment fails to improve meaningfully by 2028.

Second, the expert's demand forecast for 2027 is notably conservative, but the report does not fully explore the implications if the downturn is even deeper than expected. What if project starts continue to fall through 2027, pushing the recovery to 2029 or beyond? The report's positioning on high-efficiency players assumes they can weather the storm, but even the strongest companies face liquidity risk if the downturn extends.

Third, the distributed solar segments present a puzzle. Residential solar shows resilience, but the expert attributes this to lower IRR thresholds accepted by POE players. This raises a question about financial sustainability: are these players accepting returns that are too low to cover long-term risks? If so, the residential segment could face its own correction, removing the one bright spot in the demand picture.

Finally, the report does not address the potential for policy intervention to accelerate the recovery. China's government has shown a willingness to intervene in markets to achieve strategic objectives, and solar manufacturing is a national priority. Could the government introduce measures to boost demand directly, such as accelerating approval processes for utility-scale projects or providing subsidies for grid connection? The expert's framework suggests that policy is already shifting, but the timeline for impact remains uncertain.

## A Decision Framework for Navigating the Solar Value Chain Through 2028

For investors, the report's insights can be translated into a structured decision framework that accounts for the extended trough and the conditional nature of the recovery.

First, assess your time horizon. If your investment thesis depends on a demand recovery in 2027, the expert's analysis suggests you are assuming a risk that is not supported by the data. The evidence points to 2028 as the earliest realistic inflection point. For long-only investors, this means recalibrating expectations for earnings and cash flows.

Second, evaluate positioning by value chain segment. High-efficiency cell and module players have a structural advantage because their products command premium pricing and are less exposed to commoditization. Glass and polysilicon producers face a more challenging environment, as overcapacity and demand weakness will compress margins further. The bank's preference for Longi over Flat Glass, Tongwei, and Daqo is consistent with this framework.

Third, monitor the three drivers of the curtailment improvement thesis. Grid investment data, policy implementation at the provincial level, and electricity consumption growth are the leading indicators that will determine whether the 2028 inflection point is achieved. If any of these drivers underperform, the trough could extend further.

Fourth, consider the consolidation opportunity. The extended downturn will force capacity exits, particularly among high-cost polysilicon and glass producers. For investors with a high risk tolerance and a long time horizon, the survivors may offer significant upside once the recovery begins. But timing is critical: entering too early means bearing two more years of losses.

Finally, recognize that the solar industry's structural challenges are not unique. The grid integration problem that China faces is a global issue, and the lessons from this analysis apply to other markets where renewable penetration is rising rapidly. The companies that solve the grid integration puzzle—through technology, policy advocacy, or business model innovation—will be the long-term winners.
