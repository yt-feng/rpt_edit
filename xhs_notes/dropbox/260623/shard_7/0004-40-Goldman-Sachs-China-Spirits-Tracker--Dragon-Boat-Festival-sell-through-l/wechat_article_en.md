# The Dragon Boat Festival Data Confirms a Two-Tier Market: Ultra-Premium Spirits Are Recovering, but the Middle Is Still Stuck

The Dragon Boat Festival has historically been a minor seasonal event for China’s spirits sector, but the 2026 edition has become a critical stress test for the industry’s post-policy adjustment trajectory. The data from this year’s festival reveals a clear and sobering picture: the market is not recovering uniformly. It is bifurcating. The super-premium segment, led by Moutai and Wuliangye, posted double-digit sell-through growth, while the upper-mid tier and mass-market brands continued to struggle against a backdrop of subdued business sentiment and cautious consumer spending. This is not a cyclical upturn; it is a structural realignment of demand toward the highest-end products and away from the middle.

The significance of this festival lies in its timing. It is the first major consumption event following the anti-graft policy tightening in May 2025, which compressed government and state-owned enterprise related demand. The year-on-year comparison is therefore against a low base, but the fact that even with that base, recovery is concentrated in only a few brands and regions tells us that the forces reshaping the industry are deeper than seasonal noise. The pattern we observed is consistent with what a global investment bank report has documented: mobility declined, retail traffic was modest, and spirits sell-through improved only slightly from the prior year, remaining well below pre-policy levels.

For investors and executives alike, the Dragon Boat Festival data is not merely a snapshot of three days in June. It is a leading indicator of how the industry will navigate the remainder of 2026 and beyond. The question is no longer whether demand will recover, but which parts of the value chain will be the first to stabilize, and which will remain under structural pressure. The evidence points to a narrowing of the opportunity set, and strategy must adjust accordingly.

## Super-Premium Brands Showed 10-20% Sell-Through Growth, Confirming That Pricing Power Is the Only Reliable Shield

The most striking finding from the festival period is the performance of the super-premium category. Moutai and Wuliangye both recorded sell-through volume growth of 10% to over 20% year-on-year across most regions. This is not a small improvement; it represents a meaningful acceleration from the prior year, when sell-through for Feitian Moutai had declined by a similar magnitude. Prepayment rates for these two brands remained high, at 60% and above for Moutai and between 55% and 70% for Wuliangye, indicating that distributor confidence has not cracked at the top end.

What explains this resilience? The answer lies in the nature of demand for these products. Gifting remains the dominant use case for Feitian Moutai, and gift-giving behavior is less elastic to economic uncertainty than business entertainment or banquet consumption. When a consumer buys a bottle of Feitian to give as a gift, they are not making a discretionary decision about quality or price; they are making a social statement. That statement requires a recognized brand, and no brand in China carries the same weight as Moutai. The same logic applies, to a slightly lesser degree, to Wuliangye.

The wholesale price data supports this reading. Feitian Moutai’s original case price dipped only marginally during the festival, from RMB 1,670 to RMB 1,645, and remains significantly above its January 2026 level of RMB 1,505. The stability of wholesale pricing, even amid a festival period that typically sees some discounting, is a sign of disciplined channel management and sustained end-user demand. For Wuliangye, the price held flat or declined only slightly, depending on the wholesale market data source. The market is not panicking about the super-premium tier.

The implication for strategy is clear: if you are a brand operating in the super-premium space, your primary risk is not demand destruction but pricing discipline. The challenge is to maintain the premium without alienating your core consumer base. Moutai’s recent moves, including raising the ex-factory price of Feitian and adjusting non-standard SKU pricing on the i-Moutai platform, suggest that management understands this balance. For competitors, the lesson is that attempting to compete on price in this tier is a losing game.

## The Upper-Mid and Mass Segments Remain Under Pressure, with Even Strong Brands Showing Regional Weakness

If the super-premium tier is the story of resilience, the upper-mid segment is the story of stagnation. Luzhou Laojiao, for example, saw a significant decline in sell-through, particularly in Henan, a key market for the brand. Its prepayment rate dropped to around 30%, compared to 40-60% in the same period last year. This is not a brand with weak fundamentals; Guojiao 1573 is a respected product. But it sits in a price range that is increasingly squeezed. Consumers in the RMB 500-1,000 per bottle range are not wealthy enough to be immune to economic pressure, nor are they so price-sensitive that they will trade down to mass-market products. They are stuck, and they are cutting back.

Regional divergence further complicates the picture. Anhui remains weak, with constrained commercial and government-related activities dragging down consumption. Jiangsu and Sichuan showed slight recovery, but the nature of that recovery is revealing. In Jiangsu, improvement was driven by the development of technology sectors, not by a broad-based rebound in business activity. In Sichuan, business demand increased by a low-teens percentage year-on-year, but it remained 30-40% below 2024 levels. These are not signs of a healthy recovery; they are signs of selective, sector-specific demand that cannot be relied upon for broad-based growth.

The mass-market tier, defined as products priced below RMB 300 per bottle, is a mixed bag. Some key SKUs, such as King’s Luck’s Danya, posted double-digit sell-through growth, driven by stable channel profit margins and strict pricing policies. But these are exceptions, not the rule. Most regional mid-end brands saw muted or declining demand, with sell-through largely dependent on personal consumption and dining scenarios. The banquet channel, historically a reliable volume driver for mass-market products, remained weak.

What does this mean for strategy? Companies that rely on the upper-mid and mass segments must recognize that volume growth is not coming back quickly. The path forward is not to chase market share through discounting, which would further erode channel profitability and brand equity. Instead, the focus should be on cost discipline, inventory management, and selective market share gains in regions or sub-segments where demand is still growing. The report indicates that some brands, like Fen Wine, managed to grow by over 10% year-on-year, suggesting that execution matters even in a difficult market.

## The Online Channel Is Growing Fast but Is Not a Reliable Price Anchor for the Industry

A notable feature of the Dragon Boat Festival period was the performance of the online channel, particularly during the JD 618 shopping festival. Overall spirits GMV on JD grew 25% year-on-year, driven by strong growth in Moutai and Wuliangye. Feitian Moutai’s original package GMV reached RMB 300 million, and the Wuliangye World Cup edition GMV increased over 13-fold. On the surface, this looks like a positive sign for the industry.

But the online channel’s impact on the broader market is more complex than the headline numbers suggest. The report notes that 618 promotional intensity was relatively rational this year and less disruptive to offline wholesale pricing. The gap between online ASP after subsidies and wholesale prices was small for Feitian Moutai. This is a healthy development, but it is fragile. If online platforms decide to compete more aggressively on price in future promotional periods, they could destabilize the wholesale market, which has been the foundation of pricing discipline for the super-premium brands.

The i-Moutai app data adds another dimension. Monthly active users on the app reached 10.2 million in May, up 4.4% year-on-year, but this is still well below the 18 million average seen in the first quarter of 2026. The app’s user base surged when Feitian Moutai was launched on the platform in January 2026, but it has since normalized. The DAU/MAU penetration ratio remains at around 11%, suggesting that while the app is a useful distribution channel, it is not yet a daily engagement platform for most users.

For brands, the online channel is a double-edged sword. It offers access to a large and growing customer base, particularly younger consumers who are comfortable buying high-value goods online. But it also introduces pricing transparency and competitive pressure that can undermine offline channel relationships. The key strategic question is whether brands can maintain a consistent pricing strategy across online and offline channels, or whether the online channel will inevitably become a discounting battleground. The evidence so far suggests that the super-premium brands have managed this balance, but the risk is not zero.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: How Long Will the Bifurcation Last, and What Will Break It?

The report provides a thorough analysis of the current state of the market, but it leaves several important questions unanswered. The first is the duration of the current bifurcation. Is this a temporary phenomenon that will resolve as the economic cycle turns, or is it a permanent structural change in China’s spirits market? The answer depends on the trajectory of government policy and business sentiment. If the anti-graft measures remain in place and economic growth remains subdued, the upper-mid segment could face several more years of stagnation. If policy eases or economic activity picks up, the recovery could broaden.

The second unanswered question is the role of the mass-market tier. The report notes that some mass-market SKUs performed well, but it does not provide a clear framework for identifying which brands or products are likely to succeed in this environment. Is it a matter of distribution strength, brand heritage, or pricing strategy? The data suggests that channel profitability and pricing discipline are important, but the sample size is too small to draw definitive conclusions.

The third question is the impact of the i-Moutai app on the long-term pricing power of the brand. The app has brought Feitian Moutai to a wider audience, but it has also reduced the scarcity premium that traditionally supported secondary market prices. If the app continues to expand its user base and sales volume, it could fundamentally alter the economics of the Moutai brand. The report tracks MAU and DAU data, but it does not model the long-term implications of this channel shift.

These are not criticisms of the report; they are the natural boundaries of any single data set. The Dragon Boat Festival is one data point, and it should be interpreted as part of a longer trend. The report’s value lies in its granularity and its willingness to highlight the divergence across price tiers and regions. For investors and executives, the key is to use this data to stress-test their assumptions about which parts of the market will recover first and which will lag.

## A Decision Framework for Navigating the Two-Tier Market

Given the data, the strategic question for participants in China’s spirits market is not whether to be optimistic or pessimistic. It is how to allocate resources across the value chain in a way that maximizes returns in a bifurcated environment. The following decision framework is designed to help answer that question.

First, assess your brand’s position in the pricing hierarchy. If you are a super-premium brand with pricing power and strong gifting demand, your priority should be maintaining pricing discipline and channel relationships. The risk is not demand destruction but margin erosion through excessive online discounting or channel inventory buildup. The data suggests that the super-premium tier can sustain current pricing levels, but it requires active management of distribution and communication.

Second, if you are an upper-mid brand, your priority should be cost control and selective regional focus. The broad-based recovery in your price tier is not imminent. Instead of trying to grow volume across all markets, identify the regions and sub-segments where demand is still relatively resilient. The report points to Jiangsu’s technology sector and Sichuan’s business demand as examples. These are not large enough to drive a national recovery, but they are large enough to support a focused strategy.

Third, if you are a mass-market brand, your priority should be channel profitability and SKU rationalization. The data shows that mass-market brands that maintained stable channel profits and strict pricing policies, such as King’s Luck with its Danya SKU, were able to grow even in a difficult environment. The temptation in a slow market is to chase volume through discounting, but that approach will destroy long-term brand equity. Instead, focus on the SKUs that have the strongest margins and the most loyal consumer base, and be prepared to cut underperforming products.

Finally, for all brands, the online channel should be treated as a strategic asset, not just a sales channel. The JD 618 data shows that online can drive significant volume, but it also introduces risks to pricing and brand positioning. The key is to integrate online and offline strategies so that they reinforce each other, rather than compete. This may mean offering exclusive products or bundles online, or using the online channel to drive traffic to offline stores. The brands that succeed in the current environment will be those that treat the online channel as part of a broader ecosystem, not as a separate business.
