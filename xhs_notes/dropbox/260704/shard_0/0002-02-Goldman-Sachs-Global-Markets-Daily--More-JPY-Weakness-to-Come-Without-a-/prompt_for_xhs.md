## More JPY Weakness to Come Without a Shift in the Macro Backdrop

The Japanese Yen recently traded to its weakest level versus the US Dollar in 40 years, keeping the MoF focused on the exchange rate and seemingly ready to conduct JPY-buying operations again. In fact, the latest reports suggest that the MoF may end the warnings ahead of official intervention to further discourage short positioning. But the broader macro backdrop of higher-for-longer US yields, low recession risk, lingering fiscal concerns, and only gradual BoJ hikes strongly argues for continued depreciation pressure on the currency. That explains the quick return to an upward trend in USD/JPY within weeks of the last operations, echoing the aftermath of the April 2024 intervention and implying a similar outcome if additional operations occur soon.

The trend higher in USD/JPY over the past year has been highly correlated with rising term premium in JGB yields relative to US Treasury yields. Prior to 2025, shifts in real rate differentials between the US and Japan generally explained USD/JPY performance well (Exhibit 1). But the typically tight, positive beta became less reliable about a year ago, following the LDP's defeat (under PM Ishiba) in the Upper House elections last July.
