EN_A: So there is this report about covered bonds and supranational bonds. It starts with a big regulatory move in Canada. What exactly is OSFI proposing and why does it matter?
EN_B: OSFI, Canada's banking regulator, wants to classify highly rated covered bonds as Level 1B High Quality Liquid Assets. Right now under Basel rules, they only qualify as Level 2A. This is a big upgrade.
EN_A: So what does that actually mean in practice?
EN_B: It means a lower haircut. Level 2A has a 15 percent haircut. Level 1B has only 7 percent. That makes covered bonds much more attractive for banks to hold in their liquidity buffers.
EN_A: And why is Canada doing this?
EN_B: The main reason is that Canadian banks want their covered bonds to be recognized as Level 1 assets in the European Union. Currently, only EU covered bonds with a special label get that status. This is about achieving third country equivalence.
EN_A: So it is a strategic move to align with EU rules?
EN_B: Exactly. Canadian banks have a huge amount of covered bonds outstanding, about 183 billion euros equivalent. Nearly half of that is denominated in euros. If they get EU equivalence, those bonds become much more valuable for liquidity purposes.
EN_A: Does that mean Canadian banks will start buying more foreign covered bonds for their own portfolios?
EN_B: The report is cautious on that. Canadian provincial bonds are already Level 1 assets in Canada with no haircut. So banks might prefer those over foreign covered bonds. But overall, this is positive for Canadian covered bond spreads.
EN_A: When does this take effect?
EN_B: The public consultation runs until July 20, 2026. The final guideline is expected in February 2027, with implementation on May 1, 2027. So it is still a ways off, but the direction is clear.
