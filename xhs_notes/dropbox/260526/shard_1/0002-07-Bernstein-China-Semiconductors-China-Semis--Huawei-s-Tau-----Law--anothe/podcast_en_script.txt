EN_B: Exactly. Huawei proposes to systematically reduce delay across the entire computing stack, from transistors to circuits, chips, and systems.

EN_B: One impressive figure is that Huawei has achieved a bonding pitch of less than 2 micrometers using a technology called LogicFolding. This allows them to stack logic on top of logic, cell to cell, rather than just chip to chip.
EN_A: That sounds like an advanced version of 3D packaging. But what’s the real advantage?
EN_B: The key difference is that traditional stacking is die-to-die, like putting SRAM on top of logic. Huawei is doing cell-to-cell, stacking individual transistor units. This dramatically shortens interconnect distances and reduces latency.
EN_A: So how much performance gain are we talking about?
EN_B: The report says they aim to increase transistor density from 155 million per square millimeter in 2025 to 238 million in 2026. That’s roughly equivalent to TSMC’s N3 node density.

EN_A: So what are the obvious weaknesses or constraints of this roadmap?
EN_B: The report highlights three main challenges. First, TSMC still has a huge lead in advanced packaging technology and ecosystem. Second, stacking increases power density and thermal issues are a major concern. Third, yield and cost remain big barriers.
