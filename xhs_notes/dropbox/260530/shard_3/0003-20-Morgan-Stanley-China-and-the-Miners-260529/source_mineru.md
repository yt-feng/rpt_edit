# China and the Miners

Our monthly report summarizes key data/charts from China to give investors quick insights into China's macroenvironment and its demand for mineral resources.

China view – Energy Shock and Weaker Consumption: Our China economics team notes that April activity slowed more sharply than expected, weighed by the oil shock impacting energy-intensive sectors and broad-based consumption weakness, with retail sales dropping to a record low ex-Covid and property showing no signs of stabilization despite selective gains in top-tier secondary markets. While export-linked production (EVs, machinery, tech) remained resilient on AI-led global capex strength, they cut 2Q GDP tracking by 20bps to 4.5%Y, and expect a recovery toward 5%Y in 2H as the oil shock fades and exports stay strong, although consumption is likely to lag amid weak labour markets, fading policy support, and ongoing property adjustment (see here).

Macro – Production Declines for Majority: China's IP was 4.1% YoY in April (Mar 5.7%); April PMI was 50.3 (Mar 50.4). April CPI was 1.2% YoY (Mar 1.0%). New property starts fell 27.1% YoY in April (Mar -17.1% YoY) and GFA sold fell 10.3% YoY in April (Mar -8.0% YoY). Our China property team notes that while secondary home sales in top-tier cities exceeded expectations in March–April, momentum has slowed sharply in May, with divergence widening across cities as lower-tier markets continue to weaken in both listings and prices. Combined with fragile sentiment and fading policy support, they remain cautious on the rebound's durability, although near-term declines may stay modest on favorable base effects. FAI was down 9.4% YoY in April (Mar +1.6% YoY). April aluminum production was up 3.1% YoY to 3.9mnt, reflecting capacity resumption in Liaoning.

Commodities data – Steel Exports Slowed: Steel exports in April decreased 9% YoY to 9.5mnt, and crude steel output was down 2.8% YoY in April, while domestic steel apparent consumption fell 3.1% YoY. China's iron ore imports were up 1% YoY in April to 104mnt. Port inventories started to draw slowly during the month but remain elevated.

# Major Theme of the Month

# Tighter Copper Scrap Market in China?

China's output of refined copper and semi-finished products has grown more reliant on scrap, but availability is being limited by domestic policy while global scrap trade is changing too. This brings upside risks to copper prices, all else equal. For further details, see Commodity Matters: Tighter Copper Scrap Market in China? (14 May 2026)

Tightening copper scrap markets might weigh on refined copper output in China (and elsewhere): Tight copper concentrate markets have pushed China's copper smelters to use more scrap in recent years. However, tighter regulation of China's domestic scrap market – particularly full implementation of reverse invoicing and clampdown on circular invoicing – have increased compliance and tax scrutiny, raising costs and limiting the supply of compliant, invoice-bearing material. US-China trade tensions have also weighed on US scrap exports, while growing recognition of scrap's strategic importance is leading to increased policy focus, which may further restrict trade and global availability.

Secondary semi-fabricators may turn to more refined metal: With less scrap available, semi-fabricators are either lowering utilisation rates or using refined copper to feed their operations, supporting refined demand and potentially driving the rapid copper inventory draws since CNY.

Smelters face feed challenges too, potentially impacting refined output: Smelters may also face more challenges sourcing scrap and copper concentrate, weighing on metal output. Larger smelters may be more resilient though, and some may even see improved scrap availability as smaller secondary processors cease operations.

MS Outlook: A tighter scrap market brings upside risk to copper prices, all else equal, as reduced supply from scrap-based semi-fabricators, or more semis being made with refined metal, plus risk of smelter cuts, point to a tighter refined copper market. However, with copper already at all-time highs, demand may come under pressure too, providing some offset, while the clampdown on circular invoicing is also impacting spot copper trading.
