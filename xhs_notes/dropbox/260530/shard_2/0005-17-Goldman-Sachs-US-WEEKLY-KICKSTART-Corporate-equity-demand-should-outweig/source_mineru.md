# Corporate equity demand should outweigh record IPO supply in 2026

- IPO activity is continuing to recover after four years of muted issuance. 40 deals worth \$28 billion have launched YTD, the largest totals through May since 2021. While elevated IPO activity has characterized some previous equity market peaks, the number of IPOs YTD is only on pace to reach the historical annual average of 100. In contrast, more than 250 IPOs launched in 2021 and nearly 400 launched in 1999.   
2026 should be a record year for IPOs in dollar terms. Our IPO Barometer continues to signal a healthy macro backdrop for new launches, and news reports and prediction markets indicate an increased likelihood that some of the largest private companies will come public this year. We raise our forecast for 2026 IPO volume to \$225 billion (from \$160 billion). We expect total corporate equity supply to register \$675 billion including follow-ons and other issuance. However, this issuance scales to just 1.0% of US equity market cap, compared to an average of 1.5% since 1995.   
As investor lockups expire, IPO issuance leads to additional potential equity supply. History shows that large IPOs with initial floats of less than 10% experience an average increase in float to 46% 12 months later. Following this historical pattern, we expect nearly \$500 billion in potential equity supply from expiring lockups in 2026. However, this post-lockup potential supply may not materialize if investors choose to maintain their ownership positions.   
Buyback growth has slowed alongside the AI capex boom, rising just 4% y/y in Q1 2026. Because market values have continued to rise, the S&P 500 buyback yield has declined to 1.9%, near the lowest level since 2021. However, the magnitude of repurchases remains large and continues to grow. Financials account for 27% of S&P 500 buybacks and grew repurchases by 18% in Q1. NVDA announced an \$80 billion authorization, and total buyback announcements are running at a record \$860 billion YTD. We forecast gross corporate buybacks of \$1.3 trillion this year.   
Despite slow recent buyback growth and prospects for a record year for IPOs, corporate demand for equities should outweigh both direct corporate equity issuance and potential post-lockup equity supply in 2026. Strong M&A activity should bolster that balance; announcements have totaled nearly \$900 billion YTD, 48% higher than this time last year, with nearly 70% cash consideration.

# The outlook for IPOs and the corporate equity supply/demand balance

US IPO activity has recently accelerated following four consecutive years of muted issuance. 24 US companies have come to market so far in Q2 2026, bringing the YTD total to 40. This represents more than double the number of IPOs at this point in 2025 and the largest total through May since the 95 IPOs launched in the first five months of 2021. Nonetheless, despite the recent pickup in activity, the number of deals so far this year has lagged the average total of 50 IPOs through May since 1995.

While modest floats should limit the equity supply created immediately by potential upcoming IPOs, these deals will create additional equity supply in subsequent months and years. Since 2003, 14 IPOs have come public with gross proceeds exceeding 5 bp of Russell 3000 market cap and float shares of less than 10%. On average, the floats of these companies increased from 7% at launch to 28% 6 months following the IPO and 46% by 12 months post-IPO.

Based on the historical pattern, we estimate that recent and upcoming IPOs will result in roughly \$500 billion of additional unlocked shares available to sell in 2026 and likely a larger quantity in 2027. We expect the majority of potential equity supply from the current pipeline of IPOs will become free float in 2027. Importantly, however, these shares will not necessarily translate to additional equity supply if investors choose to maintain their ownership in recent IPOs rather than sell those positions.
