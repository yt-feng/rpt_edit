# Rare Earths Dashboard

China still sets spot, but the West is paying for security; strategic pricing and ramp-up risk now drive the debate

This is our inaugural Rare Earth Commodity Dashboard, covering key ASX equity picks and the latest price trends across NdPr, dysprosium and terbium. Key takeaways:

1) China's rare earth controls proved the leverage is real, and accelerated a structural split in the market. In April 2025, China imposed export licensing on seven medium/heavy rare earths, Dy, Tb, Sm, Gd, Lu, Sc and Y, plus related compounds, metals, alloys and finished magnets. NdPr was not targeted, but the disruption was immediate: monthly NdFeB magnet exports fell $77\%$ to 1,238t in May-25, with US shipments down $92\%$ to 46t (see Figure 10). Exports recovered from Jul-25 as licences started coming through, but the controls were not lifted. The key lesson is that China's leverage is not NdPr oxide; it is HREs, metal-making, alloying, and magnet manufacturing. Even if near-term tensions ease, we see RE pricing bifurcation as structural. Western governments have seen China turn the tap, and we do not see the market going back.

2) NdPr spot has eased from the April spike, but ex-China pricing is becoming strategic. NdPr oxide is now \~US\$103/kg, down from an April 2026 high of \~US \$120/kg, but still well above the \~US\$60-65/kg level before the 2025 price recovery. Near-term spot price direction remains a China supply story, driven more by quotas, inventories and stockpiling than by incremental EV or wind demand. But the pricing framework is changing. The US DoW / MP Materials US\$110/kg NdPr floor, LYC's revised JARE offtake at the same US\$110/kg floor, and LYC's US Government agreement with a similar pricing anchor are early evidence that the market is bifurcating: China domestic spot on one side, and ex-China strategic pricing on the other. At \~US\$103/kg, current NdPr spot sits just below the US\$110/ kg MP / LYC strategic floors. We see that level increasingly acting as a pricing anchor for ex-China supply, underwriting returns on contracted volumes even if China spot trades lower.

3) The equity debate is shifting from policy support to execution. Our May-26 expert call with an independent hydrometallurgist found that 9 of 14 hydromet plants never reached design capacity, while most feasibility studies assume full ramp-up within 12-24 months. For LYC (OW), targeting \~12kt NdPr/yr, key questions are Kalgoorlie ramp-up, heavy rare earth volumes, downstream conversion agreements and CEO succession. For ARU (N), targeting \~4.44kt NdPr/yr, the debate shifts from funding to construction and hydromet execution. For ILU (N), Eneabba (\~5.5kt NdPr capacity) is the key value driver, but capital intensity, offtake and multi-feed commissioning risk remain live. Governments can help de-risk capital; they cannot de-risk metallurgy. We prefer LYC, the only producer in our coverage already making separated rare earths at commercial scale outside China, with HRE production underway and the lowest execution risk in the peer group.
