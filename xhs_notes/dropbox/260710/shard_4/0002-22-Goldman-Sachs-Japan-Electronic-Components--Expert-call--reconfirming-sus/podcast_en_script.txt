EN_B: The report argues it is sustainable. They note that HDD makers are not just increasing volume. They are actively pushing for higher capacity per drive and raising prices. There is even talk of long-term supply agreements extending to 2030.
EN_A: Raising prices? That is a big change. HDDs have traditionally been a commodity that keeps getting cheaper.
EN_B: That is the key shift. Because demand is so strong, manufacturers now have pricing power. They prefer to sell higher-value drives rather than just more drives. This pushes the average selling price up, which is a structural change.
EN_A: If that is true, it must be great news for the upstream component suppliers.
EN_B: Exactly. The report highlights three types of winners: companies that benefit from higher component counts, those that benefit from technology-driven price increases, and those that can gain market share in nearline HDDs. TDK is a good example.
EN_A: What is the story with TDK?
EN_B: TDK has guided for a 40% increase in HDD head shipments by fiscal year 2027. For nearline HDDs specifically, that growth is 50%. A big chunk of that comes from outsourcing from Western Digital and Seagate. So TDK is taking market share from its customers.
EN_A: What about HOYA? They make glass substrates. How do they fit in?
EN_B: HOYA's story is different. They benefit more from technology upgrades. As drive capacity increases, the glass substrate needs to be flatter and more durable. That allows for a higher selling price. The report sees this "technology-driven ASP increase" as a key driver for HOYA.
