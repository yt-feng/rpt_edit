The report's estimates assume this recovery, with smartphone gross margins rising from 7.0% in 2026 to 11.4% in 2028. The second variable, EV shipment recovery, is less predictable. The report assumes a gradual recovery, with EV revenue growing from RMB 122.5 billion in 2026 to RMB 225 billion in 2028. This implies that Xiaomi will need to launch at least one or two new models that resonate with consumers. Observers should monitor Xiaomi's product roadmap and consumer reception closely.

The third variable, AI value realization, is the most uncertain but also the most potentially rewarding. The analysts' RMB 32 billion estimate is conservative, but it is also opaque. Observers need to develop their own view on how Xiaomi will monetize AI and what that monetization could be worth. A reasonable range might be RMB 30 billion to RMB 100 billion, depending on the success of AI-powered features and services.

Using this framework, observers can construct their own scenarios. In a bear case, smartphone margins remain pressured, EV shipments disappoint, and AI monetization fails to materialize. In this scenario, the stock could fall to the analysts' bear case value of HK$15. In a base case, smartphone margins recover gradually, EV shipments meet the reduced targets, and AI value is partially realized. This would support the current stock price. In a bull case, smartphone margins recover faster than expected, EV shipments exceed targets, and AI monetization surprises to the upside. This could drive the stock toward the bull case value of HK$50.

The current stock price of HK$25.30 implies that the market is pricing in something close to the base case but with a discount for uncertainty. The asymmetry in the risk-reward profile is notable. The real opportunity lies in the bull case, which offers nearly 100% upside, but this requires a favorable resolution of all three variables.
