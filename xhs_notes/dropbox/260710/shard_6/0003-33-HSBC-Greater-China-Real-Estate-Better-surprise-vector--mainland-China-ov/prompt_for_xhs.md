## Constructive view on stabilising fundamentals

We continue to believe a housing market stabilisation is actively taking shape in 2H26, led by top-tier cities and higher-end segments. Albeit sector structural optimism, we note that the recovery is still city- and segment-specific, and the wider macro backdrop still creates noise and volatility for sentiment and broader-based rebound (Residential resilience vs retail resistance, 17 June 2026). Our constructive view is mainly based on:

1) Solid demand in both primary and secondary market. The sales recovery is most evident in the secondary market, where years of price correction and supportive mortgage rates have lifted affordability to a 10-year high (Demand reawakens: Affordability at a decade-best level, 26 May 2026). Stronger resale liquidity is also creating a positive feedback loop, supporting ownership demand as risk appetite improves (From ‘exit’ to ‘upgrade’ – why resale liquidity matters, 24 March 2026).

In the primary market, projects aimed at upgraders and luxury buyers continue to outperform. Upcoming luxury launches are likely to further underpin sentiment and reinforce improving price dynamics (Widening winners' circle in 1H26; 2H gets easier, 2 July 2026).

2) Supply squeeze to accelerate new market equilibrium. Although inventory months remain elevated at a national level, absolute inventory volumes have fallen meaningfully (we estimate \~20% across 2023-25), with sharper declines in select cities such as Shenzhen. This tightening is key to rebalancing supply and demand, stabilising price expectations and potentially enabling price increases in high-end projects – supporting developers with premium exposure (e.g., CRL and C&D). Further upside could come from faster local-government buybacks of idle land. (Construction decline – Less built, less left, 18 May 2026).

3) Land market reheating – leading indicator. Scarcity of prime plots and resilient high-end demand should help anchor – and potentially lift – new-home price expectations (A positive leading indicator after valuation reset, 2 July 2026). Recent strength in land auctions points to improving forward conditions, and an expected increase in high-quality land supply could further support recovery (Figure 5), benefiting well-positioned developers through land replenishment, stronger saleable resources, and improved margins.
