ZH_A: 今天我们要聊一份很有意思的报告，关于中国6月的通胀数据。CPI回落了，PPI却还在涨，这背后到底在说什么？

ZH_B: 对，表面上看数字有点矛盾，但核心逻辑其实很清晰。CPI放缓主要是被全球油价和金价拖下来的，而PPI上涨更多是基数效应和上游能源的滞后影响。

ZH_A: 所以这不是一个“通胀全面升温”的信号，对吧？

ZH_B: 完全不是。报告里特别强调，如果剔除掉油和黄金，核心CPI其实只有0.4%左右，非常稳定。而且下游消费品价格还在跌，说明内需依然偏弱。

ZH_A: 那PPI涨到4.1%呢？连续四个月正增长，是不是意味着工业端在复苏？

ZH_B: 要小心解读。PPI的上涨全部集中在上游，像石油、有色金属、煤炭这些。下游制造业的价格甚至还在同比下降，比如汽车制造。这说明涨价没有传导到消费端，不是经济全面回暖的信号。

ZH_A: 也就是说，上游涨价更多是输入性的，不是需求拉动的？

ZH_B: 没错。报告里做了一个很关键的分解：油相关行业贡献了1.6个百分点，有色贡献了1.7个百分点，芯片相关的技术制造贡献了0.4个百分点。去掉这些，核心PPI只有0.6%左右。

ZH_A: 那对于接下来的政策，这份报告有什么判断？

ZH_B: 报告认为，尽管通胀数据有波动，但北京大概率会继续维持宽松的货币政策和加大财政支出。但有意思的是，报告维持了今年不再降准降息的预测，理由是市场流动性已经非常充裕，国债收益率也很低。

ZH_A: 这个判断挺大胆的。那7月的通胀数据会怎么走？

ZH_B: 报告预计CPI会进一步回落到0.8%，主要是因为食品价格在跌，油价支撑也在减弱。PPI可能维持在4.1%左右，但如果最近油价反弹持续，那就有上行风险了。

ZH_A: 油价反弹？最近发生了什么？

ZH_B: 报告提到，7月8日地缘政治紧张局势重新升温，布伦特原油从70美元附近一度跳涨到78美元。如果这个势头持续，会明显影响7月甚至后续的通胀读数。

ZH_A: 那猪肉呢？之前一直是拖累项。

ZH_B: 猪肉价格还在跌，同比降幅接近16%，贡献了负0.3个百分点。报告指出，供给依然充足，需求偏弱，短期内猪肉价格压力不会缓解。

ZH_A: 所以整体来看，通胀不是主要矛盾，但油价是最大的变量？

ZH_B: 对，这是报告的核心结论之一。但还有一个问题报告没有完全展开：如果油价持续上涨，会不会迫使央行调整政策？这个问题值得去读完整报告。

ZH_A: 确实，油价的走势太关键了。那黄金呢？报告里也提到了黄金的贡献在下降。

ZH_B: 是的，黄金相关产品价格同比涨幅从5月的39%降到6月的28%，贡献了0.17个百分点。报告估算，如果剔除黄金，核心CPI会更低，只有0.83%左右。

ZH_A: 所以通胀数据里藏着很多“一次性”因素？

ZH_B: 没错。去掉油和黄金，中国内生的通胀压力其实非常微弱。这也是为什么报告认为政策会继续宽松，但又不会轻易降息的原因。

EN_A: So China’s June inflation data just came out. CPI slowed, but PPI went up. What’s the real story here?

EN_B: The headline numbers look a bit confusing, but the logic is actually pretty clear. The CPI drop was mainly driven by lower global oil and gold prices. The PPI rise was more about base effects and upstream energy costs.

EN_A: So this isn’t a sign that inflation is heating up across the board?

EN_B: Not at all. The report makes a key point: if you strip out oil and gold, core CPI is only around 0.4%. And consumer goods prices are still falling at the downstream level. Domestic demand remains weak.

EN_A: But PPI hit 4.1%, four months in a row of positive readings. Doesn’t that suggest some recovery in the industrial sector?

EN_B: You have to be careful. The PPI increase is entirely concentrated in upstream sectors like oil, non-ferrous metals, and coal. Downstream manufacturing prices are actually declining year-on-year, for example in auto manufacturing. This is not a broad-based recovery.

EN_A: So the upstream price rise is more imported than demand-driven?

EN_B: Exactly. The report breaks it down: oil-related sectors contributed 1.6 percentage points, non-ferrous metals added 1.7 points, and chip-related tech manufacturing contributed 0.4 points. Excluding those, core PPI is only about 0.6%.

EN_A: What does this mean for policy going forward?

EN_B: The report expects Beijing to keep monetary policy accommodative and ramp up fiscal spending. But here’s the interesting part: they maintain their forecast for no further RRR or rate cuts this year, because market liquidity is already ample and bond yields are low.

EN_A: That’s a pretty strong call. What about July inflation?

EN_B: They expect CPI to fall further to 0.8%, dragged by food prices and a smaller boost from energy. PPI may stay around 4.1%, but there’s an upside risk if the recent oil price rebound continues.

EN_A: Oil rebound? What happened?

EN_B: On July 8, geopolitical tensions flared up again, and Brent crude jumped from around 70 to 78 dollars a barrel. If that persists, it could significantly affect July and even subsequent inflation readings.

EN_A: And pork? That’s been a big drag.

EN_B: Pork prices are still falling, down nearly 16% year-on-year, contributing negative 0.3 percentage points. Supply remains abundant and demand is weak, so pressure won’t ease soon.

EN_A: So overall, inflation isn’t the main issue, but oil is the biggest wild card?

EN_B: That’s one of the report’s core conclusions. But one question it doesn’t fully answer is: if oil keeps rising, would the central bank be forced to adjust policy? That’s worth reading the full report for.

EN_A: Definitely. And gold? The report mentioned its contribution is declining.

EN_B: Yes, gold-related product price inflation dropped from 39% in May to 28% in June, contributing 0.17 percentage points. If you strip out gold, core CPI would be even lower, around 0.83%.

EN_A: So there are a lot of one-off factors in these numbers.

EN_B: Exactly. Remove oil and gold, and China’s domestic inflation pressure is really quite mild. That’s why the report expects continued easing, but not rate cuts.
