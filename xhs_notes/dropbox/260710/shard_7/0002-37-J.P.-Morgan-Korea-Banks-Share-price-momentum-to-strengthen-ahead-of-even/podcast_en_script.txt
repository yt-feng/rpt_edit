EN_A: Korean bank stocks have been on a tear since July, up 8 percent while the broader KOSPI dropped 15 percent. What is driving this outperformance?
EN_B: The market is pricing in a strong second quarter earnings season, but more importantly, it is betting on a sustained improvement in shareholder returns. This is not a one-off bounce. It looks like a structural re-rating story.
EN_A: A structural re-rating? That sounds like more than just a short-term trade. So what is the big question this report is trying to answer?
EN_B: The core question is whether Korean financial groups can sustain their earnings momentum and use more aggressive shareholder return policies to close the valuation gap. Right now, these banks trade at less than one times book value, even though their return on equity is around 11 to 12 percent. The report argues that discount will narrow.
EN_A: So what should we watch for in the second quarter numbers? Any key metrics that stand out?
EN_B: A few things. First, net interest margins are expected to grind higher, especially for the large banks with lower funding costs. Second, loan growth will be driven by corporate lending, even though household lending is still constrained. Third, non-interest income should see a nice boost from capital markets-related fees. And fourth, credit costs remain well contained.
EN_A: So it is a pretty solid setup across the board. But what about the thing everyone really cares about — shareholder returns?
EN_B: That is actually the biggest catalyst. The previous round of buybacks is ending in July, and the market expects another round, likely larger for KB and Shinhan given their stronger earnings. Hana is also expected to update its Value-Up plan, potentially introducing a more formula-based shareholder return framework.
EN_A: Formula-based? What does that mean in practice?
EN_B: It means shareholder returns are no longer discretionary. They become tied to capital ratios and ROE targets. Once that mechanism is in place, the market tends to assign a higher valuation multiple. That is why the report believes that even with the won depreciation headwind, solid earnings and disciplined capital management can support higher payouts and more buybacks.
EN_A: Are there any risks or questions the report does not fully address? For example, what if the economy weakens and credit costs spike?
EN_B: That is a good point. The report assumes credit costs will stay in line with full-year guidance, but it does not deeply stress-test a scenario where interest rates keep rising and SME defaults increase. Also, the impact of the weak won on foreign investor returns is not fully quantified.
EN_A: So those are the open questions that the full report would explore?
EN_B: Exactly. The full report includes detailed earnings forecasts, valuation models, and a list of key questions to watch on management conference calls. For example, how will KB and Shinhan's CET1 ratios evolve, and how aggressive will Hana's new Value-Up targets be? Those are details worth digging into.
