EN_B: The industry is segmenting by patient severity, clinical need, and device invasiveness. Semi-invasive systems might be the sweet spot for severe motor impairment, while penetrating systems like Neuralink will likely stay concentrated in a very small population.

EN_B: It depends on the route. In non-invasive BCI, AI can materially shorten algorithm development cycles and accelerate individual calibration. That’s real value. But in implanted BCI, the core bottleneck isn’t AI model sophistication. Neural signals differ between individuals, change with patient state, and drift over time after implantation. We don’t have the large, standardized, longitudinal datasets needed to train a universal “brain foundation model.” So the near-term value of AI is highly asymmetric.
