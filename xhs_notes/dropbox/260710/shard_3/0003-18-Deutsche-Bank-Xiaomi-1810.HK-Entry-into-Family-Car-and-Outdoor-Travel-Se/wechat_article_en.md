This pricing strategy is not accidental. The "SkyNomad" series is Xiaomi’s answer to the question of how to keep factories running when premium BEV demand adjusts. The answer is to build vehicles with different component choices and accept a different margin structure in exchange for unit volume.

## The PHEV Strategy Introduces Structural Margin Considerations That Will Test Xiaomi’s Ability to Maintain Its Valuation Premium

Xiaomi’s stock has historically commanded a premium valuation relative to traditional automakers because investors have viewed it as a technology company entering the automotive space, not as a car company. This narrative has been supported by the company’s strong brand, its ecosystem integration capabilities, and the perception that its BEV technology is cutting-edge.

The "SkyNomad" series complicates this narrative in several ways. First, PHEVs are generally viewed as a transitional technology rather than a cutting-edge one. By entering this segment, Xiaomi positions itself closer to traditional automakers that offer hybrid options and further from the pure-play EV narrative that has supported its valuation.

Second, the use of lower-cost battery suppliers signals that Xiaomi is prioritizing cost reduction over performance optimization. While this makes business sense for a volume play, it shifts the engineering-first brand image that Xiaomi has cultivated with the SU7 and YU7.

Third, the pricing range of RMB 200,000 to RMB 450,000 places the "SkyNomad" series in direct competition with BYD’s "Formula Leopard" brand "Ti7" and "Ti9" SUVs. BYD is a dominant player in China’s PHEV market and has significant cost advantages due to its vertical integration. Competing with BYD on price in the hybrid segment is a different strategic challenge than competing with Tesla or NIO in the premium BEV segment.

The margin implications are substantial. The report forecasts 150,000 PHEV models delivered in full-year 2026. At an average selling price of, say, RMB 250,000, this represents approximately RMB 37.5 billion in revenue. But if margins on these vehicles are significantly different from the BEV lineup, the contribution to profitability may be modest. Investors will need to assess whether the volume gain aligns with the margin structure.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: Can Xiaomi Manage Brand Positioning While Scaling Its Hybrid Business?

This is a fundamentally different value proposition. It appeals to practical, value-conscious families rather than performance enthusiasts. The question is whether Xiaomi can successfully manage two distinct brand identities simultaneously, or whether the "SkyNomad" series will shift the premium positioning that the company has worked to establish.

A related question concerns the customer overlap between the BEV and PHEV lineups. If the "SkyNomad" series is priced below the SU7 and YU7, it may attract customers who would otherwise have purchased a BEV. This internal cannibalization would affect the volume thesis for the PHEV series, as the net addition to Xiaomi’s total sales would be different from the gross volume figure suggests.

The report also does not address the regulatory and policy environment for PHEVs in China. While PHEVs currently benefit from certain incentives, the Chinese government has signaled a long-term preference for BEVs. Changes to subsidy policies or license plate privileges could materially affect the demand for PHEVs in the coming years.
