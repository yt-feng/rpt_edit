## 2Q26 earnings preview – Sector leaders poised for robust profit growth, while business mix and one-offs drive divergence across names

\- ZTO is set to deliver another quarter of outperformance, with volume and profit growth outpacing the industry on the back of market share gains and strength in reverse logistics. We expect ZTO's 2Q26 parcel volume to grow faster than the industry's 4% Y/Y pace, as ZTO continues to consolidate share and leverage its leadership in reverse logistics. Operating profit is poised to rise 30–40% Y/Y, with non-GAAP net profit likely to post double-digit Y/Y growth, supported by strong underlying execution and a low base from last year. This setup reinforces ZTO's position as the sector's most consistent compounder, with both scale and margin momentum working in its favor.

\- JD Logistics is on track for another strong quarter, with robust revenue growth and internal ISC business driving operating profit, though non-IFRS net profit may be held back by one-offs. We expect JDL's 2Q26 revenue to rise 20–25% Y/Y to Rmb62–64B, reflecting continued strength in core logistics and better-than-expected performance from its internal ISC business. Operating profit is set to grow at a double-digit Y/Y rate, but non-IFRS net profit is likely to be flattish Y/Y, as positive operating trends are offset by one-off items including interest income, government subsidies, and FX loss. This quarter should further cement JDL's reputation for top-line resilience, even as bottom-line growth is temporarily capped by non-operational factors.

\- J&T is poised to set a new operational milestone, with record parcel volume and strong profit growth supporting upside to full-year guidance. J&T's 2Q26 group-level parcel volume surged 24% Y/Y to 9.18B, with daily volume exceeding 100MM for the first time—a clear sign of accelerating scale. We expect J&T's non-IFRS net profit to grow more than 50% Y/Y, and see room for upside to the company's full-year guidance of US\$700MM. This performance highlights J&T's ability to capture incremental demand and drive operating leverage as the network matures.

\- SF's 2Q26 results reflect the company's ongoing business realignment, with quality focus supporting ASP but earnings likely to remain muted on a high base. However, we expect SF's 2Q26 earnings to be muted, with net profit likely to decline by double digits Y/Y due to a high base last year and ongoing business mix adjustments.

## June express delivery data continued to show a constructive industry backdrop

\- June data reinforced the sector's quality-led growth, with revenue and ASP gains outpacing volume. The June express delivery print highlighted a sector that is steadily moving away from “volume at any cost” and toward healthier, more sustainable growth. Industry parcel volume grew 3.8% Y/Y to 17.51B, with the 6.18 shopping festival providing a modest boost. More importantly, industry revenue rose 7.7% Y/Y to Rmb136B, outpacing volume and signaling improved monetization. Industry ASP climbed to Rmb7.77 (+3.8% Y/Y), marking another month of pricing recovery. This trend reflects the ongoing impact of anti-involution efforts and a more rational competitive environment, as companies focus on quality, margin, and pricing discipline rather than chasing low-value flows. The combination of steady volume and stronger revenue growth is a clear signal that the sector’s fundamentals are on a firmer footing.

\- Competitive dynamics continued to favor scale leaders, with STO and YTO extending share gains while Yunda prioritized margin over volume. June data showed that consolidation benefits and execution strength are increasingly concentrated among the largest players. STO delivered the fastest growth, with parcel volume up 19% Y/Y to 2.59B and market share expanding 1.8ppts Y/Y to 14.8%, underscoring the value of scale and network optimization. YTO also posted solid results, with volume up 9% Y/Y to 2.85B and market share up 0.7ppt Y/Y to 16.3%, reinforcing its competitive edge. In contrast, Yunda's volume was flat Y/Y at 2.17B, and its market share slipped 0.5ppt Y/Y to 12.4%, reflecting a deliberate strategy to focus on higher-quality, more profitable flows rather than chasing market share. ASPs for the three names were broadly similar (Yunda and STO at Rmb2.11, YTO at Rmb2.06), highlighting a more rational pricing environment and the importance of mix, scale, and execution in a stabilizing market.

## June ecommerce data reflects online physical goods recovery

\- Online physical goods GMV accelerated for a second month, but overall retail and category trends remained mixed. June saw headline retail sales growth turn positive at +1.0% Y/Y (vs -0.6% Y/Y in May), providing a modest lift to sentiment. Online physical goods GMV grew 3.9% Y/Y, marking the second consecutive month of acceleration and supporting parcel demand. However, total online GMV growth slowed to 2% Y/Y, dragged by weak service GMV (-1.5% Y/Y), which underscores that the recovery is not yet broad-based. The improvement in physical goods is encouraging for logistics, as it supports network utilization and shipment frequency, but the divergence between goods and services means the sector still needs to see a more comprehensive demand recovery before calling a full inflection.

\- Category trends showed narrowing home appliance drag and a sharp rebound in communication devices, while daily-use products stayed soft. The decline in home appliance sales narrowed to -8.7% Y/Y in June, mainly due to an easier base (c.32% Y/Y growth in June-25 vs c.53% in May-25 on trade-in subsidy), rather than a true demand inflection. Communication devices surprised to the upside, with sales jumping +16.5% Y/Y, driven by higher ASPs as memory costs rose and demand was pulled forward ahead of expected 2H price hikes. This dynamic is likely to support JD's electronics sales as comps ease further into 2H.

Discretionary categories were mixed: food sales rose 16.8% Y/Y in 1H26 (c.23% Y/Y in June), apparel grew 6.2% Y/Y in 1H26 (LSD in June, cooling from May), and daily-use products remained roughly flat, continuing to be the soft spot within online goods. The overall takeaway is that while some categories are showing resilience, the sector still faces uneven demand and will need broader improvement to sustain momentum.

Share price performance review – YMM's turnaround in the last one month is most notable, with sector outperforming the index in the same month

\- The past one month saw a broad-based rally, with every China logistics name outperforming HSI and HXC as sector sentiment improved. June marked a clear shift in market tone, with all major China logistics stocks posting stronger returns than both HSI and HXC. This sector-wide outperformance stands out versus earlier months, when leadership was narrow and laggards struggled to keep up. The rally was not just about a single name or a technical bounce—investors responded to a more constructive industry backdrop, as June data showed revenue growth outpacing volume, ASP recovery holding up, and competition remaining rational. These trends reassured the market that pricing discipline is sticking and that the risk of another round of earnings downgrades has receded, making it easier for investors to add exposure across the group.

\- YMM delivered the sharpest rebound, moving from the worst YTD performer to the best in the past month, while ZTO and JD Logistics continued to anchor the quality trade. YMM's strong rally (+19% in the past month) was the most notable, as the stock had been the biggest laggard YTD. This reversal reflects a classic mean reversion as investors saw less downside risk and became more comfortable with the company's risk-reward after a period of heavy de-rating and negative sentiment. At the same time, ZTO and JD Logistics remained solid, holding up well and reinforcing that the market still values scale, execution, and margin visibility. SF also bounced, helping to close the gap with the leaders and further supporting the view that the sector is moving into a more balanced and less punitive environment.

\- The sector's broad-based rally was driven by improving confidence in pricing power, monetization, and a more stable competitive landscape. June's operating data provided the fundamental support for the rally: industry revenue growth outpaced volume, ASP continued to recover, and anti-involution efforts kept competition rational. These factors gave investors confidence that the sector is not slipping back into price wars and that margin delivery is more sustainable. As a result, the market was willing to re-rate not just the leaders but also the laggards, leading to a rare period where every major logistics name outperformed the benchmarks in tandem.
