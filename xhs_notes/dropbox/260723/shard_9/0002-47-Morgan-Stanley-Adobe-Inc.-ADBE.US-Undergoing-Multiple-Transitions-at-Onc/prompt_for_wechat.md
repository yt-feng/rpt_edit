With operating profit per employee already optimized, margins already near peak, and management still needing to invest organically and inorganically to defend Creative, we see less opportunity for a standard efficiency cycle to drive material margin upside. To create a step-function in profitability from here, Adobe would likely need a meaningful restructuring – a difficult message to square while currently needing to execute against a strategy that depends on accelerating innovation, improving freemium conversion, and proving AI can expand rather than cannibalize the Creative base.

3. Timing of Leadership Transition Compounds Strategic Risk. The CEO and CFO transitions do not necessarily change our view of Adobe's product depth, but they undoubtedly raise the execution hurdle at a difficult point in the narrative. On 1Q26 earnings, long-time CEO Shantanu Narayen announced he will transition from CEO once a successor is appointed while remaining Chair, with the Board evaluating internal and external candidates. Separately, CFO Dan Durn announced his departure (to Marvell) concurrent with 2Q26 earnings, with Steve Day appointed as interim CFO. In our view, a planned succession would be manageable in a steadier period, but this is occurring as Adobe is rewiring go-to-market around freemium, deferring Creative Cloud pricing, integrating acquisitions, and most crucially, asking investors to underwrite a delayed monetization curve as it pushes more aggressively towards a freemium model. Until the new leadership team has set priorities and the market can assess whether the freemium / AI reinvestment cycle converts into ARR acceleration, we expect 'show me' sentiment to persist.
