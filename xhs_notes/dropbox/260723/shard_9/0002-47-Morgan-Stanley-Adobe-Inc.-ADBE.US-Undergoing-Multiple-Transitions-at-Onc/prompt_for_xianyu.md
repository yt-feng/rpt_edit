Moat and Journey Framework. In our Sector Insight today, we evaluated our software coverage universe through the lens of the opportunity and threat from AI. We looked at companies across two dimensions: the Moat a company has against AI-native entrants and increasingly easier code creation, and the Journey a company must take as it adapts to the pricing model, development cycle, and interaction method that will define the next phase of software. Adobe screens in the middle of the pack overall (3rd quintile, with a 64/100 overall score) with a Moat score of 33/50 set against a Journey score of 31/50 (Exhibit 2). Rather, risks concentrate in lower workflow criticality and weaker system-of-record characteristics relative to other software franchises – Adobe remains deeply embedded in creative workflows, but portions of the workflow are task-based, promptable, and increasingly exposed to AI-native substitution, particularly where users are not anchored in enterprise approvals, compliance, or broader content supply-chain orchestration. Along with Adobe's relative position in our Moat & Journey framework analysis, we downgrade to UW on the culmination of three impending transitions creating heightened execution risk:

1. Freemium Acquisition and Monetization Transition Stifles ARR Visibility. At Adobe's 2Q print, the company effectively lowered organic ARR growth (by \~\$500M) as full year ARR growth guidance (+10.2% YoY, reflecting a trend of continued deceleration from \~11.5% in FY25, Exhibit 3) was maintained despite the closure of the Semrush acquisition. This outlook reflects the strategic choice to accelerate freemium MAU growth and defer previously planned Creative Cloud pricing action, with management framing the ARR impact as split roughly evenly between those two drivers. Management commentary suggested the freemium pivot is in response to AI pulling demand away from traditional branded purchase journeys and toward intent-based creative tasks. While this dynamic does appear to be showing up in traffic patterns (Business Professional & Consumer traffic on Adobe.com was up 35% YoY as of 2Q, and creative freemium MAUs increased from 50M to 90M YoY), management is increasingly routing that demand into Acrobat, Express, and Firefly experiences rather than direct-to-paid funnels. Accordingly, we see a near-term Creative growth inflection incrementally more difficult to underwrite. While freemium can be the correct long-term strategy (especially if the alternative is pushing high-intent traffic into a paid flow too early and losing the user), monetization is unlikely to be linear. Adobe now has to scale and optimize its existing conversion infrastructure – including paywall timing, credit consumption and upsell journeys – across a much larger creative freemium audience without undermining engagement, and that process can take time. As such, we could see ARR growth continuing to decelerate into FY27 absent a more aggressive than

historical Creative Cloud pricing effort next year, which we view as unlikely to materialize given the moving pieces around freemium execution.

With best in class margins across SaaS (Exhibit 4), Adobe has meaningful room to invest in order to participate in this innovation cycle. However, even after stepping up investments through FY25, particularly as it relates to R&D (Exhibit 5), Adobe still ranks atop our efficiency analysis (Exhibit 6 & Exhibit 7), suggesting that the company may be approaching peak efficiency, presenting less leverage opportunity on a go forward basis. 3. Timing of Leadership Transition Compounds Strategic Risk. The CEO and CFO transitions do not necessarily change our view of Adobe's product depth, but they undoubtedly raise the execution hurdle at a difficult point in the narrative. On 1Q26 earnings, long-time CEO Shantanu Narayen announced he will transition from CEO once a successor is appointed while remaining Chair, with the Board evaluating internal and external candidates. Separately, CFO Dan Durn announced his departure (to Marvell) concurrent with 2Q26 earnings, with Steve Day appointed as interim CFO. In our view, a planned succession would be manageable in a steadier period, but this is occurring as Adobe is rewiring go-to-market around freemium, deferring Creative Cloud pricing, integrating acquisitions, and most crucially, asking investors to underwrite a delayed monetization curve as it pushes more aggressively towards a freemium model. Until the new leadership team has set priorities and the market can assess whether the freemium / AI reinvestment cycle converts into ARR acceleration, we expect 'show me' sentiment to persist.
