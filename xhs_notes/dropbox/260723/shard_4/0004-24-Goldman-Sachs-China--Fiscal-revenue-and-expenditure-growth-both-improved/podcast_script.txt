ZH_A: 今天我们要聊的这份报告，来自某外资投行的中国经济学团队，讲的是6月份中国财政收支数据。B，你觉得这份报告最值得关注的核心判断是什么？

ZH_B: 最核心的判断是，二季度财政政策变成了增长的拖累，而且这个拖累不小。报告估算，二季度财政收紧对GDP增速从一季度到二季度的环比下滑，贡献了超过40%。

ZH_A: 40%？这个数字很具体。那这份报告真正要回答的问题是什么？为什么现在要关注财政数据？

ZH_B: 它要回答一个关键问题：在二季度GDP数据弱于预期之后，政策制定者到底有多大动力去加码刺激？报告通过拆解“广义财政赤字”这个指标，来判断财政政策的真实松紧程度，而不是只看表面数字。

ZH_A: 那我们先从第一个关键洞察开始。6月份财政收入增速是8.7%，支出增速是4%，收入跑赢支出。这听起来不是挺好的吗？为什么还说财政是拖累？

ZH_B: 这里有个重要区别。收入增速改善，主要是因为PPI通胀上升和一些税收征管加强，不是经济内生动力变强。而支出虽然反弹了，但增速仍然低于收入。更重要的是，土地出让收入这个“预算外”的大头，在6月份同比暴跌了42.1%。

ZH_A: 也就是说，预算内的账本看起来还行，但预算外的土地财政塌了。那这意味着什么？对整体经济有什么实质影响？

ZH_B: 这意味着地方政府可动用的真实财力在萎缩。报告把预算内和预算外合并，构建了一个“广义财政赤字”指标。这个指标在6月份继续收窄，说明财政政策实际上在收紧。土地收入塌陷，加上政策性银行支持也在减弱，就造成了二季度的负向财政脉冲。

ZH_A: 第二个洞察，报告提到一个“财政支出穿透率”指标在6月份有所上升。这又是什么意思？

ZH_B: “穿透率”上升，说明政府确实在加快花掉之前筹集的资金，比如债券资金。但问题是，这只能部分抵消整体财政收紧的压力。报告指出，广义财政赤字的收窄幅度更大，所以整体效果还是偏紧的。

ZH_A: 所以加快花钱是好事，但“水龙头”总闸在收窄，光靠拧大现有的龙头不够。第三个洞察，报告对下半年政策怎么看？有没有什么悬念？

ZH_B: 报告预期7月份的政治局会议会强化宽松措辞，承诺加快落实已计划的措施。但关键悬念有两个：第一，出口依然有韧性，全年增长目标似乎还能达到，所以政策制定者可能觉得“没必要”推出大规模、广泛的刺激。第二，明年有重要政治会议，中央和地方都存在换届期间的执行约束。

ZH_A: 也就是说，报告认为政策会加码，但力度和速度可能有限，而且有不确定性。那最后，你觉得这份完整报告里，还有哪些问题没有完全展开，值得听众去读原文？

EN_B: The key takeaway is that fiscal policy turned into a drag on growth in the second quarter. And it wasn’t a small drag. The report estimates that the negative fiscal impulse accounted for over 40% of the sequential slowdown in real GDP growth from Q1 to Q2.

EN_A: Over 40%? That’s a huge number. So what’s the main question this report is really trying to answer?

EN_B: It’s trying to answer this: after weaker-than-expected Q2 GDP, how much urgency is there for policymakers to deliver more stimulus? The report uses a proprietary metric called the “augmented fiscal deficit” to gauge the real tightness of fiscal policy, beyond just looking at headline budget numbers.

EN_A: Let’s start with the first key insight. In June, on-budget revenue grew 8.7% year-on-year, while spending grew only 4%. Revenue beat spending. That sounds pretty good, right? So why is it still a drag?

EN_B: There’s a big distinction here. The improvement in revenue was mostly driven by higher PPI inflation and, anecdotally, more aggressive tax collection by cash-strapped local governments. It’s not a sign of strong underlying economic activity. And while spending rebounded, it still lagged revenue. More importantly, off-budget land sales revenue, which is a huge source of local government funding, collapsed by 42.1% year-on-year in June.

EN_A: So the on-budget picture looks okay, but the off-budget part, the land sales, is in a free fall. What does that actually mean for the economy?

EN_B: It means the real fiscal firepower available to local governments is shrinking. When you combine on-budget and off-budget numbers, the report’s “augmented fiscal deficit” measure continued to narrow in June. That signals actual fiscal tightening. The collapse in land revenue, combined with shrinking policy bank support, created that negative fiscal impulse in Q2.

EN_A: Second insight. The report mentions that a “fiscal spend-through” ratio rose in June. What does that tell us?

EN_B: A rising “spend-through” ratio means the government is actually speeding up the use of funds it has already raised, like from bond issuance. That’s a positive sign. But it can only partially offset the overall tightening. The report’s main point is that the narrowing of the broader fiscal deficit metric is more significant, so the net effect is still contractionary.

EN_A: So spending faster is good, but the overall tap is being turned down. You can only open the existing faucets so much. Third insight, what does the report expect for policy in the second half? Any open questions?

Second, there’s a major political transition coming next year, which could create implementation bottlenecks at the local level.
