## 2. Timeline for portfolio change

The most immediate step would be utilising the existing band within the current basic portfolio, which could be enacted at any time. Any revision to the basic portfolio itself, however, is more likely to be implemented at the fiscal year-end in March 2027. It is worth emphasising that a mid-cycle revision to the basic portfolio would be entirely without precedent—the often-cited October 2014 change was not a true mid-cycle adjustment, but rather an announcement brought forward by five months from its originally scheduled March 2015 review. As such, the timeline, process, and broader mechanics of any potential revision, remain highly uncertain and difficult to anticipate.

In terms of monitoring actual flows, the quarterly GPIF performance report will be a key indicator to watch. This report is typically released on the first Friday two months after each quarter-end; following this schedule, the next release is expected on August 7th, covering April–June 2026 data, followed by November 6 $^{th}$ , covering July-September 2026 data.

An additional gauge of actual flows is the monthly flow data, due for release on August 10th. Based on our estimates, July rebalancing flows should reflect approximately JPY 0.6tn of inflows into foreign equities and JPY 0.3tn of inflows into foreign bonds. A material deviation toward the outflow side relative to these estimates could signal a shift within the basic portfolio itself. It should be noted that these estimates are subject to revision and will be refreshed at end-July.

## 3. Potential follow-through from other Japanese investors

While repatriation has been a long-standing theme in Japan, a swift follow-through from other real money investors in the wake of any GPIF shift appears unlikely. Historical patterns indicate that life insurers (lifers) increased their foreign bond and equity holdings in tandem with GPIF between 2014 and 2020, when domestic yields were suppressed. However, this correlation broke down thereafter, as lifers sharply reduced their foreign bond exposure in response to rising FX hedging costs (Figure 2, Figure 3).

Current investment plans among lifers continue to reflect limited appetite for actively accumulating JGBs, particularly in an environment of rising yields (Japan Lifer's Hidden Outflows: The Reinsurance Play). Furthermore, a prevailing view of JPY weakness provides an incentive for lifers to maintain their foreign asset exposure. While price stability in the JGB market—underpinned by GPIF support—could improve sentiment toward domestic bonds, we do not anticipate a significant near-term follow-through, given the prevailing FX outlook and the one-off nature of GPIF's support. Japanese lifers currently hold approximately JPY 100tn in foreign securities (equities and bonds combined), with an FX hedge ratio of around 40%.
