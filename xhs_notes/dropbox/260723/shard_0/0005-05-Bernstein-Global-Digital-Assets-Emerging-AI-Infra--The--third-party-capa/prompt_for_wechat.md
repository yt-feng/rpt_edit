## AI COLOCATION - GROWING ORDERBOOK, IMPROVING ECONOMICS AND ON TRACK EXECUTION

Emerging AI infra players (former crypto miners!) have so far contracted \~7 GW of gross power for long-term AI colocation leases to multiple customers including hyperscalers, neoclouds, enterprises and high investment grade tenants. Based on the names we track, the industry AI colocation orderbook stands at \~\$135Bn (excluding the expansion options). So far in July, miners have contracted 1.2GW gross power across 3 colocation leases, securing \$35Bn in total contract value. We share key takeaways from recent deals:

(i) Strong deal momentum across new and existing customers: Our emerging AI infrastructure players (former Bitcoin miners) continue to sign new colocation deals with both existing and new customers. In our view, a growing new customer base and expanding relationship with existing tenants benefits both established infrastructure players (like HUT, WULF) and new entrants like (CLSK). Recently, WULF signed a \$19Bn deal with Anthropic (link), CLSK signed a \$6.6Bn deal with global technology company (link) and HUT signed another \$9.8Bn deal with an existing high investment grade client. Repeat contracts are a strong indicator of improving execution credibility and customer relationships.

(ii) Improving economics - Triple net structure, long duration: Lease structures are converging towards infrastructure-style triple net economics. Under triple net leases, tenant bears power, operating costs, taxes and insurance, effectively generating \~100% NOI margin for the landlord. Of the three colocation deals announced in July 2026, CLSK and HUT have secured triple net leases, while WULF has not disclosed expected NOI margins from the Anthropic deal. Further, recent colocation deals have a base term of 15-20 years (initial industry deals at 10-12 years). Longer contract duration caps upside from future rent escalation but also improves future revenue visibility and de-risks data center buildout capex.

(iii) Bitcoin miners continue to guide for \$9-12Mn capex per IT MW: Colocation business benefits from a capital light structure. Despite tight supply chains and execution complexities, Bitcoin miners have not indicated any cost overruns so far. Bitcoin miners continue to guide for \$9-12 Mn capex per IT MW for the recent deals. Further, miners have secured contractual safeguards against increasing capex - for example, Amazon (covered by Mark Shmulik) reimburses capex over and above \$10.5 Mn per IT MW to Cipher for the Stingray deal.

(iv) Improving financing spreads: Financing spreads continue to improve for data center projects backed by Bitcoin miners. Bitcoin miners are now directly contracting capacity to high-investment grade tenants, improving the financeability of their data center assets. HUT's \$4.25Bn investment grade notes were rated Baa2 and priced at T+165 bps (at 6.129%). The offering was the largest, tightest priced and highest rated investment-grade bond issued to date in a single-sponsor data center construction financing.

(v) Delivery remains on track: Execution and timely delivery remain critical for Bitcoin miners trying to build scalable data center platforms. Bitcoin miners have met the initial delivery milestones with Galaxy (not covered) recently energizing 133 IT MW Phase 1 at Helios, WULF already billing 60 IT MW to Core42 etc. We expect delivery pace to pick up in H2'26 with WULF (delivering 378 IT MW to Fluidstack), CORZ (scaling to 450 IT MW with CoreWeave) and CIFR starting phased delivery of AWS and Fluidstack contracts.
