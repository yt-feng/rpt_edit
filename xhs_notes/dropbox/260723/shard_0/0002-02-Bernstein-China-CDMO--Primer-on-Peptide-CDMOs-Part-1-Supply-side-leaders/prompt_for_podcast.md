This report is the first in a multi-part primer series on CDMOs. We start with a supply-side review of the peptide industry. Future reports will examine GLP-1-driven demand growth, peptide market sizing, and the long-term supply-demand balance.

A fragmented industry increasingly dominated by scaled platforms. The peptide CDMO landscape spans three groups: diversified CRDMOs - Thermo Fisher and WuXi AppTec among others, specialty peptide leaders - Bachem, PolyPeptide, CordenPharma, and a long tail of emerging suppliers. Historically, peptide outsourcing was led by specialists with deep manufacturing know-how, but the rapid growth of metabolic peptides has increasingly favored suppliers able to deploy capital and capacity at scale. Chinese CRDMOs have been the primary beneficiaries of the GLP-1 boom. Revenue of the top four peptide CDMOs expanded from c.\$1 Bn in 2021 to \$3 Bn in 2025. WuXi AppTec's TIDES business grew from \$120 Mn to \$1.7Bn, increasing market share from 11% to 54%, while Asymchem's peptide business accelerated to 124% growth in 2025 from a much smaller base. By comparison, Bachem and PolyPeptide delivered more modest growth of mid single digit to teens, respectively, as market growth became increasingly concentrated in large-scale metabolic peptide programs.

Cost structures differ dramatically. There is a wide range of profitability across peptide CDMOs despite operating in the same value chain. WuXi AppTec generates industry-leading margins with approximately 47% gross margin and 37% EBIT margin, compared with roughly 22% EBIT margin at Bachem (already leading among Western peptide players), while PolyPeptide and EuroAPI remain near breakeven. A major factor of the diverse economics is scale. Capacity disclosures are scarce, and we prefer peptide output in metric tons over reactor volume as a measure of manufacturing scale. Differentiation increasingly comes from conjugation technologies, sustainability, and speed of execution. WuXi AppTec and Asymchem offer the broadest technology portfolios across peptide-oligonucleotide, radionuclide and other advanced conjugates, while WuXi and Bachem appear to lead in process efficiency and waste reduction. Chinese players also generally deliver shorter peptide IND-to-NDA timelines than Western peers, strengthening their competitive position as peptide innovation expands into increasingly complex modalities.

## PEPTIDE CDMOS OVERVIEW

## Lay of the land: a fragmented industry anchored by a few of scaled leaders

The peptide CDMO industry remains highly fragmented, but participants can broadly be grouped into three categories based on scale and strategic focus. The first group comprises large diversified CRDMOs with enterprise revenues exceeding \$1 Bn, including Thermo Fisher (covered by Eve Burstein), WuXi AppTec, EuroAPI (covered by Justin Smith), and Asymchem. While peptides typically represent only a portion of their broader service portfolio, these companies benefit from global customer access, integrated development-to-commercialization platforms and substantial capital resources.

Then there are the specialty peptide CDMOs, predominantly focused on peptides and related modalities like oligonucleotides. Key players include Bachem (not covered), PolyPeptide Group (not covered) and CordenPharma (private, not covered), each generating peptide-related revenues in the \$0.1-1.0 Bn range. They differentiate with long histories, peptide-specific know-how and regulatory track records, making them preferred partners for innovator companies. Historically, they have dominated peptide outsourcing through specialized manufacturing expertise. However, the rapid expansion of metabolic peptides has increasingly attracted competition from larger CRDMOs able to deploy capital at scale.

Finally, smaller CDMOs and pharma/biotech companies with peptide manufacturing operations include a long tail of companies. A few in China (Jiuzhou Pharma, Porton, and Apeloa, all not covered) are expanding their peptide business in API and intermediate CDMO and loading up capacities rapidly (Exhibit 1).

## Revenue and market share evolution: the rise of Wuxi Apptec (and Asymchem next)

The competitive landscape has shifted dramatically over the past five years. Among leading peptide CDMOs, combined revenue expanded from approximately \$1 Bn in 2021 to \$3 Bn in 2025, driven primarily by demand for GLP-1 and other metabolic peptides. WuXi AppTec has been the largest beneficiary, with TIDES revenue increasing from c.\$120 Mn in 2021 to \$1.7 Bn in 2025 with over 70% compound growth, driving market share from 11% to 54% (Exhibit 4). Early and aggressive capacity investments made during the early 2020s enabled the company to capture a disproportionate share of industry growth.

In contrast, traditional peptide specialists have seen more modest growth. Bachem's peptide revenue increased from \$716 Mn in 2023 to \$862 Mn in 2025, while PolyPeptide grew from \$365 Mn to \$444 Mn, both growing at single digit to mid-teens. Although both remain leading suppliers with strong technical capabilities, their market shares have gradually diluted as industry growth became increasingly concentrated in large-scale metabolic peptides. Meanwhile, Asymchem remains small at \$152 Mn revenue in 2025 but is entering a rapid growth phase, with revenue growth accelerating to 124% in 2025 (Exhibit 5). Asymchem is another potential future share gainer as newly built peptide capacity ramps. Overall, recent industry growth has been led overwhelmingly by Chinese CRDMOs, while Western specialty peptide CDMOs have grown more slowly despite continued expansion.

## PLAYER BENCHMARKS: SCALE, COST STRUCTURE, AND TECHNOLOGY

## Scale: despite limited disclosures in the industry, Wuxi Apptec is on a solid level of its own

Assessing peptide manufacturing scale remains challenging due to limited public disclosure. Among major peptide CDMOs, only WuXi AppTec and Asymchem have disclosed aggregate peptide capacity in kiloliters (kLs), reporting c.100kL and 45kL in 2025, respectively, with planned expansion to 130kL and 69kL in 2026. For most other players, capacity must be inferred from individual site disclosures, announced expansions, revenue scale and industry checks. More importantly, we believe metric tonnes of peptide output are a better measure than kL of reactor volume, as productivity is heavily influenced by peptide length, loading, yields and batch configuration. On this basis, WuXi AppTec stands in a category of its own, with estimated output of \~58 metric tons in 2025, while most peers remain in the range of single-digit to low-double-digit metric tons, highlighting the substantial scale advantage WuXi has established during the GLP-1 expansion cycle (Exhibit 6).

## Large batch capability remains a differentiator

Reported commercial batch sizes vary significantly across suppliers, reflecting differences in technology platforms, peptide complexity and customer mix. Most leading peptide CDMOs operate at average commercial batch sizes of roughly 15-30kg, with only a handful of players possessing demonstrated track records in consistently executing larger-scale campaigns (Exhibit 7).

## Chinese capacity expansion has yet to be globalized

Chinese players, led by WuXi AppTec and Asymchem, have expanded peptide capacity aggressively and now rank among the largest suppliers globally. However, unlike Western peers such as Bachem, PolyPeptide and Corden, whose manufacturing networks are distributed across North America and Europe, peptide production capacity at Chinese leaders remains concentrated almost entirely within China today. While WuXi plans to add peptide manufacturing capacity in Singapore by 2028, geographic diversification remains limited at present, leaving Chinese suppliers more exposed to customer concerns around supply chain concentration and geopolitical risk (Exhibit 8).
