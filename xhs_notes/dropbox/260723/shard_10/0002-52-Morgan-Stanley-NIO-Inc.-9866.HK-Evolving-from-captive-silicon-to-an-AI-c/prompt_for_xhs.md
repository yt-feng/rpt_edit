# Evolving from captive silicon to an AI chip platform

NIO's chip arm GeniTech (Shenji) presented itself at WAIC 2026 as an all-domain AI silicon platform beyond cars, widening its market to embodied AI and inference while starting to monetize NIO's heavy R&D line.

GeniTech (Shenji) made its first standalone appearance at WAIC 2026 in Shanghai. In addition to its in-house smart-driving processor, management presented Shenji as an all-domain, all-scenario silicon platform spanning three aspects: intelligent assisted driving, embodied intelligence, and agent inference. Management even called it the only Chinese chipmaker straddling all three. We think GeniTech, as one of NIO's most sizeable R&D lines, is rebuilding a sunk cost into a monetizable franchise.

A broadening scope: We think what matters for investors is GeniTech's migration from driving into training/inference workloads for humanoid robots, driverless logistics, and high-compute terminals, precisely the adjacencies that widen a captive auto supplier's addressable market and give a growth story its upside. The lineup now runs across the NX9031 family. The high-end NX9031X anchors assisted driving and already sits in every NIO and Onvo model, with cumulative shipments surpassing 300k units. The midrange NX9031U, built on the same 5nm automotive-grade node, delivers up to 800 TOPS of equivalent compute under air cooling and powers the new Ruidong embodied-intelligence development platform for robot perception and planning, intelligent computing, and advanced manufacturing. Alongside the NX9031C/NX6031 sensing chip, GeniTech also launched a distributed agent platform.

Boding well for both funding and cost: On funding, GeniTech has pulled in close to Rmb3bn since its June 2025 spin-out, with an external round this February valuing it near Rmb8.3bn post-money. On cost, in-house chip displaces imported compute from global top-notch players, with a single NX9031 rated at the compute of four Nvidia Orin processors, and every unit produced amortizes a fixed R&D base across a widening volume pool. Of note, GeniTech's external licensing of NX9031 technology to a third-party auto chip began late in 2025, adding a royalty layer. Scale plus self-supply would allow NIO's chip initiative to stop draining margin and defend it.

We think GeniTech's WAIC debut nudges NIO's stock narrative one step away from a cash-burnt EV maker and toward a vertically integrated AI-silicon platform, a re-rating vector worth a close watch. NIO retains \~63% controlling stake in GeniTech, making the chip business inside the listco an increasingly visible call option in addition to the auto business as external customers and robotics revenue ramp. What's more, external capital funding the group's heavy R&D line should also facilitate NIO's 2026 profitability target, in our view.
