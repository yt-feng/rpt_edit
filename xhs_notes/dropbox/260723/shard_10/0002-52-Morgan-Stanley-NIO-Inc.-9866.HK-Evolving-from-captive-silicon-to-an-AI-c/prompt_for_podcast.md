# Evolving from captive silicon to an AI chip platform

NIO's chip arm GeniTech (Shenji) presented itself at WAIC 2026 as an all-domain AI silicon platform beyond cars, widening its market to embodied AI and inference while starting to monetize NIO's heavy R&D line.

GeniTech (Shenji) made its first standalone appearance at WAIC 2026 in Shanghai. A broadening scope: We think what matters for investors is GeniTech's migration from driving into training/inference workloads for humanoid robots, driverless logistics, and high-compute terminals, precisely the adjacencies that widen a captive auto supplier's addressable market and give a growth story its upside. The midrange NX9031U, built on the same 5nm automotive-grade node, delivers up to 800 TOPS of equivalent compute under air cooling and powers the new Ruidong embodied-intelligence development platform for robot perception and planning, intelligent computing, and advanced manufacturing. Alongside the NX9031C/NX6031 sensing chip, GeniTech also launched a distributed agent platform.

Boding well for both funding and cost: On funding, GeniTech has pulled in close to Rmb3bn since its June 2025 spin-out, with an external round this February valuing it near Rmb8.3bn post-money. On cost, in-house chip displaces imported compute from global top-notch players, with a single NX9031 rated at the compute of four Nvidia Orin processors, and every unit produced amortizes a fixed R&D base across a widening volume pool. Of note, GeniTech's external licensing of NX9031 technology to a third-party auto chip began late in 2025, adding a royalty layer. Scale plus self-supply would allow NIO's chip initiative to stop draining margin and defend it.

We think GeniTech's WAIC debut nudges NIO's stock narrative one step away from a cash-burnt EV maker and toward a vertically integrated AI-silicon platform, a re-rating vector worth a close watch. NIO retains \~63% controlling stake in GeniTech, making the chip business inside the listco an increasingly visible call option in addition to the auto business as external customers and robotics revenue ramp.
