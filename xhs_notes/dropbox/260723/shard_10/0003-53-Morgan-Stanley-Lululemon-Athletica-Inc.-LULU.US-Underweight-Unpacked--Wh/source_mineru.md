# Underweight Unpacked: Why 'Shrink to Grow' Is the Next Chapter for LULU

Three independent frameworks suggest LULU's Americas business is materially larger than its sustainable revenue base. This increases the likelihood that new management adopts a ‘shrink to grow’ strategy, which we believe would drive another leg of negative earnings revisions.

Our Underweight rating reflects our view that consensus underestimates the risk of a deeper & longer Americas reset, while simultaneously overestimating the China opportunity. Accordingly, we forecast a \~LSD % growth profile & \~\$10-11 in EPS power vs. the Street's \~MSD % & \~\$12 view, respectively – creating a negative earnings revision path (more here).

A common investor question following our resumption has been: How much smaller should Lululemon's Americas business ultimately be? While no single methodology provides a definitive answer, every framework we examined reaches the same conclusion – consensus is underwriting an Americas business that is too large. More specifically, across three independent analyses, our work supports an Americas business in the \~\$3-6.5B range, well below the Street's \$7B+ expectation for 2026-2028e.

#1 Peer precedent analysis implies a \~\$3-5B Americas business. Benchmarking LULU against the historical growth trajectories of global sportswear & multi-brand specialty peers supports an outcome of roughly \$5B, while single-brand fashion peers point to \~\$3B. Collectively, peer precedent analysis implies an Americas business \~25-60% smaller than consensus' 2026e estimate. See Exhibit 1.

#2 A “normalized-growth” framework supports a \$6B+ Americas business. If LULU had compounded Americas revenue closer to its pre-pandemic growth rate (\~low-teens %), rather than benefiting from the extraordinary \~mid-20% pandemic-era acceleration, today's business would likely be \~\$6.5B – still about 10% below the Street's 2026e estimate. See Exhibit 2.

#3 Our proprietary scale framework points to a \$3-7B Americas business. Last fall, we developed a proprietary framework examining the characteristics of scaled Softlines businesses across ten structural criteria (more here & here). Applying that work to LULU suggests its Americas business most closely resembles peers generating \$3-7B in annual revenue, again pointing to an Americas business materially smaller than consensus currently assumes. See Exhibit 3.
