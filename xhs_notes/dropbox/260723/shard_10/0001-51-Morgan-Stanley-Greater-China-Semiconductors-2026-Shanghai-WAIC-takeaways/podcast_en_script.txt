EN_A: I also noticed the report mentioned something called P/D disaggregation. What's that about?
EN_B: It's a new approach to inference. Large model inference has two phases: prefilling and decoding. Prefilling is compute-intensive, while decoding is bandwidth and latency-sensitive. Traditionally, both run on the same accelerator pool, which creates resource contention. P/D disaggregation separates them, allowing operators to optimize each phase independently.
EN_A: That sounds practical. Did the report have any specific performance numbers?
EN_B: Yes. One configuration allocated 90% of domestic accelerators to prefilling and 10% of H2O to decoding. The result was a 54% increase in throughput and a 64% reduction in time-to-first-token. But the report also cautions that actual benefits depend on model architecture and workload patterns.

EN_B: The report mentions Attention/FFN disaggregation, which would further split the decoding phase. But it says this is still early stage — no real demonstrations or benchmark results were shown at WAIC. It's a direction to watch, but it needs more validation.
