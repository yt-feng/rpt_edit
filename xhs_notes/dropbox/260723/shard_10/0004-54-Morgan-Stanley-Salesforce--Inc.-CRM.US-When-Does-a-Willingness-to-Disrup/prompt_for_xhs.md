## Downgrade to EW on Three Key Points:

1. AI KPIs Inflecting, But Seeing Minimal P&L Impact as Legacy Areas of Portfolio Drag on Overall Organic Growth. In isolation, data points behind Salesforce's AI portfolio continue to exude momentum, with the company accelerating to 200%+ growth in Agentforce ARR at a now \$1B+ scale (Exhibit 1). Moreover, the company is seeing ramping growth in both tokens (+152% QoQ as of 1Q27) and Agentic Work Units (the measure of intelligence / work completed against those tokens, +111% QoQ as of 1Q27), underpinning the momentum in adoption of its solutions. The company has also put forth the argument that power users of AWUs are expanding their spend on Salesforce at a materially faster clip than non AI adopters, beginning to provide support for the monetization scenario presented at Investor Day (Exhibit 2). That being said, conversion of the aforementioned momentum to leading indicators and / or the broader topline has not yet materialized, with Salesforce now posting two consecutive quarters of minimal cRPO upside, a lack of inflection in CC cRPO growth / cRPO-based bookings growth (Exhibit 3), and FY27 organic revenue growth continuing to decelerate towards the high single digits. We believe this is largely a function of varying growth rates within the overall portfolio – specifically, marketing & commerce and integration & analytics clouds have shown weak performance (Exhibit 4). In our view, there are structural weights on these parts of the business, particularly in Commerce, where Shopify has been a rapid market share gainer / disruptor, and Tableau, where lighter-weight business intelligence products likely retain outsized exposure to AI disruption. Given the dynamics of these growth drags, we are hesitant to underwrite a scenario of sustained growth improvement beyond 4Q, where the acquisition of Informatica is being anniversaried (and therefore becomes organic), likely bolstering the company's 2H27 acceleration expectations. Stated differently, while Agentforce is showing encouraging leading indicators, we do not yet have confidence that AI momentum can drive a durable acceleration in organic growth as persistent drags across legacy areas of the portfolio will require sustained strength in newer, better-performing businesses to offset – a mix shift that is likely to take time.

2. Relative Moat / Journey Strength, Though Transition Still Difficult. In our AI-readiness work, Salesforce scores most constructively in areas such as system-of-record depth, enterprise distribution, proprietary data/context, and domain expertise. Perhaps more importantly, however, we would argue that Salesforce has been relatively front-footed in its willingness to disrupt itself and transition its business model instead of defending the seat-based UI at all costs. Notably, this is a company that has repeatedly been willing to expand, and at times blur, the boundaries of its own franchise – pioneering the SaaS transition, expanding beyond Sales and into adjacent areas of the front office, and now shifting from human-driven UI to agents. This manifests as relatively strong positioning on the 'journey' portion of our score-card relative to other scaled SaaS peers (such as Workday and Adobe). As value shifts from screens and seats toward outcomes and action, Salesforce is trying to move the monetization layer lower in the stack toward the data, metadata, context, permissions and workflow layer agents needed to reason and act across the enterprise.

While we believe the convergence of Informatica, MuleSoft, Data 360, and Slack enable a compelling case for Salesforce assembling the control plane for enterprise agentic AI, we acknowledge strategic correctness is not the same as growth inflection, integral for investors to re-engage with shares here. Thus, the difficulty for Salesforce remains converting agentic activity into predictable subscription growth, and in our view, the pricing path is the clearest signal that this remains too early to underwrite. Salesforce has already changed its AI pricing structure a number of times – moving from conversation-based Agentforce pricing toward more flexible enterprise agreements, action-based constructs and credit-style models as customers push for predictability and value alignment. While our survey work continues to indicate that CIOs see Salesforce constructively as a key partner they plan to leverage in deploying enterprise AI (Exhibit 5), there remains much to prove by means of effective monetization translating to durable growth acceleration, which we think is well evidenced by the longer journey for Salesforce (and SaaS broadly) relative to other areas of Software (Infra, some Security) that are actively seeing topline inflection from AI.

## 3. See More Room to Get Efficient Versus Peers; Though Current Investment

Mode Likely Near-Term Overhang. In our efficiency work, Salesforce screens below the cleanest scaled application peers, generating \~\$404k of CY25 GAAP gross profit per employee versus \~\$839k at Intuit and \~\$684k at Adobe (Exhibit 8). There is similar room on the operating profit front, with Salesforce sitting at \~\$104k/head versus \~\$281k/head at Adobe and \~\$280k/head at Intuit (Exhibit 9). We see this supporting the idea that the model can still get more efficient – particularly in GTM productivity, support intensity, and better leverage against a \$40B+ revenue base, where Sales & Marketing spend as a % of revenue remains elevated vs peers (Exhibit 10). That said, management commentary continues to point incremental dollars toward growth in the near-term as they look to scale Agentforce, Data 360, and continuing to build out the monetization vehicle behind headless. As such, while there is a self-help angle here, Salesforce is currently choosing to spend through the AI transition rather than fully harvest the model today. Longer-term, however, the company's Rule-of-50 construct by FY30 effectively implies a path toward \~40% non-GAAP operating margins versus the FY27 guide of \~34.3%, suggesting an opportunity for margins to further inflect beyond this investment period.
