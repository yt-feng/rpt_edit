EN_B: The second driver is inflation boosting demand for working capital. Producer price inflation in Asia ex China hit 9 percent, a four-year high. When upstream prices rise, downstream companies need more loans to manage their cash flow. The report notes that even non-commodity PPI rose to 4.3 percent, meaning price pressures are spreading across the supply chain.
EN_A: So companies need loans not just for investment, but also for day-to-day operations. That makes sense. What about the third driver?
EN_B: The third driver is a recovery in consumer credit. The report points out that tech exports alone did not create many jobs because the tech sector is capital-intensive. But now non-tech capex and exports are also recovering, and that is improving the labor market. Retail sales are picking up, and household loan growth has moved from 5.6 percent last September to 6.6 percent in May.
EN_A: So credit demand is spreading from corporations to households. Which economies are leading the way?
EN_B: India, Japan, Singapore, Hong Kong, Australia, and Taiwan are seeing the strongest acceleration. India stands out, with corporate loan growth at 18.3 percent, the highest since 2012. But there is one exception — Korea.
EN_A: Korea? It is a big tech exporter. Why is its credit growth weak?
EN_B: That is one of the most interesting findings in the report. Korean companies have very strong cash flows, so they fund their capex internally. They do not need to borrow much. Also, there is a policy cap on household loan growth at 1.5 percent per year. So Korea’s credit growth is actually quite soft.
