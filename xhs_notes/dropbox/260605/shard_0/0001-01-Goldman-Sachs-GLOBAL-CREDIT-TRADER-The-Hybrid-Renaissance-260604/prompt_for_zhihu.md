# The drivers of hybrid issuance and investment

With the broader debt capital markets open to a wide range of borrowers, many market participants have questioned why an issuer might opt for a subordinated (higher coupon) hybrid instrument vs. a more traditional financing option in senior unsecured form.

Issuers in capital intensive industries—which often operate with large debt capital structures—tend to favor hybrid debt for two key reasons. First is the partial equity credit it receives from the rating agencies, which reflects its ‘equity like’ characteristics, including subordination in the capital structure and the ability to defer coupon payments without triggering a default. For a capital structure with limited ‘headroom’ within a targeted leverage or rating band, this partial equity credit can be attractive—especially since it does not involve diluting shareholders.

Second is the favorable tax deductibility of hybrid interest (relative to preferred stock, for example). For a profitable corporate taxpayer, the tax shield can help optimize the weighted average cost of capital—even if the hybrid debt has a moderately higher coupon.
