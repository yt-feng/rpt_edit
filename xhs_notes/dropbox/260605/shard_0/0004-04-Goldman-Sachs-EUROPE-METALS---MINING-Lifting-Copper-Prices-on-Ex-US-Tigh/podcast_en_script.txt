EN_A: Today we are digging into a fresh report from a global investment bank. It makes a bold call on copper prices. B, what is the main argument here?
EN_B: The core thesis is that the copper market outside the US is going to get much tighter over the next two years. Two forces are driving this: weaker mine supply and trade flows shifting because of tariffs.
EN_A: So they think copper prices are heading higher?
EN_B: Exactly. But they point to one key catalyst — the US tariff decision, which is expected this month. If tariffs are introduced in January, copper prices could be pushed above 14,000 dollars per tonne in the second half of next year.
EN_A: Wait, that sounds counterintuitive. Wouldn't tariffs hurt demand? How does that push prices up?
EN_B: Great question. The logic is that tariffs would trigger a wave of pre-emptive buying from US companies. They would rush to import copper before the tariff kicks in. That pulls metal out of the rest of the world and into US warehouses. And the LME copper price is set by the market outside the US. So the more the US hoards, the tighter the rest of the world gets, and the higher the LME price goes.
EN_A: So what happens if tariffs don't happen?
EN_B: The report lays out three scenarios. If there is a definitive no-tariff outcome, the whole hoarding logic collapses. The market outside the US could flip back into surplus, and copper prices could fall back to around 12,800 dollars per tonne. If the news just stays unclear, prices might hold around current levels.
EN_A: From an investment perspective, which companies benefit the most?
EN_B: The report highlights two names. First is Lundin Mining. Copper now makes up 85 percent of its revenue, making it one of the purest copper plays in the sector. And it is trading at roughly one times price to net asset value, while peers are at 1.5 to 2 times. So it looks like a value play.
EN_A: So it is a bargain in the copper space?
EN_B: Exactly. And it has a growth catalyst too. Its Vicuna project in Argentina is expected to get approval and a final investment decision by late 2026. That could be the next leg of growth.
EN_A: What is the other stock they like?
EN_B: Antofagasta. This one is more of a scarcity premium story. It is one of the few scalable, pure copper gold equities in Europe. And it has strong execution. Its Centinela mine is expanding with a second concentrator. The report thinks the market is not fully pricing in the upside from that project.
EN_A: So two different angles — one is value, the other is scarcity. What are the risks?
EN_B: The biggest risk is the tariff uncertainty. If tariffs do not happen, the whole bull case weakens. Also, copper prices are sensitive to the global economy. If demand slows more than expected, prices could fall.
EN_A: What about the parts of the report that are still open questions?
EN_B: There are two interesting ones. The report mentions that mine supply recovery will not happen until 2028. But it does not fully answer whether scrap supply can fill the gap. And there is the question of how trade flows will rebalance in 2027 after the US stockpiling wave. Those are the mysteries the full report explores in more depth.
