ZH_A: 最近一期欧洲奢侈品研报里，研究员突然开始讨论中国房价，这背后有什么特别的逻辑？

ZH_B: 确实，这看起来有点跨界，但逻辑很直接。中国消费者贡献了欧洲奢侈品行业超过30%的销售额，而中国家庭财富中超过70%都在房地产里。所以房价的走势，直接关系到这些消费者的购买力。

ZH_A: 那么这份报告真正要回答的核心问题是什么？是房价会一直跌下去吗？

ZH_B: 它要回答的是，中国房地产市场的持续疲软，对欧洲奢侈品股意味着什么。报告的核心判断是，房价下行趋势仍在延续，但分化明显。比如，5月二手房挂牌价环比下跌0.5%，比4月的0.4%还要快一点。但一线城市相对抗跌，跌幅只有0.1%。

ZH_A: 0.5%的月跌幅听起来不算特别大，为什么研究员会这么关注？

ZH_B: 关键在于累积效应。从2021年6月的高点算起，二手房价格已经累计下跌了38%。而且，下跌的范围在扩大。5月份有93%的样本城市出现了环比下跌，而4月份是91%。更值得注意的是，64%的城市下跌速度在加快，而4月份这个数字只有19%。

ZH_A: 这意味着什么？是不是说下跌正在从个别城市向全国蔓延？

ZH_B: 没错，这正是报告的核心洞察之一。它暗示市场的底部可能还没有到来。研究员认为，买家的谨慎情绪是主要原因，大家不愿意加杠杆，对收入前景也缺乏信心。政策放松虽然在一线城市产生了一些效果，但这种由积压需求带来的提振可能正在消退。

ZH_A: 那这是否意味着奢侈品股的投资者应该完全避开这个板块？

ZH_B: 报告并没有给出这么直接的结论。它提出了一个很有意思的“对冲”视角。报告指出，过去12到18个月，中国房地产市场的价值缩水了大约31万亿人民币，但同时，A股市场却上涨了约39万亿人民币。所以，股市的财富效应在一定程度上抵消了房市的负面影响。

ZH_A: 所以，投资者需要同时关注房市和股市的互动？那报告有没有给出一个更明确的未来预测？

ZH_B: 它给出了一个谨慎的展望，但留下了悬念。报告预计，房价的疲软趋势可能会持续到2026到2027年。不过，它也提到，由于库存消化情况不同，少数一线城市可能会表现更好。但有一个关键问题，报告并没有完全展开。

ZH_A: 什么问题？

ZH_B: 就是如果房价继续下跌，而股市的上涨动能又减弱了，那么奢侈品消费会面临多大的下行风险？这个交叉点的分析，报告里只点到了，但没有深入。这也是为什么我们需要去读完整报告的原因。

EN_A: So, I just read a research note on European luxury goods, and it starts by talking about Chinese housing prices. That feels like quite a leap. What’s the connection?

EN_B: It’s a very direct one. Chinese consumers make up over 30% of total spending on European luxury goods. And more than 70% of Chinese household wealth is tied up in real estate. So when house prices move, it directly impacts how much people can spend on handbags and watches.

EN_A: That makes sense. So what’s the big question this report is really trying to answer?

EN_B: It’s basically asking: how much longer will the weakness in China’s housing market hurt the luxury sector? The report’s core finding is that the downward trend is continuing, but it’s becoming more uneven. For example, secondary home prices fell 0.5% month-on-month in May, slightly faster than April’s 0.4% drop. But top-tier cities like Beijing and Shanghai held up much better, with only a 0.1% decline.

EN_A: A 0.5% monthly drop doesn’t sound catastrophic. Why are the analysts so focused on it?

EN_B: Because of the cumulative effect. Since the peak in June 2021, secondary home prices are down 38% in total. And the weakness is spreading. In May, 93% of cities tracked saw month-on-month declines, up from 91% in April. More importantly, 64% of cities saw the pace of decline accelerate, compared to just 19% in April. That’s a big shift.

EN_A: So what does that mean? Is the downturn becoming a national problem rather than just a local one?

EN_B: Exactly. That’s one of the key insights. It suggests the bottom might not be in yet. The analysts point to cautious buyer behavior – people don’t want to take on more debt, and they’re uncertain about their income. Policy easing has helped a bit in top-tier cities, but that boost from pent-up demand is starting to fade.

EN_A: So should luxury stock investors just run away from the sector?

EN_B: The report doesn’t say that. It offers a really interesting counterpoint. It notes that over the past 12 to 18 months, while the housing market lost about 31 trillion yuan in value, the Chinese stock market gained about 39 trillion yuan. So the equity market’s wealth effect has been partially offsetting the housing drag.

EN_A: So we need to watch both markets together. Does the report give a clear prediction for the future?

EN_B: It gives a cautious outlook, but it leaves a big question mark. It expects the soft price trend to persist through 2026 and 2027. A few top-tier cities might outperform if they can clear inventory faster. But here’s the thing the report doesn’t fully answer.

EN_A: What’s that?

EN_B: What happens if housing keeps falling and the stock market rally runs out of steam? How big would the downside risk be for luxury spending then? The report touches on that intersection but doesn’t model it. That’s exactly why you’d want to read the full note.
