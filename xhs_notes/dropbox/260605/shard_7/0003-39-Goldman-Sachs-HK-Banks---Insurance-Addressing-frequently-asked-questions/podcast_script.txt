ZH_A: 最近香港银行和保险板块跌了不少，有些个股一天跌了7%，但恒指基本没动。这背后到底发生了什么？

ZH_B: 确实，市场反应挺大的。核心是两个监管信号叠加了。一个是5月22号证监会关于线上券商的监管和罚款，另一个是6月1号国务院发布的境外投资新规，第837号令。

ZH_A: 所以投资者真正担心的是什么？是开户被叫停了？

ZH_B: 对，市场最关注的就是这个。有报道说一些银行暂停了在内地分行为内地居民开香港投资账户。但我们的渠道调研显示，并没有全面叫停。开户活动在香港本地仍然在继续，只是流程要求变严了。

ZH_A: 变严在哪里？具体多了什么要求？

ZH_B: 现在客户开投资账户，需要额外提供声明，证明资金是合法来源，而且资金来自中国大陆以外。这本质上是在加强反洗钱和跨境资本风险的合规审查。

ZH_A: 这意味着之前那种“内地居民到香港开户买理财”的模式会受到明显影响？

ZH_B: 短期流程会变慢，这是肯定的。但长期看，这不等于业务逻辑被颠覆。银行也在做一件事，就是清理休眠账户，尤其是零余额账户。这不是新规直接要求的，但行业在同步推进。

ZH_A: 那这些银行到底有多依赖这块业务？如果开户收紧，对他们的收入影响有多大？

ZH_B: 影响不小。你看数据，财富管理手续费收入占银行总手续费或非利息收入的比重，在过去两年里上升了大概5个百分点。像渣打和HSBC，财富收入已经占到它们总营业收入的29%左右。

ZH_A: 接近三成？那确实很关键。

ZH_B: 对。而且增速很快。过去几年，这些银行的手续费收入年增速在百分之十几到三十几之间。一季度数据也很强，渣打的财富手续费同比增长了34%。

ZH_A: 那这些收入里，有多少是直接来自香港和中国内地客户？

ZH_B: 比例很高。以渣打为例，2025年财富解决方案收入里，大约40%来自香港。HSBC香港的财富手续费虽然只占集团财富手续费的20%左右，但绝对值很大。而且两家银行都提到，新客户里很大比例是非香港居民。

ZH_A: 非香港居民，大部分就是内地客户？

ZH_B: 对。渣打2025年香港的新增净资金流入里，超过50%来自全球华人客户，其中内地客户是主力。HSBC在香港的零售银行和财富管理业务，2025年新增了110万客户，其中非居民客户占了90多万。

ZH_A: 所以监管趋严，直接冲击的就是这个增量部分？

ZH_B: 没错。过去几年财富管理的高增长，很大程度上就是靠内地客户跨境配置驱动的。现在合规要求提高，增量速度可能会放缓。

ZH_A: 但报告里好像没有完全下结论说增长就此终结？

ZH_B: 是的。报告指出几个关键点还没有完全展开。第一，新规的实施细则要到2026年7月才生效，细则内容现在还没出来。第二，银行的实际执行尺度会怎样，还需要观察。第三，内地居民对境外资产配置的需求是否会被抑制，还是只是换一种合规的方式实现。

ZH_A: 所以完整的报告里会继续讨论这些悬念？

ZH_B: 对。比如细则出台后，哪些开户路径会被堵死，哪些反而可能被规范后放行。还有，如果增量放缓，银行靠存量客户能不能维持财富收入的增长。这些都是完整报告会深入分析的方向。

EN_A: So Hong Kong bank and insurance stocks took a hit recently, some down as much as 7% in a single day while the broader market barely moved. What triggered this?

EN_B: It really came down to two regulatory signals hitting at once. One was the CSRC announcement on May 22 about online brokers and fines. The other was the State Council decree number 837 on outbound investment, issued on June 1.

EN_A: So the big question investors are asking is whether this means account openings are being shut down?

EN_B: Exactly. There were news reports that some banks had paused opening Hong Kong investment accounts for mainland residents at their mainland branches. But our channel checks suggest there is no broad-based halt. Account opening activity in Hong Kong itself is still ongoing. What has changed is that the compliance requirements have become stricter.

EN_A: Stricter how? What new hoops do clients have to jump through?

EN_B: Now when you open an investment account, you need to provide a declaration that the funds are legally sourced and that they originate from outside mainland China. This is really about tightening KYC and mitigating cross-border capital risks.

EN_A: So this directly impacts the whole model of mainland residents coming to Hong Kong to open accounts and invest?

EN_B: In the short term, it will slow things down. But it does not necessarily break the long-term logic. Banks are also doing something else at the same time, cleaning up dormant accounts, especially zero-balance ones. That is not directly required by the new rules, but the industry is moving in that direction in a coordinated way.

EN_A: How dependent are these banks on this business anyway? If account openings slow down, how much does it hurt their revenue?

EN_B: It is significant. Wealth management fees as a share of total fees or non-interest income have risen by about 5 percentage points in just two years. For Standard Chartered and HSBC, wealth income now accounts for around 29% of their total operating income.

EN_A: Almost a third. That is a big number.

EN_B: It is. And the growth has been very strong. Fee income growth has been in the high teens to low thirties percent range over the past few years. First quarter numbers were still very robust, Standard Chartered saw wealth fees up 34% year on year.

EN_A: And how much of that is directly tied to Hong Kong and mainland Chinese clients?

EN_B: A very large portion. For Standard Chartered, about 40% of their wealth solutions income in 2025 came from Hong Kong. HSBC Hong Kong wealth fees are about 20% of the group total, but the absolute number is huge. Both banks also say a big share of their new clients are non-residents.

EN_B: Exactly. For Standard Chartered Hong Kong, more than 50% of their net new money in 2025 came from global Chinese clients. HSBC Hong Kong added 1.1 million new retail banking and wealth clients in 2025, and over 900,000 of them were non-residents.

EN_A: So tighter regulation directly hits that growth engine?

EN_B: That is the key concern. The high growth in wealth management in recent years has been largely driven by mainland clients allocating assets cross-border. If compliance gets tougher, the pace of new inflows could slow.
