# China Economic Comment

# Comment on the cross-border tightening: its not just about money

The Chinese government has introduced a new regulation (Decree No. 837) governing cross-border investments, including those directed to Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan, effective from 1 July 2026. The regulation is expected to introduce additional procedures and enhanced scrutiny for outbound investments, although detailed implementation measures have yet to be released. This comes alongside the recent issuance of the Implementation Plan for the Comprehensive Rectification of Illegal Cross-border Securities, Futures and Fund Activities, which establishes a two-year timeframe to phase out illegal cross-border financial operations.

This move was framed as a tightening of capital controls, which misses the broader picture.

Previous rounds of tightening of cross border capital transfer were typically aimed at controlling capital outflows and stabilizing RMB exchange rate amid depreciation pressures.

Capital outflow pressures eased significantly in recent months.

The need to stabilizing the RMB exchange rate is to prevent overly rapid appreciation.

Instead of seeing this as one of the cyclical tightening of capital flows, the latest move should be viewed as the establishment of broader system to control the flow of technology, data, talent and capital.

The new system should support the development of key technology sectors, especially those highlighted in the article in the recent Qiushi (Pursuit of Truth) magazine: Quantum Technology, Biomanufacturing, Hydrogen Energy, Brain-Computer Interface, Embodied Intelligence, Sixth-Generation Mobile Communications.

These key sectors are important from both growth and security perspectives. Capital is a key to the successful development of tech sectors, but other factors of production are no less important.

Overall corporate regulatory environment has been tightened in recent months, amid geoeconomic tailwind and strong economic and market performance. But for the key tech sectors such as those mentioned above policy environment remains highly supportive.

As the economy started to cool down meaningfully from April, we may see some adjustments in terms of policy stance towards non-core fintech companies as well. But regulatory moves have their momentum, market continues to be strong and geoeconomic tailwind continues, so the adjustment can take some more time.

In principle, establishing a system which gives the government controls does not necessarily have to lead to lower level of capital outflow since controls over some outflows could be loosened.
