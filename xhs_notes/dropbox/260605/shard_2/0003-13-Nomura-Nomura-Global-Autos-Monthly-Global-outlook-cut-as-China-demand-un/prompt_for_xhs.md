# April sales were up 2% y-y, led by the strength in India

Global auto sales volume, based on the sales trends across the 18 countries we track, rose 2% y-y in April. Excluding China and Russia, sales in the rest of the world rose 5% y-y, led by India, where volumes increased 25% y-y, excluding commercial vehicles. This was partly offset by a 6% y-y decline in the US, where comparisons were tougher due to pull-forward demand in 2025, as consumers rushed to secure pre-tariff inventory at dealerships.

In China, wholesale auto sales declined for the fifth consecutive month. Weak domestic demand, following the expiry of tax exemptions and reduced trade-in subsidies, continued to outweigh strong export growth.

# We have revised our global forecasts compared to six months ago

We now expect global auto sales to decline 1% y-y in 2026 and grow 2% in 2027 (Fig. 3), compared with our forecast six months ago, when we expected a 2% y-y growth in both the years.

The largest change is in China. We have revised down our estimates for new vehicle sales in China by 2.23mn units in 2026 and by 2.04mn units in 2027. These figures refer to cumulative domestic passenger vehicle registrations and commercial vehicle wholesales, as noted in Fig. 3.

This downward revision is partly offset by stronger expectations for India. We have revised up our annual sales outlook for India by 0.47mn units in 2026 and by 0.49mn units in 2027.

# China's push to grow auto exports could lead to renewed trade tensions

We have revised down our China sales forecasts because domestic demand has underwhelmed following the expiry of tax exemptions for new energy vehicles and stricter requirements for trade-in subsidies.

Given the patchiness of China auto data, no single measure gives a complete picture. However, the underlying trend in domestic passenger vehicle demand looks weak. Based on passenger vehicle registrations, Jan-Apr 2026 SAAR was at 17.33mn units, well below the Jan-Apr 2025 SAAR of 21.10mn units and the full-year 2025 SAAR of 23.59mn units.

There could still be a sequential recovery in 2H26, particularly if consumers respond to new models with updated technology and/or additional national consumption subsidies. However, weak domestic demand makes it difficult to rebalance China auto sales toward the local consumer.

We agree that, in the absence of a stronger domestic recovery, OEMs are likely to focus more heavily on exports. This applies to both domestic Chinese OEMs and transplants.
