EN_B: After the CSRC crackdown on illegal cross-border trading, the HKMA asked banks to get declarations from mainland investors when they open trading accounts. For local Hong Kong banks like BOCHK and BEA, cross-border clients have been a key growth driver over the last two to three years. EN_B: Exactly. The report says new customer growth will moderate in the near term. But the existing KYC and anti-money laundering rules are already quite strict, so the impact on the existing book is limited. EN_A: Now the second part, the new outbound investment rules. That sounds bigger.
EN_B: It is. The new regulation takes effect in July 2026 and aims to tighten scrutiny on outbound investments. The report points out that in 2025, China's trade surplus reached 1.2 trillion US dollars, but foreign exchange reserves only increased by 155.5 billion. That suggests a lot of money is staying offshore, and Hong Kong is likely one of the key destinations.
EN_A: So if capital flows are restricted, which business lines get hit?
EN_B: The report identifies several areas: excess deposits, cash management and custody services, insurance product distribution, and securities and brokerage trading. These are all tied to mainland Chinese entities and individuals. The report estimates that mainland China-related business could contribute a double-digit percentage of revenue for local Hong Kong banks.
