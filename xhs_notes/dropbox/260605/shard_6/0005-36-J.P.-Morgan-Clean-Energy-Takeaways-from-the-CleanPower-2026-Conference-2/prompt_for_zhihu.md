We attended the CleanPower 2026 Conference in Houston, Texas this week and spoke with several renewable developers, EPCs, and equipment manufacturers. Key themes from the conference included: 1) generational levels of power demand driven by AI, industrialization and electrification, 2) continued momentum in battery energy storage, 3) the increasing role of robotics in EPC and O&M, and 4) continued industry consolidation towards the best in class companies. We came away from the conference positive on the outlook for utility scale solar & storage growth in 2H 2026 and beyond. The outlook for wind remains relatively murky given what some view to be market saturation of the technology as well as federal permitting and political challenges. Against this backdrop, we believe top picks NXT and MTZ remain well positioned, and view Overweight-rated PWR and MWH as additional beneficiaries of the same demand and consolidation dynamics.

\- Demand backdrop remains profoundly robust. Practically every conversation at the conference began with talk about the unbelievable amount of power demand in the market, with most expressing conviction that solar and wind will be just as impactful as fossil fuels in meeting new generation needs. However, the economic and practical feasibility of pairing solar + storage with dispatchable resources to directly meet behind-the-meter needs of a hyperscaler data center remains a point of debate amongst the industry, with many pointing to the need for continued advancements in energy storage technology before renewables can fully meet the reliability requirements of data centers. Nevertheless, multiple industry participants pointed to insatiable demand for renewable generation from hyperscalers, even if the mega projects in question are not directly powering data centers. Developers we spoke with noted that interconnection queues — while still congested — are increasingly populated by high-quality, creditworthy offtakers with long-dated power purchase agreements, a meaningful shift from the speculative queue composition of prior project development cycles. Texas, California, and Arizona remain the hottest markets for solar & storage projects of scale.

\- Momentum in battery energy storage is real. Storage is increasingly being co-developed and co-located with solar as a default configuration rather than an optional add-on. \- Robotics is becoming increasingly important for EPC & O&M. Skilled labor shortages for both EPC and O&M are pushing developers and contractors to adopt robotic solutions to fill the gap. This dynamic is being further reinforced by hyperscalers, who are increasingly directing their procurement toward large, creditworthy counterparties capable of delivering gigawatt-scale pipelines with execution certainty. Project sizes are also trending materially larger across the board, which compounds this advantage.
