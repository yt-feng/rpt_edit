# PD-1-Directed IL-2 Has a Validation Problem: The Field is Converging on a Single Benchmark, and the Window is Shrinking

The third day of ASCO 2026 delivered a clarifying signal for the PD-1/IL-2 class, and the message is not comfortable for most developers in the space. Two assets, ANV600 and AWT020, added incremental validation for the concept of PD-1-directed IL-2 therapy, but the data also exposed a persistent structural limitation: dose window constraints remain unresolved for beta-gamma design IL-2s. Meanwhile, a single molecule, IBI363, continues to stand apart as the druggable benchmark. The field is not expanding; it is consolidating around one reference point.

For investors and strategists evaluating China biotech's immuno-oncology pipeline, this is a moment to separate signal from noise. The ASCO 2026 poster sessions and plenary data from Day 3 reveal three interconnected dynamics that will shape the next 12 to 18 months. First, the PD-1/IL-2 modality is showing signs of a ceiling effect on tolerability that may limit its therapeutic index. Second, the PD-(L)1/VEGF class, long considered mature, is generating unexpected second-life signals in first-line squamous non-small cell lung cancer, with HB0025 posting response rates that invite comparison to earlier AK112 data. Third, the KRAS G12C landscape is shifting from a pure efficacy race to a tolerability and trial design contest, where exposure management and rash-stomatitis control may matter as much as allele-specific depth.

The most important analytical question emerging from Day 3 is not which molecule wins. It is whether the current generation of bispecific and bifunctional designs has already reached a performance plateau that a single outlier, IBI363, has transcended. If so, the strategic implications for pipeline prioritization, deal-making, and clinical development are profound.

## The PD-1/IL-2 Class is Validated but Not Viable at Scale: Dose Window Constraints Persist and IBI363 Remains the Lone Outlier

ANV600 and AWT020 both demonstrated that PD-1-directed IL-2 can produce clinical activity. That is no longer the question. The question is whether these molecules can achieve a therapeutic index wide enough to justify registration programs. The data from Day 3 suggest the answer is not yet clear.

ANV600 dosing remained confined to microgram-per-kilogram levels, a range that historically signals a narrow window between efficacy and toxicity. Activity was mixed across regimens and indications, and the IL-2-related adverse events, including cytokine release syndrome, pyrexia, and transaminitis, were elevated. These are not new findings, but their persistence across multiple assets in the class reinforces the structural challenge. The biology of IL-2, even when tethered to PD-1, still activates immune cascades that are difficult to control without sacrificing potency.

AWT020, which is still in dose escalation at 0.3 to 1 mg per kilogram levels with priming explorations, showed a solid-tumor basket overall response rate of 30 percent and a disease control rate of 67 percent across 27 patients. Those numbers are respectable in a heavily pretreated population. However, Grade 3 or higher inflammatory treatment-related adverse events, including arthralgia, colitis, and hematotoxicity, emerged. The risk-benefit calculation for a broad solid-tumor indication remains uncertain.

Against this backdrop, IBI363's profile in second-line immunotherapy-resistant EGFR wild-type non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer at the 3 mg dose stands out. The median overall survival signal is supporting a registrational path. The contrast is stark: while other molecules in the class are still proving they can be tolerated, IBI363 is generating survival data that justify a pivotal trial. The question the report raises but does not fully answer is whether IBI363's advantage is structural or execution-dependent. Is its design genuinely superior, or has its clinical program simply been better managed? The answer will determine whether investors should bet on the platform or the molecule.

## PD-(L)1/VEGF is Not a Mature Class: HB0025's High Activity in First-Line Squamous NSCLC Creates a New Comparison Point

HB0025's data in first-line non-small cell lung cancer were among the most striking poster presentations on Day 3. At 20 mg per kilogram every three weeks combined with chemotherapy, the asset showed an overall response rate of 84.5 percent in squamous and 65.6 percent in non-squamous patients. Median progression-free survival reached 16.46 months in squamous and 14.65 months in non-squamous. Even more notable, the squamous PD-L1 less than 1 percent subgroup showed a median progression-free survival of 16.72 months, while the PD-L1 greater than or equal to 1 percent subgroup showed 16.46 months. The PD-L1 expression level did not meaningfully differentiate outcomes.

These numbers invite direct comparison to AK112's Phase 2 data, and the comparison is not straightforward. The caveats are significant: the HB0025 dataset has a skew in PD-L1 distribution, stage mix, and lower metastatic burden compared to the AK112 reference. Grade 3 or higher proteinuria and treatment-related adverse event deaths were also observed. The data are early, the sample size is limited to approximately 60 patients per histology, and the comparator is a historical Phase 2, not a randomized trial.

Nevertheless, the pattern is worth watching. If HB0025's activity in PD-L1-low squamous NSCLC holds up in larger, randomized data, it would challenge the assumption that the PD-(L)1/VEGF bispecific space is fully occupied by AK112. The field may not be a two-player game. The strategic implication for developers is that the first-line squamous NSCLC indication, long considered a high unmet need with limited options beyond chemotherapy and PD-1 inhibitors, may become a competitive battleground for bispecifics. The open question is whether HB0025's tolerability profile, particularly the proteinuria signal, will constrain its use in a curative-intent setting.

## KRAS G12C is Now a Tolerability and Trial Design Game: The Second-Line Window is Narrowing and First-Line Strategy is Unresolved

The LBA05 presentation of RASolute302 provided the most detailed look yet at second-line KRAS G12C inhibitor tolerability. The discontinuation rate was 1.2 percent versus 11.2 percent for chemotherapy, and median dose intensity was 93.1 percent. Rash at 17 percent and stomatitis at 6.6 percent were the key drivers of dose reduction. These are not alarming numbers, but they are meaningful in context.

The discussant's commentary aligned with the Day 2 takeaway: the second-line focus is shifting from pure efficacy to exposure and adverse event management. When multiple molecules can achieve similar depth of response, the differentiator becomes how well patients can stay on therapy. Rash and stomatitis, while manageable, erode quality of life and may affect compliance in a real-world setting where patients are not being monitored in a clinical trial.

The more consequential debate, however, is about the first-line setting. The discussant framed the first-line hurdles as trial design challenges: testing combination regimens versus RAS monotherapy versus standard of care, and parsing through the differentiated clinical manifestation of each molecule. The report notes that the first-line focus echoes Day 2 on monotherapy versus combination versus SOC and maintenance design. This is not a settled question.

For investors, the KRAS G12C story is no longer about which molecule has the best preclinical profile. It is about which company can design a first-line trial that generates a clear regulatory path while managing the competitive dynamics of an increasingly crowded space. The second-line window is closing as molecules converge on similar efficacy, and the first-line window is opening but requires strategic clarity that most companies have not yet demonstrated.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Durability of IBI363's Survival Signal and the Reproducibility of HB0025's Squamous Data

Every conference produces data that raise as many questions as they answer, and ASCO 2026 Day 3 is no exception. Two analytical gaps stand out.

First, the report does not provide sufficient detail on the durability of IBI363's median overall survival signal. A single-arm, second-line immunotherapy-resistant population is a high bar, but survival data in such a setting can be confounded by patient selection, subsequent therapies, and small sample sizes. The report frames the data as supportive of a registrational path, but it does not disclose the confidence intervals, the number of events, or the maturity of the survival curve. Without these details, it is impossible to assess whether IBI363's advantage is robust or fragile. Investors should demand clarity on these points before assigning a premium to the asset.

Second, the HB0025 data in squamous NSCLC are provocative but incomplete. The caveats around PD-L1 skew, stage distribution, and metastatic burden are not minor adjustments; they could account for a significant portion of the observed efficacy signal. The report acknowledges these caveats but does not quantify their potential impact. A proper comparison to AK112 would require a multivariate analysis or a propensity score-matched dataset. Until that analysis is performed, the HB0025 data should be treated as hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory.

These gaps are not failures of the report. They are inherent limitations of early-stage and single-arm data. But they are precisely the kind of unresolved questions that make a full report with original charts valuable. The raw data, the confidence intervals, and the subgroup analyses are where the real signal lives.

## A Decision Framework for Evaluating PD-1/IL-2 and PD-(L)1/VEGF Assets After ASCO 2026

For readers who need to translate the Day 3 takeaways into investment or development decisions, a structured framework is more useful than a list of data points. The following three-question framework applies to any asset in the PD-1/IL-2 or PD-(L)1/VEGF classes.

First, what is the dose window? For PD-1/IL-2 assets, the dose window is the single most important variable. An asset that cannot dose above the microgram-per-kilogram range without triggering Grade 3 inflammatory toxicities is unlikely to achieve the exposure needed for durable responses in a broad patient population. The threshold to watch is whether the asset can achieve milligram-per-kilogram dosing with a manageable adverse event profile. IBI363 has crossed this threshold. ANV600 and AWT020 have not yet done so.

Second, what is the PD-L1 stratification? For PD-(L)1/VEGF assets, the value of the molecule depends on its activity in PD-L1-low or negative populations. If an asset only works in PD-L1 high patients, it is competing in a crowded space with mature checkpoint inhibitors. If it works across PD-L1 expression levels, it has a potential advantage. HB0025's squamous data suggest PD-L1-independent activity, but the caveats around patient selection mean this conclusion is provisional.

Third, what is the trial design for first-line KRAS G12C? For companies developing KRAS G12C inhibitors, the strategic question is not whether the molecule works. It is whether the company has a credible plan for a first-line trial that can differentiate from standard of care and from competing molecules. The trial design must account for combination partners, maintenance strategies, and the management of rash and stomatitis. Companies that cannot articulate a clear first-line strategy are likely to be relegated to a shrinking second-line market.

This framework is not exhaustive, but it is actionable. It forces the user to prioritize the variables that most strongly correlate with clinical and commercial success, rather than getting lost in the noise of response rates and progression-free survival curves that may not replicate in larger trials.
