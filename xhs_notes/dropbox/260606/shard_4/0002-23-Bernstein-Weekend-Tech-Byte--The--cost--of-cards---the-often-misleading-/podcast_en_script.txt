EN_B: Europe capped interchange at 20 basis points for debit and 30 for credit back in 2015. And guess what? Card penetration actually soared afterward — from 33% in 2015 to 77% by 2025.
EN_A: So regulation isn’t always bad for the industry?
EN_B: That’s a key insight. The report argues that networks can be flexible with interchange to grow the ecosystem, and sometimes caps actually help adoption.
EN_A: So what’s happening in the U.S. right now?
EN_B: The U.S. is at a delicate moment. Credit card fees are higher than in most countries, and small merchants often pay over 3%. So surcharging is becoming more common.

EN_B: Right now, only about 34% of small businesses are surcharging. But the report flags a concern: if the MDL settlement gets approved, merchants will have more flexibility to add surcharges. That could change the user experience for consumers.

EN_B: Interestingly, the report says the networks are not over-earning. Visa’s net yield in the U.S. is only about 0.23% of transaction value, while the total merchant fee is 1.6%.
EN_A: So they’re only taking a small slice?
EN_B: Right. And they provide enormous value — over 100 million merchant acceptance points and more than 7 billion KYCed credentials. That’s hard to replicate.
