# The Optical Market Is Entering a Sustained Double-Digit Growth Cycle, But the Real Story Is the Structural Shift in Who Buys and Why

The optical networking market has spent the past two years in a holding pattern. Telco digestion cycles and cloud spending lumpiness created a choppy growth environment that frustrated investors and confused the narrative. That period is ending. Beginning in 2025 and accelerating through 2027, the market is entering a phase of sustained double-digit growth that is fundamentally different from previous cycles. This is not merely a cyclical recovery. It is a structural re-rating driven by AI-related cloud build-outs that are broadening in scope, deepening in intensity, and shifting the center of gravity away from traditional telecom operators toward hyperscalers.

The data from a recent global investment bank report makes this clear. After a 12% decline in 2024, the optical market is expected to grow 9% in 2025, 10% in 2026, and 12% in 2027. But the aggregate numbers mask a more important transformation. The cloud vertical, which represented roughly 18% of the market in 2020, is projected to approach 32% by 2027. Meanwhile, telco, which commanded over 70% of spending just a few years ago, is slipping below 55%. This is not a temporary shift. It is a permanent reallocation of capital that will reshape competitive dynamics, product roadmaps, and valuation frameworks.

The implications for investors are significant. Optical companies with exposure to hyperscaler demand are trading at implied AI multiples that are among the highest in the broader AI-basket coverage. One vendor, for instance, shows an implied AI price-to-earnings multiple of 115 times on CY27 estimates, compared to a core P/E of just 18 times. The market is already pricing in a future that looks very different from the past. The question is whether that future is being underestimated or overestimated.

## AI-Driven Cloud Demand Is Broadening Beyond the Initial Build-Out Phase, Creating a Multi-Year Growth Trajectory That Differs From Prior Cycles

The optical market has seen AI-related spending before. The CY19 and CY22 cycles were notable standouts as hyperscalers built out initial network capacity and telco operators responded to early demand. But those were episodic events, driven by discrete deployment phases. The current cycle is different. The report projects double-digit growth in the cloud vertical for CY26 and CY27, driven by AI-related build-outs that are not only scaling but also diversifying across multiple application layers.

Three specific use cases are driving this expansion. First, scale-across applications, which involve connecting multiple data centers within a region, are creating demand for long-haul optical systems that can handle higher capacity over longer distances. Second, direct-connect optical modules, or DCOM, are enabling more efficient intra-data-center connectivity. Third, the emergence of hyper-rail architectures, which link clusters of AI accelerators, is pushing optical deeper into the network than ever before. These are not one-time projects. They represent ongoing infrastructure investments that will require continuous upgrades as AI workloads grow in complexity and data intensity.

The report's estimates reflect this. CY26 cloud optical revenue is projected to grow to nearly $5 billion, up from roughly $2.8 billion in 2024. By CY27, that figure approaches $6.1 billion. The compound annual growth rate over this period is significant. And critically, the report notes that it does not yet include meaningful coherent optical inside the data center for CY26, which it identifies as a potential second-half 2026 or CY27 opportunity. If that timeline accelerates, the estimates could prove conservative.

## Metro Optical Will Be the Primary Growth Engine, Driven Entirely by the Pluggable Revolution, While Traditional Metro Equipment Contracts

The most striking structural shift within the optical market is happening in the metro segment. The report projects metro optical growth will average double digits in CY26 and CY27, but this growth is not evenly distributed. It is concentrated entirely in the pluggable ZR and ZR+ segment, while the traditional metro equipment market is expected to decline by a high-single-digit percentage over the same period.

The numbers are stark. In 2024, pluggable ZR and ZR+ represented 14% of the metro optical market, or roughly $750 million. By 2027, the report projects that share will rise to 51%, or over $4.1 billion. That is a compound annual growth rate of over 70% for the pluggable segment. Meanwhile, the traditional metro equipment market is projected to shrink from $5.4 billion in 2024 to $4 billion in 2027.

This is not a marginal trend. It is a redefinition of the metro optical architecture. Pluggable optics are displacing traditional line systems because they offer lower cost, greater flexibility, and faster deployment cycles. For hyperscalers, who prioritize speed and scalability, pluggables are the natural choice. For traditional telco operators, the calculus is more complex, but the direction of travel is clear.

The report notes that pluggables will remain a small piece of the overall optical market on average through CY26-27, representing high teens as a percentage of total market. But within metro, they are becoming dominant. And because metro is the largest optical segment by revenue, the implications for vendors are profound. Companies that can capture share in the pluggable market will benefit disproportionately, while those tied to legacy metro systems face structural headwinds.

## Telco Remains the Majority of the Market, But Its Composition Is Changing in Ways That Benefit Vendors With Multi-Customer Exposure

Despite the cloud narrative, telco still accounts for over 50% of optical spending through CY27. But the telco market of 2027 will look different from the telco market of 2020. Two factors are driving this transformation.

First, the Multi-Operator Fiber Network, or MOFN, opportunity is gaining traction. MOFNs involve multiple operators sharing a common fiber infrastructure, which reduces duplication and improves utilization. The report estimates that MOFN-related demand represents roughly 10-15% of one vendor's telco business. This is a new source of demand that did not exist in prior cycles. It is driven by the need to extend network reach for AI workloads and by the growing recognition that fiber sharing can accelerate deployment while reducing costs.

Second, BEAD funding, the US government's broadband equity program, is beginning to flow through the market. While the impact has been slower than initially expected, the report indicates that BEAD-related spending is now moving through the pipeline. This creates a tailwind for metro and long-haul optical systems, particularly in underserved regions.

The report also highlights a critical nuance about backlog dynamics. In the post-pandemic supply chain crisis, many companies built large backlogs that masked underlying demand weakness. Products sat in warehouses. The current backlog environment is different. Management commentary from one vendor indicates that, given their direct relationships with hyperscalers, they can see that products are being deployed. This distinction matters. A backlog that reflects real deployment is fundamentally more valuable than one that reflects inventory accumulation.

## One Vendor Is the Clear Share Gainer, But the Competitive Landscape Contains Open Questions That the Report Does Not Fully Resolve

The report identifies one company as the only vendor under coverage that has been, and is expected to continue to be, a share gainer in the optical market. Its market share is projected to move from 16% in CY18 to approximately 32% in CY27. This is a remarkable trajectory. In the cloud vertical specifically, its share is projected to climb from 28% in 2021 to 78% in 2027.

The drivers are clear. The company has deep relationships with hyperscalers, a strong position in the pluggable ZR and ZR+ market, and exposure to telco recovery and MOFN activity. Its pluggable revenue is on track to more than double from 2025 levels. Its IADC revenue, which includes pluggables, DCOM, and scale-across, is expected to grow almost 300% year-over-year.

But the report leaves several important questions unanswered. First, the implied AI multiple for this vendor is 115 times CY27 earnings. What level of AI revenue growth is required to justify that multiple? The report provides the implied multiple but does not stress-test the underlying assumptions. Second, the pluggable market is becoming increasingly competitive. Multiple players are entering the 800G ZR+ segment. The report assumes this vendor maintains mid-teens market share in pluggables through CY27, but that assumption depends on continued technology leadership. How sustainable is that advantage? Third, the report does not fully address the risk that hyperscalers could begin to vertically integrate optical components, as they have done in other parts of the data center stack. If that happens, the share-gain story could face headwinds.

## A Decision Framework for Investors: Three Questions to Evaluate Optical Exposure

For investors trying to navigate this market, the report provides the building blocks for a structured decision framework. The key is to move beyond aggregate growth rates and focus on the specific drivers that will determine which companies benefit and which do not.

The first question is about customer concentration. Is the company's revenue mix shifting toward hyperscalers? The report shows that cloud optical spending is growing at a much faster rate than telco spending. Companies with high cloud exposure will benefit from the structural tailwind. Companies still heavily dependent on telco face a more uncertain path, even as telco recovers cyclically.

The second question is about product mix. Is the company positioned in the pluggable segment, or is it tied to traditional metro systems? The report's data shows that pluggables are cannibalizing traditional metro equipment. A company with strong pluggable revenue growth is likely to gain share. A company dependent on legacy metro systems is likely to lose share, even if the overall market grows.

The third question is about backlog quality. Does the company have direct visibility into deployment, or is the backlog a black box? The report distinguishes between backlog that reflects real demand and backlog that reflects inventory buildup. Companies with direct hyperscaler relationships can offer greater transparency. Companies without that visibility carry more risk.

These three questions customer mix, product mix, and backlog quality provide a framework for differentiating among optical vendors. The report's data suggests that only a small number of companies will score well on all three dimensions. Those that do may deserve the premium multiples they are receiving. Those that do not may face re-rating risk as the market matures.
