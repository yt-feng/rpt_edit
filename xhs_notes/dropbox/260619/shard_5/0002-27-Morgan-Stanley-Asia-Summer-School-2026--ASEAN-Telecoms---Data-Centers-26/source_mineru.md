# Indonesia: Competition Expected to Improve in Java, but Rise ex-Java

## Number of industry participants (consolidated to three)

• Consolidated from 10 players in 2008 to three today.  
- Tsel is Indonesia’s market leader with \~45% subs market share.  
- ISAT and H3I merged to form Indonesia's #2 mobile player in 2022.  
• XLSmart was formed following the EXCL+FREN merger in 2025.

## Industry structure:

\- Largely prepaid subs; mobile revenue driven by data revenue (legacy voice/SMS has fallen from $>60\%$ of revenue in 2015 to $<10\%$ today).

## Recent developments:

- Competition has improved since 2H25.  
- We expect network coverage/quality to improve ex-Java now with ISAT and EXCL bigger/stronger post their respective mergers.  
• ARPU converging towards Rp45k between all three operators.

## Thailand: Competition Expected to Stay Rational in a Duopoly Market

## Number of industry participants (3 key players to 2)

- TRUE is Thailand's market leader following the merger with DTAC in 2022. It has \~40% subs market share.  
• AIS' market share is \~40%.  
• SOEs TOT/CAT merged to form National Telecom.

## Industry structure:

- Largely prepaid subs (>70%), but migration toward postpaid and transitioning towards 5G.  
- Competition has been stable post merger.

## Broadband – ARPU on the rise

• We expect broadband penetration to rise \~1ppt in 2025-28e, to \~58%.  
- We observed broadband ARPU rising through 2024 as operators removed discounts, etc.

## Singapore: Competition to Sustain in Mobile, Intensifying in Broadband

## Number of industry participants (4 players but may consolidate)

- 4-player market – SingTel is the incumbent with \~45% subs market share.  
- TPG won rights to be the fourth telco in Singapore in 2016.  
- Competition in SIM-only plans remains highly intense.  
- Simba + M1 deal suspended; SingTel is keen to participate

## Industry structure:

• Largely postpaid subs market.  
• A number of MVNOs have entered since 2016.  
• Proliferation of SIM-only plans.

## Broadband – SIMBA pushing the competition

\- Competition in broadband moved a notch higher in Singapore since SIMBA launched its broadband plan in 2024. SIMBA is currently offering a 10Gbps speed plan at S\$30.

## Philippines: Third Player is Still Hanging Around

## Number of industry participants (up from 2 to 3)

\- Historically a two-player market, with a new entrant (Dito) launched in March 2021.

## Industry structure:

\- Largely prepaid subs; data revenues driving growth and replacing legacy voice/SMS revenues (this has fallen from \~80% of revenue in 2013 to \~6% in 2026e).

## Broadband – lower pricing arising from affordability issues

- Broadband penetration to rise \~2ppt p.a. over 2025-28e to 37%; this is still lower than the ASEAN average of \~50%.  
- Affordability is an issue, with broadband ARPU as a percentage of household income at one of the highest levels in ASEAN: \~2.5% vs. the ASEAN average of 1.6%.

## Philippines: Regulation has Increased

## New Entrants

- July 2019, Dito received a license to operate as the country's third telco operator.  
- Dito launched commercially in 2021 and had acquired 17mn subs as of July 2023 (\~10% subscriber market share).  
• Starlink was given the green light to operate in 2022.

## Common Tower Policy

• The government aims to improve service quality by encouraging the construction of tens of thousands of new towers.  
- The Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) said the Philippines needs another 50k cell towers on top of the \~18k that PLDT/Globe have built.  
• Tower-sharing regime will aid Dito's rollout.  
- PLDT and Globe have sold \~15k towers.

## Foreign Ownership Limit

• In March 2022, the government removed the 40% foreign ownership cap in industries such as telcos, airlines, domestic shipping, and railways.  
• Foreign nationals can now own up to 100% of capital in telcos.  
- A reciprocity clause prohibits foreign citizens from owning $>40\%$ of critical infrastructure, unless their country provides similar access to Filipino investors.

## Others

• The SIM Registration Act was enacted December 2022 – to curb cybercriminal activities.  
- Mobile Number Portability of 2019 allowed subs to change providers without changing their number.

## Knektadong Pinoy Act

• No congressional franchise needed  
- Open Infrastructure sharing  
• Accelerated deployment
