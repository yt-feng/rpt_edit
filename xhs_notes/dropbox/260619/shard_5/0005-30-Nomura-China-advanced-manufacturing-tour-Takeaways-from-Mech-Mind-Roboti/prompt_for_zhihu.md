# Takeaways from Mech-Mind Robotics visit

Robust 3D vision franchise supports diversified clientele and overseas reach, according to management

## Robust 3D vision growth underpinned by scale and global diversification

On 16 June, we visited Mech-Mind Robotics (unlisted), a machine-vision company supplying embodied intelligence robotics with AI "brains" and 3D vision. Management indicated that the company had held the top domestic market share in large-field 3D grasping, such as de-palletizing, for six consecutive years, citing a Tianjin parcel-handling deployment processing up to 1,800 items per hour. The company does not produce robot bodies but adapts to the full range of robotic arms; in management's framing, "doing more generates more data, which improves the models," forming a positive feedback loop that lifts generality and stability." Management commented that roughly half of the clients are based overseas, with a relatively dispersed client concentration. Based on our external industry survey, Mech-Mind holds a leading position in de-palletizing, although its technology lead over peers such as Hikrobot (unlisted) may be narrowing.

## Eye-brain-hand platform targets long-tail demand; hand remains nascent

Mech-Mind derives its core revenues from the "eye" (3D vision) and "brain" (algorithms/models), which management positions to drive a shift from heavily customized integration toward standardized, easy-to-use deployment, supported by training of 2,000–3,000 customer personnel annually. Management argues the eye-brain-hand (EBH) platform addresses the robotics industry's "broad, long-tail and diversified" demand, against competition from manual labour and dedicated automation equipment, and stresses scalability — most offerings require no large on-site teams and minimal customization. However, management acknowledged the dexterous hand is yet to generate revenue at scale. The industry survey is more cautious, noting Mech-Mind's strength lies in vision rather than motion control, and that building a mature in-house dexterous hand could take time and require equity incentives to attract talent. However, the "eye" first-mover advantage can support long-run adaptation across robot form-factors even as the "hand" lags.

## Mech-Mind vs Orbbec: Targetting different segments

Mech-Mind holds a first-mover advantage in the "eye" (3D vision), which the survey suggests can be adapted to humanoid-robot perception and imitation learning. By contrast, Orbbec (688322 CH, Buy) is positioned in the general-purpose track; the two are targeting different segments rather than competing head on. Orbbec captures the larger general-purpose market — spanning payment/"tap-to-pay," social-security facial verification, service robots and humanoids; Mech-Mind, according to the survey, is better suited to segmented, high-value niches requiring mobile, high-precision and wide-area positioning, where its structured-light and anti-interference strengths stand out. The industry survey further suggests that Mech-Mind may also differentiate against motion-capture and inertial players, where imitation-learning data capture is an adjacent need.
