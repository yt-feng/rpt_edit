EN_A: So no big stimulus bazooka?
EN_B: Correct. The report says the slowdown is real, but it’s not a collapse. April and May data show a deepening divide, but the report argues that headline numbers like retail sales and fixed asset investment may overstate the pace of the slowdown.

EN_B: For example, retail sales turned negative in May, but that’s largely because of a high base from last year’s trade-in subsidy program. If you look at the two-year compound annual growth rate, May actually looks slightly better than April.

EN_B: Exactly. The two-year average still shows a visible step-down from the first quarter. A softer labor market, ongoing property adjustment, and oil-related pressures are all weighing on spending. And interestingly, export strength hasn’t really spilled over into jobs, because of automation and rising capital intensity.
EN_A: That’s a key point. Strong exports, but not much benefit for the average consumer.
EN_B: Right. And that’s why the report says the economy will remain distinctly K-shaped, even with more demand support.

EN_B: The report expects faster implementation of already-approved budgets. About 60% of the annual government bond quota is still unused. EN_B: The report highlights the “Six Networks” framework: water management, new power grids, computing networks, next-gen communications, urban pipelines, and logistics. Within that, AI computing networks, data centers, and smart grids are likely to see the most immediate support.
EN_A: So it’s strategic, not broad-based. What about the external side?
EN_B: This is where it gets interesting. Weak domestic absorption means more manufacturing output is being pushed into export markets. That’s most visible in the EU, where China’s share in steel, EVs, batteries, and solar has risen sharply.

EN_B: The report expects targeted measures, not a systemic trade war. Things like sector-specific tariffs, foreign-subsidy investigations, and anti-dumping actions. But the direction matters more than the magnitude. If Europe progressively narrows access in high-value manufacturing, China’s export engine could be redirected toward lower-margin markets.
