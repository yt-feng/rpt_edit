# Natural Gas Comment: China's Destocking Through April Suggests Higher LNG Imports Ahead of The Winter

■ Upside risks to TTF prices remain. The China April gas data released this week, combined with high-frequency data on China LNG imports and prices, reinforces the upside risks we see to our 44/40 EUR/MWh (\$14.90/\$13.65/mmBtu) 3Q26/4Q26 TTF price forecasts (to 65/53 EUR/MWh) in a scenario where a Hormuz flow normalization is delayed to late-Jul (vs our late-Jun base case).

China gas demand remains weak... China apparent gas demand for April was marginally down yoy at 379 Bcm/y, missing our expectations by 8 Bcm/y, consistent with the broader miss in China activity data for the month (Exhibit 1). Industrial production growth fell to $4.1\%$ yoy in April from $5.7\%$ yoy in March despite stronger-than-expected exports, with the gas-intensive chemicals sector (worth 40 Bcm/y or 29 mtpa of gas demand in 2023 according to the IEA $^{1}$ ) one of the main drivers of China activity weakness.

...but not enough to prevent further destocking. Despite the weakness in gas demand, a combination of April LNG imports still well below last year's levels, and China domestic gas production and pipeline imports missing our expectations led to China April gas storage building by less than we expected, taking inventories down yoy for the first time in several years (Exhibit 2). This suggests higher LNG imports sequentially will likely be needed for China to manage inventories back up yoy ahead of the next winter. This is consistent with China's high-frequency LNG import data for May (Exhibit 3), now more visibly closing its gap to last year's levels, and with the Asia LNG price premium to European gas (JKM-TTF spread) remaining strong, incentivizing supply re-routes from the Atlantic to the Pacific (Exhibit 4).

Minimal LNG supply maintenance not enough to stop European imports from falling.
