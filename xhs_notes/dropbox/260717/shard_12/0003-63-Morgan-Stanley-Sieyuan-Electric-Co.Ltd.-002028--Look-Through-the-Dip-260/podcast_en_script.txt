EN_A: So there is a Chinese electrical equipment company called Sieyuan Electric that just reported its first half results. The profit growth was a bit disappointing. What is the real story here?
EN_B: Revenue grew 27 percent, but net profit only grew 15 percent. It did miss expectations. But the key is to understand why. The report identifies three main reasons that are mostly one-off or structural, not operational.
EN_A: So what were those three reasons?
EN_B: First, foreign exchange losses from a stronger RMB. Second, higher minority interest charges because Toshiba increased its stake in their joint venture from 10 to 30 percent. Third, higher R&D spending for new products. If you strip those out, the core business is actually doing quite well.
EN_A: How well exactly? What is the evidence?
EN_B: Order growth is very strong. Domestic orders are up more than 25 percent year on year. Overseas orders are up 50 to 100 percent. And the mix is shifting toward developed markets, which is good for margins.
EN_A: Which markets are driving that overseas growth?
EN_B: North America is a major breakthrough. There is a global shortage of power transformers, and Sieyuan is accelerating its order intake there. They are also expanding capacity, which should be ready by the fourth quarter of 2027 to take even more high-value orders.
EN_A: So what does that mean for the revenue outlook?
EN_B: It means overseas transformer orders could grow from about 4 billion RMB in 2025 to over 6 billion in 2026, and then over 10 billion in 2027. That is a significant acceleration.
EN_A: Besides transformers, what other products are they working on?
EN_B: The report highlights three areas. First, 1000kV GIS and 35kV switchgear for the domestic market. Second, ultra-high voltage DC products, especially converter valves. They might get small orders this year, with bigger breakthroughs in 2027. Third, supercapacitors.
EN_A: Supercapacitors? That sounds niche. How big could that be?
EN_B: Right now, it is probably a low single-digit contribution to revenue. But the report leaves a hint: if 800V DC becomes the standard for data center power supply, the upside could be much larger. That is one of the open questions the report does not fully answer.
EN_A: What about the second half of this year? Can earnings recover?
EN_B: The report expects net profit growth of 42 percent in the second half. The logic is that product mix will improve as more 750kV GIS and overseas transformers are recognized. Gross margins are expected to stay flat, but revenue growth will drive profit acceleration.
EN_A: What about valuation? Is the stock cheap or expensive?
EN_B: At the current price, it trades at 21.5 times 2027 estimated earnings, with a PEG ratio of 0.7. That is about a 30 percent discount to global peers, which trade at 30 times. The report sees that as attractive.
EN_A: But they did cut the price target from 286 to 237 RMB. Does that mean they are less bullish?
EN_B: Not at all. The cut is purely because of the earnings revision. The new target still implies 49 percent upside from the current price of 159 RMB. The rating remains overweight.
EN_A: So what are the key catalysts to watch?
EN_B: Two things. First, the third quarter results later this year, which should show the improving product mix. Second, any update on their Hong Kong IPO, which could be a re-rating catalyst.
EN_A: Are there any risks that the report does not fully address?
EN_B: Yes. The supercapacitor opportunity depends on a technology choice in data centers that is still uncertain. Also, the sustainability of North American orders needs further verification, given geopolitical risks. Those are areas where the full report provides more detail.
