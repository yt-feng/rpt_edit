These are structural improvements that, if sustained, would strengthen the core business.

That is a 149% sequential improvement from a loss in 2Q25, but it is also meaningfully lower than the headline figure. The gap between reported and recurring profit is approximately RMB 19 million in the quarter, roughly 28% of the headline number.

This is not an unusual disclosure, but it is an essential one. When a company reports a profit beat that includes a material non-recurring gain, the market often anchors to the headline and discounts the adjustment. The risk is that investors extrapolate the headline trajectory into future quarters without accounting for the fact that a significant portion of the reported earnings will not repeat. The analyst’s forecast, which was below the reported number, likely incorporated a more conservative view of both the operating improvement and the sustainability of the non-recurring items. The beat relative to consensus, therefore, does not invalidate the report's thesis.

## Product Mix Improvement Is Real but Insufficient to Bridge the Margin Gap

The company attributes part of the gross margin improvement to a higher contribution from large payload industrial robots. This is strategically positive. China’s industrial robot market has historically been dominated by low-payload, general-purpose units, where domestic manufacturers compete primarily on price. The shift toward high-payload robots, which require more sophisticated engineering and command higher prices, represents an upgrade in the competitive position of any domestic player that can execute it.

But the scale of this shift matters. Estun’s gross margin improvement, while welcome, has not yet moved the company’s margin profile to a level that justifies the current multiple. The analyst applies a 50x 2027 P/E multiple to the core business, which is below the company’s historical two-year forward average of 65x. That discount is explicitly justified by Estun’s lower margin profile relative to historical norms. In other words, even the analyst’s valuation methodology acknowledges that the margin recovery has a long way to go before it reaches a level that would support a higher multiple.

The second-order implication is that Estun’s operating leverage is not yet proven at scale. Improving product mix and reducing expense ratios are levers that can be pulled in a single quarter. Sustaining them over multiple quarters, especially in a competitive pricing environment, is a different challenge. The report does not provide guidance on whether the gross margin trajectory is expected to continue, but the fact that the analyst’s forecast was below the reported result suggests caution about the pace of further improvement.
