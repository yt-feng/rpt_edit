# China Retail Sales – Jun 2026: Momentum Remains Uneven

China retail sales rose 1.0% YoY in June (3.4% CAGR vs. 2019), above -0.1% consensus and -0.6% in May 26. Growth improved, partly helped by the Dragon Boat Festival calendar shift and some base effects. For July, summer holiday and weather disruptions remain key factors to watch.

YoY trend by category (reported YoY, with rebasing among categories): Retail sales of goods recovered to +0.9% yoy in June vs. -0.7% in May, with restaurants improved to 1.2% from 0.6% in May, partly helped by the calendar shift of the Dragon Boat Festival holiday. Online retail sales growth moderated to 2.0% yoy (3.4% in May), partly as the earlier start of the 618 shopping festival pulled forward some demand into May.

\- Among all categories, cosmetics stood out, with growth accelerating sharply to +12.6% yoy (+2.5% in May), helped by government subsidy effects and the 618 shopping festival campaign. Alcohol & Tobacco also recorded strong growth of 12.1% yoy, versus 4.8% in May, given an easier base with more normalized controls over entertainment activities of government officers (tightened measures in June last year).

\- Staples categories improved broadly. F&B growth accelerated to 8.4% (2.8% in May), driven by Food, Grain and Oil at +7.9% (+1.9% in May) and Alcohol & Tobacco. Soft drinks remained resilient at +5.8%, although slightly slower than +6.1% in May. HPC growth also improved to 3.9% from 1.6%.

\- Discretionary categories generally remained weaker than staples, but declines narrowed across most categories. Gold & Jewelry improved to -3.4% yoy vs. -8.9% yoy in May, Electronics & Appliances to -8.7% (-15.6% in May), and Home Furnishing to -6.6% (-8.7% in May). Sports and Entertainment Equipment also improved to -2.2% from -8.0%, partly as the high-base effect from government subsidies eased. Apparel remained solid at +3.9% yoy (+3.8% in May).

Compared with 2019 on a CAGR basis, overall momentum edged up to $3.4\%$ from $3.2\%$ in May, with modest improvements in food and restaurants.
