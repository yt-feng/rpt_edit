## K-shaped economy continues

With fading fiscal stimulus, falling credit impulse, and weakening domestic demand, China's headline GDP growth slowed visibly, to 4.3% y/y in Q2 from 5% in Q1. The print was weaker than consensus and our forecast (Bloomberg consensus: 4.5%, BARC: 4.7%). We highlighted the downside risks to our GDP growth forecasts given the sharp slowdown in activity data and domestic demand in April-May. On a q/q saar basis, the growth momentum slowed to 1.8% in Q2 from 6.8% in Q1. Reflecting the weaker-than-expected Q2 real GDP growth, we revise down our 2026 full-year GDP growth forecast modestly to 4.5% from 4.6%. Despite the downward revision, we still expect China to hit the lower bound of the official 4.5-5% growth target.

We think the June activity data continued to paint a picture of a K-shaped recovery. First, demand still lagged behind supply by a wide margin, with infrastructure investment posting a double-digit decline, most property indicators deteriorating and retail sales growing only marginally, while IP growth reached a three-month high thanks to strong export growth. Second, traditional industries (construction, low-tech and labor-intensive manufacturing sectors) lagged behind the new and high-tech industries, which include renewable energy, industrial robotics, semiconductors, AI and advanced manufacturing. Third, domestic demand lagged behind external demand, with exports remaining a key growth engine for GDP.

We note that in contrast to still above-4% GDP growth, we estimate the weighted average of demand indicators (retail sales, FAI and exports), deflated by the simple average of CPI and PPI, fell 3.6% y/y in Q2 after expanding 3.4% in Q1 (Figure 6). We think the sharp deterioration in domestic demand reflects payback from front-loaded fiscal stimulus and a weakening credit impulse, and structural constraints associated with the K-shaped growth pattern.

## H2 growth outlook: momentum to remain weak in Q3 before recovering in Q4

We expect a typical intra-year pattern to persist: a strong early-year rebound, moderation in Q2–Q3 as fiscal effects fade, followed by a policy-supported pickup in Q4 to meet the annual target (Q3E: 4.1% q/q saar, Q4E: 4.9%). We note that the positive effects of previously announced policies are still unfolding. In particular, the CNY800bn new financing tool (vs CNY500bn in 2025) had not yet been deployed as of H1. We expect disbursements to commence in H2, providing incremental support to growth. On a quarterly basis, growth momentum is likely to remain weak in Q3 before recovering in Q4 as additional stimulus kicks in. Our 2026 full-year GDP growth forecast of 4.5% is underpinned by three key rationales.

\- First, China's exports have consistently exceeded market expectations year to date, partially offsetting weakened domestic demand. We expect exports to continue to benefit from the global AI capex investment cycle and the ongoing global energy transition, providing an important buffer for growth. We expect export growth to remain double-digits in H2 in view of strong export orders on hand among high-tech exporters (green-tech and AI-related), lending support to GDP growth.

\- Second, we note that the positive effects of previously announced policies are still unfolding. In particular, the CNY800bn new financing tool had not yet been deployed as of H1. We expect disbursements to commence in H2, providing incremental support to growth. It was reported that the CNY800bn new financing tool will be channelled towards sectors similar to those in 2025, including digital economy, AI, low-altitude economy, infrastructure and green transition, with a heavier focus on services $^{1}$ .

\- Third, beyond the announced (but not yet deployed) CNY800bn new financing tool, we see scope for the authorities to introduce additional (quasi-)fiscal stimulus to support domestic demand and hit growth target. As 2026 marks the first year of the 15th Five-Year Plan, policymakers likely retain flexibility to bring forward infrastructure projects and step up support if 2026 GDP growth is at risk of missing March NPC's target of $4.5 - 5\%$ .

## Watch late July and October Politburo meetings

From a timing perspective, we expect the government to have a clearer assessment of whether China is at risk of missing its growth target by 19 October, when Q3 GDP data are released. This will likely inform the decision on whether to step up additional stimulus. As a result, the late-October Politburo meeting (or the NPC Standing Committee meeting around that time) will be a key event to gauge potential new stimulus, alongside the late-July Politburo meeting. For context, China announced additional quasi-fiscal stimulus of CNY500bn in the form of a new financial policy tool in H2 25 (late September), while previously unveiling a CNY10trn package for local government debt resolution at the NPC Standing Committee meeting in November 2024.

## Key takeaways from the June activity data

\- Retail sales rose 1% y/y in June, reversing the 0.6% decline in May. However, the pace of growth remained well below the average 2.4% increase recorded in Q1 (Figure 1), reflecting continued headwinds from a challenging labour market, weak private-sector credit demand, and the persistent negative wealth effect associated with the property sector softness. While the unemployment rate edged lower during April-June, the NBS employment and income expectation indices showing only a modest rebound in June and remaining below their Q1 levels. Trade-in-related categories showed mixed performance. Auto sales value declined 16% y/y, marking a fourth consecutive month of double-digit contraction. Excluding auto sales and trade-in-related goods, retail sales growth accelerated to 5.5% y/y in June from 4.5% in May (Figure 2).

\- Fixed asset investment (FAI) contracted 5.7% y/y YTD, weakening noticeably from the 4.1% drop in the first five months of this year. The y/y decline in June stayed in double-digits for a second month, with three major categories, manufacturing, infrastructure, and property, registering outright declines (Figure 3). The continued weakness in infrastructure investment likely reflects a front-loading of projects to Q1. Moreover, after a brief stabilisation in property sales during March and early April, most property indicators deteriorated again, with deep contractions in investment and sales (Figure 4). Leading indicators of the property market remained deeply negative, with floor space started down 26% (May: -24.6%) and floor space under construction down 29.3% (May: -34.5%). Property completions also remained weak, falling 25%.

\- In contrast, industrial production growth accelerated to 5.3% y/y in June from 4.5% in May (Figure 5)– with strength particularly seen in new and high-tech sectors such as semiconductors, industrial robots, batteries and NEVs – while traditional sectors (eg, steel, coal and cement) continued to contract and underperform. That said, June IP growth remained below the Q1 average of 6.1%, even though export growth in June beat expectations and exceeded both the Q1 and May prints. In our view, this divergence – similar to April-May – suggests that the weakening in domestic demand is increasingly spilling over into production.

## Details of June activity data

\- FAI contracted $5.7\%$ y/y YTD in the first half of this year, weakening noticeably from the $4.1\%$ drop in the first five months of this year and fell faster than consensus forecast of a $5\%$ decline. For the month of June, the FAI fell y/y in double digits $(-10\%)$ for a second month, a pace last seen in Q4 25, although the pace eased slightly from $-10.7\%$ in May. Three major categories of manufacturing, infrastructure, and property registered outright declines. By ownership, SoE investment fell $7\%$ y/y in June after a $8.9\%$ drop in May, while private FAI remained in deep contraction at $-12.4\%$ (May: $-11.8\%$ ).

Manufacturing investment contracted for a third consecutive month, falling 3.2% y/y in June following a 4.2% drop in May. Resilient investment in high-tech industries may have partly offset weaker spending in traditional and labour-intensive sectors. High-tech investment rose 4.6% y/y YTD in the first six months, supported by strong gains in aerospace and related equipment manufacturing (+23.3%), information services (+15.5%), and computer and office equipment manufacturing (+8.1%). In contrast, investment in chemical raw materials and products continued to weaken, falling 6.5% YTD in H1 26 (5M 26: -5.9%).

Infrastructure investment fell 10.2% y/y in June, roughly matching the 10.8% decline in May. All three major infrastructure sub-categories posted declines. Transportation investment contracted for a second month, falling 7.7% (May: -5.7%). This was accompanied by continued declines in power, gas and water production and supply (June: -8.9%, May: -11.7%), and water conservancy and public utilities management (June: -13.4%, May: -13.7%).

Property investment declined 24.4% y/y in June, unchanged from May and remaining close to levels seen in H2 25. Weakness was broad based across key property indicators. Floor space sold fell 14.3% y/y (May: -13.2%), marking the steepest decline this year. Leading indicators remained deeply negative, with floor space started down 26% (May: -24.6%) and floor space under construction down 29.3% (May: -34.5%). Property completions also remained weak, falling 25%. On the price side, new home prices fell 0.2% m/m for a fourth consecutive month, with modest gains in tier-1 cities offset by price declines in tier-3 cities. Secondary home prices declined 0.3% m/m, unchanged from May. The number of cities recording monthly price increases continued to edge lower, falling to 9 out of 70 from a post-May 2023 high of 13 in March.

\- Retail sales picked up 1% y/y in June, after posting its first drop since China reopened from the COVID restrictions in 2023 (May: -0.6%) and exceeding market expectations for a continued contraction of 0.1%. Both goods and catering services improved. Goods sales stabilized, with growth improving to 0% y/y after contracting for two consecutive months, while catering services growth accelerated to 1.2% from 0.6% in May, which had been the weakest reading since 2023.

Goods sales increased 0.9% y/y in June after declining for two consecutive months. Performance across major trade-in categories was mixed. Sales of communication devices surged 16.5% y/y, up from a modest 0.7% gain in May, partly reflecting more favourable base effects. Sales of household appliances declined for a fourth consecutive month, although the pace of decline narrowed to 8.7% from 15.6% in May, while stationery sales growth rebounded to 12.7% y/y (May: -1.5%). Auto sales declined at a double-digit pace for a fourth consecutive month, with the drop staying at 16% in the month. Property-related consumption continued to underperform, with furniture sales declining 6.6% y/y (May: -8.7%) and construction and decoration materials falling 10.5% (May: -13.6%). Discretionary spending was mixed, with jewellery sales down 3.4% y/y, while cosmetics sales growth accelerated to 12.6% from 2.5% in May.

\- Industrial production growth accelerated to $5.3\%$ y/y in June from $4.5\%$ in May, exceeding consensus and coinciding with robust export growth of $27\%$ y/y during the month. High-tech manufacturing continued to outperform the headline print, with output growing $14\%$ y/y following a $15\%$ increase in May. Within this, output of green products remained strong, with NEV growth accelerating to 29.4 from $17.8\%$ in May, while semiconductor production increased by $19\%$ y/y in June (May: $23\%$ ). Production of industrial robots remained elevated at $28\%$ y/y, matching the pace in May.
