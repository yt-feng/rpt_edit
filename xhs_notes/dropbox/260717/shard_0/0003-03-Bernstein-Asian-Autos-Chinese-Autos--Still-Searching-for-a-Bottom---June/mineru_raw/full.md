China auto wholesale -5.3% YoY in June, with domestic retail volume -21.4% and export +80.2%. China's domestic auto demand remains in a period of adjustment, with exports helping to sustain industry production. We track mandatory first-time auto insurance volumes in China, which we believe gives the most accurate read on retail sell-through. June retail passenger vehicle sales totaled 1.71mn units, -21.4% yoy. The June retail SAAR of 20.7mn units is trending higher than 19.2 mn in May 2026, but still below our estimate of normalized annual demand of c.22mn units. June performance continued to reflect the pull-forward effect from the 2024-25 subsidy programs (which we estimate shifted over 5mn units of demand), coupled with a high base. Barring further policy measures, we see limited catalysts for a meaningful recovery in domestic demand before November–December, when base effects are expected to normalize.(Links to monthly sales volume trackers: PV Wholesale, and PV Retail.)

Allowing the market to adjust. At current levels, we remain relatively cautious on the likelihood of further major policy support from the Chinese government that would meaningfully boost auto demand. While domestic demand remains in a period of adjustment, resilient exports continue to cushion overall industry production, taking off some of the pressure for a quick demand recovery. We may see incremental measures, such as additional funding for trade-in programs or targeted campaigns in lower-tier and rural markets. However, even if further stimulus is introduced, we do not expect it to match the generosity of the trade-in incentives offered over the past two years. Importantly, a significant portion of demand has already been pulled forward, and consumers who purchased vehicles recently are unlikely to return to the market in the near term. The industry just needs time for demand to normalize organically before a more sustainable recovery can emerge.

June EV penetration reached 59.2%, with BEVs accounting for 40.9% and PHEVs 18.3%. EV sales declined -9.9% YoY, with BEVs up +2.1% and PHEVs down -28.7%. PHEV demand continues to decline sharply for several reasons: 1) Recent BEV launches have delivered meaningful improvements in driving range and fast-charging capabilities, significantly reducing range anxiety. 2) BEVs are now often priced on par with comparable PHEV models, narrowing PHEVs' value proposition. 3) At the same time, policy support has become less favorable: PHEVs with less than 100km of electric-only range no longer qualify for reduced purchase tax of 5%, but are subject to 10% ICE-equivalent purchase tax rate, effectively excluding a significant portion (estimated to be c.40%) of models that previously benefited. 4) Looking ahead, from 2027, PHEVs will also lose their exemption from the annual vehicle and vessel tax, while BEVs will remain fully exempt. Although the annual cost impact is modest, it could amount to several hundred RMB per year and several thousand RMB over a vehicle's lifetime, further eroding the relative attractiveness of PHEVs.

BYD remained China's leading EV manufacturer, delivering 225k units and maintaining a stable 22.3% market share. Demand for its latest fast-charging models remains strong, and we expect deliveries to accelerate from Q3 as battery supply constraints ease. Geely Group ranked second with 113k units and 11.2% share, followed by Leapmotor at 70k units (7.0%).

Exports grew +80% yoy in June, with ICE +29% and EV +159%. Exports made up 38% of total PV wholesale, partially offsetting the domestic demand trends. BEV & PHEV exports contributed c.56% of total export volume. Chery continued to lead with 188k units (+78% yoy), followed by BYD with 171k (+99%) and Geely with 102k units (+155%).

## INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS

We maintain a cautious outlook for the sector. The reduction in subsidies and a 5% purchase tax increase on EVs are expected to moderate market momentum. This follows strong demand pulled forward into 2024-2025 and a high comparison base, while macro conditions and consumer sentiment remain in a period of adjustment. The industry will also face considerations around material cost trends. We expect industry wholesale volumes to reach c.29 mn units, falling 3-5% yoy. We forecast domestic retail demand of 20-21mn units, declining 10-12% yoy. Exports should remain a growth driver, arriving at 8.5-9mn units, growing 40-50% yoy.

The long term secular growth outlook for EVs remains intact and even though EV transition has come to the mass adoption phase in China, we forecast domestic EV sales growth will be c.3-5% yoy for 2026 and drive EV penetration to 62%. EV exports is going from strength to strength and projected to grow c.65-75% yoy in 2026, bringing overall EV wholesale shipment to 15-20% yoy. We believe competition within the domestic market will remain intense and overseas markets are increasingly important as a strategic growth opportunity. For our EV names, we rate BYD and Xiaomi Outperform, and XPeng, Li Auto, and NIO Market-Perform. Within our traditional Chinese OEMs coverage, we rate Geely Outperform and Great Wall, GAC, and SAIC Market-Perform.
