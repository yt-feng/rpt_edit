# China's Consumption Strategy Is Shifting from Volume to Value – and Services Are the New Engine

The State Council's 15th Five-Year Plan for expanding consumption, released on 13 July, targets retail sales of approximately RMB 60 trillion by 2030, implying a compound annual growth rate of just 3.7% in nominal terms. That figure is striking not for its ambition but for its modesty. In the first five months of 2026, retail sales grew only 1.4% year-on-year. The gap between the target and the current trajectory is not a forecasting error. It is a signal that policymakers have abandoned the hope of a broad-based consumption boom and are instead placing a concentrated bet on a single vector: services consumption. This is not a plan to spend more across the board. It is a plan to spend differently.

The plan explicitly calls for raising the share of services spending in household consumption, with priority areas including elderly care, childcare, culture and tourism, health, and sports. A previous government guideline set a target for total services consumption to reach RMB 100 trillion by 2030, implying a CAGR of 4.6%. That is a full percentage point faster than the headline retail sales target. The arithmetic is clear: services must outgrow goods to meet the overall consumption goal. The implication for observers and business strategists is that the next wave of consumer demand in China will be structurally different from the last one. It will be less about acquiring things and more about purchasing time, care, experiences, and well-being.

The urgency behind this shift is driven by a property market that continues to influence household willingness to spend through wealth effects. While tier-1 cities have shown some improvement, broader recovery has not materialized. A property market in transition does not only affect consumption in the property value chain; it influences the sense of financial security that underpins discretionary spending. The plan's emphasis on optimizing affordable housing supply and advancing urban renewal is an attempt to stabilize the sector, but it is not a return to the old model of property-led growth. New urbanization could add 0.8 to 1.3 percentage points of consumption spending to annual GDP by 2030, according to analysis cited in the report. That is meaningful but gradual. The faster lever is services.

## Services Consumption Is Not a Luxury Add-On – It Is the Only Realistic Growth Vector for Household Spending

The share of services in household consumption expenditure has been rising, but there remains ample room for growth compared with developed economies. The plan's focus on elderly care and childcare responds directly to demographic pressures that are both a constraint and an opportunity. An aging population creates demand for care services, while declining birth rates mean fewer children per household, potentially freeing up income for other forms of consumption. The cultural and tourism sector, already showing elevated cross-city travel and domestic flight volumes in recent data, benefits from pent-up demand for experiences that goods cannot satisfy.

The implication for businesses is that the most attractive consumer segments in China over the next five years will not be those selling more physical products to the mass market. They will be those offering services that solve for time scarcity, care deficits, and the desire for experiential differentiation. This includes everything from premium healthcare and senior living to childcare platforms, cultural tourism, and fitness and wellness services. The plan explicitly promotes high-quality services supply and further opening of the services sector to drive growth. For foreign observers, this signals that regulatory openness in services may be a more reliable bet than in goods trade.

## The Green Transition Is Being Reset for Speed, Not Just Scale – and Grid Integration Is the Bottleneck

The 15th Five-Year Plan set a headline target of lifting non-fossil energy to 25% of total energy consumption by 2030, from 21.7% in 2025, implying an average increase of 0.66 percentage points per year. But the National Energy Administration's 2026-28 Action Plan, released on 10 July, raised that ambition to approximately one percentage point per year through 2028. The acceleration is deliberate. It suggests that policymakers believe the early years of the 15th FYP period are the right time to push hardest on energy transition, before the later years may face greater economic or geopolitical constraints.

The granular targets in the State Council's carbon-peaking action plan, also released in July, include explicit installed-capacity objectives for wind, solar, nuclear, and hydropower by 2030. However, the pace of new capacity additions for wind and solar is expected to moderate from the extraordinary levels of recent years. Combined wind and solar installed capacity increased by 360 GW in 2024 and 440 GW in 2025. The plan now targets around 200 GW of annual additions during the 15th FYP period. This is not a retreat from green energy. It is a recalibration toward quality over quantity. Policy priorities are shifting toward encouraging technological breakthroughs, curbing involutionary competition, and phasing out outdated capacity.

The most important operational insight in this section is not about how much capacity is being built. It is about how much of that capacity is actually connected to the grid. As noted in the report, China's seemingly low share of renewables in the energy consumption mix, despite massive capacity additions, partly reflects capacity that is not yet fully connected. Once it is, the share should materially increase. The plan's emphasis on enhancing the power system's capability to integrate, allocate, and manage renewable energy, improving cross-region transmission, and developing long-duration power storage is the critical enabler. For companies in the energy storage, grid equipment, and transmission sectors, this represents a more predictable demand signal than any capacity target alone.

A further priority is linking computing facilities with clean energy. With AI demand expected to remain strong, electricity consumption will continue to rise. The plan explicitly promotes using primarily non-fossil fuel electricity to supply newly built computing power facilities. This is a direct policy signal that the intersection of AI infrastructure and clean energy is a strategic priority, not an afterthought. For observers of data centers, cloud computing, and AI hardware, the implication is that location decisions and energy procurement strategies will increasingly be shaped by clean energy availability, not just cost.

## Fiscal Policy Will Accelerate, but Monetary Policy Will Remain Targeted – the July Politburo Meeting Is the Key Inflection Point

Recent data shows exports continue to provide support for growth, while domestic demand likely remained steady in June. GDP growth is expected to moderate from the first quarter's outperformance, with a forecast of 4.6% year-on-year. This creates a policy window. The July Politburo meeting, which typically occurs at month-end and provides economic policy guidance for the second half of the year, is the natural venue for an accelerated response.

The expectation is for guidance around faster implementation of fiscal policy, particularly special local government bond issuance, and the use of existing tools such as new financing policy instruments. Fiscal policy is the primary lever because it can directly support infrastructure investment and livelihood improvement, which in turn supports employment and income. The report notes that local government special bond issuance can be stepped up, and the data shows room for acceleration.

Monetary policy, by contrast, is likely to remain supportive through liquidity and targeted easing, but further policy rate adjustments are not expected. The People's Bank of China's second-quarter Monetary Policy Committee meeting readout, released on 8 July, was broadly consistent with the first quarter. It reiterated a moderately loose monetary policy stance and increased counter-cyclical and cross-cyclical policy adjustments. However, the second-quarter meeting introduced new phrasing about structural divergence in the economy. This suggests the PBOC is paying attention to the increasingly K-shaped growth trajectory: resilient exports and strong domestic industrial upgrading on one side, and consumption and investment on the other.

The PBOC pledged to use structural tools to support domestic demand, technological innovation, and small and medium-sized enterprises. For businesses, this means that access to credit will remain available but will be channeled through targeted instruments rather than broad-based easing. Companies in the technology and innovation sectors, as well as SMEs, are the likely beneficiaries. For observers, the monetary policy stance reinforces the view that the government is willing to support growth but is not prepared to reflate the economy through aggressive rate adjustments or quantitative easing.

## A Decision Framework for the Second Half of 2026

The convergence of these three policy vectors – services-led consumption, accelerated green transition with grid integration as the binding constraint, and targeted fiscal-monetary coordination – creates a specific set of strategic implications for business leaders and observers.

First, allocate capital toward services sectors that are explicitly prioritized in the consumption plan: elderly care, childcare, culture and tourism, health, and sports. These are not speculative bets. They are backed by a five-year plan with measurable targets and a demonstrated willingness to open the services sector to high-quality supply, including from foreign providers.

Second, reassess exposure to renewable energy equipment manufacturers. The moderation in annual capacity additions for wind and solar means that volume-driven growth in manufacturing may slow. The value is shifting to grid integration, energy storage, and cross-region transmission. Companies that solve the connectivity problem will capture more of the value chain than those that simply add more panels or turbines.

Third, prepare for a fiscal acceleration in the second half of the year. Infrastructure investment tied to major network projects and urban renewal will receive renewed support. Companies in construction materials, engineering, and urban infrastructure should monitor special local government bond issuance as a leading indicator.

Fourth, do not expect broad-based monetary easing. The PBOC's focus on structural divergence and targeted tools means that credit conditions will vary significantly by sector. Technology, innovation, and SMEs will have better access. Real estate and consumption goods may not.

Fifth, monitor the July Politburo meeting as the key policy event for the remainder of the year. The guidance issued there will set the tone for fiscal implementation, bond issuance, and any adjustments to the policy toolkit. The meeting is the most likely venue for an acceleration of existing plans, not a departure from them.

The strategy emerging from Beijing is not one of stimulus in the traditional sense. It is one of structural redirection. The government is not trying to make the old consumption model work harder. It is trying to build a new one. The services sector, the green grid, and targeted fiscal support are the pillars of that new model. For those who understand the shift, the opportunity lies not in betting on recovery but in positioning for reallocation.
