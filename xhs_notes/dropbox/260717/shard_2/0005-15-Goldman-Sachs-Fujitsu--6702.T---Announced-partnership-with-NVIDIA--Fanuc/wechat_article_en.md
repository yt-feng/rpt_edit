This is a hardware business with known margin characteristics,but it is also a gateway. Every customer that buys a Fujitsu AI server to run physical AI workloads is locked into the broader ecosystem.

The second component is the software stack,which includes AI platforms,AI agents,and a physical OS. This is where Fujitsu can differentiate. The AI agents are the decision-making layer that interprets data from sensors and production systems. The physical OS is the runtime environment that executes those decisions on robotic hardware. The third component is the collaborative control infrastructure. This is the most strategically important piece. It is the communication and coordination layer that transmits AI-driven decisions from the servers to the robots and networking equipment. It enables multiple devices to work in concert,which is the difference between a single robotic arm performing a repetitive task and an entire factory floor operating as a self-optimizing system. Fujitsu plans to co-develop this framework with its partners and push for it to become an open industry standard. If successful,this would give Fujitsu a structural advantage similar to what an operating system provider enjoys in the PC ecosystem.

The revenue implications are layered. Fujitsu earns hardware margins on AI servers,software license or subscription fees on the AI platform and physical OS,and potentially integration or consulting fees for deploying the collaborative control infrastructure.
