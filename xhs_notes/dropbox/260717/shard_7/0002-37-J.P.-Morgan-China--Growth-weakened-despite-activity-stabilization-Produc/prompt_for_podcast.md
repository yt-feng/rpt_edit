## External challenges meet weak absorption

The growth impulse remains narrow. External demand, trade rerouting, the global AI/IP cycle and policy-supported production are supporting activity, but spillovers to jobs, income and household demand are limited. The result is a two-speed economy: resilient production alongside weak consumption, cautious private investment, a still-adjusting housing sector and slow fiscal execution.

Risks are building on both fronts. Externally, renewed Middle East tensions raise energy, shipping and commodity-cost risks; the US is rebuilding tariff walls through Section 301/232 tools and sector restrictions; a September Trump-Xi summit may stabilize relations tactically but not resolve structural disputes; and Europe adds pressure around EVs, batteries, solar and subsidies. Domestically, weak housing, cautious firms and constrained local governments reduce fiscal multipliers and increase the risk that production resilience turns into inventories, price competition, margin pressure and disinflation.

Policy needs to shift from supply-side to absorption. The priority is faster fiscal execution: drawing down deposits, front-loading special bonds, expanding policy-bank and central-government financing, and pushing funds into projects with clear demand, productivity gains, and private-sector participation. Housing policy should focus on completion, inventory absorption, urban renewal and buyer confidence. Monetary easing can help at the margin, but without stronger credit demand, rate cuts would be more signal than stimulus.

The July Politburo meeting is likely to reinforce faster fiscal deployment, domestic demand support, and stabilization of employment, enterprises, markets and expectations. The key test is whether policy can turn available funding into activity, otherwise it will remain a buffer rather than a turnaround. The 15th Five-Year Plan for Expanding Consumption, released on July 13 by the NDRC and Ministry of Commerce, is directionally positive but short on implementation details and explicit, targeted fiscal deployment. It aims for retail sales of around CNY 60trn by 2030, up from CNY 50.1trn in 2025, and emphasizes services, digital and green consumption, AI-plus-consumption, consumer infrastructure, income growth and social security. As with past initiatives, the risk is that a consumption plan becomes state-guided, supply-side oriented and infrastructure-heavy, limiting its ability to generate genuine household demand.

Our outlook remains for managed stabilization, not a decisive rebound. We have lowered our full-year real GDP forecast to 4.6% y/y from 4.7%, reflecting weaker domestic demand and a tougher external environment. At the same time, we revise up sequential growth to 4.3% q/q saar in 3Q and 4.9% in 4Q, from 3.5% and 3.7%, respectively. The 3Q upgrade reflects likely stronger fiscal delivery after the 2Q undershoot, while the 4Q upgrade assumes possible additional out-of-budget support to keep 2026 growth within the 4.5–5.0% target range. Even so, the growth cushion remains narrow: external demand and industrial upgrading are still doing the heavy lifting, while domestic absorption is too weak to sustain a broader recovery.
