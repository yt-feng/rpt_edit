## June credit data - demand weakness and loan pricing pressure persists

The June credit data shows a consistent narrative of resilient production and weak consumption, as highlighted by our macro team (link). Both TSF and new loans missed expectations, with continued deceleration in credit growth. Our take is negative on a net basis. First, new loans continue to disappoint, with retail lending contracting 1.3% y/y in June. Second, pricing for new corporate loans contracted sequentially and on a y/y basis; this, along with mix deterioration in 2Q, will add pressure to the banks' overall loan yield, offsetting the benefit from time deposits re-pricing. Third, weak demand suggests pressure on the overall economy; asset quality of retail loans will likely remain under pressure. In addition, remarks at the PBOC press conference suggest that addressing weak loan growth is a low policy priority, as policymakers shift their focus from quantity-based to price-based policy management. Thus, we prefer high-quality banks with earnings resilience. We like CCB, BOC and ICBC among SOE banks, and Citic and CMB among JSBs.

## What we don't like about the credit data

\- New TSF and loans missed expectations: Growth rate of TSF balance continued to decelerate, to 7.4% y/y in Jun-26 from 7.7% in May, within that, RMB loan growth moderated to 5.2% y/y from 5.5% y/y in May. New TSF and RMB loans were Rmb3.36tn and Rmb1.61tn, respectively, rebounding m/m on seasonality but both missing Bloomberg expectations of Rmb3.71tn and Rmb1.95tn. Please refer to Table 1 for details.

\- Weakness in retail loans persists: New retail loans were only Rmb265bn in Jun-26, down from Rmb598bn in Jun-25, and accounted for just 16% of total new loans. This points to continued weakness in both mortgage-related and consumer-credit demand.
