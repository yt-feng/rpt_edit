## China banks

## June credit data - demand weakness and loan pricing pressure persists

The June credit data shows a consistent narrative of resilient production and weak consumption, as highlighted by our macro team (link). Both TSF and new loans missed expectations, with continued deceleration in credit growth. Our take is negative on a net basis. First, new loans continue to disappoint, with retail lending contracting 1.3% y/y in June. Second, pricing for new corporate loans contracted sequentially and on a y/y basis; this, along with mix deterioration in 2Q, will add pressure to the banks' overall loan yield, offsetting the benefit from time deposits re-pricing. Third, weak demand suggests pressure on the overall economy; asset quality of retail loans will likely remain under pressure. In addition, remarks at the PBOC press conference suggest that addressing weak loan growth is a low policy priority, as policymakers shift their focus from quantity-based to price-based policy management. Thus, we prefer high-quality banks with earnings resilience. We like CCB, BOC and ICBC among SOE banks, and Citic and CMB among JSBs.

## What we don't like about the credit data

\- New TSF and loans missed expectations: Growth rate of TSF balance continued to decelerate, to 7.4% y/y in Jun-26 from 7.7% in May, within that, RMB loan growth moderated to 5.2% y/y from 5.5% y/y in May. New TSF and RMB loans were Rmb3.36tn and Rmb1.61tn, respectively, rebounding m/m on seasonality but both missing Bloomberg expectations of Rmb3.71tn and Rmb1.95tn. Please refer to Table 1 for details.

\- Weakness in retail loans persists: New retail loans were only Rmb265bn in Jun-26, down from Rmb598bn in Jun-25, and accounted for just 16% of total new loans. Retail loan growth declined further to -1.3% y/y from -0.9% in May, with medium- to long-term retail loan growth moderating to 0.5% y/y from 0.8% and short-term retail loan growth falling to -6.7% y/y from -6.0%. This points to continued weakness in both mortgage-related and consumer-credit demand. The silver lining was that discounted bills accounted for only 7% of new loans in June, down sharply from 107% in May, while April new-loan creation was almost entirely supported by bill financing. Corporate loans excluding bills also rebounded to Rmb1.38tn in June from Rmb80bn in May. The PBOC mentioned that growth rate of SME loans was 8.3% y/y, M/L industrial loans was 5.9% y/y and M/L service sector loans (excluding real estate) was 5.9% y/y, all above the overall loan growth rate of 5.2% y/y. On a net basis, given the contraction in higher-yielding retail loan balance, we view the overall loan mix as having deteriorated.

\- New-loan pricing trended down: Aside from mix, pricing for new corporate loans has declined. The PBOC commented that the weighted-average rate on newly issued corporate loans was \~3.0% in 6M26, around -20bps y/y and below the \~3.1% rate on new corporate loans in March 2026. For mortgages, the rate on newly issued personal housing loans was \~3.1% in 6M26, broadly stable y/y. The PBOC also indicated that the new-loan rate for inclusive small and micro-enterprise loans was \~3.57% in Jun, down 16bps y/y. ## What we like about the credit data

\- Corporate bond issuance remained robust and supported aggregate financing: Corporate bond financing reached Rmb401bn in Jun-26, up 66% y/y and 139% m/m. Although government bond financing moderated to Rmb768bn in June, down 43% y/y, the outstanding balance of combined corporate and government bond financing still grew 12.8% y/y, materially above bank loan growth of 5.2%. The strength in bond financing provided support to overall TSF but may also have disintermediated bank lending and added to the slowdown in loan growth. This is consistent with the PBOC's view that loans alone no longer fully capture financing conditions and that loans and bonds should increasingly be assessed together.

\- Deposit growth (8.2% y/y) remained above loan growth (5.2% y/y), easing pressure on deposits competition: Deposit growth was 8.2% y/y in Jun-26, moderating from 8.7% in May but remaining well above loan growth of 5.2%. This continued divergence points to ample system liquidity and should ease competition for deposits. Demand-deposit ratio was broadly stable m/m at 26.5% in May, with the corporate demand-deposit ratio increasing by 35bps m/m and the retail ratio declining by 18bps. Retail deposit growth was 7.1% y/y in June, still higher than corporate deposit growth of 4.5%, but decelerated from 7.5% in May and was less than half its previous peak of 18.3% in Feb 2023. This supports our view that incremental retail deposits are rotating into financial products. NBFI deposits contracted by Rmb990bn m/m in June, but this was largely due to seasonality, compared with a Rmb520bn contraction in Jun-25.
