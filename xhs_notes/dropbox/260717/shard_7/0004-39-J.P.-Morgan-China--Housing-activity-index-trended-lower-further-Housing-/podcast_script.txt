ZH_A: 最近有份研报提到中国房地产市场的活动指数还在往下走，这到底意味着什么？

ZH_B: 简单来说，市场调整远没结束。报告指出，6月份房屋新开工、销售、竣工和开发商资金来源这些关键指标，收缩幅度都在扩大。

ZH_A: 所以这不是短期波动，而是趋势性的？

ZH_B: 对。报告里有个很核心的判断，说我们现在处于一个“数量调整”的延长周期里，价格的稳定只是暂时的。

ZH_A: 那价格方面具体怎么样？一线城市不是一直在说回暖吗？

ZH_B: 是的，但回暖的力度在减弱。6月份一线城市的新房和二手房价格涨幅都比5月份慢了0.1个百分点。更值得注意的是，全国二手房价格跌幅从-0.26%扩大到了-0.32%。

ZH_A: 这听起来像是一个K型复苏，少数高端市场在撑，大部分在跌。

ZH_B: 没错。报告明确用了“K-shaped stabilization”这个词。一线城市和改善型、高端住宅确实有短暂回升，但低线城市整体低迷，而且一线城市的恢复也开始乏力。

ZH_A: 那政策层面呢？最近不是出了“提振消费十五五规划”吗？里面怎么定位房地产的？

ZH_B: 这个非常关键。报告指出，规划把房地产的角色从“投资引擎”重新定义为“消费赋能者”。不再强调刺激销售，而是聚焦于改善可负担性、支持改善性需求和发展租赁市场。

ZH_A: 所以政策重点从“买房”转向了“提高居住质量”？

ZH_B: 对。规划里有个很重要的转变，就是住房被和汽车、家电放在一起，归为家庭消费品。同时，物业管理也被明确列为消费领域，要升级服务质量和拓展社区服务。

ZH_A: 那这是不是意味着，未来房地产的投资逻辑要彻底变了？

ZH_B: 是的。报告揭示了一个深层的矛盾，叫“政策时间不一致陷阱”。一方面想降低对房地产的依赖，另一方面又担心房价下跌冲击家庭财富和地方财政，所以价格调整很慢，主要通过“量”来出清。

ZH_A: 那这种“量”的调整什么时候能结束？报告有没有给出一个时间表？

ZH_B: 报告没有给出具体时间，但暗示调整远未完成。库存高企、居民信贷需求弱，房地产投资持续下滑，这些信号都指向调整还在进行中。

ZH_A: 那有没有什么信号是我们需要密切关注的，来判断市场是否真的触底？

ZH_B: 有的。报告里提到了一些先行指标，比如中介信心指数和二手房挂牌价指数。这些指标在7月初短暂反弹后又开始回落，说明市场信心很脆弱。另外，一线城市二手市场的恢复是否可持续，也需要继续观察。

ZH_A: 这听起来确实很复杂。那对于投资者来说，这份报告最值得深入阅读的部分是什么？

ZH_B: 我觉得最值得深读的是报告对“K型复苏”的详细论证，以及政策如何在“提振消费”和“控制风险”之间寻找平衡。还有一个悬念是，规划提到要“多渠道促进居民收入增长”，但具体如何通过住房来支持家庭信心和财富，报告里只是点到为止，没有完全展开。

EN_A: So there is a new report that says China’s housing activity index is still trending lower. What is the big takeaway here?

EN_B: The key takeaway is that the market adjustment is far from over. In June, the contraction in major indicators like housing starts, sales, and developer funding actually got worse, not better.

EN_A: So this is not just a short-term blip? It is more like a trend?

EN_B: Exactly. The report calls it a “prolonged quantity adjustment.” Price stabilization is only tentative, and the recovery we saw in the first quarter is losing steam.

EN_A: What about prices? We keep hearing about a recovery in first-tier cities.

EN_B: Yes, but even that recovery is moderating. In June, both new and secondary home price gains in tier-1 cities slowed by 0.1 percentage points compared to May. And nationally, secondary home price declines actually widened.

EN_A: That sounds like a K-shaped recovery. Some segments are doing okay, but most are not.

EN_B: That is exactly the term the report uses: “K-shaped stabilization.” The improvement is narrow and increasingly selective. Lower-tier cities remain broadly subdued, and even the luxury segment in tier-1 cities seems to be running out of steam.

EN_A: But what about the policy side? There is a new five-year plan for boosting consumption. How does it treat housing?

EN_B: This is a huge shift. The plan redefines housing from an investment engine to a consumption enabler. Instead of boosting sales, the focus is on affordability, upgrade demand, and rental markets.

EN_A: So it is less about buying homes and more about improving living quality?

EN_B: Yes. Housing is now grouped with other durable goods like cars and home appliances. Property management is even explicitly identified as a consumption category. The whole logic is moving from “buying a house” to “living better in a house.”

EN_A: That sounds like a fundamental change in the investment narrative for real estate.

EN_B: It is. But the report also points out a deep contradiction, which it calls a “policy time-inconsistency trap.” Policymakers want to reduce dependence on property, but they are also worried about household wealth and local government revenue. So they are letting the correction happen through volume, not price.

EN_A: So how long will this volume adjustment last? Does the report give a timeline?

EN_B: It doesn’t give a specific timeline, but it clearly says the adjustment is incomplete. Inventories are high, household credit demand is weak, and real estate investment is still declining.

EN_A: Are there any leading indicators we should watch to see if the market is really bottoming?

EN_B: Yes. The report mentions the sales manager confidence index and the secondary home asking price index. Both rebounded briefly in early July but then fell again. That suggests market sentiment is very fragile. Also, the sustainability of the tier-1 city recovery is still unproven.
