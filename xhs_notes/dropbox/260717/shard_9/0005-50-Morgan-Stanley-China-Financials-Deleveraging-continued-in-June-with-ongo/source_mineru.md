# Deleveraging continued in June with ongoing new household savings shift

## Key Takeaways

June TSF growth slowed further to 7.4% yoy, continuing the trend of moderating headline credit growth as policymakers prioritize quality over quantity.

Rmb1.76tn in RMB loans were added in June. Outstanding RMB loan growth fell to $5.3\%$ yoy, reflecting ongoing anti-involution efforts and reduced window guidance.

\- Household loans recovered modestly with seasonality after a net decline in May. New household loans were still $56\%$ lower yoy; new corporate loans also fell yoy.

Government bond issuance remained back-end loaded, with $46\%$ of the annual quota utilized in 1H26 vs. $55\%$ in 1H25. Nevertheless, it remains the key TSF driver.

Household deposit growth moderated to 7.1% yoy; non-FI deposit growth remained high at 28.6%. M1 and M2 growth decelerated, to 4% and 8% yoy, respectively.

Continued rationalization in credit growth: RMB loans showed some recovery at quarter-end in June thanks to seasonality. Household loans rebounded sequentially in June, but still contracted RMB367bn in 1H26, suggesting continued deleveraging. Rmb5.55tn in medium- to long-term corporate loans have been added YTD, down 23% yoy, reflecting continued emphasis on anti-involution and the removal of SME loan growth target.

We believe the slower loan growth could benefit loan and financial asset yields: Per the PBOC, new corporate loan yield was \~3% while new mortgage rate was \~3.1% in June, suggesting 5bps decline and \~4bps rebound vs. March 2026, respectively.

M1 growth dropped to 4% yoy in June from 5.5% yoy in May, likely owing to: 1) a high base in June 2025; and 2) corporate deposit shift to term – corporate demand deposit growth slowed to 8.8% in June from 10.6% in May; while term deposit growth accelerated to 2.9% from 2.2%.

We continue to believe that TSF growth of $\sim 6\%$ is a more desirable and sustainable medium-term level, and that the narrowing gap between TSF and GDP growth will benefit the financial system.
