# 2Q26 CIO Survey - An Improved Revision Risk Picture for 2026 IT Budgets

Our latest proprietary survey shows an uptick in IT budget expectations, while near-term revision risk inflected positively. Software remains the fastest growth category and is sequentially unchanged, but IT Services expectations edged lower.

Incrementally constructive for overall budgets, though IT Services remains more challenged. Despite elevated geopolitical uncertainty during the survey period (May-June), CIO spending intentions improved modestly and near-term revisions now skew slightly upwards. We view this as a moderately supportive signal for the broader IT spending environment. Software remains the fastest-growing major category, with expected 2026 growth of 4.1%, 30bps above reported 2025 growth but broadly unchanged from our prior survey. This points to a relatively resilient Software spending backdrop, although vendor-level intentions remain mixed and the results do not yet indicate a broad-based acceleration in application software demand. By contrast, IT Services continues to lag, with 2026 budget growth expectations also c. 20bps sequentially weaker than our last survey. Together with the increased perceived willingness to discount among tracked IT Services vendors, including Capgemini, this reinforces our cautious outlook for the sector.

## Key takeaways from our Software-specific survey:

\- (1) Vendor spend: Surveyed CIOs expect software spending to trend up for Microsoft (7.6% y/y growth vs 6.3% in 2Q25), ServiceNow (4.3% vs 3.9%), and Snowflake (5.3% vs 4.1%), and to be flat to down for other vendors.

\- (2) Spend on SaaS projects: CIOs expect spending on SaaS projects to increase by $+4.9\%$ in 2026 vs 2025, up from $3.6\%$ in 2025 vs 2024.

\- (3) ERP refresh cycle: 8% of CIOs anticipate upgrading their ERP/GL platform in 2026, 17% in 2027, and 35% in 2028 or later. At 22% of respondents, SAP S/4HANA is the most cited cloud-based SaaS ERP/GL platform to be used in the next upgrade cycle (up from 2Q25 at c. 19%). This is supportive of SAP's positioning, albeit timing remains weighted toward later years and the ERP net-spend outlook declined to 15% from 38% in 2Q25.

## Key takeaways from our IT Services-specific survey:

\- (1) 2026 budgets: While corporate sentiment with respect to IT Services improved vs our 4Q25 survey, we view the change as marginal and expect spending to remain broadly aligned with the subdued levels seen in 2025, as 62% of CIOs indicated. We also note that the vast majority of respondents are not seeing any delays on IT Services projects related to macro or geopolitical factors, with our survey reading here improved from 4Q25.

\- (2) Vendor discounts: The willingness of vendors to discount prices increased meaningfully on average vs our 4Q25 survey, with all the vendors we track in our survey reportedly more willing to offer pricing discounts (though with large variation between vendors). Net c. 41% of applicable CIOs (n=27 out of 60) perceive Capgemini as being more willing to offer discount pricing, an increase from our 4Q25 and 2Q25 surveys, at 18% and 17% respectively.

\- (3) Key service provider priorities: AI/ML/PA upgrade projects are top priority among surveyed CIOs, with 77% indicating that they will engage with an outside professional IT Services provider to help with the planning or execution. This result speaks to the AI bull case for IT Services – that they will be key to enabling integration/adoption of AI at the enterprise level.
