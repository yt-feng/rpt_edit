## RAS Inhibitor Combinations Have the Potential to Continue the PDAC Treatment Revolution

## Vopimetostat is Part of a Next Generation of PRMT5 Inhibitors Following Many Years of Development and Failure

Vopimetostat was developed as a second generation PRMT5i by Tango, following the discontinuation of TNG908 which was itself part of a new wave of MTA-cooperative

PRMT5is. Earlier PRMT5is worked through less selective mechanisms of action, such as substrate competition, which would inhibit PRMT5 but would not differentiate between healthy and tumor cells, creating a very limited therapeutic window. However, since MTAP-deleted tumors represent such a meaningful portion of all tumors (10%-15%), work continued to develop better, more selective, PRMT5 inhibitors and Tango's drugs are a result of this decades-long effort.

The ability of vopimetostat, TNG456 and other assets to bind and inhibit PRMT5 in the presence of high levels of MTA, which is a direct result of MTAP deletion, allows to differentiate between healthy and tumor cells, widely expanding the therapeutic window with a tolerable safety profile that opens up combination options, which has now become Tango's modus operandi.

## Combos and More Combos! Brain-Penetrant TNG456 Proof-of-Concept Coming This Year and a CDK4/6i Combo Soon After

Beyond the combination opportunity with RAS inhibitors in PDAC and NSCLC, Tango also has an ongoing Ph 1/2 trial in glioblastoma with brain-penetrant TNG456 and monotherapy data expected this year. Preclinical data has shown a lot of promise and the opportunity in this indication is meaningful with up to $45\%$ of cases originating from MTAP-deleted tumors. Tango has a collaboration agreement with Eli Lilly and if monotherapy data shows any promise (in an indication with very few treatment options), Tango has said they will initiate the second portion of the study which will combine TNG456 with abemaciclib, LLY's CDK4/6i. While it remains to be seen if this combination will play out in the clinic, the fact that MTAP deletion almost exclusively occurs in the setting of CDKN2A/B deletion, rendering cells sensitive to CDK4/6 inhibition, opens up another interesting combination approach for non-overlapping MOAs in another setting that is ripe for new options.

## Focusing on PRMT5 and Combinations: Tango is Hyper-focused with a Leaner Pipeline

Until earlier this year, Tango still had assets in its pipeline with targets beyond PRMT5, such as HBS1L (TNG961) and CoREST (TNG260), however, it recently deprioritized these assets and streamlined the pipeline to focus solely on the PRMT5i opportunity with vopimetostat and TNG456. This follows the promising monotherapy and combination data seen for vopimetostat and the large opportunities they present. Both TNG260 and TNG961 have been deprioritized and we see this as a smart move, allowing the company to execute on the combo opportunities with its PRMT5is by expanding clinical trials to multiple combinations (RAS, CDK4/6 and potentially MAT2A), collaborations (RVMD, ERAS and LLY) and to gain potential differentiation over competition in the space.

# Risks to Rating and Price Target

## Competitive Risks in the MTAP-Deletion Treatment Landscape

So far, vopimetostat has shown promising efficacy in multiple tumor histologies. However, monotherapy data has not been clearly superior to competition coming from much larger pharma companies. Since the data is still not mature enough to paint a clear differentiated picture, it remains to be seen if there is indeed a benefit to Tango's lead asset over competition using the same mechanism of action.

## Lead in Combo Setting Isn't Promised, With Competition Also Teaming Up

Combination data for vopimetostat with daraxonrasib is highly promising, however, BMS is also running a trial in combination with daraxonrasib in similar indications and while Tango has a slight lead, BMS is the larger player, and barring any monotherapy advantage for vopimetostat, with more resources and capacity to run trials in multiple indications it will be hard to differentiate vs navlimetostat.

## Cash Needs to Bring Multiple Assets to Center Stage

TNGX will need to raise additional cash to fund ongoing development across its preclinical and clinical assets. There are also non-dilutive avenues for the company, such as company partnerships or discovery collaborations, that could bring in additional funding. TNGX may also consider dilutive resources to fund its ongoing R&D efforts.

## Clinical and Regulatory Risks

There are inherent risks associated with clinical trial conduct and regulatory drug approval, which are not atypical for Biotechs at this stage in development. Factors such as trial enrollment, patient screening, and drug availability may delay milestones and our anticipated timeline, driving downside to our model assumptions.

## Company Description

Tango Therapeutics (TNGX), is a clinical-stage biotech company headquartered in Boston, Massachusetts, focused on the development of PRMT5 inhibitors for the treatment of MTAP-deleted tumors in NSCLC and PDAC patients. The company's lead asset, vopimetostat, has shown promising data both as a monotherapy and in combination with RAS inhibitors for the treatment of PDAC in Ph 1/2 trials. Tango is in the preparation stages of launching a Ph 3 trial for the combination in 1L PDAC. Additionally, TNG456, Tango's CNS-penetrating PRMT5 inhibitor, is currently in Ph 1/2 trials for the treatment of glioblastoma. Tango is collaborating with Revolution Medicines and Erasca on their pan-RAS inhibitors and with Eli Lilly on their CDK4/6 inhibitor. JPM acted as lead bookrunner on Tango's June 2026 follow-on offering.

# Company & Portfolio Overview

## Clinical-Stage Portfolio Focused on PRMT5 Inhibitors Vopimetostat and TNG456

Tango's clinical stage portfolio has been streamlined this year to focus solely on the PRMT5i opportunity led by vopimetostat and followed by CNS-penetrant TNG456. Vopimetostat is an MTA-cooperative PRMT5 inhibitor (PRMT5i), a next generation class of inhibitors designed to target only cells that have a deletion of the MTAP gene rendering them sensitive to inhibition, a phenomenon termed synthetic lethality.

Vopimetostat is being evaluated as a treatment for MTAP-deleted tumors, a phenotype that is present in 10%-15% of all tumors but is especially common in pancreatic (PDAC), lung (NSCLC) and glioblastoma (GBM) cancers. Vopimetostat has shown monotherapy efficacy in PDAC and other tumor types, with a strong safety profile, opening up the way to evaluation as part of combination therapies with other classes of drugs intended for the same indications, starting with RAS inhibitors such as Revolution Medicine's daraxonrasib and zoldonrasib and more recently Erasca's ERAS-0015. Following strong data with RVMD's daraxonrasib, Tango is planning to initiate a pivotal trial in 1L PDAC.

Moving on to Tango's second asset, also an MTAP-cooperative PRMT5i, TNG456 is designed to have similar potency and selectivity for MTAP-deleted cancer cells as vopimetostat but with much higher BBB crossing properties making it ideal for treating brain cancers such as glioblastoma for which MTAP-deletion prevalence is as high as $45\%$ . TNG456 is currently being evaluated as a monotherapy in a Ph 1/2 trial in MTAP-deleted glioblastoma and if it shows efficacy, cohorts combining TNG456 with Eli Lilly's CDK4/6 inhibitor abemaciclib will be initiated. # Vopimetostat and TNG456 - Next Generation MTA-Cooperative PRMT5 Inhibitors

Tango's MTA-cooperative PRMT5i program was built around the synthetic-lethality relationship, progressing from the earlier stage TNG908 to Vopimetostat (formerly TNG462) and CNS-penetrant TNG456. TNG908 was developed as a brain-penetrant MTA-cooperative PRMT5 inhibitor for MTAP-deleted cancers, including CNS tumors. Vopimetostat and TNG456 were designed as next-generation PRMT5is with enhanced potency, selectivity, and PK properties to improve target coverage and enable broader, deeper antitumor activity in MTAP-deleted solid tumors. While in preclinical work, TNG908 showed a 15x selectivity for MTAP deleted cells, vopimetostat showed 45x selectivity, and a more potent growth inhibition. For a while both vopimetostat and '908 were in clinical evaluation, however, in November 2024, following promising activity in NSCLC and PDAC, vopimetostat replaced '908 in the clinic for non-CNS tumors. At the same time, Tango unveiled TNG456, which has shown a 55x selectivity for MTAP deletion in preclinical work with high CNS penetration. In May 2025, the first patient was dosed in TNG456's Ph 1/2 trial in MTAP-deleted tumors with a focus on GBM.

## A Short Primer on MTAP Deletion and PRMT5 As a Target in Cancer

Methylthioadenosine phosphorylase (MTAP) is an enzyme encoded on chromosome 9p21, adjacent to the tumor suppressor gene CDKN2A and because of this genomic proximity, MTAP is almost exclusively co-deleted with CDKN2A in human cancers. MTAP loss is observed in a broad range of solid and hematologic malignancies and is generally estimated to occur in approximately 10%–15% of all human cancers. Most importantly, MTAP deletion occurs in \~22% of PDAC, \~13% of NSCLC and \~45% of GBM.

## The Roles of MTAP and PRMT5 in the Cell

MTAP normally participates in the methionine salvage pathway by metabolizing 5'-methylthioadenosine (MTA) back into methionine. Normal cells do not accumulate high levels of MTA, and PRMT5 can continue using the methyl-donor SAM (S-adenosylmethionine) to perform its normal function. PRMT5 catalyzes symmetric

dimethylation of arginine residues on histones and non-histone proteins and regulates processes central to tumor cell viability, including transcription, chromatin remodeling, RNA splicing, signal transduction, ribosome biology, and cell-cycle progression. When MTAP is deleted, MTA accumulates intracellularly and partially inhibits protein arginine methyltransferase 5 (PRMT5) and so the cell is more reliant on exogenous methionine.

## The MTA-Cooperative MoA of Most Current PRMT5is

PRMT5 is an essential protein, and so non-selective first-generation PRMT5 inhibitors (PRMT5i) had limiting therapeutic windows with associated hematopoietic toxicity. However, when MTAP is deleted, MTA accumulates, forms a complex with PRMT5 and partially inhibits it. A drug that can bind to PRMT5 only with MTA could lead to cell death, but only in cells with high MTA levels, leading to much better selectivity over healthy cells. In cells with MTAP deletion, PRMT5 is already partially inhibited due to the elevated MTA levels, opening up the possibility of synthetic lethality and sparing of wild-type cells through the development of MTA-cooperative PRMT5 inhibitors, a newer class designed to exploit MTA accumulation. Vopimetostat, and almost all current competitors are MTA-cooperative inhibitors.

## What are the Highest Value Indications for Vopimetostat?

PDAC - Highest Prevalence and a Real Opportunity to Take Part in a Revolution
Despite recent advances in targeting KRAS for PDAC (see our work on RVMD), there still exists a significant need to improve outcomes for patients with one of the most deadly cancers. There are an estimated \~66K new pancreatic cancer cases annually in the US, and PDAC comprises at least 90% of pancreatic cancers, followed by neuroendocrine tumors, according to the American Cancer Society. Despite the recent Ph 3 success for daraxonrasib, doubling OS in 2L PDAC and as we await survival data from 1L trials, we need to remember that ORR in this patient population was still only \~30% leaving much room for improvement. While PDAC isn’t one of the most common cancer types, it is one of the leading causes of cancer death in the U.S. The lethality of PDAC is multifactorial but mostly based on the inability to detect the disease in early stages before it has metastasized. This late stage diagnosis leads to a 5-year survival rate of 3% within those with metastatic disease (and this is after doubling in the past decade, but before RAS inhibitors).

Despite the recent success of daraxonrasib, which is awaiting approval, treatment options for PDAC are fairly limited and not effective: only $\sim 20\%$ of PDAC patients are eligible for surgery due to the advanced stage of the disease at diagnosis. Aside from late diagnosis, there are also risks related to tumor proximity to major vasculature and the lasting impact of removing the pancreas. Even in resection-amenable patients, $75\%$ will develop recurrence within 2 years. Thus, clinical care for metastatic PDAC relies on systemic chemo. One of the key chemo regimens is FOLFIRINOX (FFX; a combo of 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin) with the second being a combo of gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel (GnP). For progressors, 2L regimens include irinotecan with 5-FU. Following the success of RVMD in the space, there are now dozens of new KRAS-mutant and multi/pan-RAS targeting assets in the clinic with multiple Ph 3 trials assessing their efficacy in 1L PDAC in combination with or without chemotherapy.

Initially, Tango was targeting the MTAP-deleted PDAC population and was planning on starting a Ph 3 trial in 2L MTAP-deleted PDAC, which represents 19%-32% of the PDAC population, according to sources. However, following the recent success of the combo data with multi-RAS inhibitor daraxonrasib, focus is now shifting to starting a Ph 3 pivotal trial in 1L PDAC MTAP-deleted patients in combination with daraxonrasib. In PDAC, there is no need to focus on KRAS mutational status as >90% of cases are KRAS mutated and daraxonrasib is active in WT KRAS as well. Additionally, genomic landscape work has shown a co-mutation rate of \~94% in PDAC. Thus, MTAP-deletion is the only limiting inclusion factor.

## NSCLC: The Blueprint for Targeted Therapy

Lung cancer is the second most common type of cancer diagnosed worldwide. The classification of lung cancer has undergone major changes over the years, as evidenced by the increased use of sequencing to classify cancer histology and a shift towards targeted therapy guided by driver mutation characterization. The two main types of lung cancer are non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and small cell lung cancer (SCLC). NSCLC is the most common form, accounting for 80% to 85% of all lung cases, and is further sub-classified into three subtypes: (1) adenocarcinoma, (2) squamous cell carcinoma and (3) large cell carcinoma, which are differentiated based on the tissue of origin.

Adenocarcinoma is the most common form, accounting for $\sim$ 40% of cases. In adenocarcinoma, the tumor forms in the outer region of the lung where mucus-producing cells reside. In squamous cell carcinoma, which makes up $\sim$ 25%-30% of cases, the cancer forms in the central part of the airway, in the lining of the bronchi. Squamous cell carcinoma is more common in older adults and smokers. Unlike the other two, large cell carcinoma is much rarer, less centralized, and can be found anywhere in the lungs.

The American Cancer Society estimates the annual incidence of lung cancer cases to be $\sim 230\mathrm{K}$ . The five-year survival rate for all NSCLC is $\sim 28\%$ , but it further worsens as patients enter the advanced stages. Once a tumor is spotted in the lungs, a tissue biopsy is conducted to determine the type of cancer and its stage, while next-generation sequencing is used to determine its genetic signature and the presence of oncogenic driver mutations, which is especially important for Tango's strategy as it will be targeting only tumors with MTAP deletion.

NSCLC treatment is dependent on the mutational characteristics of the tumor. There are multiple types of approved targeted therapies for NSCLC, including inhibitors targeting EGFR, ALK and ROS1 rearrangements, KRAS G12C, MET exon 14, BRAF V600E, RET fusions, HER2 and more being added annually. Absent actionable mutations, 1L treatment is based on the KEYNOTE-189 protocol including Pt-based chemotherapy with immune checkpoint blockade and is dependent on PD-L1 expression levels.

In a large molecular landscape profiling study of NSCLC, MTAP deletion was present in $\sim$ 13% of cases out of almost 30K samples. Not surprisingly, as they are adjacent on the 9p21 chromosome, the most common co-mutations were CDKN2A and CDKN2B with 98% and 95%, respectively, and TP53 with 63%. Next up were all types of KRAS mutations with 29%. Taken together, co-occurrence of a KRAS mutation with an MTAP-deletion occurs in $\sim$ 4% of NSCLC cases (Kumar et al.). While seemingly low, with $\sim$ 190K cases of NSCLC a year in the US, this still amounts to $\sim$ 25K NSCLC MTAP-deletion cases a year and $\sim$ 7K with a KRAS co-mutation or $\sim$ 17K/5K stage III/IV cases.

# The Case for Co-Targeting PRMT5 with Other Targets

MTAP-loss occurs almost exclusively as part of 9p21 chromosomal loss with co-deletions of CDKN2A and CDKN2B. Prior genomic studies, and even commercial panels, have not always tested specifically for MTAP-loss, making it challenging to perform retrospective studies on the clinical outcomes of patients with tumors harboring MTAP-loss. Testing for CDKN2A and CDKN2B is nearly ubiquitous, though, and this finding allows us to extrapolate the presence of MTAP-loss or perform dedicated testing for MTAP-loss in select scenarios.

Although current MTAP-loss clinical trials are investigating MAT2A or PRMT5 inhibitors as monotherapy, combination targeted therapy may ultimately prove more efficacious.

Building on the improved safety profile seen with the MTA-cooperative PRMT5is, combining them with other targeted cancer therapeutics makes sense, especially since MTAP-deleted tumors often harbor additional actionable oncogenic drivers that can be inhibited in parallel, opening up the possibility to improve on the monotherapy data seen with vopimetostat and competitors.

An especially strong rationale exists for combining KRASi with PRMT5i in MTAP deleted PDAC, where $>90\%$ of all tumors are KRAS mutation-driven, which is believed to be the founding oncogenic event, while CDKN2A/MTAP deletion is another frequent early alteration. PRMT5 inhibition and KRAS inhibition largely affect distinct signaling networks, but their downstream consequences converge on essential tumor processes such as MYC signaling, cell-cycle control, DNA and RNA biology, translation, interferon signaling, and apoptosis. Because these mechanisms are at least partly nonoverlapping, combinations may deepen responses, delay resistance, and produce more durable tumor regressions than single-agent therapy, while maintaining tolerability if each component has an adequate therapeutic index.

Preclinical studies with PRMT5 inhibitors combined with mutant-selective RAS inhibitors showed greater growth suppression, impaired cell division, tumor regression, and delayed tumor outgrowth compared with either agent alone.

Tango has presented preclinical work supporting the combination of vopimetostat with zoldonrasib (RVMD), showing more durable tumor regression, as well as with the EGFRi osimertinib (Tagrisso).

## A Quick Primer on RAS

RAS serves as a central node in the RAS/RAF/MAPK pathway, which holds a key role in regulating cell behavior by relaying extracellular signals to the nucleus to control cell growth, proliferation, and differentiation. Dysregulation of the pathway through gain-of-function mutations in the cascade is a frequent driver of cancer formation.

The RAS family of proteins, including KRAS, NRAS and HRAS, are small GTPases that function as switches, alternating between GDP-bound (OFF) and GTP-bound (ON) states. Activation can be triggered by various stimuli. In the normal state, it is recruited to the plasma membrane following stimulation by growth factors, which leads GEF to exchange GDP for GTP turning the switch ON. In the ON state, RAS interacts with a diverse set of effectors, including the RAF proteins and PI3K, both of which are central regulators of cell functions including growth, metabolism, motility, and proliferation

(Figure 7). For more information, see our initiation on RVMD (here).

## Why is the dara with Daraxonrasib better than Zoldonrasib

One question that came up from the data was about the worse performance for the zoldonrasib combo, considering it has generally shown similar efficacy in the G12D population as a monotherapy. While daraxonrasib inhibits mutant RAS in many subtypes as well as the WT protein, zoldonrasib only targets the G12D allele (which was a prerequisite to enter the trial). The goal of the combo is to target two separate pathways that cancer cells depend on. Zoldonrasib has very little activity against WT RAS and preclinical data supports the idea that targeting the mutant allele can lead to compensatory upregulation of WT or other mutant forms of RAS. This could potentially explain the higher efficacy seen with dara in the combo. Another explanation by management was that the ECOG status for the G12D cohort was worse than that for the dara population (see table above).

# TNG456 - Tango's CNS-Penetrant PRMT5i

## Why Do We Need a CNS-Penetrant PRMT5i?

Following their first generation TNG908, Tango developed two additional assets with much better potency – vopimetostat and TNG456. While vopimetostat is slightly more potent than TNG456, it did not achieve CNS exposure, TNG908 did, but was much less potent and less selective for MTAP deletion than either of the newer assets.

Preclinical work by Tango showed that to achieve maximum monotherapy efficacy could require exposure levels so high they could also inhibit WT PRMT5 limiting the therapeutic window. As glioblastoma (GBM) is one of the cancer types with high MTAP deletion prevalence, it made sense to develop a drug with adequate brain exposure. To achieve proper brain penetration, the ratio of unbound brain-to-plasma $(\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{p,uu}})$ is a key parameter to predict the levels of peripheral exposure needed to achieve target brain exposures and would ideally be close to 1 to not affect the therapeutic window. For TNG908 this ratio was predicted to be 0.9 but the 15x selectivity vs WT was assumed to not be high enough and so it wouldn't be possible to achieve adequate CNS concentrations. Based on this, Tango discontinued the TNG908 trial in November 2024.

Tango aimed to generate a molecule that can achieve brain exposure higher than the MTAP-deleted $GI_{90}$ but lower than the WT $GI_{50}$ in the periphery to reduce on-target PRMT5 tox in non-tumor cells.

The preclinical data described below show that TNG456 has high potential to be brain penetrant in humans based on preclinical in vitro and in vivo data. Based on this, in May 2025, Tango started enrolling patients in the Ph 1/2 clinical trial for TNG456 as a monotherapy. The trial is currently in the dose escalation phase and assuming favorable monotherapy data, a combination with abemaciclib (Eli Lilly's CDK4/6i aka Verzenio) will be initiated. An update has been guided to 2H26.

## Glioblastoma Background

Glioblastoma, considered the most aggressive primary brain tumor in adults, has an incidence of $\sim 12\mathrm{K}$ cases a year (3.2/100K) in the US or $\sim 15\%$ of all primary brain and CNS tumors and $45\%$ of malignant brain tumors. The WHO classifies GBM as IDH WT Grade 4 astrocytoma. GBM originates from glial cells and is characterized by high genomic instability leading to a highly heterogeneous presentation. Prognosis remains poor with an OS of 14-16 months following SoC and a 5 year survival rate of $< 10\%$ .

Current SoC for GBM involves surgical resection followed by radiotherapy and chemotherapy with temozolomide (TMZ), an oral alkylating agent. Complete surgical removal is difficult due to tumor infiltration and anatomical proximity to important brain regions. TMZ efficacy is limited by tumor resistance and systemic tox including myelosuppression.

While we could not find any large databases describing MTAP deletion prevalence in GBM, in one study, a sample of 423 cases showed 45.6% had MTAP deletion, while in another there were 33.5% MTAP deletion samples out of 167 GBMs.

In addition to Tango, preclinical work with navlimetostat (previously MRTX-1719) showed strong anti-tumor activity in MTAP-deleted glioma cells. This proved more difficult in vivo as the presence of non-malignant, MTAP-WT cells in the tumor microenvironment can metabolize extracellular MTA, reducing its local concentration and reducing the inhibition it applies to PRMT5.

## Preclinical Data Supporting TNG456 Development

In a recent Journal of Medicinal Chemistry paper from May 2026 (link), Tango describes the development of TNG456. TNG456 showed no off-target activity against a panel of 40 methyltransferases other than PRMT5 and no other potential off-target binding against 78 known off-target assays. In cell viability assays with cell pairs that were either MTAP WT or MTAP-deleted, TNG456 showed an average of 55x selectivity for the MTAP-deleted cells.

Overall, these assays support TNG456 monotherapy activity in MTAP-deleted tumors. As discussed earlier, since MTAP deletion is usually accompanied by CDKN2A deletion, these cancers may have increased CDK4/6 activity. The CDKN2A locus on chromosome 9p21 encodes two tumor suppressors, p16INK4a and p14ARF. Normally, p16INK4a sequesters CDK4 and CDK6, keeping pRb in an active, hypophosphorylated state. In the absence of p16INK4a, CDK4 and CDK6 bind to cyclin D and phosphorylate Rb which then releases E2F promoting the G1-to-S phase transition of the cell cycle.

This means that cells with the 9p21 deletion exhibit both MTAP deletion, rendering them sensitive to PRMT5 inhibition, and have a reliance on CDK4/6 making them sensitive to its inhibition. There are multiple approved CDK4/6 inhibitors, including palbociclib, ribociclib and abemaciclib. Since these are two discrete susceptibility pathways, similar to RAS, it makes sense that targeting both would be synergistic.

In summary, preclinical data shows that TNG456 has:

1. MTAP-null $GI_{50}=20$ nM with 55-fold selectivity vs MTAP WT cells in the HAP1 cell line.

2. $K_{p.uu}$ ranging from 0.5 to 1.0 across preclinical species supporting its potential to achieve therapeutically relevant CNS exposure in patients.

3. A favorable CYP3A4 profile with no preclinical signals of CYP3A4 inhibition, suggesting a very low risk of clinical CYP3A4 mediated DDIs.

4. A preclinical DMPK profile predicting human exposure sufficient to maintain MTAP-null $GI_{90}$ coverage in the brain at trough. It had an $IC_{50}$ of 9.4 $\mu$ M representing nearly a 100-fold safety margin relative to the targeted MTAP-null $GI_{90}$ of 100 nM.

# Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks

Tango Therapeutics (Overweight; Price Target: \$46.00)

## Investment Thesis

We have an Overweight rating on TNGX shares. We see TNGX set up well following the strong combinatorial data for its lead PRMT5 inhibitor vopimetostat with RAS inhibitors in 2L+ PDAC which supports its planned Ph 3 clinical trial plans in 1L PDAC. While data is early, the unprecedented efficacy in such a difficult indication supports its penetration into the large portion of PDAC patients with MTAP-deletion. Beyond vopimetostat, Tango's CNS-penetrant PRMT5 inhibitor, TNG456, is currently in Ph 1/2 for the very difficult to treat glioblastoma and provides a meaningful virtual “call option” that we think is not reflected in shares today.

## Valuation

Our Dec 2026 price target of \$46 is based on our DCF SOTP calculation. We assume standalone contributions from vopimetostat in 1L PDAC and 2L NSCLC as well as TNG456 contributions in GBM. Our \$46 price target consists of \$39/share for vopimetostat in 1L PDAC, \~\$1/share for vopimetostat in 2L NSCLC, \$0.4/share for TNG456 in GBM and the remainder for corporate expenses/pipeline and cash.

## Risks to Rating and Price Target

Key risks to our rating and price target include 1) the inability to replicate vopimetostat's combo efficacy with daraxonrasib in a larger Ph 3 trial, 2) inability to differentiate vs competitor PRMT5is from larger established pharma, 3) clinical and regulatory risks, and 4) cash needs to bring multiple assets forward.
