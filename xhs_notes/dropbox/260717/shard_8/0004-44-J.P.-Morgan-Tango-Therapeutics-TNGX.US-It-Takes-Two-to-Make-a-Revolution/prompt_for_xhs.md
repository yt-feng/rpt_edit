## Vopimetostat is Part of a Next Generation of PRMT5 Inhibitors Following Many Years of Development and Failure

Vopimetostat was developed as a second generation PRMT5i by Tango, following the discontinuation of TNG908 which was itself part of a new wave of MTA-cooperative

PRMT5is. Earlier PRMT5is worked through less selective mechanisms of action, such as substrate competition, which would inhibit PRMT5 but would not differentiate between healthy and tumor cells, creating a very limited therapeutic window. However, since MTAP-deleted tumors represent such a meaningful portion of all tumors (10%-15%), work continued to develop better, more selective, PRMT5 inhibitors and Tango's drugs are a result of this decades-long effort.

The ability of vopimetostat, TNG456 and other assets to bind and inhibit PRMT5 in the presence of high levels of MTA, which is a direct result of MTAP deletion, allows to differentiate between healthy and tumor cells, widely expanding the therapeutic window with a tolerable safety profile that opens up combination options, which has now become Tango's modus operandi.
