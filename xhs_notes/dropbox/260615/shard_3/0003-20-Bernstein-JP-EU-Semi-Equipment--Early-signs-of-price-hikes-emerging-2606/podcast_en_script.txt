EN_A: So, semiconductor equipment stocks have been lagging lately. Is that about to change?
EN_B: That’s exactly what a new report from a global investment bank is suggesting. They think semi equipment could outperform again.
EN_A: What’s the main argument? Why the sudden shift?
EN_B: Well, the report says the recent rally in commodity tech like memory and analog chips has led to higher capital spending and capacity expansion. And that directly benefits equipment makers. But there’s another angle: equipment companies can raise their own prices.
EN_A: Raise prices? That’s new. What’s triggering that?
EN_B: The report points to Japanese equipment makers. Most of them price in yen, which has weakened about 30% against the dollar over the past three years. So they have a strong case for price adjustments. But until recently, they were hesitant to do so.
EN_A: What changed?
EN_B: Recent comments from Tokyo Electron and Screen suggest a strategy shift. And there’s news that SK Hynix has already received requests for a 3 to 4 percent price hike.
EN_A: So it’s happening. How does Tokyo Electron plan to execute this?
EN_B: They’ve made margin improvement a top priority. The report outlines a three-step approach. First, they’ll charge more for rush orders from customers, which they didn’t do before. Second, they’ll negotiate surcharges for inflation, materials, and labor. Third, when they launch new models, they’ll push for higher prices based on better technology and new features.
EN_A: And what’s the target?
EN_B: They want gross margins above 50 percent and operating margins closer to 35 percent. The street consensus for fiscal year 2029 is only 48 percent gross margin and 30 percent operating margin. So if they hit those targets, it’s a huge upside.
EN_A: What about Screen? Are they doing something similar?
EN_B: Yes, they have a two-stage plan. First, they’ve already gotten customers to accept inflation-related price increases. Now they’re negotiating for additional value from new models. Their long-term goal is 30 percent operating margin, versus the consensus of 27 percent.
EN_A: So the Japanese front-end equipment makers are the big story here. What about other players?
EN_B: The report expects Kokusai to follow suit, since they also price in yen. But companies like DISCO, Lasertec, and Advantest use a mix of dollar and yen pricing and have already seen big margin expansions. So the benefit from further price hikes might be smaller.
EN_A: And what about non-Japanese companies like ASML?
EN_B: ASML and Besi benefit more from product upgrades. For example, ASML’s new EUV machines could see a 60 percent price increase, pushing gross margins well above 60 percent. But the report also notes that ASML could charge premiums for faster delivery given the tight supply.
EN_A: Sounds like the whole equipment sector is in a sweet spot.
EN_B: The report suggests Japanese front-end names could outperform back-end ones in the near term. But there are still open questions.
EN_A: Like what?
EN_B: For example, how much of these price hikes will actually stick? And if the economy slows down, can they sustain the pricing power? The full report dives deeper into these risks and scenarios.
