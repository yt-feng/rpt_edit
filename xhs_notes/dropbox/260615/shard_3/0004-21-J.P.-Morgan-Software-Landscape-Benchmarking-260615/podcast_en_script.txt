EN_A: So I came across this big report on software valuations, data as of June 2026. What is it really trying to tell us?
EN_B: The core question is simple. After the AI hype settled down, have software stocks returned to reasonable valuations? The report compares 101 companies across a wide range of metrics.
EN_A: 101 companies, that is a solid dataset. So what is the headline? Are software stocks cheap or expensive right now?
EN_B: The overall average EV to Sales is about 5.7 times. That is much lower than the peak a couple of years ago when it was often above 10 times. But the real story is in the breakdown.
EN_A: Breakdown? You mean between large caps and small caps?
EN_B: Exactly, and the gap is huge. Large caps, those with market caps over 20 billion, trade at 10.3 times sales. Small caps, under 3 billion, trade at just 1.9 times.
EN_A: 1.9 versus 10.3, that is a massive gap. What does that tell us?
EN_B: It tells us the market is still willing to pay a big premium for scale and stability. But it is very picky about smaller names. The report also breaks it down by growth rate.
EN_A: Growth rate? I assume faster growing companies get higher multiples?
EN_B: Yes, companies growing over 20 percent trade at 13.7 times sales. But here is the twist. Those same high growth names have a free cash flow margin of only 6 percent. And next year it is projected to turn negative.
EN_A: Wait, the fastest growing companies have negative cash flow? That sounds contradictory.
EN_B: That is one of the most interesting findings in the report. High growth often comes with high spending, especially on stock based compensation. The report uses a metric called EV to FCF minus SBC.
EN_A: That strips out stock based compensation to show real cash earnings power?
EN_B: Correct. For high growth companies, that multiple is 34.7 times in 2026 and gets even higher in 2027. So the market is pricing in a lot of future profitability that has not materialized yet.
EN_A: What about security software? That is a hot area right now.
EN_B: Security software is a separate segment with 23 companies. Its average EV to Sales is 9.2 times, well above the overall average. And gross margins are 80 percent, the highest of any segment.
EN_A: 80 percent gross margins are impressive. So are these stocks fairly valued?
EN_B: It depends on how you define fair. Security software companies have a free cash flow margin of only 15 percent. That is lower than mid caps at 25 percent. So while revenue growth is solid, cash conversion is not the best.
EN_A: What are some questions the report does not fully answer?
EN_B: A big one is whether the negative cash flow trend in high growth names is temporary or a structural issue. The report does not give a clear conclusion on that.
EN_A: So that is something to watch.
EN_B: Yes. Another open question is about specific stock ratings. The report lists target prices and ratings for many companies, but it does not explain why some are rated overweight and some underweight. For example, there is one company with a market cap over 200 billion that is rated underweight.
EN_A: That sounds like a story worth digging into. I guess the full report has more details.
EN_B: Exactly. If you want to know which company that is and the reasoning behind it, I would recommend reading the full report. It goes much deeper into individual stock analysis.
