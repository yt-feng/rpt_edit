## The Fixing Model Is Not a Prediction; It Is a Policy Communication Tool

The USD/CNY fixing model is often misunderstood as a forecasting tool. In reality, it is a transparency mechanism that allows market participants to reverse-engineer the PBOC's thinking. The model takes overnight movements in the dollar index, cross-currency pairs, and other inputs to produce a "fair value" fix. The difference between this model output and the actual fix reveals the counter-cyclical factor, which is the PBOC's discretionary adjustment.

On June 24, the model projection without counter-cyclical factor was 6.7910, while the actual fix with the factor was 6.8025. This 115-pip gap is the policy signal. It tells us that the PBOC is choosing to fix the renminbi slightly weaker than the pure market model would suggest. This is not a sign of weakness. It is a deliberate decision to smooth the pace of appreciation and to prevent market expectations from becoming too one-sided.

The significance of this gap becomes clearer when compared to the previous fix. The model projection dropped by 261 pips from the prior reading, but the actual fix dropped by only 146 pips. The PBOC is allowing appreciation, but at a controlled pace. This is consistent with a central bank that wants to avoid triggering speculative flows while still allowing the currency to reflect improving fundamentals.

The critical insight for investors is that the counter-cyclical factor is not a static adjustment. It changes based on the PBOC's assessment of market conditions and policy priorities. When the factor is large, the PBOC is signaling discomfort with the current pace of movement. When it shrinks, the PBOC is signaling greater tolerance for market-driven moves. The current data suggests a moderate but deliberate intervention to slow appreciation, which implies that the PBOC sees value in maintaining a weaker currency for now.

## The Discrepancy Between Model Projection and Actual Fix Is the Most Important Data Point

The model's output is useful, but the real insight comes from comparing the model projection to the actual fix. The model without counter-cyclical factor projects a fix of 6.7910, but the actual fix is 6.8025. This 115-pip difference represents the PBOC's discretionary intervention. Understanding why this gap exists and how it changes over time is the key to anticipating future fixing decisions.

The report identifies the top four weighted overnight contributions to the projected change. These inputs include movements in the dollar index, the euro, the yen, and other regional currencies. When the model projection diverges from the actual fix, it means the PBOC is overriding one or more of these inputs. This override is the counter-cyclical factor, and it is the primary tool the PBOC uses to manage expectations.

The recent model errors, as shown in the report, provide additional context. If the opposite is true, the PBOC is leaning against appreciation.

The current data suggests a moderate leaning against appreciation. The PBOC is allowing the currency to strengthen, but it is applying a counter-cyclical factor to slow the pace. This is consistent with a central bank that wants to avoid creating a one-way bet for speculators. For investors, the key question is whether this pattern will continue. If the political calendar pushes the PBOC to allow faster appreciation, the counter-cyclical factor will shrink. If the economic calendar pushes the PBOC to maintain a weaker currency, the factor will grow. Monitoring the gap between the model projection and the actual fix is the most direct way to track the PBOC's evolving strategy.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Interaction Between the Fix and Capital Flows

The report provides a detailed analysis of the fixing model, but it leaves several important questions unanswered. The most critical of these is the interaction between the fix and capital flows. The fix sets the daily trading band, but it does not determine the actual spot rate. The spot rate can trade within a band of plus or minus 2% from the fix. This means the PBOC's control over the spot rate is indirect, and capital flows can push the spot rate to the edges of the band.

The report does not address how the PBOC manages the spot rate when it approaches the edges of the band. Does the PBOC intervene in the spot market to reinforce the signal from the fix? Or does it allow the spot rate to test the band limits as a way of gauging market pressure? The answer to this question is crucial for understanding the effectiveness of the fixing strategy.

Another unanswered question is the role of the offshore renminbi market. The CNH market in Hong Kong trades freely and often diverges from the onshore CNY rate. The fixing model only applies to the onshore market, but the offshore rate can influence expectations and create arbitrage opportunities. The report does not explain how the PBOC coordinates its fixing strategy with its interventions in the offshore market.

Finally, the report does not address the impact of the fixing strategy on China's foreign exchange reserves. A sustained intervention to slow appreciation would require the PBOC to accumulate dollars, which increases reserve levels. A sustained intervention to slow depreciation would require the PBOC to sell dollars, which depletes reserves. The fixing model provides no information about the reserve implications of the current strategy.

These unanswered questions are not weaknesses in the report. They are natural boundaries of any analytical framework. The fixing model is a powerful tool, but it is only one piece of a larger puzzle. Investors who want to fully understand the PBOC's currency strategy need to integrate the fixing model with analysis of capital flows, offshore markets, and reserve management.

## A Decision Framework for Interpreting the Fixing Model

For investors who want to use the fixing model as a decision-making tool, the following framework provides a structured approach. This framework translates the model's technical output into actionable insights.

First, track the direction of the model projection. If the model projection is moving lower, the PBOC's fair value estimate is appreciating. If it is moving higher, the fair value estimate is depreciating. This gives you the baseline expectation.

Second, measure the gap between the model projection and the actual fix. This gap is the counter-cyclical factor. A large gap indicates strong policy intervention. A small gap indicates that the PBOC is allowing market forces to dominate. The direction of the gap tells you whether the PBOC is leaning for or against the market.

Third, compare the current gap to historical gaps. If the current gap is larger than the historical average, the PBOC is unusually active. If it is smaller, the PBOC is unusually passive. This comparison helps you assess whether the current strategy is a departure from the norm.

Fourth, correlate the gap with the political calendar. If a major event is approaching, the PBOC may increase or decrease the gap to send a signal. For example, a shrinking gap before the APEC summit would signal confidence. A widening gap before President Xi's visit to Washington would signal caution.

Fifth, assess the sustainability of the gap. A large and persistent gap requires the PBOC to intervene in the spot market to defend the fix. If the PBOC is unwilling or unable to sustain this intervention, the gap will eventually collapse. The model's error analysis, as shown in the report, provides clues about whether the PBOC is struggling to maintain its desired fix.

This framework does not predict the fix. It helps you interpret the fix as a policy signal. The PBOC is not trying to hide its strategy. It is communicating through the fixing mechanism. The challenge for investors is learning to read the message.
