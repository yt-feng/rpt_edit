# China's MDI Export Surge in May Signals a Structural Shift in Global Chemical Trade, Not Just a Cyclical Rebound

The global chemical industry has been waiting for a clear signal that demand is recovering and that trade patterns are normalizing after the disruptions of 2025. The May 2026 data on China's MDI exports provides that signal, but not in the way most investors expect. The headline number is striking: China exported 101,000 tonnes of MDI in May 2026, a 49% increase year-over-year compared to the 68,000 tonnes exported in May 2025. This is not merely a bounce from a depressed base. The composition of these exports reveals a fundamental reordering of supply chains that will reshape competitive dynamics for years to come.

The argument of this article is straightforward: the May 2026 data is not a one-month anomaly but the leading edge of a structural shift in which Chinese MDI capacity is permanently displacing higher-cost production in Europe and other regions. The data shows that year-to-date 2026 net exports of 258,000 tonnes are already 55,000 tonnes higher than the same period in 2025, and that is after a disastrous 2025 in which full-year net exports collapsed by 496,000 tonnes year-over-year. The recovery is real, but it is also selective. The destinations tell the story.

This matters now because the chemical industry is at an inflection point. The 2025 collapse in Chinese MDI exports was widely attributed to a global demand slowdown and trade frictions. The May 2026 data forces a reassessment. If Chinese producers are regaining volume so quickly, and at prices that are rising sharply, then the narrative must shift from demand weakness to supply restructuring. The implications for producers, consumers, and investors are profound, and the data from this single month provides a lens through which to view the entire industry's trajectory.

The following analysis unpacks what the May 2026 data really means, why the price dynamics are as important as the volume numbers, and what questions remain unanswered. It concludes with a decision framework for readers who need to act on these signals, not just observe them.

## The European Market Is Absorbing Chinese MDI at an Accelerating Pace, Confirming a Structural Supply Gap

The most important single data point in the May 2026 report is the 63% year-to-date increase in Chinese MDI exports to the European Union. Year-to-date 2026 shipments to the EU-27 stand at 82,000 tonnes, compared to 50,000 tonnes in the same period of 2025. This is not a recovery to 2024 levels of 100,000 tonnes for the full year, but the trajectory is unmistakable. Europe is importing Chinese MDI at a rate that, if sustained, would surpass 2024's total within the next few months.

The strategic significance of this shift cannot be overstated. Europe has historically been a net exporter of MDI, with major production capacity owned by regional players. The 2025 data showed a dramatic reduction in Chinese exports to Europe, which many interpreted as a sign of European self-sufficiency or demand destruction. The 2026 data tells a different story. European buyers are returning to Chinese suppliers, and they are doing so at prices that are rising rapidly. The average Chinese export price in May 2026 was $2,193 per tonne, up 25% year-over-year and 13% month-over-month. This is not a distressed sale. Chinese producers are commanding higher prices even as they increase volumes to Europe.

The most logical explanation is that European MDI production capacity is under structural pressure. High energy costs, regulatory burdens, and aging plant infrastructure are making it increasingly difficult for European producers to compete on both cost and reliability. Chinese producers, by contrast, benefit from newer plants, lower energy costs, and a scale that allows them to serve multiple markets simultaneously. The European market is not choosing Chinese MDI because it is cheap; it is choosing it because domestic supply is insufficient or uneconomical.

This pattern is reinforced by the data on exports to Turkey, which grew 68% year-to-date from 28,000 tonnes to 47,000 tonnes. Turkey serves as both a direct market and a transit hub for goods moving into Europe and the Middle East. The surge in Turkish imports suggests that Chinese MDI is penetrating the broader European and Mediterranean supply chain through multiple channels, not just direct shipments to EU ports.

## The Collapse in 2025 Exports Was a Supply-Side Event, Not a Demand-Side Crisis, and the Recovery Confirms That Thesis

The 2025 data was alarming for anyone tracking the MDI market. Full-year exports of 805,000 tonnes represented a 33% decline from 2024's 1,204,000 tonnes. Exports to the United States fell 83%. Exports to Russia and the Netherlands also dropped sharply. The immediate reaction from many market participants was to blame a global demand downturn. The May 2026 data makes that interpretation difficult to sustain.

If the 2025 collapse had been driven by weak demand, then the recovery in 2026 would be broad-based, with all major destinations showing improvement. That is not what the data shows. The recovery is concentrated in Europe, Turkey, and a handful of Asian markets such as India and Vietnam. The United States, Russia, and the Netherlands remain far below their 2024 levels. This pattern is consistent with a supply-side disruption in 2025 that was region-specific, not global.

What caused the 2025 disruption? The report does not specify, but the data points to a combination of factors: logistical bottlenecks, trade policy uncertainty, and perhaps most importantly, a strategic decision by Chinese producers to allocate capacity away from certain markets. The 83% drop in exports to the United States, for example, is too large to be explained by demand alone. It suggests that Chinese producers either could not ship to the US or chose not to, possibly due to tariff exposure or a shift in corporate strategy.

The recovery in 2026, therefore, is not a sign that global demand is surging. It is a sign that the supply-side constraints of 2025 have been resolved for certain markets, and that Chinese producers are now aggressively recapturing the European market share they lost or ceded. This is a competitive move, not a cyclical one.

## The Price Explosion in Chinese Import Data Reveals a Two-Tier Market That Is Unsustainable

The price data in the report contains a puzzle that demands explanation. Chinese export prices for MDI rose 25% year-over-year to $2,193 per tonne in May 2026. That is a strong increase, but it is dwarfed by the movement in import prices. The Chinese import price for MDI in May 2026 was $2,979 per tonne, a staggering 115% increase month-over-month from $1,906 in April 2026, and a 115% increase year-over-year from $1,385 in May 2025.

This divergence between export and import prices is unusual and potentially unsustainable. Chinese producers are selling MDI abroad at $2,193 per tonne while paying $2,979 per tonne for imported MDI. The spread of nearly $800 per tonne suggests that the imported material is either of a different grade, or that it is being sourced from regions with structurally higher costs, or that the market is temporarily segmented.

The most likely explanation is that Chinese imports of MDI are coming from high-cost producers, possibly in Europe or the Middle East, and that these imports are necessary to meet specific domestic demand that Chinese producers cannot or will not serve. This creates a two-tier market in which Chinese producers are exporting at competitive prices while importing at premium prices. This is not sustainable in the long run. Either Chinese export prices will rise to close the gap with import prices, or Chinese imports will fall as domestic production ramps up further.

For investors, this price spread is a signal that the global MDI market is not fully integrated. Regional pricing power is still significant, and the ability to arbitrage between markets is limited by logistics, trade barriers, and product specifications. The narrowing or widening of this spread in the coming months will be a critical indicator of whether the market is moving toward equilibrium or toward further fragmentation.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Role of Capacity, Inventory, and the 2025-2026 Volume Disconnect

The report provides a wealth of data on trade flows and prices, but it leaves several critical questions unanswered. The most important of these is the relationship between Chinese production capacity, inventory levels, and the dramatic swing in net exports from 2025 to 2026.

In 2025, Chinese net exports of MDI were 400,000 tonnes, a collapse of 496,000 tonnes from 2024's 896,000 tonnes. In the first five months of 2026, net exports are already 258,000 tonnes. If this pace continues, full-year 2026 net exports could approach 620,000 tonnes, still below 2024 levels but a significant recovery. The question is: where is this additional volume coming from?

The report provides estimates for one producer's volume trajectory: lower by 10% in 2022, lower by 14% in 2023, higher by 7% in 2024, and higher by 2% in 2025. These are estimates for a single company, not the industry as a whole. Without data on total Chinese MDI production capacity and utilization rates, it is impossible to determine whether the export recovery is being driven by new capacity coming online, by higher utilization of existing capacity, or by a drawdown of inventories.

The answer to this question has significant implications. If the recovery is driven by new capacity, then the structural shift toward Chinese dominance is accelerating, and the price increases seen in May 2026 may be the beginning of a sustained upward trend. If the recovery is driven by inventory destocking, then the volume surge may be temporary, and prices could reverse as inventories are depleted. The report does not provide the data needed to distinguish between these scenarios.

Another unanswered question is the role of the US market. Exports to the US fell 83% in 2025, and the 2026 data does not show a recovery. Is this permanent? If Chinese producers have permanently exited the US market, then the global trade map has been redrawn. If the decline is temporary and related to trade policy, then a future normalization would add further upward pressure on Chinese export volumes. The report does not address this.

## A Decision Framework for Industry Participants: Three Scenarios and Their Implications

For readers who need to translate this data into actionable strategy, the following framework organizes the key uncertainties into three scenarios. Each scenario has distinct implications for pricing, sourcing, and investment.

Scenario One: Structural Capacity Shift. In this scenario, the May 2026 data is the leading edge of a permanent increase in Chinese MDI exports, driven by new capacity and lower costs. European and US producers continue to lose market share. The implication for buyers is that they should lock in long-term supply agreements with Chinese producers at current prices, as prices are likely to rise further. The implication for non-Chinese producers is that they must either reduce costs or exit the MDI business. The implication for investors is that Chinese chemical companies with MDI exposure are undervalued relative to their growth trajectory.

Scenario Two: Cyclical Recovery with Temporary Price Spike. In this scenario, the export surge is driven by a one-time inventory rebuild and a temporary supply disruption in Europe. As these factors fade, volumes and prices will moderate. The implication for buyers is that they should avoid locking in long-term contracts at current prices and instead maintain flexibility. The implication for non-Chinese producers is that they should resist the temptation to cut capacity, as demand will return. The implication for investors is that the current price spike is a selling opportunity.

Scenario Three: Fragmented Market with Regional Pricing. In this scenario, the divergence between Chinese export and import prices persists, indicating that the global MDI market is becoming more regionalized rather than more integrated. Trade barriers, logistics costs, and product differentiation prevent arbitrage. The implication for buyers is that they need multiple regional suppliers rather than a single global source. The implication for producers is that they should focus on regional dominance rather than global scale. The implication for investors is that the most valuable companies will be those with strong positions in regions with high barriers to entry.

Which scenario is most likely? The data favors Scenario One, but the evidence is not yet conclusive. The key indicator to watch is the trajectory of Chinese export prices relative to import prices over the next three to six months. If export prices continue to rise and the spread narrows, that supports the structural shift thesis. If export prices stabilize or decline, that supports the cyclical recovery thesis. For now, the prudent approach is to plan for Scenario One while monitoring for signals that would shift to Scenario Two.
