# China Autos & Shared Mobility | Asia Pacific

# PHEV Duty Concerns

Concerns over the EU potentially extending countervailing duties to PHEVs have furthered the recent China auto sector unwind. We think the sell-off prices in a worst-case scenario and think OEMs will adapt accordingly.

Potential duties on PHEV: Beyond the broader index correction, we think today's 3-5% decline across OEMs vs. HSI (-0.5%) is likely driven by concerns over potential EU countervailing duties being imposed on Chinese-made PHEVs, after a June 19 report in Handelsblatt (see here for details). The EU has not commented. Chinese-made BEVs have attracted EU duties since 2024, and OEMs have shifted their European export mix accordingly. If duties were imposed on PHEVs, this could raise costs and alter this strategic pivot. We note the sharpest auto share price declines are concentrated in those with significant southbound floats, rather than those with the highest direct PHEV exposure.

PHEV shift after BEV duties: The EU's 2024 BEV duties (7.8-35.3% plus the 10% base tariff) exempted PHEVs, prompting Chinese OEMs to redirect exports accordingly. PHEVs now account for 24% of Chinese exports to the EU, up from 6% in 2024.

Could PHEVs follow the same path as BEVs? The introduction of duties on BEVs appears to have had little effect on China's overall export momentum or competitive dynamics, despite some shift in the mix toward PHEVs. We wonder if the EU will seek a different approach if it is to introduce duties on PHEVs.

EU exports could see a short-term boost: Given details are unlikely to be confirmed in the next 1-2 months, Chinese OEMs could opt to expedite channel stuffing, as was the case with BEVs in 2024.

PHEV exposure varies across OEMs: According to MarkLines data, SAIC, Chery (not covered), and BYD could derive more than 20% of their overseas sales from PHEVs sold in the EU following the volume push since 2024, followed by Geely and Leapmotor (not covered). While EV start-ups such as NIO and XPeng have also sold off today, as sentiment turns increasingly binary, their direct exposure remains limited.

Global collaboration: A broader escalation from BEVs towards NEV stack could see Chinese EV OEMs localize their production in the EU or sell from factories outside of China. We continue to favor XPeng (9868.HK, OW), given its local partnerships, and SAIC (600104.SS, OW) for rerating opportunities, while BYD (1211.HK, OW) and Geely (0175.HK, OW) also appear well-equipped to navigate the associated risks with multiple production sites, despite potential knee-jerk market reactions.
