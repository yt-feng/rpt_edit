ZH_A: 最近有份关于中国消费品和美容行业的研报，里面重点分析了恒安、中顺洁柔和上海家化。你觉得这份报告真正想回答的问题是什么？

ZH_B: 核心问题是：在消费复苏缓慢、渠道剧烈变化的背景下，这些公司到底有没有可持续的增长动力？报告给出的判断偏谨慎。

ZH_A: 偏谨慎？那我们先从恒安开始。纸巾业务一直是它的主要增长引擎，但报告说增长质量是混合的。这是什么意思？

ZH_B: 意思是量在增长，但价格在承压。中小厂商退出，恒安作为龙头确实能抢到份额。但线上和新零售渠道占比越来越高，这些渠道促销多、价格低，拉低了平均售价。所以收入增长只能维持在中等个位数。

ZH_A: 所以量增价跌，听起来利润也不会有太大惊喜？

ZH_B: 对。报告预计2026年纸巾毛利率大概稳定在22.5%左右。2025年因为纸浆价格低，毛利率改善到23%，但2026年这个改善不太可能重复。而且卫生巾和纸尿裤业务还在承压。

ZH_A: 卫生巾一直是恒安的高毛利业务，为什么说压力大？

ZH_B: 竞争太激烈了。国内和国外品牌都在用激进促销抢份额。2025年下半年虽然有所改善，报告认为更多是低基数效应。预计2026年卫生巾销售额可能持平甚至微降。

ZH_A: 那纸尿裤呢？报告提到产品升级，成人纸尿裤和高端品牌还在增长。

ZH_B: 但整体量在下降。高端化能提升均价，但量的下滑幅度更大。报告预计2026年纸尿裤销售额下降1%。所以整体来看，恒安2026年利润可能还会小幅下滑。

ZH_A: 但恒安的估值不高，股息率有6%左右，这不就提供了下行保护吗？

ZH_B: 没错，报告也说估值不贵，股息率是支撑。但问题是缺少上调催化剂。如果没有更强的收入加速或者卫生巾业务明显回暖，很难支撑更高的估值。所以评级是中性。

ZH_A: 那中顺洁柔呢？报告给了低配评级，为什么？

ZH_B: 核心问题是盈利复苏慢于预期。报告把2026和2027年的盈利预测下调了20%和27%。收入增长靠量和份额驱动，但定价权很有限。线上渠道虽然支持量，但促销拉低均价，毛利率改善空间被压缩。

ZH_A: 它的估值呢？

ZH_B: 现在交易在2026年预期市盈率24倍左右，但ROE低、定价权有限、利润率改善空间小。报告认为风险回报不吸引人，所以维持低配。

ZH_A: 那上海家化呢？报告说它重回增长，但也给了低配。

ZH_B: 是的，增长主要靠线上和美容板块恢复，比如佰草集和玉泽。但这增长是靠品牌投入驱动的，不是运营效率的提升。线上和抖音渠道占比上升，需要持续投入内容和流量，所以短期运营杠杆有限。

ZH_A: 所以它的利润率改善空间也不大？

ZH_B: 毛利率会随着品类结构改善逐步提升，但销售费用会维持高位。报告把2026和2027年盈利预测下调了35%和39%。现在股价交易在35倍以上市盈率，但利润可见性有限，所以估值显得偏贵。

ZH_A: 这三家公司有一个共同点吗？

ZH_B: 有。都面临渠道变化带来的定价压力，都需要持续投入品牌和渠道，盈利复苏速度都慢于预期。但报告里还有一个没完全展开的问题。

ZH_A: 什么问题？

ZH_B: 就是如果消费环境进一步走弱，这些公司的防御性到底有多强？恒安有高股息，但其他两家呢？报告没有完全回答这个问题。

ZH_A: 那如果想了解更多，应该去看完整报告？

ZH_B: 对。完整报告里还有更详细的估值模型、熊牛情景分析，以及每个业务线的收入拆分。这些在播客里不可能全讲完。

EN_A: So we just looked at a research note on Chinese consumer companies — Hengan, C&S Paper, and Shanghai Jahwa. What's the big question this report is trying to answer?

EN_B: The real question is whether these companies can deliver sustainable growth given a slow consumption recovery and a rapidly shifting retail landscape. And the report's answer is cautious.

EN_A: Cautious? Let's start with Hengan. Tissue is supposed to be its main growth driver. But the report says the quality of growth is mixed. What does that mean?

EN_B: It means volume is growing but pricing is under pressure. Smaller players are exiting, so Hengan is gaining share. But online and new retail channels are becoming a bigger part of the mix. Those channels have heavy promotions and lower prices. So revenue growth stays in the mid-single digits.

EN_A: So it's a volume-up, price-down story. Not great for margins, right?

EN_B: Right. The report expects tissue gross margin to stay around 22.5% in 2026. 2025 saw a nice improvement to 23% thanks to low pulp prices. But that's unlikely to repeat. And the bigger problem is sanitary napkins and diapers are still under pressure.

EN_A: Sanitary napkins used to be Hengan's high-margin business. What's going wrong?

EN_B: Competition is fierce. Both domestic and international brands are using aggressive promotions to grab share. There was some improvement in the second half of 2025, but the report thinks that's mostly a low base effect. They expect sanitary napkin sales to be flat or slightly down in 2026.

EN_A: What about diapers? The report mentions premium products and adult diapers are still growing.

EN_B: They are, but the overall volume is declining. The mix shift to higher-end products helps average selling price, but not enough to offset the volume drop. The report forecasts diaper sales to decline 1% in 2026. So overall, Hengan's profit might actually dip in 2026.

EN_A: But the stock is cheap, right? The dividend yield is around 6%. Doesn't that provide a floor?

EN_B: It does. The report says valuation is not demanding and the dividend yield provides downside support. But there's no clear catalyst for a rerating. Without stronger revenue acceleration or evidence that hygiene products can return to sustainable growth, it's hard to justify a higher multiple. So the rating is equal-weight.

EN_A: What about C&S Paper? The report has it at underweight. Why?

EN_B: The main issue is the earnings recovery is slower than expected. The report cut 2026 and 2027 earnings estimates by 20% and 27%. Revenue growth is driven by volume and market share gains, but pricing power is very limited. Online channels support volume but also bring heavy promotions that drag down average selling prices. So margin upside is capped.

EN_A: And what about its valuation?

EN_B: It's trading at around 24 times 2026 estimated earnings. But return on equity is low, pricing power is weak, and margin improvement is modest. The report thinks the risk-reward is not compelling. So it stays at underweight.

EN_A: And Shanghai Jahwa? The report says it's back to growth, but still underweight.

EN_B: Yes. The growth is driven by online channels and the beauty segment recovery — brands like Herborist and Dr. Yu are doing well. But this growth is fueled by brand investment, not operational efficiency. The rising mix of online and Douyin channels requires continuous spending on content and traffic. So near-term operating leverage is limited.

EN_A: So margin improvement is also limited there?

EN_B: Gross margin should improve gradually as the product mix gets better. But selling expenses will stay high as the company rebuilds brand equity. The report cut 2026 and 2027 earnings estimates by 35% and 39%. The stock is trading at over 35 times earnings, but profit visibility is limited. So valuation looks demanding.

EN_A: Is there a common theme across all three companies?

EN_B: Definitely. All three face pricing pressure from channel shifts. All three need to keep spending on branding and distribution. And the earnings recovery for all three is slower than expected. But there's one question the report doesn't fully answer.

EN_A: What's that?

EN_B: If the consumption environment weakens further, how defensive are these companies? Hengan has a high dividend yield. But what about the other two? The report doesn't fully explore that scenario.
