# The Fed Has Not Changed the EM Story: Why Local Bond Yields Are Still Too High and Set to Fall

A new Federal Reserve posture does not rewrite the emerging market thesis. The core argument remains intact: EM currencies are poised to rally against G3 currencies, local bond yields have room to decline, and the asset class is positioned for a multi-month recovery. The plot twist of a hawkish FOMC has tested this view, but it has not broken it.

The resilience of EM currencies against a stronger dollar is the first piece of evidence. Despite renewed talk of US exceptionalism following the June FOMC meeting, our index of EM currency total returns continues to make new highs against the G3 and a broader DM basket. Over the past month, quarter, and year to date, EM currencies have delivered positive returns against virtually every funding currency. This is not a coincidence. It reflects a structural shift in risk appetite, lower oil prices, and improving fundamentals that are not dependent on US monetary policy staying dovish.

Yet the bond market has not caught up. EM local currency government bond yields have not fallen as much as the collapse in energy prices would suggest. The correlation between index yields and oil prices was tight for the first two and a half months after the US-Iran conflict began, but that relationship has broken down. Yields are now roughly 10 to 15 basis points too high relative to what the energy price move alone would imply. This gap is the opportunity.

The question is not whether EM yields will fall. The question is what needs to change for them to do so. The answer lies in three headwinds that are already beginning to fade: higher core rates, USD strength, and continued market pricing of EM rate hikes. Each of these forces has kept a lid on the duration rally. Each is now showing signs of reversal.

## Lower Oil Prices Are the Engine, But EM Bond Yields Have Not Yet Boarded the Train

The most striking disconnect in EM markets today is between energy prices and local currency bond yields. Oil prices have fallen back to levels seen before the US-Iran conflict began. The Bloomberg Energy Index tells a similar story. Yet EM index yields remain stubbornly elevated.

This is not simply a matter of USD-denominated energy prices being misleading. Even after adjusting for the roughly 3 percent weakening in EM spot FX, energy prices in local currency terms are only marginally higher than in USD terms over the relevant period. The gap between what yields should be and what they are cannot be explained away by currency movements.

What explains it is a combination of factors that are each beginning to shift. US Treasury yields have moved higher on strong data and a hawkish Fed, creating an unfavorable backdrop for EM duration. But EM has actually outperformed in relative terms: nominal yield differentials between EM and US bonds have narrowed by roughly 50 basis points from their highs and are now below pre-conflict levels. In real terms, EM has reversed its post-conflict underperformance almost entirely.

The message is clear. The headwind from higher core rates is fading at the margin. If US yields stabilize or decline, the compression trade in EM has significant room to run. The 10 to 15 basis point cheapness in EM yields relative to energy prices is not noise. It is a signal that the market has not yet fully repriced the improvement in the external environment.

## EM Central Banks Are More Dovish Than Markets Price, and That Gap Will Close

One of the most underappreciated drivers of elevated EM bond yields is the market's persistent pricing of rate hikes across the asset class. At the index level, forward rates continue to imply tighter monetary policy over the next twelve months. This is a hangover from the inflation shock and the US-Iran conflict. It is increasingly at odds with the underlying economic reality.

MS economists are more dovish than the market on the path for central bank policy across virtually every major EM economy. This is not a marginal difference. In many cases, the gap between market pricing and our baseline is substantial. We see most EM central banks cutting rates over the next twelve months, not hiking.

The implications for bond yields are direct. If the market begins to converge toward a more dovish central bank path, short-end rates will decline. The 1y1y swap rates that remain comfortably above pre-conflict levels will compress. The front end of EM curves, which has been the most resistant to the rally, will become the primary source of returns.

This is not a forecast that depends on the Fed cutting rates. It is a forecast that depends on EM central banks regaining control of their own policy narratives. Lower oil prices reduce inflation pressure across the asset class. Stronger currencies reinforce that disinflation. The combination gives EM policymakers room to deliver a more dovish path than markets currently expect. When they do, bond yields will follow.

## Currency Strength Is the Missing Link That Will Unlock the Bond Rally

EM currencies have been the unsung heroes of the asset class this year. Against the G3, they have delivered consistent positive total returns. Against a broader DM basket, the story is the same. The only exception is a marginal underperformance against the USD on a month-to-date basis, which is more a reflection of the dollar's post-FOMC strength than any EM weakness.

The correlation between FX performance and bond performance is well established. Markets that have seen the largest currency gains have also seen the largest declines in bond yields. COP, HUF, and ILS are at the bullish end of the scale. TRY, IDR, KRW, INR, and THB are at the bearish end. BRL is an outlier, with yields moving higher despite a resilient currency, but that exception may prove the rule.

The key insight is that currency strength does more than just tighten financial conditions. It also directly reduces the local currency cost of imported energy. When an EM currency appreciates, the pass-through from lower global oil prices to domestic inflation is amplified. This gives central banks more confidence to ease, which in turn supports bond prices.

We continue to recommend a diversified funding approach to capture EM FX exposure. The debate over the USD cycle is far from settled, and investors who want to isolate their EM exposure from dollar directionality should consider funding positions with a G3 or broader DM basket. The thesis is simple: EM currencies have fundamental support from strong risk appetite, limited global investor allocation, and improving current accounts. The USD cycle is an uncertainty, not a barrier.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The Sustainability of EM Inflows and the Risk of a Positioning Shock

The report provides a detailed flow tracker covering nine EM local currency bond markets, and the data show a clear improvement. After heavy outflows in March and stability in May, flows have started to pick up again in June. The rolling three-month sum of flows, which dropped from roughly $40 billion in mid-February to around negative $20 billion at the end of May, is now rebounding.

This is encouraging, but it raises a question the report does not fully address: how much of the improvement in flows is driven by genuine fundamental conviction, and how much is simply a normalization after the extreme outflows of March? The correlation between flows and returns is well established, but the current data point is firmly in the neutral zone. Returns have softened modestly, and flows have not yet reached the extremes that would signal a compelling risk-reward entry point.

The FX options data add another layer of ambiguity. Investors remain long USD, and they added to those positions modestly after the FOMC meeting. The long HUF position remains heavy, and there has been some profit taking, but the overall positioning in EM FX is constructive. BRL exposure has moved to neutral after being overweight. The question is whether this positioning leaves the asset class vulnerable to a sudden reversal if risk appetite deteriorates.

The report does not provide a clear answer to this question, and it is the most important uncertainty facing EM investors today. The fundamental case is strong, but positioning is not extreme in either direction. This means the market is more susceptible to exogenous shocks than it would be if flows were at clear extremes. Investors should watch the flow data closely for signs of either a breakout to the upside or a renewed deterioration.

## A Decision Framework for EM Investors: Three Conditions to Watch

The report's analysis can be distilled into a simple decision framework. EM local bonds are attractive, but the timing of the rally depends on three conditions being met. Investors should use these conditions as signposts for when to add or reduce exposure.

First, watch the path of US Treasury yields. The most immediate headwind to EM duration is the level of core rates. If US yields stabilize or decline, the compression trade in EM has significant room to run. If US yields continue to rise on hawkish Fed expectations, EM yields will remain under pressure regardless of the improvement in energy prices and fundamentals.

Second, watch the pricing of EM central bank policy. The gap between market pricing and our dovish baseline is the single largest source of potential alpha in the asset class. If 1y1y swap rates begin to decline in response to lower inflation prints or more dovish central bank commentary, the front end of EM curves will rally. If the market continues to price hikes, the duration rally will be limited to the back end.

Third, watch EM currency performance against the USD. The correlation between FX and rates is tight, and a sustained USD rally would make it difficult for EM bonds to rally regardless of the improvement in energy prices. If EM currencies continue to deliver positive total returns against the G3, the bond rally will follow. If the dollar strengthens further and EM FX weakens, the bond market will remain range-bound.

These three conditions are not independent. They interact in ways that can either amplify or offset each other. The most favorable scenario for EM bonds is declining US yields, converging central bank pricing, and stable or appreciating EM currencies. The least favorable scenario is the opposite. The current environment is somewhere in between, which is why yields have not yet fully reflected the improvement in energy prices.

## The Opportunity Is Real, But It Requires Patience and a Diversified Approach

EM local bonds are cheap relative to the improvement in the external environment. The gap between where yields are and where they should be is 10 to 15 basis points on a conservative estimate. The headwinds that have kept yields elevated are all beginning to fade. The fundamental case for the asset class is as strong as it has been all year.

But the market does not move in a straight line. The path to lower yields will be punctuated by periods of volatility as investors digest conflicting signals from the Fed, the USD, and EM central banks. The key is to maintain a diversified approach that captures the value in EM without taking excessive directional risk.

The specific trades recommended in the report reflect this philosophy. In CEEMEA, the South Africa 1y1y-2y2y steepener is a bet on the front end rallying as the market converges toward our dovish baseline. The long NGOMOB position is a FX-unhedged bet on Nigerian local bonds that captures both carry and potential appreciation.

In Asia, the pay 5y HKD IRS versus USD IRS trade is a bet on Hong Kong rates normalizing as the USD strengthens. The pay 5y INR NDOIS trade is a bet on the RBI holding rates steady while the market prices cuts. The 1s5s THB NDTHOR steepener is a bet on the Bank of Thailand delivering a more dovish path than currently priced.

In Latin America, the buy 2028 MBonos trade is a bet on Mexican local bonds benefiting from a stable currency and a dovish central bank. The pay Jan 29 DI trade is a bet on Brazilian rates declining as the Selic cycle turns.

Each of these trades is a specific expression of the broader thesis. They are not meant to be held in isolation. They are meant to be combined into a portfolio that captures the value in EM while managing the risks from the USD cycle, core rates, and positioning.
