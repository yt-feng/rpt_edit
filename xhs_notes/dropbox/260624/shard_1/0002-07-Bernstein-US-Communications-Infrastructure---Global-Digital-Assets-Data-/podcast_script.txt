ZH_A: 最近我注意到一个很有意思的话题，数据中心和GPU云服务商签的那些大合同，到底划不划算？

ZH_A: 所以他们的利润率反而很稳定？

EN_A: I keep seeing headlines about these massive data center and GPU cloud contracts. How do we even know if a deal is good?

EN_A: So what are the two main models here?
EN_B: The first is the colocation model, or the landlord model. Companies like Equinix and Digital Realty build data centers and lease out powered space. They are like real estate investment trusts, or REITs.
EN_A: And the second?
EN_B: That is the GPU cloud model, or the neocloud. Companies like CoreWeave do not just lease space. They buy the GPUs, install them, and sell computing power as a service. They capture value from the hardware and the software layer.
EN_A: So the neocloud model generates much higher revenue per megawatt?
EN_B: Significantly higher. The report shows CoreWeave generates over 20 million dollars in annualized revenue per megawatt. Digital Realty, a traditional colo provider, is around 3.4 million.
EN_A: That is a huge gap. So neocloud is clearly the better business?
EN_B: Not so fast. Revenue is only half the story. The report highlights that neoclouds also have much higher costs. They pay for the GPUs, the electricity, the software, and the engineering support.
EN_A: So their margins might not be that great?
EN_B: Exactly. The colo providers are asset-heavy but OpEx-light. Most power and facility costs are passed through to tenants. For a neocloud, almost all of that is on their own books. CoreWeave's operating expense per megawatt is nearly 19 million dollars.
EN_A: So what is the bottom line? Which model is better for investors?
EN_B: The report clearly states a preference for the colo model, especially for smaller or emerging players. It offers stable, predictable returns with lower risk.
EN_A: Does that mean the neocloud model is a bad investment?
