# China: Fiscal deployment still slow in May

Expect a two-stage fiscal path ahead

\- General budget expenditure stayed in contraction despite resilient revenue, still weighed down by weak infrastructure outlays.

\- Fund revenue and expenditure both saw sharp declines, amid a recent acceleration in land-sales contraction.

\- Fiscal deposits rose to the second-highest May level over the past decade, leaving more fiscal room for 2H.

\- Special LGB issuance rebounded to \~500bn yuan mtd in June, signaling reactive acceleration, but project deployment likely lags.

\- Outlook is a two-stage fiscal path: execution of NPC approved budget first, with additional support more likely if recent growth slowdown lingers.

Aligned with the continued FAI decline in May, fiscal expenditure (across both the general budget and government-managed fund accounts) stayed in contraction, though May is typically a seasonally low month. Fiscal deposits increased further, reaching May levels second only to 2025 over the past decade. This reinforces the view that fiscal deployment remains slow, leaving more room to provide support in 2H after the current quarter's growth slowdown.

Budget revenue growth remained firm while expenditure stayed in contraction, with infrastructure spending the key drag. Headline general budget revenue maintained solid growth at 6.6% oya in May, lifting ytd growth to 4.0% (vs 2.2% full-year budgetary target). Tax revenue rose 6.8% oya, led by VAT (+7.9%), while securities trading stamp duty revenue surged 145.9%. Non-tax revenue rebounded to 5.6% oya from -5.3% in April, marking its strongest annual rate increase since March 2025. General budget expenditure stayed in contraction for the second month this year (-1.6% oya), lagging 1Q's 2.6% growth and the 4.4% full-year budgetary target, though the decline narrowed modestly. By category, infrastructure outlays were the main drag, falling 12.0% oya, which helps explain the continued weakness in infrastructure FAI (-9.4% oya) and public FAI (-8.9%) in May. Livelihood spending (related to “investing in people” policy initiative) picked up to 2.5% oya, but remained slow compared with 1Q's 5.3%.

Land sales contraction deepened further, dragging funds account revenue and expenditure. Outside the general budget, government-managed funds, which are now the largest financing source among fund accounts, remain heavily land-sale dependent, with spending tied to land costs and special local government bond proceeds deployment. In May, fund revenue dropped 20.3% oya, as the decline in land sales deepened further to -35.8%, while expenditure fell 11.5%. The renewed acceleration in land-sales contraction (vs -14.7% for 2025 full-year) underscores the ongoing drag from the housing/land downturn on local-government fiscal conditions.
