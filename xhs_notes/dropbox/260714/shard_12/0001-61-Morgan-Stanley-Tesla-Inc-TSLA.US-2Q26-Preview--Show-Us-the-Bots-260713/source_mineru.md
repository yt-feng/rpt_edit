## Outside of the numbers, four key themes we're tracking through earnings:

1) Auto resurgence – does it matter? We believe the combination of 1) elevated gas prices, 2) rebound in Europe volumes vs. easy comps and ahead of potential EU FSD approval, and 3) potential tailwinds from HW3 trade ins aided strong 2Q auto deliveries. Anticipation for improvements to FSD technology along with the newly announced Model YL release in the US are likely to continue to support volumes this year and into 2027. As a result, we increase auto volume forecasts in 2026 and 2027 to 1.67mn / 1.86mn from 1.57mn / 1.82mn previously.

The question will turn to whether it's enough to reinvigorate investor interest in the core auto business. Tesla shares traded lower on the 2Q delivery result, which was 18% above sell-side consensus expectations and the highest auto growth rate since 3Q23. Nevertheless, stability in this core segment, which still represents \~70% of total company revenues, is crucial to afford investors the flexibility to place higher probabilities on Tesla's success in larger TAMs in physical AI (Robotaxi and Optimus).

## 2) Pulse Check on Autonomy.

TSLA FSD hit a key autonomy milestone in 2Q; can the momentum continue? We look to an update on Unsupervised FSD, v15 rollout, and global approval. Based on Tesla's public tracker, FSD passed the 10bn miles driven milestone on May 3rd, weeks ahead of expectations as FSD miles accelerated. This represents an important milestone on Tesla's path toward Unsupervised FSD, alongside the consequential task of scaling an unsupervised robotaxi fleet. Per our assumptions, Tesla owners are now driving \~26.7mn miles/day with FSD (up from 18.5m miles/day in April).

We continue to track this metric as it has bearings on FSD adoption (both via new subscribers and existing subscribers using the technology more frequently) which at a \~90% GM can uplift profitability and give credence to Tesla's autonomous tech as it scales its unsupervised robotaxi fleet, though would note that Elon Musk has pushed timelines for unsupervised FSD for consumer TSLA vehicles to 4Q26 at the earliest, per the 1Q earnings call. We also wait for an update on FSD v15, which is seen as a pivotal model to enable scaled unsupervised robotaxi and personal unsupervised FSD. Finally, we're watching for China and broader EU approval after a handful of European countries approved the tech within the quarter.

In a year when capex is set to more than double and FCF is flipping negative, we believe Tesla's ability to demonstrate tangible progress in scaling unsupervised autonomous driving capabilities will be critical in supporting valuation.

Robotaxi ramp? Tesla launched robotaxi Miami (including unsupervised vehicles) on Friday, July 3rd – notably the first launch outside of Texas that started without a safety monitor in the vehicle. Miami was listed as "preparations underway" in the 1Q26 slide deck, along with Phoenix, Orlando, Tampa, and Las Vegas. We expect Tesla to launch in all of these metro areas by year-end. Additionally, there have been some sightings of presumed robotaxi test vehicles in New Orleans (not confirmed by the company), which was not one of the cities listed as being in the preparation stage in the last earnings presentation. We forecast 1,500 robotaxis (supervised + unsupervised) in the fleet by year-end, ramping to 30,000 by 2030. While the absolute number of robotaxis is likely immaterial to earnings this year, the rate of

change in the rollout provides more clarity for investors who may be skeptical about Tesla's autonomous technology at scale.

Cybercab targets industry-high battery efficiency. Tesla released its 2025 Impact Report, which among other innovative energy solutions like waste heat recovery and battery material recycling, detailed the technological innovations in Cybercab. We published about Tesla ushering in the manufacturing of the future with reaction injection molding ('RIM') and the modular unboxed architecture following our March Giga Texas tour: Tesla Inc: Unlocking the Robotaxi Flywheel (18 Mar 2026). What was new in the report was the target for battery efficiency, at 6.1 miles/KWh, a meaningful increase from 5.5mi/KWh we have modeled, above the Model Y AWD at 4.3/KWh, and above the industry high Lucid Air Pure at 5.0mi/KWh, which has a starting MSRP of \$71,000.

3) Optimus momentum appears to be building. Recent reporting highlights Tesla has begun issuing specific procurement guidance for Optimus components, requesting suppliers to ramp up production capacity to \~1,000 units per week by September and 2,000-2,500 units per week by the end of the year. With 2Q earnings, we expect the company to provide an update on the final design, production ramp cadence, and early use case opportunities across Tesla's manufacturing footprint. A complete unveil of the product is less likely, in our view, based on recent commentary from Elon Musk.

4) Are the Capex Revisions Done? We think so, for now. We do not expect another meaningful increase in the capex guide for this year (we forecast \$26.8bn in 2026), but we are looking for more clarity on the duration of this capex cycle.
