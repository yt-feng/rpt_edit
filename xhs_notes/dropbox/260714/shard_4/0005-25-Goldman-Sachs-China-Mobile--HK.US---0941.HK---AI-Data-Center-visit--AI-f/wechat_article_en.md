# AI for AIDC: How China Mobile Is Transforming Its Data Center Business Into an AI Operating Advantage

China Mobile's AI Data Center (AIDC) revenue increased 35% year-over-year in 2025, while its broader data center business grew only 9%. This 26-percentage-point divergence is not a marginal story about one product line outperforming another. It signals a structural shift in how the world's largest mobile operator by subscribers is repositioning its infrastructure business for the generative AI era. The company is no longer merely a provider of rack space and bandwidth. It is building a differentiated operating model in which artificial intelligence manages the data center itself, and that self-management capability is becoming the competitive moat that drives revenue acceleration.

The catalyst for this thesis is China Mobile's Global Intelligence Center in Hong Kong, which began operations in March 2026. It is the company's second-largest data center in the market, and it is entirely self-built and self-operated. The facility is designed from the ground up to support AI workloads spanning both training and inference, serving clients from foundation model development through to production AI applications. But the more consequential design choice is that the center uses AI to run itself. From intelligent scheduling to autonomous inspections via robots and drones to digital twin simulations, the facility embeds operational intelligence into its own infrastructure. This is not a cost-saving footnote. It is the mechanism that allows China Mobile to offer a full-stack AIDC service—computing, networking, power, cooling, and operations—at a scale and efficiency that legacy data center operators cannot replicate.

The timing matters. Demand for computing power is accelerating across both local and overseas markets as AI applications proliferate. China Mobile's leading position in data center operation, built over years of managing one of the world's largest telecommunications networks, positions it to capture this demand in a way that pure-play cloud providers or real estate–focused data center REITs cannot. The company is not just building more data centers. It is building data centers that learn how to operate themselves better over time, and that capability is becoming the binding constraint for enterprise AI adoption.

## The 26-Point Growth Gap Between AIDC and Traditional Data Centers Reveals a New Revenue Architecture

The headline numbers from the report are stark. In 2025, China Mobile's AIDC revenues grew 35% year-over-year, while its overall data center revenues grew only 9%. That gap is not a one-time anomaly driven by a single large client. It reflects a fundamental change in what customers are buying. Traditional data center services—colocation, bandwidth, basic power and cooling—are becoming commoditized. Growth in that segment is in the single digits because the addressable market is mature and competition is high. AIDC services, by contrast, bundle computing power with AI-optimized infrastructure. Customers are not paying for space. They are paying for the ability to train and run foundation models without having to build their own AI infrastructure from scratch.

This distinction has direct implications for revenue quality. AIDC services carry higher unit economics because they include GPU computing, specialized networking, and the operational expertise required to keep AI workloads running at high utilization. A data center that sells rack space at a fixed price per kilowatt has a capped revenue per customer. A data center that sells AI computing power can scale revenue with the customer's model size and inference volume. The 35% growth rate suggests that China Mobile is successfully converting a portion of its traditional data center customers into AIDC customers while also attracting new clients who would not have considered a telecommunications company for AI infrastructure.

For observers, the critical metric to watch is not just AIDC revenue growth but the mix shift between AIDC and traditional data center revenue over the next two to three years. If AIDC continues to grow at a 30%-plus clip while the base business grows in the single digits, the blended revenue growth rate for the data center segment will rise meaningfully. That would change the valuation narrative for China Mobile's infrastructure business from a stable, low-growth utility to a high-growth AI enabler.

## AI for AIDC Creates an Operational Moat That Competitors Cannot Easily Copy

The Global Intelligence Center's use of AI for its own operations is the most underappreciated aspect of China Mobile's strategy. The facility employs intelligent scheduling to allocate computing resources dynamically across training and inference workloads. It uses autonomous robots and drones for inspections, reducing the need for human intervention in environments that are increasingly dense with high-heat GPU clusters. Digital twin technology allows the operations team to simulate changes to power distribution, cooling configurations, or workload placement before implementing them in the physical facility.

This is not a technology demonstration. It is a structural cost advantage that compounds over time. A data center that can schedule workloads intelligently achieves higher GPU utilization rates than one that relies on manual allocation. Higher utilization means more revenue per megawatt of power capacity, which is the fundamental unit of data center economics. Autonomous inspections reduce labor costs and, more importantly, reduce downtime. A digital twin that can predict cooling failures before they happen prevents thermal throttling of GPUs, which directly protects revenue.

The competitive implication is clear. A traditional data center operator can acquire the same GPUs and the same cooling equipment that China Mobile uses. But it cannot acquire the operational software and the accumulated data on how to run those systems efficiently. China Mobile's AI for AIDC capability is a learning system. Every workload it schedules, every inspection it automates, every simulation it runs generates data that makes the system better. A competitor starting from scratch would need years to replicate that operational knowledge, and by then, China Mobile would have moved further ahead.

## Hong Kong Serves as a Strategic Bridgehead for Cross-Border AIDC Demand

The location of the Global Intelligence Center in Hong Kong is not incidental. Hong Kong remains the most important gateway for Chinese enterprises expanding globally and for international enterprises seeking access to Chinese AI ecosystems. China Mobile's AIDC business outside mainland China can ride on the wave of Chinese enterprises' global business expansion, as the report notes. This creates a dual demand driver. Domestic Chinese AI companies need computing power for training and inference. Chinese enterprises expanding overseas need data centers in international hubs that can connect back to mainland infrastructure. Hong Kong serves both groups.

The strategic advantage for China Mobile is that it already operates a telecommunications network that spans the mainland and Hong Kong. It can offer connectivity as part of the AIDC package in a way that a pure data center operator cannot. For a client training a foundation model in Beijing and running inference in Hong Kong, the ability to move data seamlessly between the two locations without going through third-party carriers is a significant operational benefit. It reduces latency, simplifies compliance, and lowers total cost.

This cross-border capability also positions China Mobile to capture demand from multinational corporations that need AI infrastructure in Asia but are constrained by regulatory requirements around data sovereignty. A Hong Kong–based AIDC that is operated by a Chinese state-owned enterprise but located outside mainland jurisdiction offers a middle ground. It provides access to Chinese AI ecosystems and computing power while operating under Hong Kong's legal framework. That positioning is difficult for non-Chinese operators to replicate.

## A Decision Framework for Evaluating China Mobile's AIDC Trajectory

For readers assessing whether China Mobile's AIDC growth is sustainable, three variables determine the outcome. The first is GPU supply. China Mobile's ability to grow AIDC revenue depends on its access to high-performance GPUs, which remains constrained by export controls. The company must demonstrate that it can secure sufficient supply from available sources, including domestic alternatives, to meet demand. The 35% revenue growth in 2025 suggests it has managed this challenge so far, but the constraint will tighten as demand accelerates.

The second variable is utilization. AIDC revenue growth is not just about adding GPUs. It is about keeping those GPUs busy. China Mobile's AI for AIDC capability directly addresses this variable by optimizing workload scheduling. The company should be able to achieve higher utilization rates than competitors that lack this capability. Observers should track utilization metrics as a lead indicator of margin performance.

The third variable is customer concentration. If AIDC revenue is concentrated among a small number of large AI companies, the business is vulnerable to churn or pricing pressure. If it is diversified across many clients and use cases, the revenue base is more stable. China Mobile's position as a telecommunications operator gives it a natural distribution advantage. It already serves millions of enterprise customers through its core business. Cross-selling AIDC services into that base should lead to a more diversified revenue structure than a pure-play data center company can achieve.

The interaction among these three variables determines the outcome. If GPU supply remains adequate, utilization stays high due to AI-driven operations, and customer diversification continues, China Mobile's AIDC business can sustain 30%-plus growth for several years. If any one of these variables deteriorates, growth will slow. The most likely risk is GPU supply. The most powerful mitigant is the operational efficiency advantage embedded in the AI for AIDC model, which allows China Mobile to generate more revenue per GPU than competitors can.
