EN_A: So there is a new report out on Taiwan electronic components and the BBU sector. It seems to be getting a lot of attention. What is the big story here?

EN_B: The core thesis is straightforward. As AI rack power density keeps going up, backup power at the rack level, or BBU, is shifting from a nice-to-have to a must-have. The report argues this demand is structural, not just a short-term fad.

EN_A: Okay, but what is the single most important question this report is trying to answer?

EN_B: It is really trying to address five key investor concerns. For example, would a delay in high-voltage direct current racks or next-gen GPU platforms kill the BBU investment thesis? And could power supply unit suppliers just start making BBUs themselves and cut out the middlemen?

EN_A: Those are the big risks. So on the first one, if HVDC is delayed, is the whole BBU story dead?

EN_B: The report says no. It sees HVDC as an incremental long-term driver, not a prerequisite. Near-term demand is driven by existing 3kW and 5kW products under current architectures. The higher-power stuff, like 8kW and 12kW, does not really ramp until late 2026. So a delay just pushes out the peak, it does not derail the trend.

EN_A: That means the underlying demand is solid even if the technology roadmap shifts. What about the second risk? Can the PSU guys just take over?

EN_B: The report is pretty clear here. It is unlikely. BBU requires a completely different skill set, things like battery pack design, battery management system integration, thermal management, and safety certifications. Given the safety-critical nature, PSU suppliers are more likely to keep partnering with qualified battery pack makers. So companies with strong PSU partnerships, like Dynapack, might actually benefit from this model.

EN_A: That sounds like a structural moat. But what is the one big question the report does not fully answer yet?

EN_B: The timeline for Dynapack's HVDC BBU products. The report says product verification is around the first half of 2027, with initial shipments possibly starting in the second half. Meaningful revenue contribution is more likely from late 2028. So the earnings for 2026 and 2027 still depend on existing products. That timeline uncertainty is something the full report explores in more detail.
