# SAP: 2Q26 preview - S/4 migration reality vs AI narrative

SAP's 2Q26 results should reinforce the durability of its S/4 transition, in our view. Ahead of the 23 July print, we reiterate our Outperform rating. The key debate is whether SAP could sustain a mid-20s current cloud backlog (CCB) growth trajectory. Our proprietary analysis of SAP's S/4 migration progress suggests the latter: we estimate only \~55% of the ECC installed base has migrated to S/4, leaving a substantial conversion opportunity. We forecast results modestly above consensus and believe stable \~24-25% CCB growth would strengthen confidence in FY26 guidance and medium-term cloud growth.

The cloud backlog remains the critical KPI. Consensus expects CCB growth to slow modestly from 25% in 1Q26 to 24% in 2Q26. We believe backlog growth can remain in the mid-20s, supported by ongoing S/4 migrations, Cloud ERP adoption and strong visibility from contracted revenue. The key signal for investors will be whether demand remains resilient despite macro uncertainty and recent concerns around large-deal timing.

The S/4 migration runway remains underappreciated. Based on our analysis, only around 55% of SAP's ECC installed base has migrated to S/4. With end-of-support deadlines approaching and ERP modernization increasingly mission-critical, we see a large remaining conversion opportunity that should continue supporting cloud revenue growth into FY27.

AI is increasingly a migration accelerator rather than a standalone revenue story. While investors remain skeptical about near-term AI monetization, SAP's embedded AI strategy, Business Data Cloud initiative, and recent acquisitions could support higher cloud adoption, larger transformation projects and stronger customer retention over time.

## Investment Implications

Stay constructive. We expect SAP to reiterate FY26 guidance, deliver modest upside versus consensus and demonstrate that cloud growth, margin expansion and cash generation remain intact, reinforcing our Outperform stance.

## WHERE ARE WE COMPARED TO CONSENSUS?

SAP is scheduled to report its 2Q26 results on 23 July after market close. Following a solid 1Q26 print, we believe that investor attention is likely to remain focused on the sustainability of cloud growth, current cloud backlog (CCB) momentum, and the credibility of SAP's FY26 outlook rather than on a simple revenue or EPS beat.

## WHAT ARE THE KEY INVESTOR DEBATES HEADING INTO 2Q26 RESULTS?

Ahead of SAP's 2Q26 results, we believe, based on our discussions with them, that investors are focused less on the quarter itself and more on a number of key strategic debates that will shape the company's medium-term outlook. These discussions center on the sustainability of cloud backlog growth, the ability of AI initiatives to generate meaningful revenue and accelerate ERP migrations, the credibility of FY26 guidance, the durability of margin expansion, the potential of Business Data Cloud and recent acquisitions to create new growth avenues, and whether the recent share price weakness represents an attractive entry point or signals a more structural slowdown in growth. Collectively, these debates will determine whether SAP can continue delivering strong growth and profitability while successfully navigating a rapidly evolving technology and macroeconomic environment.

## DEBATE 1: CURRENT CLOUD BACKLOG - SUSTAINABLE LOW-TO-MID-20S GROWTH OR A TEMPORARY PEAK?

The key debate for investors is whether SAP's strong cloud performance in 1Q26 confirms a durable low-to-mid-20s CCB growth trajectory that can support FY26 and FY27, or whether the quarter represents a temporary peak following exceptional deal activity.

## Bull case: CCB growth remains strong enough to support FY26 and FY27

Bulls argue that 1Q26 reinforced confidence in SAP's cloud growth outlook. CCB increased 25% cc to €21.9bn, while cloud revenue grew 27% cc and Cloud ERP Suite revenue grew 30% cc. Management indicated that only modest backlog deceleration should occur during FY26 vs FY25 and reiterated confidence in the medium-term demand environment.

The backlog provides substantial visibility because it represents contracted future revenue, supporting SAP's target of 23-25% cc cloud revenue growth in 2026. Management also highlighted that several large contracts signed in late 2025 are still ramping and should contribute more meaningfully in FY27.

A key structural support remains the ongoing S/4 migration cycle. We estimate that approximately 55% of the ECC installed base has migrated, leaving a large conversion opportunity ahead of end-of-support deadlines (Exhibit 4 and Exhibit 5). For many customers, these multi-year migrations are mission-critical and difficult to postpone. We can further argue that SAP is relatively insulated from macroeconomic weakness because ERP systems are core infrastructure rather than discretionary software spending.

What bulls want to see in 2Q26: CCB growth remaining around 24-25% cc; Cloud ERP growth staying near 30% cc; continued confidence in FY26 and FY27 demand trends; and no evidence of meaningful deal slippage or execution issues.

Bull summary: SAP's cloud growth remains structurally supported by backlog visibility, S/4 migrations, and market share gains, making low-to-mid-20s CCB growth sustainable through FY26 and potentially FY27.

## Bear case: 1Q26 was the high-water mark

Bears contend that the 1Q26 strength may have reflected exceptional execution and quarter-end deal closures rather than a lasting acceleration in demand. They note that SAP continues to guide for some moderation in backlog growth during FY26 and has acknowledged quarter-specific factors that benefited cloud revenue in 1Q26 but are unlikely to repeat.

Skeptics also point to the market's reaction following 4Q25, when backlog growth disappointed relative to expectations despite strong reported cloud revenue. This highlighted investor sensitivity to any signs of weakening future demand. The concern is that SAP could once again deliver solid near-term revenue while backlog growth slows, raising questions about the durability of future growth.

Further risks stem from longer sales cycles, particularly for large public-sector, sovereign cloud, and RISE transformation projects. Because quarterly backlog growth can be heavily influenced by a few large transactions, delays in mega-deal closures could materially affect reported growth rates even if underlying demand remains healthy.

A CCB growth rate closer to 22-23% in coming quarters could revive concerns that SAP's cloud growth is gradually normalizing and may drift toward the high teens beyond FY26.

Key bear triggers in 2Q26: CCB growth below approximately 23% cc; evidence of elongating sales cycles or delayed large deals; more cautious management commentary regarding 2H26 bookings; and signs that backlog growth is trending toward the mid-teens rather than the low 20s.

Bear summary: The strong 1Q26 results may have been boosted by one-off factors and large deal timing, while slowing backlog growth or longer sales cycles could signal that SAP's cloud trajectory is beginning to normalize.

## Bottom line

The central question is whether 1Q26 confirmed a durable cloud growth trajectory or simply masked the underlying slowdown that first appeared in the weaker 4Q25 backlog performance. Investors will focus closely on 2Q26 backlog growth, cloud revenue growth excluding one-off factors, and management's commentary on large-deal activity. A CCB growth rate at or above 24-25% cc would strengthen the bull case, whereas a decline toward 22-23% cc or below would likely reignite concerns about the sustainability of SAP's long-term cloud growth outlook.
