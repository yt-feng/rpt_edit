# The Analog Playbook: 2Q26 800v dynamics in play

We launch our quarterly analog playbook identifying market focus into the print. This quarter we expect investor focus on (i) whether improving orders are a reflection of inventory restocking or genuine end-market acceleration, and (ii) power semi deployment in the data center.

We expect the Q2 quarterly prints to build on the improving demand trends that emerged last quarter. Channel inventories continue to screen lean, gradual replenishment is underway and pricing pressure is easing providing a supportive backdrop for another quarter of above-seasonal performance. The key debate will be whether improving orders reflect the start of a broader restocking cycle or genuine end-market acceleration. Based on our channel checks, we continue to favour the latter, with companies highlighting pricing discipline, limited order cancellations and longer customer order visibility as signs of healthier underlying demand rather than speculative inventory builds. Alongside improving factory utilisation and lower underutilisation charges, we expect these dynamics to support further margin expansion, reinforcing our view that the analog sector is entering the early stages of a more durable earnings recovery. With this note we launch our global analog model tracking group revenue, margin trajectory, FCF dynamics and valuation metrics by stock.

Investor focus this earnings season is likely to centre on the implications of Nvidia replacing the Kyber rack architecture with a new solution, and the implications for power semiconductor deployment into 2027/28. While we acknowledge execution risk around the phasing of 800V adoption as rack architectures evolve, our channel checks indicate sidecar power systems are being introduced independently at lower voltages, supporting shipments from 3Q26 and helping limit downside to our server rack assumptions. We therefore expect Infineon to reaffirm its €1.5bn (FY26) and €2.5bn (FY27) data centre sales guidance, underpinned by a structurally rising power content per rack and improving \$/kW economics. Meanwhile, accelerating demand for optical networking solutions remains a key upside driver for STMicroelectronics, where rollout of the PIC100 and higher-margin optical products could support further upgrades to revenue and margin guidance.

Stock in focus: STMicroelectronics. We are calling out STMicroelectronics as our preferred analog stock this quarter for an earnings surprise. Ahead of the quarter, we lifted our PT to €78 on growing confidence in STM's optical exposure and cyclical recovery. For FY26, we estimate 9% and 30% yy growth for the automotive and industrial segments respectively and now see data center sales growing at a 77% CAGR FY26 to FY28, surpassing \$3bn in FY28. Debate into the quarter: restocking vs durable demand. Across our US and European analog coverage, inventory screens early-cycle constructive. Customer and distributor inventories are lean, gradual replenishment has begun, lead times are improving, and pricing pressure is easing. However, the key debate is restocking versus durable demand. # 2Q26 Theme: Data Center Power and Networking

## 1. Powering the Data Center

Intrigue on potential 800v delays hasn't gone away. Reports on delayed Kyber architecture have halted the YTD run of power semi beneficiaries. For instance, while Infineon has traded up 92% YTD, the stock is down 3% in the last month as the market has struggled to interpret what potential delays may mean for the power architecture. Into this reporting season we expect investor focus to remain on the nature of these delays and what the implications may be for the trajectory of power semi deployment to the data center into 2027 and 2028. While we acknowledge that the Kyber architecture is being replaced (see NVDA NDR highlights diversified growth opportunities), we think our thesis on the roll out of power semi content in the data center remains intact. Our channel checks suggest that the side car is being introduced at lower voltages and isn't contingent on the deployment of Kyber architecture. Rather, we expect side cars to begin shipping in 3Q26 (see Computex 2026 Takeaways), suggesting little downside risk to our server rack estimates.

Expect Infineon to reaffirm data center guidance. In the meantime, there is a growing \$/kw story regardless of a replacement Kyber architecture. We continue to model power semi content per rack ramping from \$128/kW for Rubin to \$191/kW for Feynman. At the last print management spoke to a \$175/kW average of power semi content in the data center and reaffirmed guidance of €1.5bn and €2.5bn sales for FY26 and FY27, respectively. With demand coming in earlier than expected and potential price improvements, we don't think the company will cut estimates and instead at least confirm this guidance.

# Stock in Focus: STMicroelectronics

We are Overweight STM, PT €78. We value the core business at 18x, consistent with the cyclical story, and optical/LEO at 36x to reflect structural growth potential. We continue to expect optics to become an increasing share of the business as data centre networking shifts from copper to photonics while LEO is also emerging as a material growth vector. As with the peer group, the underlying business is improving with better visibility and easing pricing pressure.

Cyclical recovery signalled last quarter. STMicro printed broadly in-line Q1 sales at \$3.1bn and GM% at 33.8%. Both the Q2 sales and margins beat consensus, with sales guided to be up 11.6% sequentially at \$3.45bn. Notably, management spoke to lower unused capacity charges at 100bps (vs our estimate at c.200bps) while speaking to improved visibility and shortening lead times. After a prolonged inventory correction across industrial and automotive markets, management is now seeing stronger order intake, improving visibility, cleaner channel inventories and a book-to-bill ratio above one across all major end markets. Industrial demand appears to have bottomed, general-purpose MCU inventories are normalising and pricing has stabilised, with selective MCU price increases demonstrating improving pricing power.

Data centre guidance lifted; optical exposure underappreciated. STMicroelectronics data centre revenue can be segmented into two categories: (i) power and (ii) optics. The real structural opportunity lies in silicon photonics, where ST is becoming a key supplier of PICs/EICs, optical engines and high-performance MCUs for hyperscaler AI networks. Management has repeatedly increased its data-centre outlook, with growth increasingly driven by photonics rather than power, and we expect optical to become the dominant component of AI data-centre revenue as the PIC100 ramps and NPO architectures emerge. This mix shift should not only accelerate revenue growth but also lift margins, with optical and LEO expected to generate materially higher gross margins than the legacy business due to favourable product mix and improving utilisation. In April we upgraded STMicro on the optical opportunity (see Optical Upside: Move to Overweight) and recently lifted our PT to €78 (see Underappreciated optical earnings power).

Low-earth orbit taking off. We see STMicro as one of the strongest semiconductor beneficiaries of the emerging low earth orbit (LEO) satellite ecosystem, with a differentiated portfolio spanning RF BiCMOS, digital ASICs, analog ICs, MCUs and advanced panel-level packaging across user terminals, satellites and gateway infrastructure. Management expects cumulative LEO revenue to exceed \$3bn over FY26-28, with sales growing from c.\$600m in 2025 to around \$1bn in 2026 as deployments accelerate and customer diversification expands beyond its largest programme. User terminals represent the largest revenue opportunity, supported by STM's leading RF front-end and beamforming technologies, while satellite content provides additional upside through higher-value RF, ASIC and MCU solutions. We expect LEO sales to grow at a c.48% CAGR through FY28, whereby we see user terminals as the most significant growth driver at a 52% CAGR.
