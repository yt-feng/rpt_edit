EN_A: So there is a new quarterly report on analog semiconductors from a global investment bank. B, what is the big picture here?

EN_A: What is the difference between restocking and real demand?
EN_B: Big difference. Restocking is short term, companies just refilling shelves. Real demand means end markets are accelerating, supporting a more durable earnings recovery. Our channel checks point to the latter.
EN_A: What makes you say that?
EN_B: Pricing discipline, limited order cancellations, and longer customer order visibility. These are not signs of speculative inventory builds. Factory utilization is also improving, which supports further margin expansion.
EN_A: So analog semiconductors are entering an early stage of a durable earnings recovery?
EN_B: Exactly. That is our core call. The other focus is power semiconductors in data centers, especially after Nvidia replaced its rack architecture.
EN_A: Nvidia is changing its architecture? How does that affect power semiconductor deployment?
EN_B: Nvidia is replacing the Kyber rack architecture with a new solution. The market worries about delays in 800V deployment. But our channel checks show sidecar power systems are being introduced independently at lower voltages, starting shipments from the third quarter of 2026. This limits downside to our server rack assumptions.
EN_A: So Infineon should be able to maintain its data center sales guidance?
EN_B: Yes. We expect Infineon to reaffirm its 1.5 billion euro guidance for fiscal 2026 and 2.5 billion for fiscal 2027. The driver is structurally rising power content per rack and improving dollar per kilowatt economics.
EN_A: What about STMicroelectronics? The report seems to highlight it.
EN_B: STM is our preferred analog stock this quarter. We like its optical networking exposure. The rollout of PIC100 and higher-margin optical products could drive further upgrades to revenue and margin guidance. We just raised our price target to 78 euros.
EN_A: Optical networking demand is accelerating?
EN_B: Yes. It is a key upside driver. We estimate data center sales will grow at a 77% CAGR from fiscal 2026 to 2028, surpassing 3 billion dollars in 2028.
EN_A: And there is also the low Earth orbit satellite story?
EN_B: Right. We model LEO sales ramping from 815 million dollars in fiscal 2026 to 1.8 billion in fiscal 2028. We recently moved to a sum-of-the-parts valuation to better reflect these structural growth opportunities.
EN_A: Sum-of-the-parts? How does that work?
EN_B: We apply a 36x P/E multiple to growth segments like LEO and optical, and an 18x multiple to the cyclical segment. That sets our 78 euro price target.
EN_A: That makes sense. But what questions does the report leave unanswered?

EN_A: And the sustainability of inventory improvement?
EN_B: Yes. While we see inventory improving, whether this persists into 2027 is not fully answered. The full report includes a global analog model to track these metrics company by company.
