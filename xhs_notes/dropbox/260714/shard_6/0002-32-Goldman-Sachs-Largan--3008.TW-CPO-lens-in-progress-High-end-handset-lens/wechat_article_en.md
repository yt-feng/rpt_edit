## Largan's High-Precision Alignment Capability Is a Direct Bridge from Smartphone Lenses to CPO Fiber Arrays

This matters because CPO switches require fiber arrays to be positioned with micron-level accuracy relative to photonic integrated circuits. The tolerance requirements for grating coupling, which is the current mainstream CPO architecture, are significantly tighter than for edge coupling alternatives.

Largan's fiber array product is designed specifically for grating coupling, which places it in direct competition with the highest-value segment of the CPO optics market. The company's internal development of alignment and testing equipment means it is not dependent on third-party tooling vendors for the most critical step in the manufacturing process. This is the same strategy Largan employed to dominate the handset lens market, where it built proprietary mold design and assembly capabilities that competitors have struggled to replicate. The parallel is instructive. The company's current CPO pipeline confirms that this is not a research experiment. The pilot line for fiber array production is scheduled for completion in late 3Q26. This sequencing is consistent with a company that is methodically building manufacturing capability before committing to volume production. ## The GlassBridge Competitive Threat Is Overstated Because the Two Technologies Address Different Coupling Architectures

Observers following the CPO ecosystem have heard about GlassBridge as an alternative approach to optical coupling in switches. Largan's management addressed this directly, and their response should give confidence to those concerned about technological disruption. GlassBridge is designed for edge coupling CPO switches, while the current mainstream architecture is grating coupling. Largan's fiber array is optimized for grating coupling. The two technologies do not directly compete at this stage of the market's development.

More importantly, GlassBridge remains at an early stage of commercialization. Management noted that it will take time for mass production, ecosystem development, and customer qualification to mature. The precision level required for fiber arrays in edge coupling applications is also lower than what grating coupling demands. This means that even if GlassBridge eventually gains adoption, it addresses a different segment of the CPO market where Largan's precision advantage is less relevant. The company's current positioning in grating coupling is the higher-value, higher-barrier-to-entry part of the CPO optics value chain.

This distinction matters for valuation because it reduces the risk that Largan's CPO investment becomes stranded by a competing technology standard. The company has placed its bet on the architecture that currently has the most industry traction, and its manufacturing capabilities are specifically tailored to that architecture. If the industry shifts toward edge coupling in the future, Largan can adapt, but the precision requirements will be lower, which means more competitors could enter. The grating coupling path is the one that plays to Largan's existing strengths.
