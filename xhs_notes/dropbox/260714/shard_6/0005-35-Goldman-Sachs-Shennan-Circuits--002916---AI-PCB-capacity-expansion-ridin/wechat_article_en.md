## The AI PCB Specification Upgrade Is Driving Dollar Content Expansion, Not Just Volume Growth

Shennan's revenue growth is accelerating on a compound basis, but the more important shift is happening within the margin structure. Gross margin improved from 24.8% in 2024 to 28.3% in 2025, and is projected to reach 29.5% in 2026, 31.6% in 2027, and 31.7% in 2028. Operating margin follows a similar trajectory, rising from 11.3% in 2024 to an estimated 22.4% by 2028. These are not cyclical improvements. They reflect a permanent shift in the product mix toward higher-complexity boards.

The mechanism is straightforward. AI servers require PCBs with more layers, tighter impedance control, and higher thermal reliability than standard server boards. High-speed switches for data center fabrics demand even greater signal integrity. Each generation of AI infrastructure raises the technical bar, and each raise forces out lower-tier PCB suppliers while rewarding those with the process capability to qualify. Shennan has been qualifying at the top end of this spectrum, which is why its gross margin is expanding even as revenue nearly doubles from 2025 to 2027.

The 1.6T optical module PCB ramp is a particularly important vector. Optical modules are the connective tissue of AI clusters, and the transition from 800G to 1.6T modules requires PCBs that can handle higher data rates without signal loss. Shennan's early positioning in this transition gives it a multi-year design win cycle that is difficult for competitors to dislodge. The revenue contribution from optical module PCBs is not separately disclosed, but the implied growth in the company's data center exposure suggests it is becoming a material earnings driver.

## Diversified Data Center Exposure Reduces Single-Point-of-Failure Risk

Shennan's exposure to AI infrastructure is not monolithic. The company serves three distinct segments within the data center: AI servers, high-speed switches, and optical modules. Each segment has different demand drivers, different customer bases, and different competitive dynamics. This diversification matters because it reduces the risk that a slowdown in any single segment derails the overall growth trajectory.

AI server PCBs are the largest and most visible driver, but they are also the most competitive. High-speed switch PCBs require even higher technical specifications and have fewer qualified suppliers, which supports pricing power. Optical module PCBs are smaller in dollar terms but have faster product cycles and higher turnover, which can support margin stability. The combination creates a portfolio effect that makes Shennan's earnings stream more predictable than a pure-play AI server PCB supplier.

The memory BT substrate business adds another layer of diversification. While not directly tied to AI compute, memory substrates benefit from the same data center buildout, as higher memory content per server drives demand for advanced packaging substrates. The ABF substrate business, which has been a drag on margins in prior years, is showing gradual improvement. If ABF margins turn positive on a sustained basis, it would add another leg to the earnings story.

## A Decision Framework for Evaluating Shennan's Risk-Reward Profile

Observers should assess Shennan against four criteria that determine whether the current valuation is justified by the underlying business dynamics.

First, monitor the pace of capacity utilization at the Wuxi factory. The Rmb6 billion capex plan assumes that new capacity will be filled within 12 to 18 months of coming online. If utilization rates fall below 80% in the first two quarters of operation, it would signal that demand is not keeping pace with supply additions. Quarterly revenue growth rates and management commentary on utilization will be the key indicators.

Second, track gross margin trajectory by segment. The company's blended gross margin is rising, but the composition matters. If margin expansion is driven entirely by mix shift toward higher-margin products, it is sustainable. If it is driven by temporary pricing power in a supply-constrained market, it is vulnerable to mean reversion as competitors add capacity. The analyst team's gross margin estimates imply a gradual improvement that stabilizes around 32% by 2028, which is consistent with a mix-driven rather than a pricing-driven story.

Third, assess customer concentration. The report identifies customer concentration as a risk, and it is a legitimate concern. Shennan's revenue is increasingly tied to a small number of large AI infrastructure buyers. If any one customer shifts its supply chain or develops in-house PCB capabilities, the impact on revenue and margins could be material. Observers should look for signs of customer base broadening in quarterly disclosures.

Fourth, evaluate the competitive response. The PCB industry has historically been characterized by capacity cycles that lead to margin compression. Shennan's current advantage lies in its ability to qualify for the most demanding specifications. If competitors invest in similar capability, the qualification advantage erodes. The share issuance plan gives Shennan a funding advantage, but it is not insurmountable.
