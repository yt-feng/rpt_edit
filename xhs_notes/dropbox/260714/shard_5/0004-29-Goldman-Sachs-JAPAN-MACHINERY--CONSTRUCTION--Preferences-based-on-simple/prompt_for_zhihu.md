# Preferences based on simple risk-reward amid an unclear cycle; stay Buy on Kubota

The construction machinery sector is currently in a phase of structurally poor visibility where entirely different cyclical phases co-exist depending on end demand, making it difficult to uniformly determine the direction of the sector as a whole. While macroeconomic tailwinds, such as the easing of US tariffs announced in June, are acting to support earnings, stock selection remains difficult, as companies showing clear visibility on their earnings direction are limited, primarily due to fluctuations in demand. In this report, we summarize the demand environment for each end market, and update our earnings estimates and views for the companies in our coverage.

## General construction: Upturn in inventory cycle a tailwind, underlying end demand the focus

Looking at construction demand trends, particularly for the largest market of North America, firmness in certain sectors such as data centers is attracting attention. At the same time, US construction investment has been trending mostly flat from the end-FY2024 level, even before adjusting for inflation. While it remains at a historically high level, slight downside risks are beginning to be recognized, rather than a scenario of significant upside in construction investment from here, due to factors such as declining construction investment for the manufacturing sector.

We see the recent improvement in shipments and production as mainly due to an upturn in the inventory cycle, rather than a full-scale recovery in end demand. Inventory at the distributor and other dealer levels underwent adjustments from 2024 to 2025, and as a result of excess inventory being cleared to a fairly healthy level, we believe it is currently in an upcycle. Robust wholesale demand is likely to continue for the next few quarters until this inventory restocking is complete, but looking further ahead, structural demand expansion is unlikely unless end demand fully recovers. The near term is favorable, but we think the next focus will be the trend in end demand after this inventory cycle runs its course.

## Compact construction machinery: Excess inventory of mini excavators a drag, reliance on loaders likely to strengthen further

As recent US housing starts data show, housing-related construction demand is clearly slowing. With concerns over additional interest rate hikes in the second half also lingering, if this scenario materializes, further deterioration in the US housing market is possible.

This softening in housing-related demand will likely hit mini excavators particularly hard, as they have a high weighting toward housing usage. In this segment, inventory levels are excessive. We have repeatedly pointed out the need for inventory adjustments for some time, and we believe the likelihood of this has increased further in light of the recent deterioration in data. Although temporary strength was seen in early spring, the background to this was not necessarily clear. We believe that the inevitable downcycle has finally materialized.

On the other hand, loaders, which have a broad range of customers, continue to trend firmly. However, inventories have already reached a healthy level, making it difficult to replicate the strong demand expansion seen over the past few years. Considering this together with the challenging situation for mini excavators, the overall compact construction machinery segment is in a phase of strong downward pressure, in our view. With no full-scale recovery in end demand in sight, we believe a cautious stance is warranted for the time being.

## Tractors: Both compact and mid-sized retail demand in North America weaker than expected

The current demand environment is missing initial expectations, with recent retail sales for both compact and mid-sized tractors in North America trending below our initial assumptions. However, ample room remains for recovery from a wholesale perspective, given that a considerable degree of inventory adjustment took place through last year; a sense that demand is bottoming out is gradually building. From a medium- to long-term perspective, historically low tractor sales volumes provide important implications. This low level signifies an accumulation of replacement demand, and we thus see a high likelihood of a turn to positive growth in 2027.

On the other hand, risk factors also remain. In a scenario where interest rate hikes are implemented in 2H, farmers' purchasing appetite would be further suppressed, which could serve as a headwind. Moreover, if signs of improvement in retail demand cannot be confirmed in 2H, the 2027 recovery scenario might not eventuate. Based on these assumptions, while we forecast wholesale sales for FY12/26 on the assumption that recent retail demand continues, we factor in a gradual demand recovery of c.5% for compact and mid-sized tractors as our base scenario for 2027. Rather than a strong rebound, we assume a cautious but steady recovery on the back of inventory cycle normalization and the materialization of replacement demand.

Demand trends in Asia, particularly Indonesia, are important for Komatsu. Phases when the company's earnings are strong generally coincide with favorable thermal coal prices. Given the widening spread between thermal coal prices and diesel prices for construction machinery in Indonesia, the natural interpretation is that demand is bottoming out. However, recent construction machinery shipments in Indonesia remain sluggish, and an actual recovery has not yet been confirmed.

In addition, caution is required as the policy environment in Indonesia is changing significantly, and this situation may continue for the time being. The main policy trends are as follows:

\- Negative factor: The centralized management of coal exports by state-owned enterprise DSI announced on June 1 (full operation from January 1, 2027) brings uncertainty to the business operations of private mining companies.

■ Positive factor: The Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (ESDM) clarified its policy to accept applications (application period: July 1 to 31) for revisions to production quotas in the 2026 RKAB (coal business plan and budget). This could lead to an expansion of production capacity.

■ Positive factor: A review of the coal prices applied to domestic market obligations (DMO) is under consideration, and if realized, this could help improve the profitability of mining companies.

Viewed overall, if coal market conditions do not deteriorate further, we would expect a gradual positive direction as a whole. Given the underlying strength in copper, we view the most likely scenario for overall mining equipment demand to be flat at high levels to a slight increase. However, as Indonesian policy trends and the direction of thermal coal market prices will remain important variables, we will be closely watching trends heading in 2H.

## Our view

In terms of earnings, yen depreciation and the reduction in US Section 232 tariffs announced in June are acting as tailwinds, so the external environment is generally heading in an improving direction. On the other hand, demand has been weaker than expected, with US tractor demand in 1H missing initial assumptions. Furthermore, tractor demand in Thailand, which was expected to rebound from last year's sluggish levels, is likely to post negative yoy growth as higher fuel prices dampen farmers' purchasing appetite.

Overall, external factors such as forex and tariffs are offsetting the downside in demand, leaving the earnings outlook for FY12/26 largely unchanged as a whole. However, considering that current US and Thai tractor demand is at historically low levels, we see a high likelihood of a turn to positive growth in FY12/27, albeit not a sharp one, on the back of the accumulation of replacement demand and the normalization of the inventory cycle. Based on this assumption, we expect profits to continue to grow in FY12/27 as well.

Based on the above, we revise our estimate assumptions as follows. We change our forex assumption for USD/JPY to ¥160, from ¥155, and factor in the impact of reduced US tariffs, raising our FY12/26 operating profit estimate by 7%. Furthermore, from FY12/27, we raise our estimates by c.20%, reflecting the demand recovery scenario and revisions to our forex and tariff assumptions.

On the other hand, taking into account the impact of higher interest rates on the balance sheet and cost of equity, we lower our target price to ¥3,300, from ¥3,400. However, we maintain our Buy rating, given the likelihood of a demand recovery and the continuation of profit growth from FY12/27.

The current valuation is c.14X based on the consensus P/E estimate for FY12/26, positioning it at the median level of the average range (12-16X) in past cycles. Given that the current demand environment is in a transitional phase that is difficult to definitively call either an upcycle or a downcycle, the current share price level could be considered a reasonable valuation.

In addition, the I/B/E/S consensus operating profit forecast remains conservative at ¥320 bn, and considering the divergence from our estimate, there is room for upward revision to consensus. Going forward, if potential catalysts such as an upward revision to full-year guidance or the announcement of additional share buybacks were to materialize, this could lift both earnings forecasts and the share price. Current valuations price in neither excessive optimism nor pessimism, and this could therefore be viewed a good opportunity to add to positions while waiting for catalysts, in our view.
