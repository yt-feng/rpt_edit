## Hygon's Integrated "CPU Plus GPU" Model Creates a Structural Advantage That Pure-Play GPU Competitors Cannot Match

The conventional framing in China's semiconductor research community has been that GPU companies deserve premium valuations because GPUs are the scarce, high-growth bottleneck in AI compute. CPUs, by contrast, are seen as commoditized, slower-growing, and largely unrestricted by export controls. This framing misses the most important strategic reality of China's AI infrastructure buildout: the system integrator, Sugon, owns 27.96% of Hygon. That ownership link is not a passive financial holding. Sugon is China's leading supercomputer and AI infrastructure builder, spanning chips, computing, storage, networking, cooling, applications, and services. When Sugon designs a cluster like the Sugon 8000, it has every incentive to maximize the use of Hygon chips across the entire architecture. This is not a vendor selection process. It is an ecosystem optimization.

Hygon's product portfolio covers the CPU that manages general-purpose server workloads, the GPU that accelerates AI training and inference, the scale-up switch ASIC that interconnects GPUs within a board, the PCIe 5.0 switch chip that connects CPUs and GPUs, the 400/800G switch ASIC for scale-out networking, and the 400G NIC ASIC. No other Chinese semiconductor company offers this breadth. A pure-play GPU firm can provide accelerators, but it cannot optimize the data flow between CPU memory and GPU memory, cannot tune the network fabric for its own chips, and cannot guarantee that the system software stack is fully compatible. Hygon can do all of these things because its chips are designed together and validated together inside Sugon's systems. This architectural integration translates into real performance advantages in latency, power efficiency, and software reliability. It also translates into a procurement advantage: when a Chinese government entity or state-owned enterprise issues a tender for AI infrastructure, a Sugon system with Hygon chips is a single-vendor, fully domestic solution. A system built with a mix of domestic GPUs and foreign CPUs, or with chips from multiple domestic vendors, introduces integration risk and supply-chain complexity. Hygon eliminates that risk.

## China's Server CPU TAM Is Growing Faster Than Its AI GPU TAM, Making the CPU Business a Higher-Growth Opportunity Than Many Assume

Many observers assign lower valuation multiples to CPU businesses because they assume CPU growth is structurally slower than GPU growth. In the global market, that assumption is reasonable. In China, it is not. A global research report projects that China's server CPU total addressable market will grow at a 31% CAGR through 2030, reaching $42 billion. Over the same period, China's AI GPU TAM is projected to grow at a 23% CAGR, reaching $91 billion. The CPU TAM is growing faster for two reasons. First, agentic AI architectures, in which multiple AI agents coordinate to execute complex workflows, place dramatically higher demands on CPU performance. In a multi-agent system, the CPU is responsible for workflow orchestration, task scheduling, memory management, and inter-agent communication. These are not GPU-accelerable workloads. They are CPU-bound. As AI systems evolve from single-model inference to multi-agent collaboration, the CPU becomes a bottleneck and a value driver, not a peripheral component.

Second, China's CPU localization drive is accelerating due to policy mandates, procurement requirements, and supply-chain security considerations. Unlike GPUs, which are subject to U.S. export controls that have already created a domestic substitution imperative, CPUs have historically been less restricted. That is changing. Chinese government entities and state-owned enterprises are increasingly required to source domestic CPUs for new server deployments. The combination of agentic AI demand and localization policy creates a structural growth vector for domestic CPU suppliers that does not exist in other markets. Hygon, as one of the few domestic CPU vendors with proven performance in hyperscale deployments, is positioned to capture a disproportionate share of this growth. The report projects Hygon's share of China's server CPU market will reach 18% by 2030, up from a significantly lower base today.

## A Decision Framework for Assessing Hygon's Relative Positioning in China's AI Semiconductor Market

Observers evaluating Hygon against pure-play GPU competitors should apply a structured framework based on three dimensions: architectural breadth, ecosystem lock-in, and demand tailwind composition.

On architectural breadth, Hygon scores higher than any pure-play GPU competitor because it controls the CPU, GPU, and networking chips. This breadth reduces system-level integration risk and enables performance optimization across the entire compute stack. Pure-play GPU firms can only optimize the accelerator layer. They depend on third-party CPUs and networking chips, which introduces compatibility risk and limits their ability to deliver turnkey solutions.

On ecosystem lock-in, Hygon benefits from its ownership relationship with Sugon, which is the dominant domestic system integrator for AI superclusters. This relationship is not contractual. It is structural. Sugon has a financial incentive to maximize Hygon content in its systems. No pure-play GPU firm has a comparable relationship with a system integrator of Sugon's scale and capability. This lock-in extends beyond procurement to software optimization, system validation, and aftermarket support. When a customer buys a Sugon cluster, the Hygon chips inside it are not just components. They are part of a validated, supported, and optimized system.

On demand tailwind composition, Hygon benefits from three overlapping growth drivers: AI GPU demand, general-purpose server CPU demand, and agentic AI CPU demand. Pure-play GPU firms benefit from only the first driver. The CPU demand tailwinds, which are growing faster than GPU demand and are reinforced by localization policy, provide Hygon with a diversified revenue base and a higher sustainable growth trajectory than its GPU-only peers.

Applying this framework leads to a clear conclusion: Hygon deserves a valuation multiple that is at least comparable to, and potentially higher than, that of pure-play GPU competitors. The CPU business is not a drag on valuation. It is a growth engine that the market is underestimating.
