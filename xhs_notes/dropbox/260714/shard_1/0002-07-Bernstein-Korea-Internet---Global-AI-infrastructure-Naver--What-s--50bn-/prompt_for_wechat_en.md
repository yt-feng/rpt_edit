Structurally, Naver's AI factory shares many characteristics with leading neocloud providers such as CoreWeave today. The building blocks are familiar: SPVs, contract-backed capex, GPU-native infrastructure, and an NVIDIA-centric ecosystem designed for speed, density, and rapid deployment. At first glance, the model resembles the emerging neocloud playbook seen across the industry.

Where Naver may ultimately differentiate itself is not necessarily in how it builds AI infrastructure today, but in what it intends to build on top of that infrastructure over time. While CoreWeave and other neocloud players are seeking to monetize growing demand for AI compute, Naver's longer-term ambition appears broader - leveraging AI infrastructure as a foundation for search, services, and a regional AI ecosystem.

## Why Naver resembles a Neocloud today

At the infrastructure level, Naver's strategy shares many of the attributes that have defined the neocloud model. The buildout relies on dedicated project financing structures, visibility on long-term demand, and accelerated deployment of AI-native capacity rather than the traditional cloud investment cycle.

Like its global peers, Naver is emphasizing high-density GPU infrastructure and close alignment with NVIDIA. The initial 55 MW deployment and longer-term expansion ambitions suggest an approach that is more consistent with purpose-built AI infrastructure providers than with traditional telecom operators or colocation businesses.

In this respect, Naver's economics during the early phases of development may look similar to those of CoreWeave and other neocloud operators. Success will depend on many of the same factors: utilization, power availability, financing execution, and the ability to secure long-term customer demand.

## Where Naver's long-term vision may differ

The key distinction, in our view, lies less in today's infrastructure model and more in the potential evolution of the platform. While neocloud providers increasingly offer software and platform services alongside infrastructure, Naver enters the AI era with several assets that extend beyond compute: a search franchise, consumer and enterprise distribution, Korean-language AI capabilities, local content ecosystems, and a strategic position within Korea's broader digital infrastructure landscape.

As a result, we believe Naver is attempting to build more than a pure AI infrastructure business. Over time, management's ambitions appear increasingly focused on connecting infrastructure, models, applications, and services into a broader regional AI ecosystem. This is not to suggest that Naver is fundamentally different from other neoclouds today. Rather, we believe the strategic opportunity may extend beyond infrastructure monetization if the company successfully leverages its existing platform assets.
