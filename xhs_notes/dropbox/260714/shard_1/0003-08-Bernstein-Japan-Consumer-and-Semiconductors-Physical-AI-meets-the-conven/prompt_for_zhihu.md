Last Friday, SoftBank Corp (9434.JP, not covered, consolidated by SoftBank Group) came shopping for convenience stores — and the real story is bigger than retail. SoftBank, PayPay and Sumitomo Mitsui Card are reportedly in talks to invest up to ¥300bn (US\$1.9bn) in Seven & i, operator of the world's largest convenience-store network (link). On its face, a telco buying into a mature retailer.

Look closer, and a different question emerges: where does AI actually prove it can do physical work? Not in a demo video, not in a data center — but somewhere with millions of daily transactions, chronic labor shortage, and thousands of identical floor plans on which an experiment can repeat itself. Japan's convenience stores are arguably the best such laboratory on earth: \~55,000 stores, tens of millions of visits a day, and an industry so starved of workers that automation is not a choice but a survival condition. That is why every deal of the past six years — Itochu×FamilyMart, Mitsubishi/KDDI×Lawson, and now the SoftBank coalition×Seven — has brought outside capital into a format everyone agrees is past its prime.

The format war has been decided — and yet ¥1.4tn (c. US\$9bn) has flowed to the losing side. CVS stagnation is structural: real sales flat for a decade, a 36.2-point fault line between the unit-price index (123.7) and the traffic index (87.5), a store network at its \~55,000 ceiling, and the overworked salaryman — the customer who bought time with money — going extinct as a demographic. Capital keeps coming anyway, because what is being bought is not the format but the network: the last physical piece of the economic zone, spanning supply chains (Itochu makes its money through FamilyMart's, not in it), payments traffic, finance (Seven Bank's ATM corner outearns the store around it, roughly 40x per square meter), and retail media (a ¥1.3tn / US\$8.4bn market by 2029).

And on that network, the story stops being about retail. For SoftBank Group, this is the missing bottom layer of the Physical AI stack Masayoshi Son has been assembling. SoftBank Group has invested in the full stack of AI, including infra, model, chip, and physical AI — but the physical AI portion still lacked the one place where AI meets the physical world daily, at scale, and gets graded. Japan's convenience stores are that place: a deployment field, a data engine, and the source of the most every day, falsifiable receipts for the thesis that AI rewrites every industry. Our conclusion: a losing format, a contested network — and survival decided by the execution that carries AI from showcase to chain-wide core systems. Plenty of investors read AI's future in data centers. Part of the answer sits on the shelf of the nearest convenience store...

We rate Seven & i Market-Perform; we remain Outperform on SoftBank Group on our constructive view of AI.
