Not in a demo video, not in a data center — but somewhere with millions of daily transactions, chronic labor shortage, and thousands of identical floor plans on which an experiment can repeat itself. Japan's convenience stores are arguably the best such laboratory on earth: \~55,000 stores, tens of millions of visits a day, and an industry so starved of workers that automation is not a choice but a survival condition. That is why every deal of the past six years — Itochu×FamilyMart, Mitsubishi/KDDI×Lawson, and now the SoftBank coalition×Seven — has brought outside capital into a format everyone agrees is past its prime.

Capital keeps coming anyway, because what is being bought is not the format but the network: the last physical piece of the economic zone, spanning supply chains (Itochu makes its money through FamilyMart's, not in it), payments traffic, finance (Seven Bank's ATM corner outearns the store around it, roughly 40x per square meter), and retail media (a ¥1.3tn / US\$8.4bn market by 2029).

Japan's convenience stores are that place: a deployment field, a data engine, and the source of the most every day, falsifiable receipts for the thesis that AI rewrites every industry. Our conclusion: a losing format, a contested network — and survival decided by the execution that carries AI from showcase to chain-wide core systems.
