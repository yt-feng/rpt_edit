【金字塔原理写作原则】
1. 结论先行：文章开头先回答“这份报告最值得看的判断是什么”，而不是介绍背景。
2. 统领思想：全文只能服务一个主判断，避免变成摘要合集。
3. 纵向回答：每一层都要回答上一层提出的“为什么”或“所以呢”。
4. 横向 MECE：每个一级小节必须彼此独立、共同支撑主判断，避免重叠。
5. Synthesis over summary：不要复述报告段落，要提炼“这些事实合在一起意味着什么”。
6. So what：每个小节末尾必须落到对行业、公司、竞争格局、市场定价或读者观察框架的含义。
7. Action title：所有 `##` 小标题必须是“直接讲述洞察的完整句子”，不能是目录标签。

Last Friday, SoftBank Corp (9434.JP, not covered, consolidated by SoftBank Group) came shopping for convenience stores — and the real story is bigger than retail. SoftBank, PayPay and Sumitomo Mitsui Card are reportedly in talks to invest up to ¥300bn (US\$1.9bn) in Seven & i, operator of the world's largest convenience-store network (link). On its face, a telco buying into a mature retailer.

Look closer, and a different question emerges: where does AI actually prove it can do physical work? Not in a demo video, not in a data center — but somewhere with millions of daily transactions, chronic labor shortage, and thousands of identical floor plans on which an experiment can repeat itself. Japan's convenience stores are arguably the best such laboratory on earth: \~55,000 stores, tens of millions of visits a day, and an industry so starved of workers that automation is not a choice but a survival condition. That is why every deal of the past six years — Itochu×FamilyMart, Mitsubishi/KDDI×Lawson, and now the SoftBank coalition×Seven — has brought outside capital into a format everyone agrees is past its prime.

CVS stagnation is structural: real sales flat for a decade, a 36.2-point fault line between the unit-price index (123.7) and the traffic index (87.5), a store network at its \~55,000 ceiling, and the overworked salaryman — the customer who bought time with money — going extinct as a demographic. Capital keeps coming anyway, because what is being bought is not the format but the network: the last physical piece of the economic zone, spanning supply chains (Itochu makes its money through FamilyMart's, not in it), payments traffic, finance (Seven Bank's ATM corner outearns the store around it, roughly 40x per square meter), and retail media (a ¥1.3tn / US\$8.4bn market by 2029).

Japan's convenience stores are that place: a deployment field, a data engine, and the source of the most every day, falsifiable receipts for the thesis that AI rewrites every industry. Our conclusion: a losing format, a contested network — and survival decided by the execution that carries AI from showcase to chain-wide core systems. Plenty of investors read AI's future in data centers. Part of the answer sits on the shelf of the nearest convenience store...
