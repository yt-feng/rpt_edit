## JPM FX Positioning Monitor

## First evidence of yen short covering; USD/CNY downside demand ebbs

\- First signs of yen short-covering. Following Japanese officials' comments about potential GPIF portfolio adjustments, we observed +2.5-sigma net USD/JPY put demand on Friday (Figure 1). In the options space at least, that 1d USD/JPY downside figure screens as the largest such flow since August 2025, just before the resumption of Fed easing in September. While we are skeptical that this may ultimately reverse the structural JPY depreciation trend (see here), this nevertheless represents the first signs of yen-short deleveraging, and more positioning adjustments could follow. Figure 2 shows a distribution of USD/JPY put strikes transacted on Friday, which observed a median of 159.75 and the lower $10^{\text{th}}$ percentile at 156.0.

\- Appetite for USD/CNY downside has markedly cooled. Really since Liberation Day, USD/CNY put volume has outpaced call volume as the market turned broadly short - a stark pivot from the persistent USD/CNY long bias from 2022-2024. That downside USD/CNY demand crescendoed in put options flow metrics in the first quarter of 2026, and more recently has been followed lower by alternative measures of CNY positioning (Figure 6). But now, that demand is ebbing - as evidenced by Figure 5 which shows the balance of USD/CNY calls and puts turning more neutral. To be sure, this doesn't screen as a wholesale capitulation of USD/CNY shorts, and we haven't seen the market turn decisively long even on short-term windows. It also remains the case that USD/CNY volumes remain understandably quite modest compared to the aforementioned 2022-2024 period (Figure 7) for various macro and geopolitical reasons. This is notable as well in the context of some other USD/Asia downside demand over the last week (TWD, KRW - Figure 8).
