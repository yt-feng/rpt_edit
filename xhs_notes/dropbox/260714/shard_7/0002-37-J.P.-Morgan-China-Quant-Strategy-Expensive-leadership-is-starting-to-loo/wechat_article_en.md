## Cross-Sectional Volatility at the 99th Percentile Signals That Stock-Level Disagreement Has Reached Breaking Point

Cross-sectional volatility, which measures the dispersion of individual stock returns around the market average, rose to the 99th percentile of the past 10 years in June. This is not a normal level of dispersion. It means that the difference between the best-performing and worst-performing stocks is at an extreme rarely observed outside of crisis periods. Index-level volatility also caught up, moving from the 29th percentile in May to the 77th percentile in June, but the index vol remains well below cross-sectional vol. That gap is itself a signal.

When cross-sectional vol is far higher than index vol, it indicates that the market is not moving as a whole. Instead, a small number of stocks are driving all the action while the rest of the market drifts. This is precisely what happened in June. The Momentum and Growth factors rose sharply, but 73% of stocks fell. The index declined because the broad market overwhelmed the narrow winners. The dispersion is not a sign of healthy differentiation. It is a sign that the market has bifurcated into two parallel universes: a small group of stocks where investors are willing to pay any price and a large group of stocks where investors are unwilling to pay any premium at all.

This level of dispersion is historically fragile. It can persist for a period, but it eventually resolves through one of two mechanisms: either the broad market catches up to the winners, pulling the index higher, or the winners fall back to the broad market, pulling the index lower. Given that the winners are trading at a median P/E of 114.9 times, the second path is more probable. The early July data supports that view.
