EN_A: So we have this new report on China’s high-frequency economic trackers. B, what’s the big takeaway here?
EN_B: The big picture is that exports remain the bright spot, but domestic demand weakness is tilting second quarter growth risks to the downside. The report uses real-time data from ports, production, fiscal and monetary channels to give us a clearer picture before official numbers come out.
EN_A: So what’s the core question this report is trying to answer?
EN_B: It’s really about whether we can predict economic momentum before the monthly hard data arrives. For example, port data suggests exports are accelerating, but production indicators like crude oil processing and steel are still contracting. So there’s a clear tension.
EN_A: Let’s start with the first key insight. How bright is the export picture?
EN_B: Very bright. In June, departing container and bulk ship tonnage both saw strong year-on-year growth. Overall departing shipments, excluding tankers, jumped from 7.7 percent in May to 17.9 percent in June. That means both prices and volumes are picking up. But oil tanker arrivals remain subdued, which suggests limited recovery in oil supply.
EN_A: What does that mean? Can strong exports offset the weak domestic demand?
EN_B: It helps, but it’s not enough to fully offset the drag. Domestic production is still weak. For instance, processed crude oil output may have deepened its contraction. Auto production growth is slowing, and steel is also contracting. The report specifically notes that operating rates at petroleum asphalt plants have declined, which directly signals weaker refining activity.
EN_A: And the second insight? How are fiscal and monetary policies responding?
EN_B: On the fiscal side, government bond issuance slowed in June to 616 billion yuan, down from 1.14 trillion in May. The report says that without a big month-end surge, fiscal delivery will remain less front-loaded by mid-year. On the monetary side, the PBOC injected 410 billion yuan via reverse repos but also withdrew 300 billion via outright operations. The report suggests a rate cut becomes more likely in the second half if growth headwinds intensify.
EN_A: So that means policy is still in a wait-and-see mode?
EN_B: Exactly. It’s more about supporting than stimulating. The report hints that if domestic demand stays weak, bond issuance and fund deployment could accelerate in the third quarter. But that’s something we’ll need to verify with upcoming data.
EN_A: And the third insight? What about housing and autos?
EN_B: Housing has some bright spots. New and secondary home sales improved year-on-year in June, but momentum has eased recently. The report is cautious—it doesn’t confirm whether this marks a bottom. For autos, passenger car retail sales dropped 22 percent year-on-year in May, though NEVs did better. The first week of June saw continued declines, partly due to lower trade-in subsidies and higher fuel costs.
EN_A: Sounds like there are still several open questions in the report.
EN_B: Definitely. For example, the sustainability of the housing sales improvement is uncertain. The report doesn’t give a definitive answer on whether the market has bottomed. Also, on inflation, while food price declines are narrowing, pork wholesale prices remain low due to overcapacity. These are questions that the full report explores with more data and models.
