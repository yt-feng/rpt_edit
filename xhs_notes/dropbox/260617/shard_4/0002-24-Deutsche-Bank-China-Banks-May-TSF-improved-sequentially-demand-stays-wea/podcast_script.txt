ZH_A: 今天我们要聊一份关于中国银行业的最新研报，这份报告分析了五月份的社融和信贷数据，数据本身似乎有回暖，但报告却用了一个很谨慎的判断。所以，B，这份报告的核心结论到底是什么？

ZH_B: 一句话概括就是，五月的数据确实比四月有所改善，但改善的力度非常有限，实体经济融资需求依然疲弱。社融和贷款余额的增速都创下了历史新低，这是一个需要警惕的信号。

ZH_A: 历史新低？这听起来比我们想象的更严峻。四月的数据确实很差，但五月不是反弹了吗？为什么增速反而创了新低？

ZH_B: 对，四月的数据是一个异常低的基数，所以五月环比改善是必然的。但报告强调的是同比和趋势。五月的新增社融是2万亿，比四月的6250亿高很多，但比去年五月的2.3万亿低。更关键的是，社融余额增速从四月的7.8%降到了7.7%，贷款余额增速也降到了5.5%，这两个数字都是历史最低点。

ZH_A: 所以，我们看到的“反弹”其实更像是一次技术性修复，而不是需求真正回暖的信号。那报告里有没有提到，是哪些具体的融资渠道拖了后腿？

ZH_B: 拖后腿的主要是居民贷款。五月居民贷款依然是负增长，减少了1410亿。虽然比四月的负7870亿收窄了很多，但连续两个月为负，说明居民在消费和买房上依然非常谨慎，还在主动去杠杆。另外，企业中长期贷款也是负的，减少了20亿，这直接反映了企业投资意愿不足。

ZH_A: 居民和企业都在收缩，那信贷增长靠什么撑住？政府债券看起来是个大头。

ZH_B: 没错，政府债券是五月社融的最大贡献者，发了1.2万亿。但报告也指出，这个发行速度比四月的1.5万亿是放缓的。所以财政的支持力度在边际上并没有继续加码。企业债券和股权融资倒是同比有所增长，但体量太小，不足以扭转大局。

ZH_A: 我注意到报告里还提到了一个很有意思的现象，就是存款的变化。五月新增存款1.8万亿，但居民和企业存款都是负的，钱去哪了？

ZH_B: 这恰恰是市场关注的一个焦点。钱主要流向了非银行金融机构存款，增加了1.1万亿。这背后反映的是存款搬家，居民和企业把存款取出来，去买理财、买基金、或者投入到资本市场。这跟M1增速的改善是相互印证的，说明有一部分资金开始活跃起来，但这未必是进入了实体经济。

ZH_A: 这听起来像是一个“脱实向虚”的迹象。那对于银行股的投资来说，这份报告给出了什么明确的指引吗？

ZH_B: 报告的逻辑很清晰。在整体信贷需求疲弱的环境下，银行之间的分化会加剧。他们更偏好那些对公业务占比大、经营稳健、股息率较高的大型银行。但这里有一个关键问题没有完全展开，就是如果居民和企业端的需求持续不振，大型银行的对公业务是否还能独善其身？这个风险敞口到底有多大？

ZH_A: 所以，这份报告虽然给出了方向，但留了一个很大的悬念给我们自己去深挖。如果你想了解这个风险敞口的具体分析，以及银行股后续的详细投资策略，我建议你去读一读这份完整报告。里面还有更多关于货币供应和存款结构的细节图表，能帮你把整个逻辑链补全。

EN_A: Alright, let’s talk about the latest research on China’s banks. The May credit data came out, and on the surface, it looks like a rebound from April. But the report’s tone is quite cautious. So, B, what is the one big takeaway here?

EN_B: The big takeaway is that while May showed some sequential improvement, the underlying demand for financing remains very weak. In fact, the growth rates for total social financing and bank loans both hit new historical lows. That’s the key number to focus on.

EN_A: New historical lows? That sounds more serious than a simple rebound. April was terrible, so a bounce was expected, but how can the growth rate be at a record low?

EN_B: Exactly. The improvement is mostly a technical recovery from a very low base. May’s new TSF was 2 trillion RMB, better than April’s 625 billion, but still below the 2.3 trillion from a year ago. The real story is the year-on-year growth rate, which dropped to 7.7 percent for TSF and 5.5 percent for loans. Both are the lowest levels ever recorded.

EN_A: So the “rebound” is more of a stabilization, not a sign of genuine demand recovery. Which parts of the economy are pulling the numbers down the most?

EN_B: The biggest drag is clearly the household sector. Household loans stayed negative for a second month, down by 141 billion RMB. It’s a smaller drop than April’s massive 787 billion, but it’s still negative. This means consumers are still very cautious about borrowing for mortgages or consumption. They are deleveraging. Corporate long-term loans were also negative, which tells you companies aren’t investing.

EN_A: So both households and companies are pulling back. What’s holding the whole thing up then? Government bonds, I assume?

EN_B: Yes, government bonds are the main pillar, contributing 1.2 trillion in May. But the report points out that this pace actually slowed from April’s 1.5 trillion. So fiscal support is not accelerating. Corporate bonds and equity issuance did grow year-on-year, but their volumes are too small to change the overall picture.

EN_A: I also noticed something interesting about deposits. Total deposits went up by 1.8 trillion, but both household and corporate deposits fell. Where did all that money go?

EN_B: That’s the fascinating part. It went into non-bank financial institution deposits, which surged by 1.1 trillion. This is a classic sign of deposit migration. People are pulling money out of bank accounts to buy wealth management products or put it into the stock market. It aligns with the slight improvement in M1 money supply, suggesting some cash is becoming more active, but not necessarily flowing into the real economy.

EN_A: So it sounds like money is moving from the real economy into financial markets. What does this mean for bank stocks? The report seems to have a clear preference.

EN_B: The report is quite direct. In this environment of weak credit demand, big banks with strong corporate lending exposure, stable operations, and decent dividend yields are the preferred picks. But here is the big question the report hints at but doesn’t fully answer: how long can these large banks sustain their performance if corporate and household demand remains this weak? The risk of a spillover is the real open question.

EN_A: So the report gives us a direction, but it leaves a major puzzle for us to solve. If you want to dig into that risk and see the full analysis with all the charts on money supply and deposit structure, you really need to read the complete report. It connects all the dots you need to make your own judgment.
