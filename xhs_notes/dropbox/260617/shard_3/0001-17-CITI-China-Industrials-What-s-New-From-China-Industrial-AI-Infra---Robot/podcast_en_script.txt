EN_A: So there’s this new report from a global investment bank on China’s industrial AI infrastructure and robotics. What’s the single biggest takeaway?
EN_B: The clearest message is that Chinese AI infrastructure suppliers are about to gain much more share inside Nvidia’s supply chain. And this is just the beginning. It could lead to multiple earnings upgrades.
EN_A: So you’re saying these Chinese players used to be bit players, and now they’re becoming key suppliers?
EN_B: Exactly. The report points out that in the GB200 architecture, Chinese suppliers had much lower wallet share compared to Japanese, Korean, and Taiwanese competitors. But for GB300 and Vera Rubin, that’s going to reverse. And it’s not because of price cuts. It’s because they’ve upgraded their technology, they respond faster to Nvidia’s constantly changing architecture, and they’re investing more in capacity.
EN_A: Which companies are the main beneficiaries?
EN_B: The report highlights three areas. First, PCB equipment, like Han’s CNC. Second, copper clad laminate, or CCL, with Shengyi Tech as the leader. Third, AI-grade fiberglass fabric, with KBL as the key name. One interesting detail: KBL’s AI fabric capacity is expected to surpass another major player, Grace Fabric, by 2027.
EN_A: Does that mean we’re heading for oversupply?
EN_B: Not right now. The report says the biggest capacity shortages in the entire CCL and PCB supply chain are in AI fabric and e-glass fabric. And Nvidia keeps upgrading its architectures, like Vera Rubin and Ultra Rubin, which demand higher-end materials. So demand is growing in sync.
EN_A: What about the robotics side? The report covers that too, right?
EN_B: Yes. The report mentions that Hengli Hydraulic and Rongtai have started shipping to a major US robotics company. They expect Optimus Gen 3 production to be 10,000 to 20,000 units this year, and then 10x that to over 100,000 next year. That’s why Hengli is now the top pick in the embodied AI chain.
EN_A: 100,000 units is a huge number. What about factory automation and excavators?
EN_B: For factory automation, companies like Tsugami and Bozhon see demand growth from Apple’s supply chain and AI-related manufacturing. For excavators, Hengli’s component line is already running at full capacity this year, driven by mining demand and exports. But the report also notes that property and fixed asset investment in China remain weak.
EN_A: So what is the report bearish on?
EN_B: It has sell ratings on names like CRRC, Dingli, Topband, Oppein, Heli, and Envicool. Take Envicool as an example. The report argues that AI liquid cooling is not a high-barrier technology, and the company is struggling to build a US team. That’s a very different view from what the market might expect.
EN_A: Any big unanswered questions from the report?
EN_B: Yes, a major one. The report does not conclude which material Nvidia will choose for Ultra Rubin — PTFE or Q-fabric. Shengyi Tech has prepared multiple solutions, but the real technical bottleneck is in PCB manufacturing, not CCL. That uncertainty could reshape the entire supply chain. The full report dives much deeper into this.
