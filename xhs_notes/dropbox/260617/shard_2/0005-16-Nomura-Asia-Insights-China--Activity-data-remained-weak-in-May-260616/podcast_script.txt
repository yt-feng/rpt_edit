ZH_A: 今天这份研报，直接指向了一个让市场非常不安的问题：五月份的中国经济数据，到底有多弱？

ZH_B: 简单说，比大多数人想的要弱得多。零售销售转负了，固定资产投资跌到了两位数，而工业生产的反弹非常勉强。

ZH_A: 零售销售转负，这好像是疫情以来头一回？

ZH_B: 对，而且是2022年底突然放开之后，第一次出现同比负增长。五月是-0.6%，四月份还有0.2%，市场预期也才-0.2%。

ZH_A: 那这个负增长意味着什么呢？是大家普遍在缩减开支吗？

ZH_B: 报告认为，这背后是消费者信心非常疲弱，叠加了以旧换新补贴退坡的“后遗症”。还有一个很关键的因素，就是油价和芯片价格上涨，实际上抑制了购买。

ZH_A: 说到油价，报告里专门提到了一个石油相关的供给冲击，这又是什么情况？

ZH_B: 这可能是整个报告里最容易被忽视的线索。五月份中国的原油进口量降到了每天780万桶，是九年来的最低点，比地缘冲突前的水平还要低三成。

ZH_A: 这么大幅度的缩减，直接冲击了工业生产？

ZH_B: 没错。报告里列出了很具体的链条：原油加工量暴跌，然后导致硫磺短缺，进而影响硫酸生产，再影响到整个化工产业链。五月份化工产品的产出增速几乎掉到了零。

ZH_A: 那固定资产投资负增长超过10%，这数字听起来很吓人。

ZH_B: 是的，五月是-10.7%，四月是-8.0%，市场预期只有-4.2%。而且这次是全面下滑，制造业、基建、房地产，三个板块都在跌。

ZH_A: 基建投资跌了两位数，这有点反常。不是一直在说发债、搞数据中心吗？

ZH_B: 这就是报告里提到的“谜团”。资金并不缺，但钱可能没有真正花出去。报告认为，问题出在项目审批或地方执行的“堵点”上，而不是融资不足。

ZH_A: 那房地产呢？有没有任何好转的迹象？

ZH_B: 一线城市房价依然坚挺，但全国层面，新房和二手房价格跌幅都在扩大。报告特别指出，三四线城市的困境可能还会持续很久，因为人口在流出，预期也很悲观。

ZH_A: 所以现在市场上有一种声音，认为AI热潮和股市上涨能带动经济复苏，报告同意吗？

ZH_B: 报告明确不认同。它认为，AI带来的财富效应主要集中在少数几个“聪明城市”，根本不足以对冲房地产下行带来的经济阵痛。这个判断是报告最核心的观点之一。

ZH_A: 那报告对二季度GDP的预测是多少？

ZH_B: 4.1%，低于市场共识的4.7%。而且报告认为，北京应该保持高水平的财政支出，不能因为一季度数据反弹就放松。

ZH_A: 听起来，这份报告其实留了很多悬念。比如，基建投资的“堵点”到底什么时候能打通？石油供给冲击还会持续多久？

ZH_B: 对，这些都是报告没有完全展开的。完整报告里，对这三个问题有更深入的推演和情景分析，我们这里就不剧透了。

ZH_A: 那我们就留给听众一个悬念。想了解更多细节，可以去阅读这份完整报告。

ZH_B: 没错，完整的报告里还有更多关于行业和区域的具体数据，值得一看。

EN_A: So this report basically dropped a bombshell on the May data. How bad was it, really?

EN_B: Worse than most people expected. Retail sales went negative for the first time since the reopening. Fixed asset investment plunged into double-digit contraction. And industrial production only managed a very soft rebound.

EN_A: Retail sales turning negative, that is a big deal. What does that tell us about consumer sentiment?

EN_B: It tells us consumers are really holding back. The report points to weak confidence, the payback effect from a scaled-back trade-in program, and also higher oil and chip prices that are actually discouraging purchases.

EN_A: And then there is this oil-linked supply shock the report talks about. What is that all about?

EN_B: This might be the most overlooked story in the report. China's crude oil imports in May fell to 7.8 million barrels per day, the lowest in nine years, and 30 percent below pre-conflict levels.

EN_A: That is a massive drop. How does that hit the broader economy?

EN_B: It creates a chain reaction. Less crude processing means a shortage of sulfur, which hits sulfuric acid production, which is basically the mother of all chemicals. By May, chemical output growth had almost stalled.

EN_A: And then fixed asset investment fell by more than 10 percent year on year. That is a severe number.

EN_B: It is. May came in at minus 10.7 percent, far worse than the consensus forecast of minus 4.2 percent. And the weakness was broad-based across manufacturing, infrastructure, and property.

EN_A: Infrastructure investment dropping by double digits is a surprise. We keep hearing about all this bond issuance and data center spending.

EN_B: That is the puzzle the report highlights. Funding is not the issue. The most likely explanation is that the money is being raised but not actually spent. It points to bottlenecks in project approval or local government execution.

EN_A: And what about housing? Any signs of a bottom?

EN_B: Tier-one cities are still holding up, but nationally, both new and existing home prices are falling faster. The report warns that the situation in smaller cities will likely persist due to population outflows and deeply pessimistic expectations.

EN_A: So there is this narrative that the AI boom and a rising stock market can lift the whole economy. Does the report buy that?

EN_B: Not at all. It argues that AI-driven wealth is concentrated in a few smart cities and is nowhere near enough to offset the property bust. That is probably the single most important takeaway.

EN_A: What is the report's GDP forecast for the second quarter?

EN_B: 4.1 percent, well below the consensus of 4.7 percent. And it says Beijing needs to maintain a high level of fiscal spending, not get complacent after the first quarter rebound.

EN_A: It sounds like the report leaves a lot of open questions. Like when will those infrastructure bottlenecks clear? And how long will the oil supply shock last?

EN_B: Exactly. Those are the big unknowns. The full report actually goes deeper into scenario analysis on all three of these questions. We are not going to spoil it here.
