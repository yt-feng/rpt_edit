ZH_A: 最近中国利率市场波动不小，听说有一份外资投行的最新研报给出了一个非常明确的交易建议，B，你能先给我们说说这份报告的核心判断是什么吗？

ZH_B: 好的，A。这份报告的核心观点很直接：他们建议继续持有支付3年期NDIRS的头寸，目标是在7月底之前看到利率上升到1.60%。这个判断背后有两个关键驱动力：流动性终于开始收紧，以及政府债券供给即将增加。

ZH_A: 流动性收紧这个说法我们听了好几个月了，这次为什么说是“终于”发生了？报告里有什么具体的证据吗？

ZH_B: 对，这次不一样。报告指出，7天回购利率已经回升到1.4%以上。更关键的是，央行从3月份开始通过中期货币政策工具净回笼流动性，这种累积效应现在才开始真正传导到市场。你可以理解为，水龙头一直在慢慢关，现在水池里的水位终于开始明显下降了。

ZH_A: 那债券供给这边呢？为什么说第三季度会成为一个压力点？

ZH_B: 报告里的数据很清晰。到5月底，国债和地方债的发行只完成了全年目标的35%，这个进度比去年慢。这意味着下半年，尤其是第三季度，会有大量债券集中发行。当市场突然要消化这么多新债，流动性会进一步被抽紧，对利率形成上行压力。

ZH_A: 听起来逻辑很顺。但我们也看到，就在5月底利率还一度大幅下行过，这又是什么原因？

ZH_B: 没错，5月底确实有一波4到6个基点的下行，因为当时央行通过MLF净投放了1000亿，还意外降低了MLF利率5个基点。但报告认为这只是短期扰动。进入6月后，利率已经反弹了4到5个基点。这说明市场的核心矛盾还是流动性，而流动性的方向是收紧的。

ZH_A: 那关于需求端呢？我们看到之前基金等机构大量买入长债，他们现在是什么动作？

ZH_B: 这正是报告里一个非常有趣的观察。在4月和5月，境内基金累积了大量超长久期国债和政策银行债。但进入6月，他们开始减持了。第一周还在买10年以上，第二周就开始全面卖出，连后端也卖了。这意味着之前的买盘力量在减弱。

ZH_A: 那30年国债呢？最近卖压大吗？

ZH_B: 30年国债的卖出规模目前还比较小，而且有一个重要缓冲：目前10年和30年的利差大约在49个基点，这个宽度一般会支撑30年国债的表现。另外，报告提到保险公司在30年国债收益率接近2.25%的时候开始买入，这给利率上行提供了一个天花板。所以，长端的上行空间可能有限。

ZH_A: 所以报告更看好做空哪个期限？

ZH_B: 他们明确表示，在经济增长数据出现实质性改善之前，或者流动性收紧幅度远超预期之前，他们不认为收益率曲线会显著陡化。因此，他们继续偏好支付3年期NDIRS，而不是5年期。因为3年期更能直接反映流动性和资金面的收紧。

ZH_A: 说到流动性收紧的幅度，报告对未来几周的货币市场利率有什么具体预测吗？

ZH_B: 有的。他们预计7天回购利率在6月余下时间会继续走高，目标是1.50%甚至略高。6月还有大额存单到期、税收上缴和地方政府债发行加速等因素，这些都会推高资金利率。报告认为，7天回购利率与7天逆回购利率之间的平均利差，会从5月的负3个基点扩大到6月的正5到10个基点。

ZH_A: 听起来这个判断非常明确。但这份报告里有没有一些他们自己也没有完全搞清楚的问题？

ZH_B: 有的。比如，他们提到基金在6月减持债券，但这种流出能否持续，还是一个问号。当10年和30年国债收益率分别到达1.75%和2.25%的关键点位时，会不会出现买跌的抄底资金？这个问题报告没有给出答案。

ZH_A: 这确实是一个悬念。还有吗？

ZH_B: 另一个悬念是关于央行操作信号的。报告提到央行在6月初罕见地暂停了逆回购操作，虽然金额很小，但可能是一个政策信号。但报告自己也说，这到底是真正的信号，还是仅仅是技术性调整，目前还无法完全确认。这些都需要在完整的报告里继续追踪。

EN_A: So we have a new report out from a major investment bank on China rates, and it is making a pretty clear call. B, what is the main trade they are recommending?

EN_B: They are staying short on the 3-year NDIRS, targeting a yield of 1.60 percent by the end of July. The logic comes down to two main drivers: liquidity is finally tightening, and government bond supply is about to pick up significantly.

EN_A: People have been talking about liquidity tightening for months. Why is this time different?

EN_B: Because we are now seeing the evidence. The 7-day repo rate has moved back above 1.4 percent. More importantly, the central bank has been draining medium to long-term liquidity since March through tools like outright reverse repos and MLF. The cumulative effect is now starting to hit the market. It is like a tap that has been slowly turning off, and now the water level is actually dropping.

EN_A: And on the supply side, what is the pressure point?

EN_B: By the end of May, only 35 percent of the annual quota for central and local government bonds had been issued. That is behind schedule. So the second half of the year, especially the third quarter, is going to see a big wave of new bond supply. When the market has to absorb all that, it drains liquidity further and pushes rates up.

EN_A: But we did see a big rally in late May. What happened there?

EN_B: That was a temporary move. The central bank injected 100 billion yuan via MLF and also cut the MLF rate by 5 basis points. That caused a 4 to 6 basis point rally. But the report sees that as a short-term blip. Since June, rates have already bounced back by 4 to 5 basis points. The underlying trend is still towards tighter liquidity.

EN_A: What about the demand side? We know funds have been piling into long bonds. Are they still buying?

EN_B: That is one of the most interesting observations in the report. Onshore funds accumulated a massive amount of ultra-long government and policy bank bonds in April and May. But in June, they started selling. In the first week, they were still adding the long end. By the second week, they were selling across the curve, including the back end. So the buying momentum is fading.

EN_A: Does that mean the 30-year bond is under big selling pressure?

EN_B: Not yet. The selling is still small relative to the inflows we saw earlier. And there is a buffer. The spread between 10-year and 30-year yields is around 49 basis points, which is quite wide. That usually supports the 30-year. Also, insurance companies tend to buy the 30-year when yields approach 2.25 percent. So there is a ceiling on how high yields can go at the long end.

EN_A: So the report prefers to focus on the front end?

EN_B: Exactly. They do not expect a significant steepening of the curve unless we see a real improvement in economic data, like credit demand or retail sales, or unless liquidity tightens much faster. So they prefer to pay the 3-year NDIRS rather than the 5-year. The 3-year is more directly sensitive to the tightening in funding conditions.

EN_A: What is their specific forecast for money market rates in the coming weeks?

EN_B: They expect the 7-day repo fixing to move higher towards 1.50 percent or slightly above through the rest of June. There are large NCD maturities, tax payments due on June 15th, and a potential pickup in local government bond supply in the last week of June. The spread between the 7-day repo and the 7-day OMO rate could widen from minus 3 basis points in May to plus 5 to 10 basis points in June.

EN_A: That is a pretty clear view. But are there any big questions the report leaves open?

EN_B: Yes, a couple. One is whether the selling from onshore funds will continue, or if we will see buy-on-dip flows when 10-year and 30-year yields hit key levels like 1.75 and 2.25 percent. The report does not have a definitive answer on that yet.

EN_A: And the other?

EN_B: The other is about the central bank's signal. The PBoC skipped open market operations for a couple of days in early June for the first time in months. That could be a signal, but the report says it is not clear if it is a real policy signal or just a technical adjustment. That is something the full report will keep tracking.
