# The Optical Interconnect Build-Out Is Creating a PCB Opportunity Larger Than Apple's Entire HDI Demand

The AI investment cycle is entering a new phase. For the past two years, the narrative has centered on compute—GPUs, networking chips, and the optical modules that connect them. That narrative is now incomplete. As hyperscalers scale AI clusters from tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of GPUs, the optical connectivity required to link these systems is growing exponentially. And within each optical transceiver lies a printed circuit board that is becoming more complex, more valuable, and more strategic with every generation.

The market for AI transceiver PCBs is on track to grow from approximately USD 620 million in 2025 to nearly USD 3.8 billion by 2028, representing an 83% compound annual growth rate. To put that in perspective: Apple, the world's largest consumer of HDI PCBs, generates roughly USD 3.0 to 3.5 billion in annual HDI PCB demand. By 2027, the AI transceiver PCB market will be comparable in size. By 2028, it will surpass it. This is not merely a growth story. It is a structural shift in where the PCB industry's center of gravity resides.

For years, Apple has been the primary driver of technology advancement, capacity investment, and profitability within the HDI PCB industry. That era is being supplemented—and potentially superseded—by a new demand engine rooted in AI infrastructure. The implications for investors are significant, but they are not evenly distributed. The winners will be determined by technical capability, manufacturing know-how, and the ability to capture share in a market where qualification barriers are rising rapidly.

This article argues that the most compelling opportunity in the AI transceiver PCB supply chain is not the incumbent leaders with the largest current market share, but rather a company with proven advanced manufacturing expertise that is positioned to gain share as the industry transitions to higher-speed transceivers. The argument rests on three pillars: the accelerating volume growth of optical transceivers, the rising content per module as technology migrates to 1.6T and beyond, and the increasing technical barriers that favor suppliers with mSAP process experience.

## The AI Transceiver PCB Market Will Grow Faster Than Transceiver Shipments Themselves, Driven by a Step-Change in Content Per Module

Volume growth alone would make the AI transceiver PCB market attractive. According to a global investment bank report, AI transceiver shipments are expected to grow at approximately 60% CAGR from 2025 to 2028, driven by hyperscaler demand for scale-out, scale-up, and scale-across architectures. But the PCB opportunity is amplified by a second factor: content per module is rising dramatically.

When the industry migrated from 400G to 800G transceivers, PCB layer counts increased from 10-12 layers to 12-14 layers. With the transition to 1.6T, layer counts will reach 14-16 layers. The copper-clad laminate materials are upgrading from M6 grade to M8 grade to preserve signal integrity at higher speeds. And the manufacturing process is shifting from standard HDI to mSAP—modified semi-additive process—which allows for denser conducting path layouts in the compact form factor of an optical transceiver.

The result is a roughly 2.5x increase in PCB average selling price per module, from approximately USD 10 for a 400G transceiver to USD 25 for a 1.6T transceiver. Gross margins are expected to follow a similar trajectory, improving from 20-30% to 40-50%+, approaching the profitability levels of ABF substrates. This combination of unit growth and content expansion means the AI transceiver PCB market will grow at an 83% CAGR, outpacing the 60% CAGR of the underlying transceiver market by a wide margin.

The significance of this comparison extends beyond the numbers. For an industry that has long been cyclical and commoditized, the emergence of a structurally growing, high-margin segment is transformative. The AI transceiver PCB market is evolving from a niche sub-billion-dollar segment into one of the most compelling growth vectors in the global PCB industry, representing roughly 23% of the current global HDI market by 2028.

## The Transition to 1.6T Transceivers Raises Qualification Barriers, Favoring Suppliers with mSAP Expertise

Not all PCB suppliers are equally positioned to capture this opportunity. The migration to 1.6T and beyond is raising the technical bar in ways that favor companies with specific manufacturing capabilities.

The key differentiator is mSAP technology. While 400G transceivers could be manufactured using standard HDI processes, 1.6T modules require mSAP to achieve the necessary signal integrity and density. mSAP is not a new technology—it was first adopted by Apple for the iPhone 8 and X generation in 2017, where it was used in the mainboard to save space and enable thinner devices. But its application in optical transceivers is new, and the manufacturing know-how required to produce mSAP PCBs at scale and with high yields is not widely distributed.

This creates a natural advantage for suppliers that have already invested in mSAP capability and have years of production experience. The qualification process for new suppliers is lengthy and rigorous, particularly for 1.6T transceivers where signal integrity requirements are demanding. Customers are unlikely to risk their supply chain on unproven manufacturers. As a result, the pool of qualified suppliers for next-generation transceiver PCBs is likely to be limited, and those with established mSAP expertise are positioned to capture disproportionate share.

The incumbents in the optical transceiver PCB market—Unimicron and Shennan Circuits—have strong positions and will benefit from both volume and content growth. But the expanding addressable market is creating opportunities for new entrants, and the most compelling candidate is Zhen Ding (ZDT). ZDT has been a supplier to Apple's iPhone mainboard supply chain since the adoption of mSAP-based SLP architectures in 2017, giving it years of experience with the exact manufacturing process that 1.6T transceiver PCBs require.

## ZDT Is Positioned to Capture Disproportionate Share Gains as the Market Expands and Technology Requirements Intensify

The evidence for ZDT's market share gains is already visible. According to the report, ZDT's share of the AI transceiver PCB market increased to approximately 7.5% in 2025, up from essentially zero in 2024. In the 1.6T segment, ZDT entered with roughly 5% share in 2025. By 2028, the report estimates that ZDT could capture approximately 20% of the overall AI transceiver PCB market and 25% of the 1.6T segment.

These share gains are not speculative. They are being driven by concrete actions: ZDT and its competitor Compeq are actively expanding capacity to support the ramp of 800G and 1.6T optical transceiver PCBs. The market is growing so rapidly that existing industry capacity appears insufficient to meet anticipated demand, creating a natural opening for qualified new entrants. And ZDT's mSAP expertise gives it a qualification advantage that competitors without Apple supply chain experience may lack.

The financial implications are significant. ZDT's AI transceiver PCB revenue is estimated to grow from approximately USD 30 million in 2025 to USD 850 million by 2028, representing a 176% CAGR. This growth is expected to drive earnings revisions upward: the report's 2027 and 2028 EPS estimates for ZDT are 15% and 17% above consensus, respectively, reflecting higher market share assumptions. The stock has already rallied approximately 145% since April 2025, but the report argues that further upside remains, with a price target of NT 666 implying 20x 2028 P/E and a PEG ratio of 0.5x.

## What the Report Does Not Fully Answer: The CPO Risk and the Sustainability of mSAP Advantage

No investment thesis is without uncertainties, and the AI transceiver PCB opportunity has several that deserve scrutiny. The most significant is the long-term risk from co-packaged optics (CPO). CPO represents a fundamental architectural shift in which optical engines are integrated directly into switch ASICs, potentially eliminating the need for pluggable transceivers and their associated PCBs.

The report acknowledges this risk but argues that large-scale adoption of CPO is unlikely before 2027-2028, given challenges with manufacturing yield, thermal complexity, cost, ecosystem readiness, and serviceability. It expects transceivers and CPO to coexist through the 3.2T transition expected in 2028. But the report does not fully explore what happens after 2028. If CPO adoption accelerates, the PCB content per optical connection could decline significantly, potentially compressing the TAM that the report projects.

A second unanswered question is whether mSAP will remain the dominant manufacturing technology for next-generation transceivers, or whether alternative processes could emerge that erode ZDT's advantage. The PCB industry has a history of process innovation cycles, and the current mSAP advantage may not be permanent. Investors need to monitor whether competitors develop alternative approaches that can match mSAP's density and signal integrity at lower cost.

A third question concerns capacity. The report assumes that existing industry capacity is insufficient to meet demand, creating an opening for new entrants. But if multiple suppliers race to add capacity simultaneously, the market could become oversupplied, compressing margins and returns. The report's profitability assumptions for ZDT—gross margins of 40-50%+ for 1.6T PCBs—may prove optimistic if pricing competition intensifies.

## A Decision Framework for Investors Evaluating the AI Transceiver PCB Opportunity

For investors considering exposure to the AI transceiver PCB theme, a structured framework can help evaluate the opportunities and risks across the three primary suppliers identified in the report.

The first dimension is market share trajectory. Unimicron and Shennan are established leaders with significant current share, but their growth rates are likely to be more modest given their larger bases. ZDT, by contrast, is starting from a small base and has the potential to grow revenue at a 176% CAGR as it captures share. The key question is whether ZDT's share gains can be sustained as competitors respond and capacity expands.

The second dimension is technology positioning. All three suppliers have some mSAP capability, but ZDT's deep experience from the Apple supply chain gives it a potential edge. Unimicron has broader PCB capabilities across multiple end markets, which provides diversification but may dilute focus. Shennan has strong positions in China but may face technology transfer challenges as it scales mSAP production. Investors should evaluate which company has the strongest qualification track record with the customers that matter most—the hyperscalers and their optical module suppliers.

The third dimension is valuation. The report's price targets imply significant upside for ZDT and Unimicron, with more modest upside for Shennan. ZDT trades at 15x 2028 P/E with a PEG ratio of 0.52x, while Unimicron trades at 17x 2028 P/E with a PEG ratio of 0.25x. Shennan trades at 25x 2028 P/E with a PEG ratio of 0.63x. The valuation differential reflects different growth expectations and risk profiles. Investors need to decide whether ZDT's higher growth potential justifies its valuation, or whether Unimicron's lower PEG ratio offers a more attractive risk-reward profile.

The fourth dimension is downside risk. All three suppliers face the CPO risk, but the impact would vary. ZDT has the highest exposure to AI transceiver PCBs as a percentage of revenue, making it most vulnerable to a CPO-driven disruption. Unimicron and Shennan have more diversified PCB businesses that could partially offset any decline in transceiver PCB demand. Investors should stress-test their assumptions about CPO adoption timing and assess whether the current valuations adequately reflect this risk.
