EN_A: So everyone is talking about GPUs and optical modules in the AI boom. But there is a report that looks at a much less obvious part of the supply chain: printed circuit boards. What is the big idea here?
EN_B: The core argument is that AI optical connectivity is becoming the next major growth engine for the PCB industry. And the market for these PCBs is actually growing faster than the transceiver shipments themselves.
EN_A: Why would the PCB market grow faster? Shouldn't it just follow the volume of transceivers?
EN_B: It is not just about volume. The value per board is going up. As transceivers move from 400G to 800G and then 1.6T, the PCB becomes much more complex. More layers, better materials, and more advanced manufacturing processes.
EN_A: So the price per board goes up significantly?
EN_B: Exactly. The report estimates the average selling price per PCB goes from about 10 dollars to 25 dollars, a 2.5 times increase. And gross margins jump from 20-30% to over 40-50%, approaching levels seen in ABF substrates.
EN_A: That means even if transceiver shipment growth slows down a bit, PCB suppliers could still see strong revenue growth?
EN_B: That is the idea. The report forecasts the AI transceiver PCB market to grow from 620 million dollars in 2025 to nearly 3.8 billion by 2028. That is an 83% compound annual growth rate, compared to 60% for the transceiver units themselves.
EN_A: So which company does the report like the most?
EN_B: It highlights Zhen Ding, or ZDT, as the most compelling beneficiary. While incumbents like Unimicron and Shennan Circuits will also benefit, ZDT has the potential to capture significant market share as the industry shifts to higher speeds.
EN_A: What gives ZDT that edge?
EN_B: The key is the mSAP manufacturing process required for 1.6T transceivers. ZDT has been making mSAP motherboards for Apple iPhones since 2017. So when the technology bar rises and customers need more qualified suppliers, ZDT is perfectly positioned.
EN_A: So what is the expected path for ZDT's market share?
EN_B: The report estimates ZDT's share in 800G transceiver PCBs could go from about 7.5% in 2025 to 20% by 2028. And in the 1.6T segment, it could expand from 5% to 25% over the same period.
EN_A: That sounds like a strong growth story. But what are the unanswered questions in this report?
EN_B: One big one is co-packaged optics, or CPO. It is a long-term risk to pluggable transceivers. The report says CPO won't be a major factor until 2027 or 2028, but the long-term impact on PCB suppliers is not fully resolved.
EN_A: So the report leaves that question hanging?
EN_B: Yes. Another question is about other potential competitors. Companies like Compeq are also expanding capacity, but the report does not cover them. So there is more research to be done there.
EN_A: It sounds like this report is really good at finding a hidden gem in the AI supply chain. To get into the details, like the valuation and risks, you would have to read the full report.
EN_B: Absolutely. The full report has the detailed financial models and risk factors. It is worth a deep dive.
