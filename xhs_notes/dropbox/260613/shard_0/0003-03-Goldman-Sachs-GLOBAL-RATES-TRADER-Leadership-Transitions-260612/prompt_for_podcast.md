## Europe

Inflation persistence driving the EUR front-end. The ECB became the first major central bank to hike in the aftermath of the war in Iran, motivated by a persistent inflation overshoot in its latest projections. President Lagarde refrained from providing explicit guidance on the upcoming July decision, but relief headlines on a potential US-Iran deal have taken July pricing towards 6bp as of time of writing. This looks low to us, and is likely only to fall further if a conclusive deal is able to restart commodity flows through the Strait of Hormuz – as our oil analysts have argued, while demand reduction and supply diversion have helped keep oil prices rangebound, relief for longer-dated oil prices is likely to be limited. This matters because the hawkish aspects of the ECB’s inflation projections come from the persistence of inflation, rather than the magnitude of the near-term oil spike. This is also evident in the market – while front-end rates have repriced sharply on the rise in oil prices, we have seen little yield decline despite the fall in front-contract oil. The better guide in recent weeks has been z6 oil, which has remained flat and as a result reflects more persistent inflationary risk. This suggests to us there is more room to price sharper relief in the UK front-end (given the gap to our policy rate views), but also that the ECB’s front-footed approach to policy tightening vs G4 peers should see the EUR curve — beyond 1y rates — outperform on selloffs. We maintain our long in 5y5y real OIS on the potential for both nominal rate relief and inflation curve steepening on any concrete prospects for lower energy prices.

UK front-end remains an inflation relief trade. With no change in rates expected at next week's BoE meeting, focus will be on the Governor's message. So far, Governor Bailey has emphasised that the shift away from cuts already constitutes a tightening, which has served to neutralise some hawkish messaging from other MPC members. With hike premium elevated, we think that UK front-end longs represent reasonable risk reward given the UK activity picture remains soft and the distribution for energy prices has shifted lower from both oil market rebalancing and the prospects for a US-Iran deal. At the same time, ongoing debates around defence spending are a reminder of the government's limited fiscal room, which we think is unlikely to resolve without some macro relief on inflation and rates. As a result, we continue to expect Gilt risk premium to remain sticky and for UK yields to move lower as a function of inflation relief — stay in GBP forward 2s10s steepeners.

As we have shown, bank ownership of Gilts has been increasing in recent years (see here for more detail, Exhibit 4), and an increase in balance sheet capacity could add further support for Gilts.
