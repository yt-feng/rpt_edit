# Rio Tinto Ltd. (RIO.AX): 1H26 Result: Simplification Strategy Accelerating

RIO reported 1H26 underlying EBITDA/NPAT of US$14.8bn/US$6.9bn (+28%/+43% YoY) vs. estimates of US$14.9bn/US$6.2bn and consensus at US$15.1bn/US$6.6bn. Earnings exceeded expectations due to a lower underlying tax rate of ~25% (vs. estimated 30%; guidance now lowered to 25%). At the divisional level, copper (Bingham; better revenue on higher US copper premiums), iron ore (Pilbara; unit costs) and aluminium (Primary metal costs) all performed well, partially offset by higher than expected project evaluation costs and other operations (higher provisions due to inflation).

Net debt of US$14.1bn was below the US$14.8bn estimate on higher than expected operating cash flow and lower capex. The interim dividend of US211cps (US$3.4bn) was above the estimated US190cps on the earnings beat, representing the typical 50% payout for the interim.

On growth, all 22 major development projects are on or ahead of schedule (Simandou, Pilbara replacement, Argentina lithium, Oyu Tolgoi underground ramp-up). Studies and drilling on longer-dated copper options are progressing, including Resolution, La Granja, Winu, Bingham pit extension, Nuevo Cobre and La Comita. Project evaluation/studies increased to US$1bn in 1H, up from US$0.45bn in the prior comparable period. While costs are being reduced elsewhere in the portfolio, RIO continues to invest in advancing its growth pipeline. The company continues to demonstrate strong project execution capabilities.

There was no change to 2026 production guidance, with capex guidance of up to US$11bn for 2026/27 and Pilbara unit cost guidance of US$23.5-25/t. However, cost-out targets (outlined at the investor day in late 2025 under the "stronger, sharper, simpler" agenda) have been increased from US$0.65bn (by end of 1Q26) to US$1.8bn by year-end. While some of this cost reduction is structural, a large proportion relates to productivity improvements. The US$1.8bn represents approximately 5% of RIO's cost base, so this initiative should help offset broader inflationary pressures. The US$5-10bn of non-core divestments (estimated at approximately US$10bn) are progressing, with opportunities to release around US$5bn by the end of 2026 being advanced. Pilbara power could potentially unlock US$3-4bn in 2H26. RIO has identified further cost reductions beyond the US$1.8bn run rate, some of which will be realized through divestments (borates, mineral sands).

## Key Takeaways

- **Divisional performance**: At the divisional level, copper (Bingham; revenue), iron ore (Pilbara; unit costs) and aluminium (Primary metals; costs) all performed well, partially offset by higher than expected project evaluation costs and other operations (including marketing, shipping, etc.) which included non-cash increases on rehabilitation provisions related to inflation.

- **Cash flow and balance sheet**: Net cash flow from operations of US$9.2bn and net debt of US$14.1bn compared to estimates of US$8.8bn and US$14.8bn respectively. Capex for 1H26 was US$5.0bn (including spend on Simandou MineCo and excluding Equity Accounted Units) vs. the US$5.6bn estimate, due to timing of spend on the Simandou and Pilbara replacement projects.

- **Guidance/cost out/asset sales**: There was no change to 2026 production, approximately US$11bn capex guidance for 2026 and 2027 (and rehabilitation cash spend of approximately US$1bn) and Pilbara unit cost guidance of US$23.5-25/t.

However, productivity/cost-out targets (outlined at the investor day in late 2025 under the "stronger, sharper, simpler" agenda) have been increased by US$1.2bn to US$1.8bn (from US$650mn), with US$1.3bn annualized run rate achieved in 1H and US$0.87bn banked year-to-date. Of this, US$0.34bn relates to volumes and US$0.5bn to cash cost improvements. Management stated that the additional approximately US$1bn to be delivered in 2H will come roughly evenly from volumes and costs, with real momentum month on month. The US$1.8bn represents approximately 5% of group underlying costs (approximately US$37bn per annum). Overall, RIO is transitioning from a centralized model to a leaner decentralized operating model, pushing more decision-making back to the mine sites.

- The US$5-10bn of non-core divestments (estimated at approximately US$10bn) are progressing, with opportunities to release around US$5bn by the end of 2026 being advanced.

- The tax rate for 2026 is now expected to be approximately 25% (down from prior guidance of approximately 30%), compared to 31.5% in 2025, but is expected to increase back to approximately 30% from 2027.

## Growth Projects (of Interest)

- **Resolution copper**: RIO commenced surface drilling in newly accessible areas following the land exchange, with underground drilling scheduled to commence in 3Q26 (in line with the schedule in the recent deep dive report on the project) to support further resource definition and geological data collection.

- **Oyu Tolgoi**: RIO recently announced a change to the underground mine plan at OT as a result of a delay in the Mongolian government's approval of the license transfer from Entrée Resources to Oyu Tolgoi LLC, which could see a possible impact of approximately 50k to copper and gold production in 2027 and 2028. The Government of Mongolia recently agreed to commence work on resolution of the Entrée licence transfer for Panel 1, but RIO continues to advance the lower grade Panels 0 and 2 North followed by Panel 2 South. Panel 1 is expected to be the final panel brought into production.

- **Brazil Aluminium acquisition**: RIO recently entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Votorantim's controlling approximately 69% shareholding in major Brazilian aluminium producer Companhia Brasileira de Alumínio (CBA), through a joint venture with RIO's major shareholder Chalco (67% Chalco / 33% RIO) for approximately US$903mn (approximately US$298mn RIO's share). Following closing, the joint venture will launch a mandatory tender offer for the remaining approximately 31% of shares in CBA, as required by Brazilian law. This transaction highlights RIO's broader Atlantic portfolio and strategy, noting that RIO is long 3.3Mtpa of alumina production in the Pacific Basin but is short 2.6Mt of alumina in the Atlantic Basin.

## EBITDA/NAV Changes and Investment Thesis

The 2027/28 EBITDA estimates have been adjusted by +2%/+1% after decreasing Primary Aluminium and Pilbara iron ore costs, and increasing Bingham copper revenue. The NAV is up 2% to approximately A$199/sh with these changes and on the lower net debt. Overall, the 12-month price target has increased by 2% to A$181.9/sh (from A$178.9/sh).

The RIO.AX share price has retraced approximately 15% from the highs seen in June when valuation peaked at approximately 8.5-9x EBITDA, and is now back into value territory, trading at approximately 0.8x NAV and approximately 7x EBITDA, around the 25-year historical average of 6.5-7x.

Key considerations include:

1. **Valuation and cash flow**: RIO is trading at approximately 0.8x NAV (approximately A$199/sh) vs. peers (BHP approximately 0.9x NAV and FMG approximately 1.1x NAV), and RIO.AX is trading at approximately 7x NTM EBITDA, in line with BHP.AX on approximately 7x, and the 25-year historical average of 6.5-7x. Additionally, the cash flow and dividend profile appears attractive, driven by a positive view on aluminium and copper (approximately 45-50% of group EBITDA in 2026E, and approximately 60% by 2030E).

2. **Medium-term production and EBITDA growth**: RIO maintains sector-leading production and EBITDA growth. Copper equivalent production is forecast to grow by approximately 10% and EBITDA by approximately 30% from 2025 to 2030, driven mostly by the ramp-up of the Oyu Tolgoi underground copper mine, higher grades at Bingham Canyon copper from 2027, the ramp-up of the Simandou iron ore mine in Guinea, higher Pilbara iron ore shipments with the ramp-up of new mines (such as Rhodes Ridge), expanding aluminium margins, and lithium growth in Argentina.

The company also has attractive longer-dated growth copper projects including Resolution and La Granja copper and lithium options in Chile. Copper equivalent production growth is forecast at approximately 1% per annum in 2026, but an average of 3% per annum from 2027-2030.

3. **Simplification strategy to lower costs and assist deleveraging**: The company's strategy remains anchored on the "stronger, sharper, simpler" agenda presented at the recent capital markets day, which includes US$5-10bn of non-core divestments and cost reductions.

## Price Target Methodology and Investment Risks

The 12-month price target is set at an equal 50:50 blend of NAV and EV/EBITDA with a 7.0x target multiple (unchanged).

**Considerations to the outlook include:**

- Iron ore and base metal price movements: changes in demand could affect prices for iron ore, copper and aluminium.
- Ongoing impacts from Western Australia heritage matters to permitting timelines and operations in the Pilbara: delays to heritage approvals in WA could affect permitting timelines and operations that may impact production output, product grade, costs and capex (note Pilbara represents approximately 50% of group NAV).
- Delays to further growth: Oyu Tolgoi (underground ramp-up/Mongolia tax matters), Pilbara (replacement mines/Rhodes Ridge), Simandou (ramp-up), Lithium, Escondida and decarbonisation projects.
- Higher capex and opex due to industry cost inflation: Cost inflation across both capital and operating expenditure is an ongoing consideration. RIO is spending approximately US$10-11bn per annum in capex, and elevated input costs would affect free cash flow.
