## The Restructuring Program Provides a Measurable but Modest Margin Boost, and the Market Has Already Priced Most of It In

GSK’s restructuring is designed to deliver 1.9 billion pounds in annual savings by 2029, with the full profit-and-loss benefit expected in that year. The company plans to reinvest the majority of those savings into R&D and business development, but a portion will flow through to the margin. The investment bank estimates that the net effect is a roughly 200 million pound benefit to core EBIT by 2029, or about 60 basis points of margin improvement. The savings are being redeployed into the pipeline, which is where the real value creation must come from.

## The Pipeline’s Most Promising Assets Face Competitive Timelines That Are Two to Three Years Ahead, Making Differentiation Critical

GSK’s near-term pipeline catalysts are concentrated in oncology, where the company has three Phase III readouts expected in 2028. The first is ris-rez, a B7H3 antibody-drug conjugate in second-line extensive small cell lung cancer. The Phase I data from the ARTEMIS-001 trial in China showed a 60 percent confirmed objective response rate, with median progression-free survival of 6.3 months and median overall survival of 14.9 months. That compares favorably to the 40 percent response rate and 13.6 month median overall survival seen with Amgen’s Imdelltra in its Phase III trial. However, the China-only Phase III for ris-rez recently reported a clinically meaningful overall survival benefit, and the full details will be needed to assess whether the data can be reproduced in a global setting. The global Phase III trial, EMBOLD SCLC-301, includes patients previously treated with Imdelltra, which could dilute the overall survival result. Merck’s I-Dxd, another B7H3 ADC, has shown a 48 percent response rate and has a PDUFA date in October 2026. The competitive bar is high, and the investment bank assigns only a 50 percent probability to a 500 million pound peak sales opportunity for ris-rez in this indication.

The second asset is velzatinib in second-line gastrointestinal stromal tumors. Data presented at ASCO showed a 38 percent response rate and median progression-free survival of 13.7 months in 38 patients. That is below the 16.5 month median progression-free survival reported for the combination of bezuclastinib plus sunitinib in the Phase III PEAK trial, although the control arm in that trial showed only 9.2 months. The combination approach has a roughly two-year head start, and the investment bank expects it to capture the majority share. GSK is positioning velzatinib on the basis of a lower toxicity profile, but that may not be sufficient to overcome the efficacy gap.

The third asset is efimosfermin in F2/F3 MASH. GSK is highlighting a potentially differentiated efficacy profile based on a roughly 24 percentage point placebo-adjusted improvement in fibrosis improvement, compared with 20 percentage points for Novo’s efruxifermin. However, on the composite primary endpoint of fibrosis improvement of at least one stage and MASH resolution, efimosfermin showed a 21 percentage point placebo-adjusted benefit versus 36 percentage points for efruxifermin. Novo’s Phase III histology data is expected toward the end of 2026, two years before GSK’s readout. The investment bank already includes efimosfermin at a 50 percent probability of a 2 billion pound peak sales opportunity, assuming 30 percent share of the FGF21 class. That assumption may prove optimistic if the competitive data continue to favor Novo.

## In HIV, GSK’s Long-Acting Strategy Is Credible but Faces Earlier Competition from Weekly Orals That Could Reshape the Market

GSK’s HIV pipeline is built around long-acting injectable regimens that extend dosing intervals. The Phase III CUATRO study of cabotegravir plus rilpivirine, a three-times-yearly regimen, was initiated in June 2026, with expected approval in late 2028. The company also expects to start a Phase III trial for the combination of VH-499 and VH-184, a twice-yearly regimen, in 2028, with targeted approval in the latter part of 2030. The primary endpoint for that trial is likely at 48 weeks, and recruitment time could push approval into 2031, limiting the contribution to the 2031 sales target.

The more significant competitive factor comes from weekly oral regimens. Gilead and Merck recently reported positive Phase III data for lenacapavir plus islatravir, and the investment bank’s survey work shows strong physician interest in weekly oral options, with an expected 18 percent market share longer term. GSK has started a Phase I trial for VH-359, a capsid inhibitor, and has multiple potential weekly oral integrase inhibitors and capsid inhibitors in preclinical development. But Gilead and Merck are ahead, and their weekly oral data are likely to crystallize competition for GSK’s Dovato franchise by 2027. GSK expects the HIV treatment market to remain stable at roughly 22 billion pounds in 2031 relative to 2025, but that assumption may be challenged by generic dolutegravir and Dovato, which will erode value over time.

GSK’s long-acting strategy is logical and addresses a real patient need, but the timing gap matters. By the time GSK’s twice-yearly regimen reaches the market, weekly orals may have already captured a meaningful share of the patient population that values convenience. The company’s best approach is to accelerate development and demonstrate that its long-acting options offer superior efficacy or tolerability, but the data to support that claim are not yet available.
