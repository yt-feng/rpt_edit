## Mercedes: Some positive notes amidst a cacophony of challenges

Mercedes delivered respectable 2Q26 results in an extremely challenging environment. The group reported pre-market on 28 July and the stock initially rose by up to 6%, in part we suspect due to a short squeeze, after beating the company gathered analysts' consensus on adjusted operating income at Passenger Cars (4.0% reported vs 3.5% consensus). For FY26, Mercedes lowered its Passenger Cars revenue guidance but reiterated its adjusted margin guidance corridor of 3-5%, stating it would likely land toward the lower part of that range. The stock cooled somewhat intraday, and finished up 2.9% at the close.

The messaging on Europe, especially on demand for its new ranges of BEVs, and on the USA was positive, and the group is taking credible steps towards reducing its dependence on its high-cost German base, of which the most visible was the official opening earlier this month of the next phase of development at its Kecskemét plant in Hungary where capacity has been doubled to 400k units. There, factor costs (basically labour, energy, plant overhead and logistics, and do not include raw materials and parts purchases) are some 70% lower than those in Germany.

In China, Mercedes does not appear at first glance to be taking on as much water as BMW, whose 16 June warning on margins was mainly due to a recalibration of expectations for the Middle Kingdom. Mercedes did not scape through unscathed; a €796m non-cash impairment of the Chinese operations to reflect lower anticipated future returns was mainly responsible for reducing Passenger Cars' reported operating margin to only 0.2% in 2Q26.

On capital returns, Mercedes announced it was launching another €1bn share buyback that will likely end some weeks before the April 2027 AGM, and adds to the cumulative €5bn of dividend and stock buybacks that occurred in 1H26. Mercedes is also steadily reducing its stake in Daimler Truck (DTG), with €417m of proceeds in 2Q26 adding to that quarter's FCF. As DTG's stock hit record highs in July, Mercedes sold down a further €600m of its holding, providing further future funding firepower for capital returns.

We increase our SOTP discount from 20% to 25%, reflecting the more uncertain outlook for Mercedes' operations in China. This causes our price target to fall to €56, from €61 previously.

## 2Q26 RESULTS AND KEY TAKEAWAYS

\- Mercedes-Benz delivered solid 2Q26 results in a highly challenging environment, with group EBIT up 22% year-on-year to €1.55bn and free cash flow of €1.10bn, despite softer demand, geopolitical headwinds, and intense competition in China.

\- 2Q26 group revenue was slightly below the prior year at €32.1bn, but net industrial liquidity remained strong at €30.4bn after Mercedes returned €5bn to shareholders via dividends and buybacks in 1H26. At the group level, Mercedes expects 2026 revenue slightly below last year's €132.2bn (driven by lower Passenger Car volume), EBIT significantly above 2025's €5.8bn, and Industrial FCF slightly below 2025's €5.4bn, supported by both organic cash generation and M&A (including further Daimler Truck stake monetization and Athlon disposal) and an additional €1bn share buyback to be completed some weeks before the April 2027 AGM.

\- Mercedes Passenger Cars printed €909m adjusted EBIT (-26% yoy) for a 4% adjusted return on sales in 2Q26 (5.1% in 2Q25), within the 3–5% full-year guidance range, supported by efficiencies in purchasing/manufacturing and positive industrial performance despite China-related headwinds. Mercedes now expects full-year Passenger Car sales slightly below 2025 due to China, but raised xEV share guidance to 23–25% and confirmed 3–5% adjusted RoS, guiding towards the lower half of the range given raw-material, energy, logistics, depreciation, tariff, and higher BEV-mix headwinds.

\- Vans division remains a profit benchmark, with 94,075 units (+1% yoy), €4.45bn revenue (+5% yoy), €454m adjusted EBIT (+3% yoy), and 10.2% adjusted RoS (10.4% in 2Q25). xEV vans were up 46% YoY, lifting xEV share to 10.7%, as VLE series production began in June. Vans guidance for 2026 is unchanged: stronger 2H26 versus 1H26 in volume/mix but lower profitability than 1H26 due to ramp-up costs and raw-material headwinds, with full-year adjusted RoS still targeted at 8–10%.

\- Mercedes is carrying out major product and technology launches across cars and vans, including new AMG GT 4-Door, GLS Maybach, C-Class BEV, GLE, GLS, VLE, and upcoming GLA and electric AMG SUV. Mercedes notes that these have already received a very positive market and media reception. Mercedes' powertrain strategy is dual-track: an in-house highly efficient and compact axial flux motor developed by its YASA subsidiary (acquired in 2021) as a BEV performance USP and a new EU7-ready, high-tech electrified V8 to sustain ICE positioning, underpinning both BEV growth and a strong ICE franchise.

\- Mercedes notes that the China market fell about 20%, materially impacting its own volumes and JV economics, while ex-China sales grew 2% and sales of BEVs rose 51% globally with Europe +87%, reflecting strong BEV momentum outside China. Management reaffirmed its strategic commitment to China, emphasizing localization (China-specific long-wheelbase GLE produced locally from August), deeper tech partnerships, strengthened local R&D, and a long-term view despite intense competition and price pressure. Mercedes continues to lead the premium ASP hierarchy in China even amid pricing pressure, and is targeting measured BEV volumes (for the new GLC and C-class BEVs) to avoid cash-burning price wars in a crowded, volatile segment. The group recorded €752m of non-cash valuation adjustments (impairments) on its China joint ventures in 2Q26 due to market and cost developments, indicating lower future profit contribution from those JVs but not altering the long-term China strategy.

\- MB.OS, the proprietary chip-to-cloud operating system, is being rolled out as a universal software platform across BEV and ICE, with the new S-Class described as the first fully software-defined high-tech ICE vehicle, and ADAS/Level 2++ functionality as a key highlight. AI-based point-to-point assisted driving has already launched in China and the US, with plans to expand into Europe under new legislation, positioning Mercedes at the forefront of global ADAS among established OEMs. MB.OS is intended to boost perceived product value (infotainment, ADAS, personalization) and enable new revenue streams from software/feature sales over the vehicle life.

\- In Europe, the Chinese automakers' presence is currently more visible in volume segments, while Mercedes' new-generation BEVs are "flying off the shelf," with BEV sales as noted up 87% in Europe and orders strong, allowing Mercedes to raise xEV share guidance (BEV and PHEV combined) to 23–25% for 2026 (was 21-23% previously) at group level while defending margins through segment focus rather than pure volume.

\- Industrial strategy focuses on resilience and cost competitiveness, including a global productivity initiative centered on Germany and expanded, lower-cost capacity in Kecskemét, Hungary for electric C-Class, GLC, and compact G derivatives.

A Germany-focused Productivity Initiative targets cost structure via internal efficiencies, union/political negotiations on working time and competitiveness, and leveraging Eastern European sites (Hungary, Poland, Romania) to lower the weighted average production cost. Management reiterates two core priorities: executing the product/technology plan (MB.OS, ADAS, BEVs, new launches) and sustaining performance via productivity, resilience, disciplined capital allocation, and continued shareholder returns, even in a highly uncertain macro and geopolitical backdrop.

\- Mercedes-Benz is running a multi-year “performance enhancement” and productivity programme that sits alongside its largest-ever product and tech offensive, targeting structurally better margins, lower cost, and stronger cash conversion across Cars, Vans, and Financial Services. Mercedes achieved a 19% fixed-cost reduction between 2019 and 2024 (net of inflation) and is targeting a further >10% cut in the next three years, primarily via headcount optimization, management reduction, and process streamlining. The group plans to lower production costs by 10% by 2027, on top of earlier capacity adjustments, using logistics efficiency, increased use of AI/digitalisation, and higher reliance on renewable energy across plants. A Germany-focused “Productivity Initiative” aims to address structurally high labour and energy costs through internal measures (process simplification, efficiency drives) and negotiations with unions and policymakers on working-time and competitiveness. Mercedes is shifting more volume to cost-attractive locations such as Hungary, Romania, and Poland, with the share of low-cost countries in the production footprint planned to rise from roughly 15% to 30%, lowering the weighted average manufacturing cost.

\- The company targets an increase in local-for-local production from 60% to 70% by 2027, reducing currency exposure, logistics costs, and tariff risk while speeding up response to regional demand shifts. Expanded capacity at Kecskemét (Hungary) enables European production of electric C-Class, GLC and compact G derivatives, enhancing flexibility and resilience of the network and supporting margin via lower unit cost. Under the “Next Level Production” strategy, Mercedes will introduce more than 40 vehicles across its global network in three years, with mixed BEV/ICE/hybrid assembly on the same lines to maximize asset utilization.

\- Mercedes uses Daimler Truck stake disposals and other M&A (such as the Athlon sale) to boost free cash flow and net cash. CFO Harald Wilhelm explicitly highlighted the “first disposals” of €417m of the Daimler Truck stake in April and June of 2Q26, and then noted that there had been additional divestments of around €600mn in July, indicating a phased, opportunistic reduction of the holding rather than a one-off exit. These stake sales are a component of Mercedes’ broader capital allocation strategy, supporting both shareholder returns (€5bn cash return in 1H26 composed of dividends and share buybacks) and an additional €1bn buyback commitment by the 2027 AGM. Harald Wilhelm stressed flexibility in how Daimler Truck shares are sold (piecemeal or potential ABBs, market condition dependent), preserving optionality on timing, discount, and market impact.

## QUICK TAKE RECAP

The results were ahead of the company compiled consensus, with a Cars adjusted margin at 4.0%, higher than the 3.5% consensus and well within the 3-5% FY26 guidance range, which was reiterated today. Management had recently hinted at a potential impairment in the Chinese JVs and this was indeed reflected in a €704m impairment which reduced the reported Cars EBIT to €49m in 2Q26 (consensus for Cars reported EBIT was €654m). Mercedes share of the equity result of BBAC, the main China JV, swung to a negative €560m in 2Q26 from a positive €115m in 1Q26, reflecting the impact of the impairment. Mercedes notes the impairment did not result in a corresponding cash outflow in 2Q26. The other noteworthy aspect was the €417m boost to free cash flow from the drip fed sale of part of its stake of Daimler Truck Group over the quarter, which realized a €160m gain, booked in the reconciliations line.

Revenue: 2Q26 Group revenues of €32.1bn, 1% ahead of consensus of €31.9bn Exhibit 1. Mercedes-Benz Cars' and Mercedes-Benz Vans' revenues were €22.99bn and €4.46bn respectively, both 1% ahead of consensus. Daimler Mobility revenue of €6.2bn was in line with consensus expectations.

Adj. EBIT: 2Q26 Group adj. EBIT at €2.3bn, for a corresponding adj. EBIT margin of 7.2%. This was 40% ahead of the €1.64bn consensus expectation and 200bps ahead on the margin. This beat was driven by all segments. Cars adj. EBIT came in at €909m for a margin of 4.0%, 13% ahead of consensus at €804m (3.5% margin).
