EN_B: Because the beat had some one-off factors. Part of the profit came from selling Daimler Truck shares and from liability revaluations related to buybacks. More importantly, China is becoming a real drag.
EN_A: How bad is China?
EN_B: The report highlights that the China joint venture, BBAC, swung from a positive 111 million euro contribution a year ago to a negative 560 million euros this quarter. That was mainly due to a 796 million euro non-cash impairment, reflecting lower expected future returns.
EN_A: So Mercedes is losing money in China?
EN_B: Not exactly losing money, but the pressure is real. The Chinese market shrank by about 20%. Mercedes is avoiding price wars and focusing on premium positioning. Its new EVs are flying off the shelf in Europe, up 87%, but in China they are being more cautious.
EN_A: Europe is a bright spot. What is Mercedes doing to lower costs?
EN_B: A big move is expanding its plant in Kecskemét, Hungary. Capacity there doubled to 400,000 units. Factory costs there are about 70% lower than in Germany. Mercedes plans to raise the share of production from low-cost countries from 15% to 30%.
EN_A: So they are using Eastern Europe to offset high German costs?
EN_B: Exactly. They also have a Germany-focused productivity initiative, targeting another 10% fixed cost reduction by 2027. But the report notes that this involves negotiations with unions and politicians, so it’s not a sure thing.
EN_A: What about shareholder returns? Can they keep up the buybacks?
EN_B: Mercedes returned 5 billion euros to shareholders in the first half. And they added another 1 billion buyback. But here’s the catch: a big chunk of that cash comes from selling their stake in Daimler Truck.
EN_A: So if Daimler Truck’s stock falls, or they run out of shares to sell, the buybacks could slow down?
EN_B: That’s exactly the question the report doesn’t fully answer. It only says the stake sales are “opportunistic and phased.” Investors need to judge how sustainable this capital return really is.

EN_B: The analyst cut the price target from 61 euros to 56 euros. The main reason was increasing the discount on the China business from 20% to 25%. So the uncertainty in China is now a key factor in how the whole company is valued.
