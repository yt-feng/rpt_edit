【金字塔原理写作原则】
1. 结论先行：文章开头先回答“这份报告最值得看的判断是什么”，而不是介绍背景。
2. 统领思想：全文只能服务一个主判断，避免变成摘要合集。
3. 纵向回答：每一层都要回答上一层提出的“为什么”或“所以呢”。
4. 横向 MECE：每个一级小节必须彼此独立、共同支撑主判断，避免重叠。
5. Synthesis over summary：不要复述报告段落，要提炼“这些事实合在一起意味着什么”。

## 2Q26 RESULTS AND KEY TAKEAWAYS

\- Mercedes-Benz delivered solid 2Q26 results in a highly challenging environment, with group EBIT up 22% year-on-year to €1.55bn and free cash flow of €1.10bn, despite softer demand, geopolitical headwinds, and intense competition in China.

At the group level, Mercedes expects 2026 revenue slightly below last year's €132.2bn (driven by lower Passenger Car volume), EBIT significantly above 2025's €5.8bn, and Industrial FCF slightly below 2025's €5.4bn, supported by both organic cash generation and M&A (including further Daimler Truck stake monetization and Athlon disposal) and an additional €1bn share buyback to be completed some weeks before the April 2027 AGM.

\- Mercedes Passenger Cars printed €909m adjusted EBIT (-26% yoy) for a 4% adjusted return on sales in 2Q26 (5.1% in 2Q25), within the 3–5% full-year guidance range, supported by efficiencies in purchasing/manufacturing and positive industrial performance despite China-related headwinds. Mercedes now expects full-year Passenger Car sales slightly below 2025 due to China, but raised xEV share guidance to 23–25% and confirmed 3–5% adjusted RoS, guiding towards the lower half of the range given raw-material, energy, logistics, depreciation, tariff, and higher BEV-mix headwinds.

\- Vans division remains a profit benchmark, with 94,075 units (+1% yoy), €4.45bn revenue (+5% yoy), €454m adjusted EBIT (+3% yoy), and 10.2% adjusted RoS (10.4% in 2Q25). xEV vans were up 46% YoY, lifting xEV share to 10.7%, as VLE series production began in June. \- Industrial strategy focuses on resilience and cost competitiveness, including a global productivity initiative centered on Germany and expanded, lower-cost capacity in Kecskemét, Hungary for electric C-Class, GLC, and compact G derivatives.

A Germany-focused Productivity Initiative targets cost structure via internal efficiencies, union/political negotiations on working time and competitiveness, and leveraging Eastern European sites (Hungary, Poland, Romania) to lower the weighted average production cost. Management reiterates two core priorities: executing the product/technology plan (MB.OS, ADAS, BEVs, new launches) and sustaining performance via productivity, resilience, disciplined capital allocation, and continued shareholder returns, even in a highly uncertain macro and geopolitical backdrop.

\- Mercedes-Benz is running a multi-year “performance enhancement” and productivity programme that sits alongside its largest-ever product and tech offensive, targeting structurally better margins, lower cost, and stronger cash conversion across Cars, Vans, and Financial Services. Mercedes achieved a 19% fixed-cost reduction between 2019 and 2024 (net of inflation) and is targeting a further >10% cut in the next three years, primarily via headcount optimization, management reduction, and process streamlining. The group plans to lower production costs by 10% by 2027, on top of earlier capacity adjustments, using logistics efficiency, increased use of AI/digitalisation, and higher reliance on renewable energy across plants. A Germany-focused “Productivity Initiative” aims to address structurally high labour and energy costs through internal measures (process simplification, efficiency drives) and negotiations with unions and policymakers on working-time and competitiveness. Mercedes is shifting more volume to cost-attractive locations such as Hungary, Romania, and Poland, with the share of low-cost countries in the production footprint planned to rise from roughly 15% to 30%, lowering the weighted average manufacturing cost.

\- The company targets an increase in local-for-local production from 60% to 70% by 2027, reducing currency exposure, logistics costs, and tariff risk while speeding up response to regional demand shifts. Expanded capacity at Kecskemét (Hungary) enables European production of electric C-Class, GLC and compact G derivatives, enhancing flexibility and resilience of the network and supporting margin via lower unit cost. Under the “Next Level Production” strategy, Mercedes will introduce more than 40 vehicles across its global network in three years, with mixed BEV/ICE/hybrid assembly on the same lines to maximize asset utilization.

\- Mercedes uses Daimler Truck stake disposals and other M&A (such as the Athlon sale) to boost free cash flow and net cash. CFO Harald Wilhelm explicitly highlighted the “first disposals” of €417m of the Daimler Truck stake in April and June of 2Q26, and then noted that there had been additional divestments of around €600mn in July, indicating a phased, opportunistic reduction of the holding rather than a one-off exit. These stake sales are a component of Mercedes’ broader capital allocation strategy, supporting both shareholder returns (€5bn cash return in 1H26 composed of dividends and share buybacks) and an additional €1bn buyback commitment by the 2027 AGM. Harald Wilhelm stressed flexibility in how Daimler Truck shares are sold (piecemeal or potential ABBs, market condition dependent), preserving optionality on timing, discount, and market impact.
